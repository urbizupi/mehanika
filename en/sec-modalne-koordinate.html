Chapter 10 Vibration of systems with several degrees of freedom and modal analysis (nihanje sistemov z več prostostnimi stopnjami in modalna analiza)

10.4 Modal coordinates and free vibration (modalne koordinate in prosto nihanje)

Two-storey frame A (okvir A) from section 10.1 has two floors of mass \(m = 500\) kg and storey stiffness \(k = 501\,760\) N/m, so \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\) and \(\mat K = k\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\). We push on the upper floor with the force \(F = 2508{,}8\) N. The floors then move statically by \(q_1 = 5\) mm and \(q_2 = 10\) mm, because \(\mat K\vek q = k\,(2 \cdot 5 - 10,\ -5 + 10)\) mm \(= (0,\ k \cdot 5\ \text{mm}) = (0,\ 2508{,}8\ \text{N})\), as it must be for a force on the upper floor only. Then we suddenly remove the force, with no initial velocity. How does each floor move, and which part of the motion belongs to which mode?

Section 10.2 showed that the frame can vibrate in a single mode: both floors move along the same cosine with the same frequency. The initial shape (5 mm, 10 mm) is along neither of the two modes, so the motion is not that simple. Section 10.3, however, showed that every column with \(n\) elements is a sum of modes. So at every time \(t\) we can write the column \(\vek q(t)\) as the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\), except that the number \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) at each mode now depends on time. These numbers are the new coordinates. We will show that each of them satisfies its own equation, which contains no other, and that it is the equation of a system with one degree of freedom, known from chapter 9. The coupling that looks like a property of the frame in the coordinates \(q_1\), \(q_2\) is therefore a consequence of the choice of coordinates.

10.4.1 Modal coordinates (modalne koordinate)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric and \(\mat M\) a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\), and \(\Phi = [\boldsymbol\varphi_1\ \dots\ \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{n}}]\) their modal matrix (definition 10.15), so \(\Phi^\T\mat M\,\Phi = \mat I\) and \(\Phi^\T\mat K\,\Phi = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{\mathrm{n}})\) by lemma 10.16. By the same lemma \(\Phi^{-1} = \Phi^\T\mat M\). Hence for every column \(\vek q\) there is exactly one column \(\boldsymbol\eta\) with \(\vek q = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\), namely \(\boldsymbol\eta = \Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek q\). This is the expansion in modes from corollary 10.14, written with a matrix.

Definition 10.19 Modal coordinates (modalne koordinate)

Let \(\mat K\), \(\mat M\) and \(\Phi\) be as above and \(\vek q(t) \in \mathbb R^n\) the column of generalized coordinates of a system with \(n\) degrees of freedom. The modal coordinates (modalne koordinate, also principal coordinates, glavne koordinate) of the system are the numbers

\[\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q(t) , \qquad r = 1, \dots, n , \tag{10.22}\]

collected in the column \(\boldsymbol\eta(t) = \Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek q(t)\). It holds that \(\vek q(t) = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta(t) = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t)\).

Source: CK (11.8)-(11.9) PDF str. 344-345: "The coordinates \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t)\) will be referred to as principal coordinates or modal coordinates." GR (2.64) PDF str. 95-96: "vector of modal coordinates (also called normal coordinates)".

The number \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) tells how much of the mode \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is in the current column \(\vek q\). We normalized the modes with \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\), so with \(\mat M\) in kg and \(\vek q\) in m they have the unit kg\(^{-1/2}\) and the modal coordinates have m kg\(^{1/2}\). We introduced this unit ourselves by the normalization, so we always state it. If we instead normalize the modes so that the largest component equals 1, the number in front of the mode is in metres, and the share \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) in the sum \(\vek q\) is the same (GR PDF str. 97: the contribution of a mode does not depend on the scale of the mode).

Let us look at this for frame A. The normalized modes from section 10.2 are the columns of the modal matrix

\[\Phi = \begin{bmatrix} 0{,}023511 & 0{,}038042 \\ 0{,}038042 & -0{,}023511 \end{bmatrix}\ \text{kg}^{-1/2} ,\]

and with the largest component 1 they are \((0{,}618034,\ 1)\) and \((1,\ -0{,}618034)\), where \(0{,}618034 = 1/1{,}618034\) and \(1{,}618034\) is the ratio \(q_2/q_1\) of mode 1. For the initial displacement \(\vek q(0) = (5\ \text{mm},\ 10\ \text{mm})\) equation (10.22) gives \(\eta_1(0) = 0{,}24899\) m kg\(^{1/2}\) and \(\eta_2(0) = -0{,}022451\) m kg\(^{1/2}\). If \(\boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is the mode with the largest component 1, the coefficient in front of it is \(a_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q(0)\,/\,\boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}}\) (we divide by the norm of the mode, because \(\boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\boldsymbol\psi_{\mathrm{r}} \ne 1\)). We get \(a_1 = 9{,}472\) mm and \(a_2 = -0{,}854\) mm, which is the displacement of the upper floor in each mode. In both cases the contributions of the modes are equal, \(\boldsymbol\varphi_1\eta_1(0) = (5{,}854\ \text{mm},\ 9{,}472\ \text{mm})\) and \(\boldsymbol\varphi_2\eta_2(0) = (-0{,}854\ \text{mm},\ 0{,}528\ \text{mm})\). They add up to (5 mm, 10 mm), as they must (figure 10.12).

10.4.2 Decoupling of the equations (razklopitev enačb)

The question remains what the equation of motion does in the new coordinates. We write the linear equation from section 10.1 without damping as \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\vek q = \vek Q(t)\), where \(\vek Q(t)\) is the column of generalized forces. We insert \(\vek q = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\) and multiply the equation from the left by \(\Phi^\T\). The matrices \(\Phi^\T\mat M\,\Phi = \mat I\) and \(\Phi^\T\mat K\,\Phi = \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}})\) appear. Both are diagonal, so row \(r\) contains only \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\).

Theorem 10.20 Decoupling of the equations of motion (razklopitev gibalnih enačb)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric and \(\mat M\) a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\), \(\Phi\) their modal matrix with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{\mathrm{n}}\), \(\vek Q(t)\) a column of \(n\) functions of time and \(\vek q(t)\) a twice differentiable column with modal coordinates \(\boldsymbol\eta = \Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek q\). Then

\[\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\vek q = \vek Q(t)\]

holds at every time if and only if at every time

\[\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q(t) , \qquad r = 1, \dots, n . \tag{10.23}\]

The kinetic energy \(T = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\) and the potential energy \(V = \tfrac12\,\vek q^\T\mat K\,\vek q\) are

\[T = \tfrac12\sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n}\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2 , \qquad V = \tfrac12\sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n}\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2 . \tag{10.24}\]
Proof

We must show that multiplication by \(\Phi^\T\) turns the equation of motion into \(n\) separate equations and that nothing is lost in the process.

  1. By definition 10.19 \(\vek q = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\). The matrix \(\Phi\) does not depend on time, so \(\ddot{\vek q} = \Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta}\), and the equation of motion is \(\mat M\Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \mat K\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta = \vek Q\).
  2. We multiply from the left by \(\Phi^\T\): \(\Phi^\T\mat M\Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \Phi^\T\mat K\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta = \Phi^\T\vek Q\). By lemma 10.16 this is \(\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_{\mathrm{n}})\,\boldsymbol\eta = \Phi^\T\vek Q\). Row \(r\) is equation (10.23), because the \(r\)-th component of the column \(\Phi^\T\vek Q\) is \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q\). This proves one direction.
  3. The converse needs that multiplication by \(\Phi^\T\) loses nothing. From \(\Phi^{-1} = \Phi^\T\mat M\) (lemma 10.16) it follows that \(\Phi\,\Phi^\T\mat M = \mat I\). We multiply this from the left by \(\mat M\) and from the right by \(\mat M^{-1}\) and get \(\mat M\,\Phi\,\Phi^\T = \mat I\). Together with \(\Phi^\T\mat M\,\Phi = \mat I\) this means \(\Phi^\T\) has the inverse \(\mat M\Phi\) from both sides.
  4. If equation (10.23) holds for all \(r\), then by step 2 \(\Phi^\T\big(\mat M\Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \mat K\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta - \vek Q\big) = \vek 0\). We multiply from the left by \(\mat M\Phi\) and by step 3 get \(\mat M\Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \mat K\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta - \vek Q = \vek 0\). This is the equation of motion from step 1.
  5. The energies: \(T = \tfrac12\,(\Phi\dot{\boldsymbol\eta})^\T\mat M\,(\Phi\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}) = \tfrac12\,\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}^\T\Phi^\T\mat M\Phi\,\dot{\boldsymbol\eta} = \tfrac12\,\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}^\T\dot{\boldsymbol\eta}\), and likewise \(V = \tfrac12\,\boldsymbol\eta^\T\Phi^\T\mat K\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta = \tfrac12\,\boldsymbol\eta^\T\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}})\,\boldsymbol\eta\).

Source: CK (11.8)-(11.10) PDF str. 344-345 (decoupling), GR (2.64)-(2.68) PDF str. 95-96, (2.72)-(2.73) PDF str. 97 (energies). Steps 3 and 4 (converse direction) are not from the source.

The proof showed that each mode acts as an independent oscillator with one degree of freedom, with mass 1 and stiffness \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\), and that the system vibrates as if it were composed of \(n\) independent oscillators (GR PDF str. 96). The coupling has disappeared because we chose coordinates in which the energies are a sum of squares without mixed terms, and not because the system changed (GR PDF str. 97).

Let us test this on frame A. With \(\lambda_1 = 383{,}31\) 1/s\(^2\) and \(\lambda_2 = 2627{,}25\) 1/s\(^2\) (section 10.2) the equations

\[\ddot\eta_1 + 383{,}31\,\eta_1 = 0 , \qquad \ddot\eta_2 + 2627{,}25\,\eta_2 = 0\]

are for free vibration. Before the release the frame was at rest under the force \(F = 2508{,}8\) N on the upper floor, that is with \(\vek Q = (0,\ F)\). The modal forces (modalni sili), the right-hand sides of equation (10.23), are \(\boldsymbol\varphi_1^\T\vek Q = 0{,}038042\,F = 95{,}44\) N kg\(^{-1/2}\) and \(\boldsymbol\varphi_2^\T\vek Q = -0{,}023511\,F = -58{,}99\) N kg\(^{-1/2}\). At rest \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), so \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q/\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\), which is \(0{,}24899\) and \(-0{,}022451\) m kg\(^{1/2}\). We obtained the same numbers from equation (10.22). The initial condition for each mode is therefore its rest position under the force, and the release is, for each of them, a release from rest. Mode 1 dominates for two reasons that multiply: its stiffness \(\lambda_1\) is 6,854 times smaller than \(\lambda_2\), so for the same force its coordinate is larger, and its element \(\varphi_{21}\) is larger than \(|\varphi_{22}|\) in the ratio 1,618 (the golden ratio from section 10.2). The ratio of the coordinates is \(6{,}854 \cdot 1{,}618 = 11{,}09\), which is \(0{,}24899 / 0{,}022451\).

10.4.3 Free vibration (prosto nihanje)

What does the frame do after the release? Each modal coordinate is a solution of equation (10.23) with \(\vek Q = \vek 0\) and its two initial conditions. We know the equation \(\ddot\eta + \lambda\eta = 0\): for \(\lambda > 0\) it is solved by \(\cos\omega t\) and \(\sin\omega t\) with \(\omega = \sqrt\lambda\) (theorem 8.12). We took the matrix \(\mat K\) of arbitrary sign, so \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\) need not be positive either. Then the solutions are different, and the theorem lists all three.

Theorem 10.21 Free vibration of a system with several degrees of freedom (prosto nihanje sistema z več prostostnimi stopnjami)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric and \(\mat M\) a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\), \(\Phi\) their modal matrix with eigenvalues \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\), \(t_1 > 0\) and \(\vek q_0\), \(\vek v_0\) arbitrary columns. Then the equation \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\vek q = \vek 0\) has on \([0, t_1]\) exactly one twice differentiable solution with \(\vek q(0) = \vek q_0\) and \(\dot{\vek q}(0) = \vek v_0\), namely

\[\vek q(t) = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) , \qquad \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q_0 , \quad \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek v_0 , \tag{10.25}\]

where

  1. for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} = \sqrt{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}\): \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\cos\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t + \dfrac{\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)}{\omega_{\mathrm{r}}}\sin\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t\),
  2. for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\): \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) + \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\,t\),
  3. for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm{r}} = \sqrt{-\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}\): \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\cosh\nu_{\mathrm{r}} t + \dfrac{\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)}{\nu_{\mathrm{r}}}\sinh\nu_{\mathrm{r}} t\).

The energy of each mode, \(E_{\mathrm{r}} = \tfrac12\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2 + \tfrac12\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2\), is constant, and \(T + V = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} E_{\mathrm{r}}\).

Proof

We must show that every solution gives modal coordinates with the stated properties, that the stated functions really solve the equations, and that the \(\vek q\) assembled from them solves the initial value problem.

  1. Let \(\vek q\) be a twice differentiable solution. By theorem 10.20 with \(\vek Q = \vek 0\) every modal coordinate \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q\) satisfies \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). The initial values are \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q_0\) and \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek v_0\), since \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\).
  2. The equation \(\ddot\eta + \lambda\eta = 0\) is linear with constant coefficients of order 2 and characteristic polynomial \(s^2 + \lambda\). By theorem 9.36 it has exactly one solution for given values \(\eta(0)\), \(\dot\eta(0)\). So \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) from step 1 is this solution.
  3. The stated functions are solutions. For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\), by theorem 8.12 \((\cos\omega t)'' = -\omega^2\cos\omega t\) and \((\sin\omega t)'' = -\omega^2\sin\omega t\), so \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\). For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\), by lemma 9.42, item 1, \((\cosh\nu t)'' = \nu^2\cosh\nu t\) and the same for \(\sinh\), so \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \nu_{\mathrm{r}}^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\).
  4. The initial values. For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) the formula at \(t = 0\) gives \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\cos 0 + \dfrac{\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)}{\omega_{\mathrm{r}}}\sin 0 = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\), and its derivative gives \(-\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\sin 0 + \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\cos 0 = \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\). For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\) the same with \(\cosh 0 = 1\), \(\sinh 0 = 0\) and the derivatives from item 1 of lemma 9.42. For \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) the function \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) + \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\,t\) at \(t = 0\) equals \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\) and has the derivative \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\). By step 2 the stated functions are the only solutions.
  5. Existence. With the stated \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) we assemble \(\vek q = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\). By step 3 and the converse direction of theorem 10.20 (with \(\vek Q = \vek 0\)) \(\vek q\) solves the equation of motion. At \(t = 0\), \(\vek q(0) = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q_0 = \vek q_0\) by corollary 10.14, and with the same expansion for \(\vek v_0\) we get \(\dot{\vek q}(0) = \vek v_0\).
  6. Uniqueness. If \(\vek q\) and \(\tilde{\vek q}\) are solutions with the same initial values, by step 1 they have the same modal coordinates, so \(\vek q = \Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta = \tilde{\vek q}\).
  7. Energy: \(\dot{E}_{\mathrm{r}} = \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\,(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}) = 0\), so \(E_{\mathrm{r}}\) is constant (corollary 1.85). The sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} E_{\mathrm{r}}\) equals \(T + V\) by equation (10.24).

Source: CK (11.14)-(11.18) PDF str. 345-346, GR razdelek 2.5, (2.64)-(2.71) PDF str. 95-97 (case \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) with unwinding from the sine and cosine), GR PDF str. 99-100 (constant energy). The cases \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} \le 0\) and the proof of uniqueness by theorem 9.36 are not from the source.

The proof showed that the \(2n\) numbers in \(\vek q_0\) and \(\vek v_0\) determine exactly the \(2n\) coefficients \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\) and \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\), and that the motion of a system with \(n\) degrees of freedom arises from \(n\) independent oscillators. The case \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) is in section 10.5. The case \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\) corresponds to unstable equilibrium: since \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat K\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\), the matrix \(\mat K\) has a column with negative quadratic form, and theorem 8.119 says that the equilibrium is not stable. The formula from item 3 shows how: for \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = 0\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) \ne 0\), \(|\eta_{\mathrm{r}}|\) grows for large \(t\) like \(\tfrac12\,|\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)|\,\mathrm e^{\nu_{\mathrm{r}} t}\), because \(\cosh\nu t\) is close to \(\tfrac12\,\mathrm e^{\nu t}\).

10.4.4 Frame A after the release (okvir A po sprostitvi)

The initial conditions are \(\vek q_0 = (5\ \text{mm},\ 10\ \text{mm})\) and \(\vek v_0 = \vek 0\), so \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = 0\). By item 1 of theorem 10.21, \(\eta_1(t) = 0{,}24899\cos(19{,}578\,t)\) and \(\eta_2(t) = -0{,}022451\cos(51{,}257\,t)\) in m kg\(^{1/2}\), where \(t\) is in seconds, and the motion of the floors by equation (10.25) is

\[\vek q(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 5{,}854 \\ 9{,}472 \end{bmatrix}\cos(19{,}578\,t)\ \text{mm} + \begin{bmatrix} -0{,}854 \\ 0{,}528 \end{bmatrix}\cos(51{,}257\,t)\ \text{mm} . \tag{10.26}\]

Figure 10.13 shows both floors and both modal coordinates in the first two seconds. Above, the motion looks complicated: the excursions of the floors are not equal, the curves do not repeat with the same shape. Below, the modal coordinates are two pure cosines: the large one with period \(T_1 = 0{,}32093\) s and the small one with period \(T_2 = 0{,}12258\) s.

The motion never repeats exactly. It would repeat at a time \(\tau > 0\) if \(\vek q(\tau) = \vek q(0)\) (and \(\dot{\vek q}(\tau) = \vek 0\)). From \(\vek q(\tau) = \vek q(0)\) by equation (10.26) we get \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\,(\cos\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\tau - 1) = \vek 0\). Since \(\boldsymbol\varphi_1\) and \(\boldsymbol\varphi_2\) are independent (corollary 10.14) and both numbers \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\) are nonzero, \(\cos\omega_1\tau = \cos\omega_2\tau = 1\). So \(\omega_1\tau = 2\pi p\) and \(\omega_2\tau = 2\pi s\) with integers \(p\), \(s\), and the ratio \(\omega_2/\omega_1 = s/p\) is a fraction. For A, \(\omega_2/\omega_1 = (3 + \sqrt5)/2 = 2{,}618034\). Let us show that this is not a fraction.

  1. If \((3 + \sqrt5)/2 = s/p\), then \(\sqrt5 = 2s/p - 3\), so a fraction \(a/b\) with integers \(a\), \(b > 0\). Among all such representations we choose the one with the smallest \(b\) (every nonempty set of natural numbers has a smallest element).
  2. From \(a^2 = 5b^2\), \(a^2\) is divisible by \(5\). If \(a\) is not divisible by \(5\), then \(a = 5k + j\) with \(j \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}\) and \(a^2 = 5\,(5k^2 + 2jk) + j^2\), where \(j^2 \in \{1, 4, 9, 16\}\) leaves the remainder \(1\) or \(4\) on division by \(5\). This contradicts the divisibility. So \(a = 5k\).
  3. From \(25k^2 = 5b^2\) it follows that \(b^2 = 5k^2\), so by the same argument as in step 2 also \(b\) is divisible by \(5\), and \(\sqrt5 = (a/5)/(b/5)\) has a smaller denominator. This contradicts the choice in step 1.

So the ratio \(\omega_2/\omega_1\) is not a fraction and the motion never repeats. For example, after the first period \(T_1\) mode 1 is again at its start, but mode 2 has made 2,618 oscillations, so its phase is \(222{,}49^\circ\) and \(\cos 222{,}49^\circ = -0{,}7374\), hence \(\vek q(T_1) = (6{,}484\ \text{mm},\ 9{,}083\ \text{mm})\) and not \((5\ \text{mm},\ 10\ \text{mm})\). After half a period, \(\vek q(T_1/2) = (-5{,}545\ \text{mm},\ -9{,}663\ \text{mm})\).

Which part of the motion belongs to which mode is told by the energy. By theorem 10.21 the energy of mode 1 is \(E_1 = \tfrac12\cdot 383{,}31\cdot 0{,}24899^2 = 11{,}882\) J and of mode 2 \(E_2 = \tfrac12\cdot 2627{,}25\cdot 0{,}022451^2 = 0{,}662\) J. The sum is \(12{,}544\) J, the strain energy of the frame under the force, \(\tfrac12\,F\,q_2 = \tfrac12\cdot 2508{,}8\ \text{N}\cdot 0{,}010\ \text{m} = 12{,}544\) J. The share of mode 1 is 94,72 %, of mode 2 5,28 %, and these shares do not change. The kinetic and potential energy of the whole system, however, oscillate: within each mode the energy is exchanged between \(\tfrac12\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) and \(\tfrac12\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}^2\), and between the modes nothing passes (figure 10.14).

10.4.5 Bracket B after the weight is removed (konzola B po odstranitvi teže)

Another example where the initial displacement is not along a mode. Bracket B (konzola B) from section 10.1 has bars \(AC\) and \(BC\) with \(EA = 19{,}6\) MN (from chapter 4) and a mass \(m = 1000\) kg in the pin \(C\). The motion is in the plane, the coordinates are the horizontal and vertical displacement of the pin \(C\), and \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\). The weight \(m g = 9{,}81\) kN, close to the load of 10 kN from chapter 4, displaces \(C\) by \((-2{,}669\ \text{mm},\ -10{,}511\ \text{mm})\) relative to the position without the weight, that is \(\vek q_0 = \mat D\,(0,\ -m g)\) with the flexibility matrix \(\mat D\) from section 10.1. What happens if we suddenly remove the weight, as if the rope holding the load snapped? The bracket returns towards the position \(\vek q = \vek 0\), from which the displacement is measured, and vibrates.

The modes from section 10.2.2 are the unit columns \(\vek e_1 = (0{,}27653,\ 0{,}96101)\) at \(\omega_1 = 29{,}492\) 1/s and \(\vek e_2 = (0{,}96101,\ -0{,}27653)\) at \(\omega_2 = 89{,}164\) 1/s. For \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\), \(\mat M\)-orthogonality is ordinary orthogonality, so the contribution of mode \(r\) to \(\vek q_0\) equals \((\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\vek q_0)\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), regardless of the normalization of the mode (normalization multiplies \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) and divides \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) by the same value). We get

\[\vek e_1\cdot\vek q_0 = -10{,}839\ \text{mm} , \qquad \vek e_2\cdot\vek q_0 = +0{,}341\ \text{mm} .\]

The excursion of mode 2 is therefore only 3,15 % of the excursion of mode 1. The reason is the direction: the static displacement lies at the angle \(-104{,}25^\circ\) to the \(x\) axis, while the line of mode 1 is at \(-106{,}05^\circ\) (the direction of \(\vek e_1\) is \(73{,}947^\circ\), the opposite direction \(-106{,}053^\circ\)). The angle between them is \(1{,}80^\circ\) and \(\tan 1{,}80^\circ = 0{,}0315\), which is the ratio of the two contributions, since the contribution of mode 2 is the component of the static displacement perpendicular to the line of mode 1.

The energy of the modes is \(E_{\mathrm{r}} = \tfrac12\,m\,\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\vek q_0)^2\) and gives \(E_1 = 51{,}092\) J and \(E_2 = 0{,}463\) J. The sum \(51{,}555\) J is the strain energy under the weight, \(\tfrac12\,m g\cdot 10{,}511\ \text{mm}\). The share of mode 2 is therefore \(0{,}90\ \%\), and of mode 1 \(99{,}10\ \%\). The square of the ratio of excursions, \(0{,}0315^2 = 0{,}099\ \%\), would give nine times less. The difference is in the ratio \(\lambda_2/\lambda_1 = 9{,}14\): for the same excursion the stiff direction stores nine times more energy than the compliant one. The coordinate \(\vek q\) therefore soon after the release vibrates almost along the line of mode 1 with period \(T_1 = 0{,}21305\) s. A small transverse vibration with period \(T_2 = 0{,}07047\) s and amplitude \(0{,}341\) mm, however, prevents the pin \(C\) in the plane from moving along an exact line. In section 10.5 the same system without bar \(AC\) awaits us, where one of the eigenvalues falls to zero.
