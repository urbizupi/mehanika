Chapter 11 The boundary value problem of linear elasticity

11.6 Stresses, strains and equilibrium in cylindrical and spherical coordinates (napetosti, deformacije in ravnotežje v valjnih in krogelnih koordinatah)

The tube of section 11.1 has an inner radius of 40 mm and an outer radius of 60 mm, and it holds oil at a pressure of 30 MPa. What are the stresses in its wall? The Cartesian components \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) are a poor tool for this. Take two points of the wall, \((50, 0)\) and \((0, 50)\) mm. The tube is round, so the state is the same at both points: a stress that pulls along the wall in the circumferential direction and a stress that squeezes the wall in the radial direction. But at the first point the radius points along the axis \(x\), at the second along the axis \(y\), so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\) is the radial stress at the first point and the circumferential stress at the second. The Cartesian components change from point to point, although the state is the same at all points on the same radius. So we need the components in directions that follow the body: in the radial direction, in the circumferential direction and along the axis.

The coordinates of section 11.5 give us such directions. Each point has there its own triple of perpendicular unit vectors \(\vek e_1\), \(\vek e_2\), \(\vek e_3\), the local frame (lokalna baza), which turns from point to point. In this section we answer three questions. What are the components of stress and strain in this frame? How do we compute strains from displacements in cylindrical and spherical coordinates, and why does one strain contain a term \(u_{\mathrm{r}}/r\) without a derivative? And what are the equilibrium equations, in which the term \((\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})/r\) appears? We use the coordinates of section 11.5: cylindrical coordinates (valjne koordinate) \((r, \theta, z)\) and spherical coordinates (krogelne koordinate) \((R, \varphi, \theta)\) with the local frames \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}, \vek e_\theta, \vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) and \(\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}, \vek e_\varphi, \vek e_\theta\) (lemma 11.15).

11.6.1 Components in the local frame (komponente v lokalni bazi)

How do we measure the stress in the radial direction? By theorem 2.42 we know how the stress components change when the axes are rotated. The local system at a given point is such a rotated system, so its components need no new theory. They need only an agreement on what the indices mean.

Definition 11.19 Local components in orthogonal coordinates (krajevne komponente v ortogonalnih koordinatah)

Let \(q_1, q_2, q_3\) be orthogonal coordinates (definition 11.13) and \(P\) a point of their domain, and let \(\vek e_1, \vek e_2, \vek e_3\) be the local frame at \(P\), that is, a right-handed perpendicular system with the same origin and the same unit of length as the Cartesian one. Let \(\ten\sigma\) be the stress matrix, \(\ten\varepsilon\) the matrix of the small strain tensor and \(\vek u\) the displacement at the point \(P\) in Cartesian axes. The local components (local components) at \(P\) are the numbers

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{ij}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} , \qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\ten\varepsilon\,\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} , \qquad u_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek u\cdot\vek e_{\mathrm{i}} , \tag{11.24}\]

where the indices \(i\) and \(j\) run over \(1, 2, 3\). Instead of numbers we write the names of the coordinates, \(r, \theta, z\) for cylindrical and \(R, \varphi, \theta\) for spherical ones, so for example \(\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\), \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}\), \(u_{\mathrm{z}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\).

Source: SADD §2.7 (PDF p. 55) introduces the components of displacement and strain in cylindrical axes with (2.7.1), CM §3.5.4 (PDF p. 145) expands the tensors in dyadic form in the basis vectors of the coordinate system. The connection with the rotation of axes is ours.

What does this mean in terms of the earlier chapters? By definition 2.37 the rotation matrix \(\mat Q\) from the Cartesian system to the local frame is the matrix whose row \(i\) holds the components of the vector \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\). By theorem 2.42 and lemma 2.44 the stress matrix in the local frame is \(\mat Q\,\ten\sigma\,\mat Q^\T\), and its element in row \(i\) and column \(j\) is exactly \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\). So the components (11.24) are the components in rotated Cartesian axes \(\vek e_1, \vek e_2, \vek e_3\), rotated as the point \(P\) requires. By definition 2.25, and because stress is symmetric (theorem 2.36), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ij}}\) is component \(j\) of the stress vector on the face with normal \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\). Hence \(\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\) is the component \(\theta\) of the stress vector on the face that the coordinate surface \(r = \text{const.}\) has at the point \(P\), and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\), \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\) are the normal stresses on the faces with the three normals \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), \(\vek e_\theta\), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\).

For the tube the components really do not change from point to point. The stresses that we will compute for the tube later in this chapter are, at the radius \(r = 50\) mm, the radial stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} = -10{,}56\) MPa, the circumferential stress \(\sigma_{\theta\theta} = 58{,}56\) MPa, and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = \sigma_{\mathrm{rz}} = \sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}} = 0\). Now we take these two numbers and look at what they give in Cartesian components (figure 11.15). The point with \(\theta = 0\) is \((50, 0)\) mm, there \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\vek e_\theta = \vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\), so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -10{,}56\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = 58{,}56\) MPa. At the point with \(\theta = 90^\circ\), that is \((0, 50)\) mm, \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(\vek e_\theta = -\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = 58{,}56\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = -10{,}56\) MPa. At the point with \(\theta = 45^\circ\) we have \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(1, 1)\) and \(\vek e_\theta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(-1, 1)\). The stress matrix is \(\ten\sigma = \sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}^\T + \sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\vek e_\theta\vek e_\theta^\T\). Because \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) and \(\vek e_\theta\) are perpendicular unit vectors, namely \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\), \(\vek e_\theta\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_\theta = \sigma_{\theta\theta}\) and \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_\theta = 0\), and because \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) is perpendicular to both, these are by (11.24) the local components of this expression, and the matrix is determined by them, since the triple \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}, \vek e_\theta, \vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) of perpendicular unit vectors determines a rotation \(\mat Q\) and \(\ten\sigma = \mat Q^\T(\mat Q\,\ten\sigma\,\mat Q^\T)\mat Q\). In components we therefore have

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = \tfrac12(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} + \sigma_{\theta\theta}) = 24\ \text{MPa} , \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \tfrac12(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}) = -34{,}56\ \text{MPa} .\]

The same state is described at three points by three different triples of Cartesian components, but in the local frame by the same triple \((-10{,}56,\ 58{,}56,\ 0)\) MPa.

Why is this useful? For the tube, moving along the circumference or along the axis does not change the components in the local frame, while the Cartesian ones do change. For the sphere, they do not change when we move along a circle centred at the centre of the sphere. So in the next sections a single function of the radius, for example \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(r)\), will describe the stress in the whole wall.

11.6.2 Strains in orthogonal coordinates (deformacije v ortogonalnih koordinatah)

What strains does a displacement in the radial direction cause? Take a ring of radius \(r\) and the displacement \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = c\), equal everywhere, so a ring that expands uniformly (figure 11.16). Both ends of a short fibre along the radius move by the same number \(c\), so the fibre keeps its length and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} = \partial u_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial r = 0\). The arc of a fibre along the circumference between two radii whose angles differ by \(\Delta\theta\) was \(r\,\Delta\theta\) long and is now \((r + c)\,\Delta\theta\) long. It has lengthened by \(c\,\Delta\theta\), so relatively by \(c/r\). The growing ring is therefore stretched in the circumferential direction, although nothing stretches along the radius. The derivative \(\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial r\) does not contain this strain. It arises because the length of an arc between two radii is proportional to \(r\).

We make the same computation for an arbitrary displacement field. The derivatives of the displacement components with respect to the coordinates are not enough, because the local frame also changes when we move along the coordinates. The derivation will keep to this.

Theorem 11.20 Strains in orthogonal coordinates (deformacije v ortogonalnih koordinatah)

Let \(q_1, q_2, q_3\) be orthogonal coordinates (definition 11.13) with scale factors \(h_1, h_2, h_3\) and local frame \(\vek e_1, \vek e_2, \vek e_3\). Let \(\vek u\) be a displacement field that is continuously differentiable at the points of the domain, and \(u_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek u\cdot\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\) its local components, written as functions of the coordinates. Then the local components of the small strain tensor are

\[\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac12\Big[\frac{1}{h_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}} \\ &\quad + \frac{1}{h_{\mathrm{i}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\cdot\frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{i}}}\Big] , \\ &\frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}} \\ &\quad + u_{\mathrm{k}}\,\frac{\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\Big) . \end{aligned} \tag{11.25}\]
Proof

We must show that \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\ten\varepsilon\,\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\) equals the expression in (11.25). We write the strain matrix with the displacement gradient, and the columns of the gradient with derivatives with respect to the coordinates.

  1. By definition 1.115, \(\ten\varepsilon = \tfrac12(\mat H + \mat H^\T)\). By definition 11.19, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac12\big(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\mat H\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} + \vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\mat H^\T\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\big)\). By equation (1.41) (point 4 of lemma 1.101), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\mat H^\T\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k},\mathrm{l}}(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}})_{\mathrm{k}}\,(\mat H^\T)_{\mathrm{kl}}\,(\vek e_{\mathrm{j}})_{\mathrm{l}}\), and since \((\mat H^\T)_{\mathrm{kl}} = H_{\mathrm{lk}}\), this is \(\sum_{\mathrm{k},\mathrm{l}}(\vek e_{\mathrm{j}})_{\mathrm{l}}\,H_{\mathrm{lk}}\,(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}})_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\cdot\mat H\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\). Hence \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac12\big(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\mat H\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} + \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\cdot\mat H\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\big)\).
  2. By point 2 of lemma 11.16, \(\mat H\vek e_{\mathrm{j}} = \dfrac{1}{h_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\dfrac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\), and likewise for the index \(i\). Inserting into step 1 gives the first equality in (11.25).
  3. We expand the displacement in the local frame, \(\vek u = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} u_{\mathrm{k}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\), where \(u_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\) are functions of the coordinates. The partial derivative with respect to \(q_{\mathrm{j}}\) is the ordinary derivative with respect to \(q_{\mathrm{j}}\) with the other coordinates held constant (definition 1.25), so by the rules of differentiation (lemma 1.81, points 1 and 3), applied to each Cartesian component of the sum, \(\dfrac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}} + u_{\mathrm{k}}\,\dfrac{\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\Big)\). This is the second equality.

Source: SADD (2.7.1)-(2.7.3) (PDF p. 55), CM §3.5.4 (PDF p. 145-146). SADD expresses the gradient with the operator \(\nabla\) in coordinates and takes the derivatives of the basis vectors from (1.9.10) and (1.9.17). Our proof uses only lemma 11.16 and the product rule.

The theorem gives the strains if we know the derivatives of the local frame. For cylindrical and spherical coordinates the table of lemma 11.15 gives them, so we derive the formulas by the same procedure: first the derivative of the displacement with respect to each coordinate in the local frame, then an element of the gradient, then the symmetric part.

Corollary 11.21 Strains in cylindrical coordinates (deformacije v valjnih koordinatah)

Let \(\vek u\) be a continuously differentiable displacement field in the domain of cylindrical coordinates \((r, \theta, z)\). Then the local components of the small strain tensor are

\[\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial r} , \\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} &= \tfrac12\Big(\frac1r\,\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial r} - \frac{u_\theta}{r}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} &= \frac1r\Big(u_{\mathrm{r}} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\theta \mathrm{z}} &= \tfrac12\Big(\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial z} \\ &\quad + \frac1r\,\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial z} , \\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{rz}} &= \tfrac12\Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial z} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial r}\Big) . \end{aligned} \tag{11.26}\]
Proof

We must show that (11.26) follows from theorem 11.20 for the table of lemma 11.15.

  1. By lemma 11.15, \(h_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\), \(h_\theta = r\), \(h_{\mathrm{z}} = 1\), \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\theta = \vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) and all other derivatives of the local frame are zero.
  2. By the second equality of (11.25), inserting the derivatives of step 1,
    \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial r} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial r}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial r}\,\vek e_\theta \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial r}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}} , \\ \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial\theta} &= \Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad - u_\theta\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} \\ &\quad + \Big(\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta} + u_{\mathrm{r}}\Big)\vek e_\theta \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}} , \\ \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial z} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial z}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial z}\,\vek e_\theta \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial z}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}} . \end{aligned}\]
    In the derivative with respect to \(\theta\), the term \(u_{\mathrm{r}}\,\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\theta = u_{\mathrm{r}}\,\vek e_\theta\) contributes to the component \(\theta\) and \(u_\theta\,\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -u_\theta\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) to the component \(r\).
  3. Let \(A_{\mathrm{ij}} = \dfrac{1}{h_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\), the local components of the displacement gradient. From step 2 and \(h_\theta = r\), in the columns \(r\), \(\theta\), \(z\),
    \[\begin{bmatrix} A_{\mathrm{rr}} & A_{\mathrm{r}\theta} & A_{\mathrm{rz}} \\ A_{\theta \mathrm{r}} & A_{\theta\theta} & A_{\theta \mathrm{z}} \\ A_{\mathrm{zr}} & A_{\mathrm{z}\theta} & A_{\mathrm{zz}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial r} & \dfrac1r\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial\theta} - u_\theta\Big) & \dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial z} \\[3mm] \dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial r} & \dfrac1r\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta} + u_{\mathrm{r}}\Big) & \dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial z} \\[3mm] \dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial r} & \dfrac1r\,\dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta} & \dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial z} \end{bmatrix} .\]
  4. By theorem 11.20, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{ij}} + A_{\mathrm{ji}})\). The diagonal gives \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}}\), and off the diagonal \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{r}\theta} + A_{\theta \mathrm{r}})\), \(\varepsilon_{\theta \mathrm{z}} = \tfrac12(A_{\theta \mathrm{z}} + A_{\mathrm{z}\theta})\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rz}} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{rz}} + A_{\mathrm{zr}})\), which is (11.26).

Source: SADD (2.7.3) (PDF p. 55), App. A (A.2) (PDF p. 546), CM (3.5.26) (PDF p. 146). The formulas of the three sources agree. SADD writes the strains as \(e_{\mathrm{ij}}\) and the last shear component as \(e_{\mathrm{zr}}\), CM writes \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{z}\theta}\). The derivation from theorem 11.20 is ours, and the formulas are also verified with SymPy: the derivation from the Cartesian expressions by the change of variables gives zero difference for all six.

Corollary 11.22 Strains in spherical coordinates (deformacije v krogelnih koordinatah)

Let \(\vek u\) be a continuously differentiable displacement field in the domain of spherical coordinates \((R, \varphi, \theta)\). Then the local components of the small strain tensor are

\[\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{RR}} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial R} , \\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{R}\varphi} &= \tfrac12\Big(\frac1R\,\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\varphi} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial R} - \frac{u_\varphi}{R}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\varphi\varphi} &= \frac1R\Big(u_{\mathrm{R}} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\varphi}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\mathrm{R}\theta} &= \tfrac12\Big(\frac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial R} - \frac{u_\theta}{R}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} &= \frac1R\Big(u_{\mathrm{R}} + u_\varphi\cot\varphi \\ &\quad + \frac{1}{\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta}\Big) , \\ \varepsilon_{\varphi\theta} &= \frac{1}{2R}\Big(\frac{1}{\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\varphi} - u_\theta\cot\varphi\Big) . \end{aligned} \tag{11.27}\]
Proof

We must show that (11.27) follows from theorem 11.20 for the table of lemma 11.15. The procedure is the same as in corollary 11.21, only there are more derivatives of the local frame.

  1. By lemma 11.15, \(h_{\mathrm{R}} = 1\), \(h_\varphi = R\), \(h_\theta = R\sin\varphi\) and \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\varphi = \vek e_\varphi\), \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\theta = \sin\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\varphi/\partial\varphi = -\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}\), \(\partial\vek e_\varphi/\partial\theta = \cos\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -\sin\varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} - \cos\varphi\,\vek e_\varphi\). The other derivatives are zero.
  2. By the second equality of (11.25),
    \[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial R} &= \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial R}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial R}\,\vek e_\varphi \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial R}\,\vek e_\theta , \\ \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial\varphi} &= \Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\varphi} \\ &\quad - u_\varphi\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} \\ &\quad + \Big(\frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\varphi} + u_{\mathrm{R}}\Big)\vek e_\varphi \\ &\quad + \frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\varphi}\,\vek e_\theta , \\ \frac{\partial\vek u}{\partial\theta} &= \Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad - \sin\varphi\,u_\theta\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} \\ &\quad + \Big(\frac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad - \cos\varphi\,u_\theta\Big)\vek e_\varphi \\ &\quad + \Big(\frac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \sin\varphi\,u_{\mathrm{R}} \\ &\quad + \cos\varphi\,u_\varphi\Big)\vek e_\theta . \end{aligned}\]
    In the derivative with respect to \(\theta\), the term \(u_{\mathrm{R}}\sin\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\) comes from \(u_{\mathrm{R}}\,\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\theta\), the term \(u_\varphi\cos\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\) from \(u_\varphi\,\partial\vek e_\varphi/\partial\theta\), and the terms \(-u_\theta\sin\varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}\) and \(-u_\theta\cos\varphi\,\vek e_\varphi\) from \(u_\theta\,\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta\).
  3. The elements \(A_{\mathrm{ij}} = \dfrac{1}{h_{\mathrm{j}}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\vek u}{\partial q_{\mathrm{j}}}\) are, in the columns \(R\), \(\varphi\), \(\theta\) (with \(h_\varphi = R\) and \(h_\theta = R\sin\varphi\)),
    \[\begin{bmatrix} A_{\mathrm{RR}} & A_{\mathrm{R}\varphi} & A_{\mathrm{R}\theta} \\ A_{\varphi \mathrm{R}} & A_{\varphi\varphi} & A_{\varphi\theta} \\ A_{\theta \mathrm{R}} & A_{\theta\varphi} & A_{\theta\theta} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial R} & \dfrac{1}{R}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\varphi} - u_\varphi\Big) & \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial\theta} - \sin\varphi\,u_\theta\Big) \\[3mm] \dfrac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial R} & \dfrac{1}{R}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\varphi} + u_{\mathrm{R}}\Big) & \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\theta} - \cos\varphi\,u_\theta\Big) \\[3mm] \dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial R} & \dfrac{1}{R}\,\dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\varphi} & \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \sin\varphi\,u_{\mathrm{R}} + \cos\varphi\,u_\varphi\Big) \end{bmatrix} .\]
  4. By theorem 11.20, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{ij}} + A_{\mathrm{ji}})\). The diagonal gives \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{RR}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\varphi\varphi}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta}\) (in the last one \(\sin\varphi\,u_{\mathrm{R}}/(R\sin\varphi) = u_{\mathrm{R}}/R\) and \(\cos\varphi\,u_\varphi/(R\sin\varphi) = u_\varphi\cot\varphi/R\)). Off the diagonal, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{R}\varphi} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{R}\varphi} + A_{\varphi \mathrm{R}})\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{R}\theta} = \tfrac12(A_{\mathrm{R}\theta} + A_{\theta \mathrm{R}})\) and \(\varepsilon_{\varphi\theta} = \tfrac12(A_{\varphi\theta} + A_{\theta\varphi})\), where the last one contains \(\dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial u_\varphi}{\partial\theta} - \cos\varphi\,u_\theta\Big) + \dfrac1R\,\dfrac{\partial u_\theta}{\partial\varphi}\), and after factoring out \(1/R\) this is (11.27).

Source: SADD (2.7.5) (PDF p. 56), App. A (A.3) (PDF p. 546). Both agree with our formulas, SADD writes the last two shear components as \(e_{\varphi\theta}\) and \(e_{\theta \mathrm{R}}\). The table of derivatives of the local frame is not from the source (lemma 11.15), so the derivation is not from the source. The formulas are also verified with SymPy: the derivation from the Cartesian expressions by the change of variables gives zero difference for a general displacement field for all six.

How do we test this? In both corollaries there appear terms without derivatives of the displacement components, which do not exist in Cartesian coordinates. SADD points out one of them next to (2.7.5): in spherical coordinates a uniform displacement \(u_{\mathrm{R}}\) causes the transverse strains \(\varepsilon_{\varphi\varphi} = \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = u_{\mathrm{R}}/R\), which we can picture as blowing up a balloon, where the points on its surface move apart (SADD, PDF p. 56). So the term belongs to the curvature of the coordinate system, not to the material. Let us look at three examples with the ring of chapter 1.

The expanded ring. Take \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = c\) and \(u_\theta = u_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\) with \(c = 10\) µm. By (11.26), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} = \partial c/\partial r = 0\), \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = c/r\) and the other components are zero. At \(r = 40\) mm, \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = 2{,}500 \cdot 10^{-4}\), and at \(r = 60\) mm it is \(1{,}667 \cdot 10^{-4}\). This is the same as the geometric computation at the start of the section: the circumference \(2\pi r\) lengthens by \(2\pi c\), relatively by \(c/r\). We check also with a Cartesian computation for the field \(\vek u = c\,(x, y)/\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}\) (SymPy): at the point \((40, 0)\) mm the only nonzero strain is \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = c/40\) and at the point \((0, 60)\) mm it is \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = c/60\). The circumferential direction is \(y\) at the first point and \(x\) at the second, as it must be.

The rigid rotation. Take \(u_\theta = \omega\,r\) with a small \(\omega\) and \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = u_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\), so we move each point along the circumference in proportion to its distance from the axis, like a rigid rotation about the axis \(z\) by the angle \(\omega\). By (11.26), \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = \tfrac1r\,\partial(\omega r)/\partial\theta = 0\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = \tfrac12\big(0 + \partial(\omega r)/\partial r - \omega r/r\big) = \tfrac12(\omega - \omega) = 0\) and all the other strains are zero. The rotation really does not deform the body, as theorem 1.134 says, since in Cartesian axes this field is \(\vek u = \omega\,(-y, x, 0)\) with a constant antisymmetric matrix. The terms \(\partial u_\theta/\partial r\) and \(-u_\theta/r\) cancel. This is a test that the formula for \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\) correctly adds the term due to the change of direction of the local frame.

The ring of the chapter on strain. Section 1.18.5 prescribed the strain of the ring with radii 40 and 60 mm in words: the fibres along the ring shorten by one thousandth, the fibres towards the centre keep their length, and the right angles between the two kinds of fibres stay right. In local components this is \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = -k\) with \(k = 10^{-3}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = 0\), so in one line. A field that gives it is \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) and \(u_\theta = -k\,r\,\theta\) (we have cut the ring, so that the angle \(\theta\) runs over \(-\pi < \theta < \pi\), like a fan that we close): by (11.26), indeed \(\varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = \tfrac1r\,\partial(-k r\theta)/\partial\theta = -k\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = \tfrac12\big(\partial(-k r\theta)/\partial r - (-k r\theta)/r\big) = \tfrac12(-k\theta + k\theta) = 0\). But the displacement \(u_\theta\) has a jump at the cut: \(u_\theta(\pi) - u_\theta(-\pi) = -2\pi k\,r\), which is \(-0{,}2513\) mm at \(r = 40\) mm and \(-0{,}3770\) mm at \(r = 60\) mm. So the ring of chapter 1 has strains that satisfy the compatibility equations, but the displacement is not a continuous function on the whole ring. We will meet this jump again later in this chapter, in the stress function in polar coordinates.

11.6.3 Equilibrium equations in orthogonal coordinates (ravnotežne enačbe v ortogonalnih koordinatah)

Why does the equilibrium equation in the radial direction have the term \((\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})/r\)? In Cartesian coordinates it is absent, because an element of the body has parallel faces, but in cylindrical coordinates the element is curved. Let us look at the simplest case: an element of the tube between the radii \(r\) and \(r + \Delta r\), between the angles \(\theta - \Delta\theta/2\) and \(\theta + \Delta\theta/2\) and with the height \(\Delta z\) (figure 11.17). The stress is axisymmetric, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\) and \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\) depend only on \(r\), there are no shear components, and the body force is \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\). We add up the radial components of the forces on the six faces.

  1. On the outer face with radius \(r + \Delta r\) the area is \((r + \Delta r)\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\) and the force in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(r + \Delta r)\,(r + \Delta r)\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\). On the inner face the outward normal is \(-\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), the area is \(r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\) and the force in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) is \(-\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(r)\,r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\). Together this is the difference of the products \(r\,\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\) on the two faces, so \(\big[(r\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}})(r + \Delta r) - (r\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}})(r)\big]\Delta\theta\,\Delta z \approx \dfrac{\mathrm{d}(r\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}})}{\mathrm{d}r}\,\Delta r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\). The outer face is larger than the inner one, so the contribution differs from zero even for a constant stress: \(\mathrm{d}(r\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}})/\mathrm{d}r = \sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} + r\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}/\mathrm{d}r\), and the term \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\) is the one that comes from the larger area.
  2. On the side face with the angle \(\theta + \Delta\theta/2\) the area is \(\Delta r\,\Delta z\), the outward normal is \(\vek e_\theta(\theta + \Delta\theta/2)\) and the force is \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\vek e_\theta(\theta + \Delta\theta/2)\). Its component in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}(\theta)\), that is the central direction of the radius, is \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\big(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}(\theta)\cdot\vek e_\theta(\theta + \Delta\theta/2)\big) = -\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\sin(\Delta\theta/2)\), since for every real number \(\delta\), by the addition formulas (corollary 8.15, applied to \(\sin(\theta + \delta)\) and \(\cos(\theta + \delta)\)), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}(\theta)\cdot\vek e_\theta(\theta + \delta) = -\cos\theta\sin(\theta + \delta) + \sin\theta\cos(\theta + \delta) = -\sin\delta\,(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) = -\sin\delta\) (lemma 8.10, point 1). There are no shear components, so the stress vector on this face is \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\vek e_\theta\).
  3. On the face with the angle \(\theta - \Delta\theta/2\) the normal is \(-\vek e_\theta(\theta - \Delta\theta/2)\), so the component of the force in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}(\theta)\) is \(-\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\big(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}(\theta)\cdot\vek e_\theta(\theta - \Delta\theta/2)\big) = -\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\sin(\Delta\theta/2)\), because by step 2 with \(\delta = -\Delta\theta/2\) and \(\sin(-x) = -\sin x\) (lemma 8.10, point 4) the scalar product is \(+\sin(\Delta\theta/2)\). Both contributions point towards the axis, because the side faces are not parallel: their normals differ by the angle \(\Delta\theta\). Together this is \(-2\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta z\,\sin(\Delta\theta/2) \approx -\sigma_{\theta\theta}\,\Delta r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\).
  4. The body force contributes \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\,r\,\Delta r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\), because the volume of the element is approximately \(r\,\Delta r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\). The element is in equilibrium, so the sum of the forces is exactly zero, while the sum of our approximations is zero only approximately. We divide by \(r\,\Delta r\,\Delta\theta\,\Delta z\). As \(\Delta r\), \(\Delta\theta\) and \(\Delta z\) tend to zero, the approximations approach the exact values and what remains is \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}}{\mathrm{d}r} + \dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}}{r} + b_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). This is an idea, not a proof: we have not shown here that the errors of the approximations vanish in the limit. Theorem 11.23 below proves the equation without the element.

In this we used two approximations, \(\Delta r \to 0\) and \(\Delta\theta \to 0\). Are they good? Let us try on the field \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} = A - B/r^2\), \(\sigma_{\theta\theta} = A + B/r^2\) with \(A = 24\) MPa and \(B = 24 \cdot 3600\) MPa mm\(^2\), for which we will show later in this chapter that it is the stress in the wall of a 40/60 mm tube under an internal pressure of 30 MPa. Here we take it only as a trial field. At \(r = 50\) mm, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} = -10{,}56\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\theta\theta} = 58{,}56\) MPa, the derivative is \(\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}/\mathrm{d}r = 2B/r^3 = 1{,}3824\) MPa/mm and \((\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})/r = -1{,}3824\) MPa/mm, the sum is zero, as the equation requires for \(b_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). Let us test also a finite element between \(49\) and \(51\) mm with the angle \(\Delta\theta = 0{,}1\) and the height \(\Delta z = 1\) mm. There \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(49) = -11{,}985\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(51) = -9{,}218\) MPa. The force on the outer face in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(51) \cdot 51 \cdot 0{,}1 = -47{,}01\) N, and on the inner face the force in the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) is \(-\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}(49) \cdot 49 \cdot 0{,}1 = +58{,}73\) N, so the sum of the forces on the faces with a radius is \(+11{,}715\) N. The radial components of the circumferential forces, computed with the integral of \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}\) over the wall, give \(-11{,}710\) N. The remainder is \(0{,}005\) N, that is \(0{,}04\,\%\) of each term, and it comes from the two approximations. So the term \(-\sigma_{\theta\theta}/r\) really comes from the inclination of the side faces, and the term \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}/r\) from the larger outer area.

The general equation in all three directions has the same structure as the computation on the element. The force across a coordinate surface is the product of the area of the surface and the stress vector on it, and the areas of the faces of a local element are, by lemma 11.17, proportional to \(J/h_{\mathrm{q}}\). The curvature that we saw in the tube in the term \(-\sigma_{\theta\theta}/r\) comes from the derivatives of the local frame.

Theorem 11.23 Equilibrium in orthogonal coordinates (ravnotežje v ortogonalnih koordinatah)

Let \(q_1, q_2, q_3\) be orthogonal coordinates (definition 11.13) with scale factors \(h_{\mathrm{q}}\), Jacobian determinant \(J = h_1h_2h_3\) and local frame \(\vek e_{\mathrm{q}}\). Let \(\ten\sigma\) be a symmetric stress field with continuous first derivatives and \(\vek b\) the body force, written as a function of the coordinates. Denote \(\vek t_{\mathrm{q}} = \ten\sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{q}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\sigma_{\mathrm{kq}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\), the stress vector on the coordinate surface \(q = \text{const.}\) (with normal \(\vek e_{\mathrm{q}}\)). Then at every point of the domain the equilibrium \(\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma + \vek b = \vek 0\) (theorem 2.28) is equivalent to

\[\sum_{\mathrm{q}}\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\Big(\frac{J}{h_{\mathrm{q}}}\,\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\Big) + J\,\vek b = \vek 0 . \tag{11.28}\]
Proof

We must show that \(J\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma = \sum_{\mathrm{q}}\partial\big((J/h_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\big)/\partial q\), because then (11.28) follows from the vector equilibrium equation multiplied by \(J > 0\).

  1. Let \(\vek c\) be a constant vector. The vector \(\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma\) has component \(i\) equal to \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}}\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{ji}}/\partial x_{\mathrm{j}}\) ((2.31)). Hence \(\vek c\cdot\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} c_{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{\mathrm{j}}\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{ji}}}{\partial x_{\mathrm{j}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{j}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{\mathrm{j}}}\Big(\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\sigma_{\mathrm{ji}}\,c_{\mathrm{i}}\Big)\), where we took the constant numbers \(c_{\mathrm{i}}\) under the derivative (lemma 1.81, point 2). The inner sum is component \(j\) of the vector \(\ten\sigma\,\vek c\), so \(\vek c\cdot\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma = \operatorname{div}(\ten\sigma\,\vek c)\) (definition 4.13).
  2. The field \(\ten\sigma\,\vek c\) is continuously differentiable. By lemma 11.17, \(J\operatorname{div}(\ten\sigma\,\vek c) = \sum_{\mathrm{q}}\partial\big((J/h_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek e_{\mathrm{q}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek c\big)/\partial q\).
  3. Because \(\ten\sigma\) is symmetric (theorem 2.36), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{q}}\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek c = \vek c\cdot\ten\sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{q}} = \vek c\cdot\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\). The vector \(\vek c\) is constant, so by the rules of differentiation (lemma 1.81, points 1 and 2, on each component) we may take it out in front of the derivative with respect to \(q\), and \(J\,\vek c\cdot\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma = \vek c\cdot\sum_{\mathrm{q}}\partial\big((J/h_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\big)/\partial q\).
  4. The equality holds for every \(\vek c\), so also for \(\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\), which gives all three components of the vector equality \(J\operatorname{div}\ten\sigma = \sum_{\mathrm{q}}\partial\big((J/h_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\big)/\partial q\).

Source: SADD (3.8.6) (PDF p. 80), CM §5.3.2 (PDF p. 230-231). Both books obtain the equations with the operator \(\nabla\) in coordinates. The proof with lemma 11.17 is not from the source.

The theorem contains the derivative of the vector \(\vek t_{\mathrm{q}}\), which is expanded in the local frame, so we must differentiate it by the product rule, as for the strains.

Corollary 11.24 Equilibrium in cylindrical coordinates (ravnotežje v valjnih koordinatah)

Let \(\ten\sigma\) be a symmetric stress field with continuous first derivatives in the domain of cylindrical coordinates \((r, \theta, z)\) and \(\vek b\) the body force. Then the equilibrium is equivalent to the three equations

\[\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}}{\partial r} + \frac1r\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{\partial z} + \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}}{r} + b_{\mathrm{r}} &= 0 , \\ \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{\partial r} + \frac1r\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}}{\partial z} + \frac{2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{r} + b_\theta &= 0 , \\ \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{\partial r} + \frac1r\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta} + \frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}}{\partial z} + \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{r} + b_{\mathrm{z}} &= 0 . \end{aligned} \tag{11.29}\]
Proof

We must show that (11.29) follows from theorem 11.23 for the table of lemma 11.15.

  1. By lemma 11.15, \(J = r\), \(h = (1, r, 1)\), so \(J/h = (r, 1, r)\) for \(q = r, \theta, z\), and \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\theta = \vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), the other derivatives are zero. Equation (11.28) is \(\dfrac{\partial(r\,\vek t_{\mathrm{r}})}{\partial r} + \dfrac{\partial\vek t_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \dfrac{\partial(r\,\vek t_{\mathrm{z}})}{\partial z} + r\,\vek b = \vek 0\), and after dividing by \(r\) it is \(\vek t_{\mathrm{r}}/r + \dfrac{\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial r} + \dfrac1r\,\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \dfrac{\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial z} + \vek b = \vek 0\).
  2. Because of the symmetry of stress we write the components of the vectors \(\vek t_{\mathrm{q}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\sigma_{\mathrm{kq}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\) with indices in the order \(r, \theta, z\), so \(\vek t_{\mathrm{r}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\vek e_\theta + \sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\), \(\vek t_\theta = \sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} + \sigma_{\theta\theta}\vek e_\theta + \sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) and \(\vek t_{\mathrm{z}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} + \sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}\vek e_\theta + \sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\). The local frame depends only on \(\theta\), so \(\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial r\) is the sum of the derivatives of the components, and likewise \(\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial z\). In the derivative with respect to \(\theta\), by the product rule, \(\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\theta}{\partial\theta} = \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{r}} + \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\Big)\vek e_\theta + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\), because \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\theta = \vek e_\theta\) and \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\).
  3. The component \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\) of the equation of step 1 is \(\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}}{r} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}}{\partial r} + \dfrac1r\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big) + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{\partial z} + b_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), which is the first equation. The component \(\vek e_\theta\) is \(\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{r} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}}{\partial r} + \dfrac1r\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\Big) + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}}{\partial z} + b_\theta = 0\), where the terms \(\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}/r\) add to \(2\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}/r\), and this is the second equation. The component \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) is \(\dfrac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{r} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{rz}}}{\partial r} + \dfrac1r\,\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{z}}}{\partial\theta} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}}{\partial z} + b_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\), which is the third.

Source: SADD (3.8.6) (PDF p. 80), App. A (A.5) (PDF p. 547), CM (5.3.24) (PDF p. 231). CM writes the first term of the second equation as \(\partial\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{r}}/\partial r\) and \((\sigma_{\theta \mathrm{r}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta})/r\), which by the symmetry of stress is the same equation, and SADD writes \(\tau\) instead of \(\sigma\) for the shear components.

Corollary 11.25 Equilibrium in spherical coordinates (ravnotežje v krogelnih koordinatah)

Let \(\ten\sigma\) be a symmetric stress field with continuous first derivatives in the domain of spherical coordinates \((R, \varphi, \theta)\) and \(\vek b\) the body force. Then the equilibrium is equivalent to the three equations

\[\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{RR}}}{\partial R} + \frac1R\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} + \frac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{RR}} - \sigma_{\varphi\varphi} - \sigma_{\theta\theta} + \sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\cot\varphi}{R} + b_{\mathrm{R}} = 0 , \\ &\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{\partial R} + \frac1R\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} + \frac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{(\sigma_{\varphi\varphi} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})\cot\varphi + 3\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{R} + b_\varphi = 0 , \\ &\frac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{\partial R} + \frac1R\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\varphi} + \frac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\,\frac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} \\ &\quad + \frac{2\sigma_{\varphi\theta}\cot\varphi + 3\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{R} + b_\theta = 0 . \end{aligned} \tag{11.30}\]
Proof

We must show that (11.30) follows from theorem 11.23 for the table of lemma 11.15. The procedure is the same as in corollary 11.24.

  1. By lemma 11.15, \(J = R^2\sin\varphi\), \(h = (1, R, R\sin\varphi)\), so \(J/h = (R^2\sin\varphi,\ R\sin\varphi,\ R)\) for \(q = R, \varphi, \theta\). Because of the symmetry of stress we write the components of the vectors \(\vek t_{\mathrm{q}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}}\sigma_{\mathrm{kq}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\) with indices in the order \(R, \varphi, \theta\). We divide equation (11.28) by \(R^2\sin\varphi\) and use the product rule for the factors \(R^2\sin\varphi\) and \(R\sin\varphi\) (in the derivative with respect to \(R\), \(\partial(R^2)/\partial R = 2R\), in the derivative with respect to \(\varphi\), \(\partial(\sin\varphi)/\partial\varphi = \cos\varphi\)): \(\dfrac{2}{R}\,\vek t_{\mathrm{R}} + \dfrac{\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{R}}}{\partial R} + \dfrac{\cot\varphi}{R}\,\vek t_\varphi + \dfrac1R\,\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\varphi}{\partial\varphi} + \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\,\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\theta}{\partial\theta} + \vek b = \vek 0\).
  2. By lemma 11.15 the local frame depends only on \(\varphi\) and \(\theta\), so \(\partial\vek t_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial R\) is the sum of the derivatives of the components. In the derivative with respect to \(\varphi\), by the product rule, \(\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\varphi}{\partial\varphi} = \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} - \sigma_{\varphi\varphi}\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} + \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} + \sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\Big)\vek e_\varphi + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\varphi}\,\vek e_\theta\) (from \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\varphi = \vek e_\varphi\), \(\partial\vek e_\varphi/\partial\varphi = -\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}\), \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\varphi = \vek 0\)). In the derivative with respect to \(\theta\), \(\dfrac{\partial\vek t_\theta}{\partial\theta} = \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \sin\varphi\,\sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big)\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} + \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \cos\varphi\,\sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big)\vek e_\varphi + \Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \sin\varphi\,\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta} + \cos\varphi\,\sigma_{\varphi\theta}\Big)\vek e_\theta\) (from \(\partial\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}/\partial\theta = \sin\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\varphi/\partial\theta = \cos\varphi\,\vek e_\theta\), \(\partial\vek e_\theta/\partial\theta = -\sin\varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{R}} - \cos\varphi\,\vek e_\varphi\)).
  3. The component \(\vek e_{\mathrm{R}}\) of the equation of step 1 is
    \[\begin{gathered} \dfrac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{RR}}}{R} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{RR}}}{\partial R} + \dfrac{\cot\varphi\,\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{R} + \dfrac1R\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} - \sigma_{\varphi\varphi}\Big) \\ {}+ \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \sin\varphi\,\sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big) \\ {}+ b_{\mathrm{R}} = 0 \end{gathered}\]
    The term \(\sin\varphi\,\sigma_{\theta\theta}/(R\sin\varphi)\) is \(\sigma_{\theta\theta}/R\), and this is the first equation.
  4. The component \(\vek e_\varphi\) is
    \[\begin{gathered} \dfrac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{R} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}}{\partial R} + \dfrac{\cot\varphi\,\sigma_{\varphi\varphi}}{R} + \dfrac1R\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\varphi}}{\partial\varphi} + \sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\Big) \\ {}+ \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\theta} - \cos\varphi\,\sigma_{\theta\theta}\Big) \\ {}+ b_\varphi = 0 \end{gathered}\]
    The terms with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\) give \(3\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}/R\), and the terms with \(\cot\varphi\) give \((\sigma_{\varphi\varphi} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})\cot\varphi/R\), so the second equation.
  5. The component \(\vek e_\theta\) is
    \[\begin{gathered} \dfrac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{R} + \dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}}{\partial R} + \dfrac{\cot\varphi\,\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{R} + \dfrac1R\,\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\varphi\theta}}{\partial\varphi} \\ {}+ \dfrac{1}{R\sin\varphi}\Big(\dfrac{\partial\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{\partial\theta} + \sin\varphi\,\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta} + \cos\varphi\,\sigma_{\varphi\theta}\Big) \\ {}+ b_\theta = 0 \end{gathered}\]
    The terms with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}\) give \(3\sigma_{\mathrm{R}\theta}/R\), and the terms with \(\sigma_{\varphi\theta}\) give \(2\cot\varphi\,\sigma_{\varphi\theta}/R\), so the third equation.

In (3.8.8) the first terms of the second and third rows are printed as \(\partial\tau_{\mathrm{r}\varphi}/\partial R\) and \(\partial\tau_{\mathrm{r}\theta}/\partial R\) with a small \(r\), which is \(\tau_{\mathrm{R}\varphi}\) and \(\tau_{\mathrm{R}\theta}\) as in (A.6) and as in the other terms of these rows (a printing error of SADD, PDF p. 81, confirmed on the image of the equation). What have we shown? The theorem gave, from the single equation (11.28), the equilibrium in all orthogonal coordinates. Both corollaries developed it with the derivatives of the local frame, and every term without derivatives, for example \(-\sigma_{\theta\theta}/r\) or \(\cot\varphi\), is a derivative of the local frame that does not exist in Cartesian coordinates. 11.6.4 Hooke's law in local components (Hookov zakon v krajevnih komponentah)

We wrote Hooke's law in chapter 3 in Cartesian axes. Does it also hold for components in the local frame? Isotropy means exactly this: the rule that gives strains from stresses is the same in every perpendicular Cartesian system.

Corollary 11.26 Hooke's law in local components (Hookov zakon v krajevnih komponentah)

Let the material be isotropic and linearly elastic with modulus of elasticity \(E\), Poisson's ratio \(\nu\) (\(\nu \neq -1, \tfrac12\)) and Lamé constants \(\lambda\), \(\mu\) (theorem 3.11). Let \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ij}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}}\) be the local components at a point of orthogonal coordinates (definition 11.19). Then at this point the equations (3.12) and (3.13) hold with the indices \(x, y, z\) replaced by \(1, 2, 3\). For example in cylindrical coordinates

\[\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} = \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \nu\,(\sigma_{\theta\theta} + \sigma_{\mathrm{zz}})}{E} , \\ &\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} = \lambda\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} + \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}}) \\ &\quad + 2\mu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} , \\ &\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta} = 2\mu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{r}\theta} . \end{aligned} \tag{11.31}\]
Proof

We must show that theorem 3.11 is applied in the local frame.

  1. The triple \(\vek e_1, \vek e_2, \vek e_3\) is at the point a right-handed perpendicular system with the same unit of length as the Cartesian one (definition 11.19), so a perpendicular Cartesian coordinate system at the point.
  2. By theorem 3.11, the equations (3.12) and (3.13) hold at every point of the body and in every perpendicular Cartesian coordinate system, so also in the system of step 1.
  3. The components of stress and strain in this system are, by theorem 2.42 and lemma 2.44, the elements of the matrices \(\mat Q\,\ten\sigma\,\mat Q^\T\) and \(\mat Q\,\ten\varepsilon\,\mat Q^\T\), and by definition 11.19 these are the local components. The trace \(\tr\ten\varepsilon\) in this system is the sum of the diagonal local components, for example \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} + \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}}\).
  4. The equations (3.12) and (3.13), written in the system of step 1 with the components of step 3, are therefore equations with local components. For cylindrical coordinates the first three equations of (11.31) follow from them (\(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}}\) from the first row of point 1, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{r}\theta}\) from point 2 of the theorem).

Source: SADD (A.8) (PDF p. 548) for cylindrical and (A.9) for spherical coordinates. For the shear components SADD writes \(\tau\) and \(e\) instead of \(\sigma\) and \(\varepsilon\).

Isotropy is essential here. For a material with different properties in different directions we would have to rotate Hooke's law as well, and the form of the equations in local axes would change. So in the next two sections we can insert the strains of corollaries 11.21 and 11.22 into Hooke's law and then into the equilibrium equation, without returning to Cartesian components.

The general Navier-Lamé equations of section 11.2 could be obtained in these coordinates by the same procedure (SADD (A.11), (A.12), PDF p. 549), but we do not need them. The tube and the sphere will need only the radial equation with the displacement \(\vek u = u(r)\,\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), which follows from the corollaries of this section. The same equations in cylindrical coordinates will be a tool also for circular shafts in chapter 16.

With this we have answered the questions from the start of the section. The local components (definition 11.19) are components in rotated Cartesian axes that follow the body, so for the tube and the sphere they are the same all around the circumference. The strains and equilibrium in them follow from the general theorems (theorems 11.20 and 11.23) with the derivatives of the local frame that lemma 11.15 gives, and every term without derivatives (\(u_{\mathrm{r}}/r\), \((\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} - \sigma_{\theta\theta})/r\)) is a consequence of the curvature of the coordinates. Hooke's law does not change in these components if the material is isotropic (corollary 11.26).
