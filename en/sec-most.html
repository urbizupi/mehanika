Chapter 7 Basics of FEM for bars and trusses

7.7 A larger truss: a bridge (večje paličje: most)

With 12 joints and 21 bars nobody writes the equations by hand any more, there are 24 unknowns. What does the matrix of such a truss look like, and how do we know that the computed numbers are right? A program can run without an error and the model can still be wrong. So besides the results the section shows how we check the computation by hand and by energy, and a model in which the check of the reactions succeeds although the model is wrong.

7.7.1 The bridge model (model mostu)

Take a steel bridge (figure 7.26) with six bays of \(3\) m, so a span of \(18\) m, and a height of \(3\) m. The joints of the bottom chord (spodnji pas) are \(b_{\mathrm{i}} = (3i, 0)\) m for \(i = 0, \dots, 6\) and the joints of the top chord (zgornji pas) are \(t_{\mathrm{i}} = (3i, 3)\) m for \(i = 1, \dots, 5\). The bars are: six bars of the bottom chord \(b_{\mathrm{i}}b_{\mathrm{i}+1}\), four bars of the top chord \(t_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}+1}\), five verticals (navpičnice) \(b_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}}\), two inclined end posts (poševna stebra) \(b_0t_1\) and \(b_6t_5\) and four diagonals (diagonale) \(t_1b_2\), \(t_2b_3\), \(t_5b_4\), \(t_4b_3\), which descend towards the middle. There are \(21\) bars in all, each with cross-section \(A = 10\ \text{cm}^2\) and \(E = 196\) GPa. The cross-section is ten times larger than for the bracket in section 4.1.4, so that the largest stress, as we shall see \(45\) MPa, is well below the yield stress \(220\) MPa.

The bridge rests on a fixed pin support at \(b_0\), which prevents both displacements, and on a roller support at \(b_6\), which prevents only the vertical displacement \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) and allows the horizontal one (chapter 5). The joints are pins, as definition 4.4 requires, which also requires that forces act only at the joints. The deck is therefore carried by cross-beams (prečni nosilci) resting on the bottom joints, which pass to them its weight and the weight of traffic: at each of the joints \(b_1, \dots, b_5\) acts \(10\) kN downwards, \(50\) kN in all. The third assumption of the definition of a truss, that the weight of the bars is negligible, is poor for a bridge. The bars have a total length of \(15 \cdot 3\ \text{m} + 6 \cdot 4{,}243\ \text{m} = 70{,}46\) m (five verticals and ten chord bars of \(3\) m, six inclined bars of \(\sqrt{18}\) m), and their weight is \(70{,}46\ \text{m} \cdot 10^{-3}\ \text{m}^2 \cdot 7800\ \text{kg/m}^3 \cdot 9{,}81\ \text{m/s}^2 = 5{,}39\) kN, which is \(10{,}8\ \%\) of the load. This is not a small error. If we split the weight of each bar between its ends (still pins), the forces in the chords and inclined bars grow by about \(10\ \%\) and the sag in the middle grows from \(7{,}31\) mm to \(8{,}03\) mm (computed in NumPy). The model without the weight of the bars is therefore an example for learning the method and for comparison, not a design of a bridge.

There are \(24\) unknowns, three are prescribed (\(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) at \(b_0\), \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) at \(b_6\)), and \(21\) are free. The number of bars and reactions is \(21 + 3 = 24 = 2 \cdot 12\) (the count from section 4.7.5), so the truss is statically determinate if there is no mechanism. The count does not exclude a mechanism (section 7.5.3), so after the computation we check the pivots: all \(21\) are positive, the smallest is \(10{,}9 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, and the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite. We number the joints along the span, \(b_0 = 1\), \(b_1 = 2\), \(t_1 = 3\), \(b_2 = 4\), \(t_2 = 5\) and so on up to \(b_6 = 12\), so that neighbouring joints have close numbers and the nonzero entries of the matrix lie close to the diagonal. The unknowns of joint \(i\) are \(2i - 1\) and \(2i\), first the displacement \(u_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the direction \(x\), then \(v_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the direction \(y\), as in section 7.5.2. Below we also write the components of the displacement of a node as \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\), as in definition 7.34. The connectivity table (definition 7.10) is in the table of results below: each row has the numbers of the first and second joint of the bar, the unit vector points from the first towards the second.

7.7.2 Results (rezultati)

The program carries out the steps of the previous sections. For each bar it computes \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) by lemma 7.28, inserts the \(21\) matrices into the \(24 \times 24\) matrix \(\mat K\) (theorem 7.13), crosses out the rows and columns of the three prescribed unknowns (theorem 7.18) and solves the \(21 \times 21\) system by elimination. The results are the displacements, then the reactions from the rows of the prescribed unknowns and the forces in the bars from \(N = \tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}})\).

The reactions are \(25\) kN upwards at \(b_0\) and at \(b_6\), the horizontal reaction at \(b_0\) is zero. The sum of the vertical reactions is \(50\) kN, like the load, and the moment about \(b_0\) is \(25 \cdot 18 - 10\,(3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + 15) = 450 - 450 = 0\) kN m (lemma 7.30). The forces in the bars are in the table, positive for tension.

The bottom chord is in tension and the top chord in compression, as in a beam bent downwards by the loads. The forces are symmetric, for example \(N_{\mathrm{b}_0\mathrm{b}_1} = N_{\mathrm{b}_5\mathrm{b}_6}\) and \(N_{\mathrm{t}_1\mathrm{b}_2} = N_{\mathrm{t}_5\mathrm{b}_4}\) (we show why in the next subsection). The largest stress is \(45\ \text{kN}/10\ \text{cm}^2 = 45\) MPa in the compressed bars \(t_2t_3\) and \(t_3t_4\), which is \(20{,}5\ \%\) of the yield stress. The displacement of the middle is \(7{,}305\) mm downwards at \(b_3\) and the same at \(t_3\) (figure 7.27).

The vertical \(b_3t_3\) carries nothing. Why? At the joint \(t_3\) three bars meet, both bars of the top chord, \(t_2t_3\) and \(t_3t_4\), which are horizontal, and the vertical. Without a load at \(t_3\) the equilibrium of the joint in the direction \(y\) contains only the force of the vertical, because the horizontal bars have no component \(y\), so \(N_{\mathrm{b}_3\mathrm{t}_3} = 0\). The bar is not superfluous all the same. If we removed it, \(t_3\) would be a joint with two horizontal bars on one line and without a load, the row of the unknown \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) of the joint \(t_3\) in \(\mat K\) would be all zeros and the matrix would be singular (section 7.5.3). The vertical holds this joint.

Check by hand, with a section. We check the computation with the equilibrium of a part of the bridge (chapter 2, equations (2.2) and (2.35)), not with matrices. We cut the bridge at \(x = 7{,}5\) m (figure 7.26), where only the chord bars \(t_2t_3\) and \(b_2b_3\) and the diagonal \(t_2b_3\) are cut. On the left part act the reaction \(25\) kN at \(b_0\), the loads \(10\) kN at \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) and the forces of the three cut bars. A bar with axial force \(N\) acts on the left part with a pull towards the right part (for tension).

  1. Moments about \(b_3 = (9, 0)\) m: the diagonal and the bottom chord pass through \(b_3\) and have no moment. The reaction \(25\) kN upwards at \(b_0\), \(9\) m to the left, gives the moment \(-225\) kN m, the loads downwards at \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) (\(6\) m and \(3\) m to the left) give \(+60 + 30 = 90\) kN m. The top chord acts at the point \(x = 7{,}5\) m, \(y = 3\) m with the force \((N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{t}_3}, 0)\) and the moment \(-3\,N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{t}_3}\) (arm \(3\) m). The sum is zero: \(-225 + 90 - 3\,N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{t}_3} = 0\), so \(N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{t}_3} = -45\) kN.
  2. Moments about \(t_2 = (6, 3)\) m: the top chord and the diagonal pass through \(t_2\). The reaction gives \(-150\) kN m (horizontal arm \(6\) m), the load at \(b_1\) gives \(+30\) kN m, the load at \(b_2\) (\(x = 6\) m) has zero moment. The bottom chord acts at the point \(x = 7{,}5\) m, \(y = 0\) with the force \((N_{\mathrm{b}_2\mathrm{b}_3}, 0)\) and the moment \(+3\,N_{\mathrm{b}_2\mathrm{b}_3}\). The sum \(-150 + 30 + 3\,N_{\mathrm{b}_2\mathrm{b}_3} = 0\) gives \(N_{\mathrm{b}_2\mathrm{b}_3} = 40\) kN.
  3. Vertical forces on the left part: \(25 - 10 - 10 - N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{b}_3}\cdot\tfrac{3}{\sqrt{18}} = 0\), because the diagonal from \(t_2\) to \(b_3\) points downwards with the vertical share \(3/\sqrt{18}\). It follows that \(N_{\mathrm{t}_2\mathrm{b}_3} = 5\sqrt{2} = 7{,}071\) kN.

All three forces agree with the table: \(-45\) (bar 8), \(40\) (bar 3), \(7{,}071\) (bar 19). The second check is by energy. By Clapeyron's theorem (theorem 4.86) \(U = \tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\vek f = \tfrac12 \cdot 10\ \text{kN} \cdot (3{,}914 + 6{,}324 + 7{,}305 + 6{,}324 + 3{,}914)\ \text{mm} = 138{,}90\) J, and the sum over the bars by equation (4.5), \(\sum N^2 L/(2EA)\), is also \(138{,}90\) J.

What does the matrix look like? The unknowns are in the order \(u_{\mathrm{b}_1}, v_{\mathrm{b}_1}, u_{\mathrm{t}_1}, v_{\mathrm{t}_1}, \dots, u_{\mathrm{b}_6}\). The entry \(K_{\mathrm{ij}}\) is nonzero only if the unknowns \(i\) and \(j\) belong to joints connected by a bar or to the same joint (corollary 7.14). Of the \(441\) entries of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\), \(93\) are nonzero (of the \(576\) entries of \(\mat K\), \(114\)), all close to the diagonal (figure 7.28). The \(3 \times 3\) matrix of the space bracket is full, while the matrix of the bridge is about four fifths empty, and how fast we can solve it depends on this (this is the subject of a later section on sparse matrices).

7.7.3 Symmetry and the half model (simetrija in polovični model)

The bridge is symmetric about the vertical \(x = 9\) m: geometry and loads. The supports are not: the fixed one is on the left, the roller on the right. May we compute with one half only? COOK §2.11 (PDF p. 69) says that when the structure, the supports and the material are symmetric, symmetric loads give symmetric results, and that one half with suitable boundary conditions suffices. In the bridge the supports are not symmetric, so we first prove what holds. Let \(\bar i\) denote the joint to which the reflection in \(x = 9\) m maps the joint \(i\) (\(b_{\mathrm{i}}\) becomes \(b_{6-\mathrm{i}}\) and \(t_{\mathrm{i}}\) becomes \(t_{6-\mathrm{i}}\)), and let \(c\) be the displacement \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) at \(b_6\).

Lemma 7.38 Symmetry of the bridge solution (simetrija rešitve mostu)

For the solution \(\vek d\) of the bridge problem with supports at \(b_0\) (both displacements prescribed) and \(b_6\) (only \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) prescribed) and with vertical symmetric loads:

  1. the horizontal reaction at \(b_0\) is zero,
  2. \(u_{\mathrm{y}}(\bar i) = u_{\mathrm{y}}(i)\) and \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(i) + u_{\mathrm{x}}(\bar i) = c\) for every joint \(i\),
  3. the axial forces of a bar and of its mirror bar are equal.
Proof

We must show that the mirror image of the solution, shifted by a rigid displacement, solves the same system, and use uniqueness. We write the unknowns of all joints into a column \(\vek d\), so that \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \vek r\), where \(\vek r\) is the column of reactions, nonzero only at the prescribed unknowns (theorem 7.18).

  1. The sum of the horizontal components of the loads and reactions is zero by lemma 7.30. The loads are vertical and there is only one horizontal reaction, at \(b_0\) (the unknown \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) at \(b_6\) is free, so the reaction there is zero), so this one is zero. This is point 1.
  2. Let \(\mat P\) be the reflection matrix: \((\mat P\vek d)\) has at joint \(i\) the components \(\big({-u_{\mathrm{x}}(\bar i)},\ u_{\mathrm{y}}(\bar i)\big)\). The mirror bar has the same stiffness \(EA/L\) and a unit vector with the component \(x\) reversed, so its elongation under \(\mat P\vek d\) equals the elongation of the original bar under \(\vek d\). The mirror image of a bar is again a bar of the bridge, so the energy \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \tfrac{EA}{L}(\text{elongation})^2\) does not change under \(\vek d \to \mat P\vek d\), \((\mat P\vek d)^\T\mat K\,(\mat P\vek d) = \vek d^\T\mat K\vek d\) for every \(\vek d\). The matrices \(\mat P^\T\mat K\mat P\) and \(\mat K\) are symmetric and have the same quadratic form. So they are equal: for \(\vek d = \vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\) the diagonal entries agree, for \(\vek d = \vek e_{\mathrm{i}} + \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\) the form is \(A_{\mathrm{ii}} + 2A_{\mathrm{ij}} + A_{\mathrm{jj}}\), so the off-diagonal entries agree too. Hence \(\mat P\,\mat K\,\mat P = \mat K\) (\(\mat P^\T = \mat P\)) and, since \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\), \(\mat K\,\mat P = \mat P\,\mat K\). The loads are symmetric, \(\mat P\vek f = \vek f\).
  3. Therefore \(\mat K\,(\mat P\vek d) = \mat P\,\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \mat P\vek r\). The column \(\mat P\vek r\) is nonzero only at \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) at \(b_0\) and \(b_6\), because \(r_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0) = 0\) by point 1 (otherwise it would be nonzero at \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) at \(b_6\), which is a free unknown).
  4. Let \(\vek s\) be the column in which \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\) at all joints and everything else is zero. This displacement changes no elongation (the elongation \(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}}) = 0\) for equal displacements of the ends), so \(\vek s^\T\mat K\vek s = 0\) and by lemma 7.16 \(\mat K\vek s = \vek 0\). Hence \(\vek d^{**} = \mat P\vek d + c\,\vek s\) is also a solution of the rows of the free unknowns. The support conditions are satisfied: \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0)\) is \(-u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_6) + c = 0\) and \(u_{\mathrm{y}}(b_0) = u_{\mathrm{y}}(b_6) = 0\).
  5. The rows of the free unknowns give for both columns, \(\vek d\) and \(\vek d^{**}\), the same reduced system \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\), since the prescribed values of both are zero. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite (all pivots are positive, section 7.7.2), so the system has exactly one solution (theorem 6.29) and \(\vek d^{**} = \vek d\). The components give point 2.
  6. The elongation of the mirror bar at \(\vek d = \vek d^{**}\) is the elongation of the original bar at \(\vek d\) (step 2), and a rigid displacement changes no elongations. By \(N = \tfrac{EA}{L} \cdot \text{elongation}\) point 3 follows.

Source: COOK §2.11 (PDF p. 69). The proof is not from the source.

From the lemma: \(c = 2{,}755\) mm, so \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_3) = c/2 = 1{,}378\) mm, and for example \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_1) + u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_5) = 0{,}383 + 2{,}372 = 2{,}755\) mm. The displacements are thus symmetric up to the rigid displacement \(c\). What does this mean for the half model? It is as if we subtracted \(c/2\) from all displacements: the displacements \(\tilde u_{\mathrm{x}} = u_{\mathrm{x}} - c/2\) are antisymmetric in the mirror, \(\tilde u_{\mathrm{x}}(\bar i) = -\tilde u_{\mathrm{x}}(i)\), and on the axis of symmetry (\(b_3\), \(t_3\)) they are zero.

Lemma 7.39 Half model of the bridge (polovični model mostu)

Let the model \(H\) have the joints \(b_0, b_1, b_2, b_3, t_1, t_2, t_3\), the bars of the left half, where the bar \(b_3t_3\) has half the cross-section \(A/2\), the loads \(10\) kN at \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) and \(5\) kN at \(b_3\), and the prescribed unknowns \(u_{\mathrm{y}}(b_0) = 0\), \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_3) = 0\) and \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(t_3) = 0\). It has \(11\) unknowns. Its solution has in all bars the same axial forces as the full model (the bar \(b_3t_3\) half the force of the full bar, here zero), the same \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) and the displacements \(u_{\mathrm{x}} - c/2\).

Proof

We must show that the displacements \(\tilde{\vek d}\) of the full model, reduced by \(c/2\), satisfy the equations of the model \(H\), and use uniqueness.

  1. Let \(\tilde{\vek d} = \vek d - \tfrac c2\,\vek s\). Since \(\mat K\vek s = \vek 0\), we have \(\mat K\tilde{\vek d} = \vek f + \vek r\). By point 2 of lemma 7.38, \(\tilde u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_3) = \tilde u_{\mathrm{x}}(t_3) = 0\) and \(\tilde u_{\mathrm{y}}(b_0) = 0\), so \(\tilde{\vek d}\) satisfies the prescribed conditions of the model \(H\).
  2. The rows of the unknowns at the joints \(b_0, b_1, b_2, t_1, t_2\) contain only bars of the left half (every bar that meets these joints is in the model \(H\)), the same load, and on the right-hand side at \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0)\) there is the reaction \(r_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0) = 0\). These rows are therefore the same as the rows of the model \(H\).
  3. The row \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) of the joint \(b_3\) has contributions of the bars \(b_2b_3\), \(t_2b_3\) (left), \(b_3b_4\), \(t_4b_3\) (right) and \(b_3t_3\). The contribution of a bar to this row is the \(y\)-component of its force on the joint, \(\pm N\,n_{\mathrm{y}}\). By point 3 of the lemma the mirror bar has the same force \(N\) and the same component \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\) (reflection in the vertical does not change the component \(y\)), so the contribution is equal. The row is thus equal to \(2 \times\) (contribution of the left bars) \(+\) the contribution of \(b_3t_3\) with the full cross-section \(= f_{\mathrm{y}}(b_3) = -10\) kN. Halved, this is the row of the model \(H\): (contribution of the left bars) \(+\) the contribution of \(b_3t_3\) with the cross-section \(A/2\) \(= -5\) kN. The same holds for \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) at \(t_3\) (the load there is zero).
  4. The rows \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) at \(b_3\) and \(t_3\) in the model \(H\) are not equations, because these unknowns are prescribed, and their values are reactions.
  5. So \(\tilde{\vek d}\) satisfies all equations of the model \(H\). The matrix of the model \(H\) is positive definite (all \(11\) pivots are positive, the smallest is \(2{,}14 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, computed in NumPy), so the system has exactly one solution (theorem 6.29), and this is \(\tilde{\vek d}\). The axial forces and \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) of the full model are therefore equal in \(H\).

Source: COOK §2.11 (PDF p. 69): "Load on this octant is Px/2, because the original load Px is bisected by the symmetry plane" and "only half its stiffness is retained in the portion analyzed". The proof is not from the source.

The support at \(b_0\) is thus a roller in the half model, not a fixed support. The model \(H\) gives \(11\) unknowns instead of \(21\), all axial forces of the full model, the displacement of the middle \(7{,}305\) mm and the reactions on the cut \(+45\) kN at \(b_3\) and \(-45\) kN at \(t_3\). These are the horizontal forces with which the other half acts on this half. At \(b_3\) this is the force of the bottom chord \(40\) kN and the horizontal component of the diagonal \(t_4b_3\), \(7{,}071 \cdot \cos 45^\circ = 5\) kN, and at \(t_3\) the force of the top chord \(-45\) kN. Together they form a couple with the moment \(45 \cdot 3 = 135\) kN m, which is the bending moment of the bridge in the middle, \(25 \cdot 9 - 10\,(6 + 3) = 135\) kN m.

What happens if at \(b_0\) instead of the roller support we prescribe also \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0) = 0\), because the bridge is in reality fixed on this side? We compute with the model \(H^*\): the same as \(H\), only with a fixed support at \(b_0\) (figure 7.29). The solution has \(10\) free unknowns. The axial force of the bar \(b_0b_1\) is \(-5\) kN instead of \(25\) kN, of the bar \(b_1b_2\) \(-5\) kN instead of \(25\) kN and of the bar \(b_2b_3\) \(10\) kN instead of \(40\) kN, and the sag of the middle is \(5{,}469\) mm instead of \(7{,}305\) mm. The reaction at \(b_0\) is \((30, 25)\) kN, at \(b_3\) the horizontal one is \(15\) kN and at \(t_3\) the horizontal one is \(-45\) kN. We check the equilibrium of the reactions: \(\sum F_{\mathrm{x}} = 30 + 15 - 45 = 0\), \(\sum F_{\mathrm{y}} = 25 - 10 - 10 - 5 = 0\) and the moment about \(b_0\) is \(-(10 \cdot 3 + 10 \cdot 6 + 5 \cdot 9) + 45 \cdot 3 = -135 + 135 = 0\). The check of the reactions succeeds, but the model is wrong.

Why does the check of the reactions not catch the error? Lemma 7.30 says that the reactions balance the load for every solution of the system \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \vek r\), because \(\mat K\,\vek d_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek 0\) for every rigid motion (step 3 of lemma 7.30). This holds also for a model with wrong supports, since this is again a correctly solved problem, only a different problem. The correct solution has \(\tilde u_{\mathrm{x}}(b_0) = -c/2 = -1{,}378\) mm: the end of the bottom chord must move so that the chord can stretch. The model \(H^*\) forbids this, since it holds the joints \(b_0\) and \(b_3\), which are \(9\) m apart, in place horizontally. The chord cannot stretch, the forces in it change, the half of the bridge is supported too stiffly and the sag is too small by a quarter. The check of the reactions is necessary, not sufficient.

Other checks catch the error. The first is the horizontal reaction: lemma 7.38 says that the horizontal reaction at \(b_0\) is zero in the full model, while the model \(H^*\) gives \(30\) kN. The second is the computation with a section: the force in the bottom chord \(b_2b_3\) is \(40\) kN (step 2 of the check with a section above), while the model \(H^*\) gives \(10\) kN. The third is a comparison of the half model with the full one: the displacement of the middle must be the same, \(7{,}305\) mm. None of the three needs new knowledge about the bridge, they only require that we compare the written computation with what we know from equilibrium.
