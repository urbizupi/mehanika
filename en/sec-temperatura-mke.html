Chapter 7 Basics of FEM for bars and trusses

7.9 Temperature and bars of wrong length

The steel bar that we heated by 30 K in section 3.3.3 was pressed by two rigid walls with a force of 6927 N, because the walls did not let it lengthen. A bar in a truss is not clamped between rigid walls. Its ends are nodes, which move if the rest of the truss lets them. What does heating do to a truss? And what if a bar was made a millimetre too long and we must fit it into a place that is a millimetre shorter? In both cases no external force acts on the truss, yet the nodes move and forces can appear in the bars. We do not need a new theorem for this. Only the relation between the strain and the force in a bar must change, and everything else, the matrix \(\mat K\), assembly, supports and reactions, stays as in sections 7.1 to 7.5.

The model is the same as before. The truss is made of steel bars with pins (definition 4.4), \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\alpha = 15 \cdot 10^{-6}\) 1/K, and the axial stiffness \(EA\) is constant in each bar. The temperature of each bar is constant along the bar and differs by \(\Delta T\) from the reference temperature, at which the truss is free of stress, so the bar has the thermal strain of definition 3.13. Displacements are small, and everything is computed on the shape before loading (section 7.5). The examples from chapters 4 and 5 are already known: the wall truss with bars \(AC\) and \(BC\) (loaded with \(10\) kN, \(A = 1\ \text{cm}^2\) in each bar, \(EA = 1{,}96 \cdot 10^7\) N) and the same truss with the post \(CD\) (section 4.7.5).

7.9.1 Initial strain (začetna deformacija)

Take a single bar with no forces at its ends. If we heat it, it lengthens by \(\alpha\,\Delta T\,L\) and there is no force. If it was made \(\delta\) too long, its length without force is \(L + \delta\), and if we place it into a gap of length \(L\), it would have, measured against the gap, the strain \(\delta/L\) without a force. What the two bars have in common is that they would have a strain different from zero even if nothing acted on them. The relation between force and strain of a bar must therefore measure the strain from this value onward.

Definition 7.48 Initial strain (začetna deformacija)

Let a bar with axial stiffness \(EA\) be fitted between two nodes at distance \(L\), and let \(\varepsilon\) be its strain, that is the elongation between the nodes divided by \(L\) (a number without a unit). The initial strain (začetna deformacija) of the bar is the number \(\varepsilon_0\) at which the axial force of the bar is zero when \(\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0\), and the axial force is

\[N = EA\,(\varepsilon - \varepsilon_0) . \tag{7.46}\]

For a bar heated by \(\Delta T\) relative to the reference temperature, \(\varepsilon_0 = \alpha\,\Delta T\). For a bar that is \(\delta\) longer than the distance \(L\) between the nodes it connects, \(\varepsilon_0 = \delta/L\).

Source: FELIPPA (4.16) to (4.18) (PDF p. 58), COOK §2.10, (2.10-1) (PDF p. 67), ONATE1 (4.17) (PDF p. 147). A bar that is too long is not in the sources, we get it from the same relation.

Relation (7.46) is the one-dimensional row of Hooke's law with temperature (theorem 3.15). For \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\) we have \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}/E + \alpha\,\Delta T\), hence \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} - \alpha\,\Delta T)\), and the axial force is the stress times the cross-section, \(N = A\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\). It means only that in every step where we previously wrote \(N = EA\,\varepsilon\) we subtract \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\). For the bar between walls from section 3.3.3 we have \(\varepsilon = 0\), so \(N = -EA\,\varepsilon_0 = -EA\,\alpha\,\Delta T\), which is equation (3.18) multiplied by the cross-section. If the temperature varies along the bar, we take the average temperature of the bar. COOK shows that a linear temperature distribution, inserted as an initial stress at the ends, gives a stress that varies along the element, although it must be constant in the element, and therefore recommends the average temperature (COOK, PDF pp. 67-68).

The numbers we will need: for the steel bar from chapter 3 (\(EA = 1{,}5394 \cdot 10^7\) N, \(\Delta T = 30\) K) we have \(\varepsilon_0 = 15 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 30 = 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}\) and \(EA\,\varepsilon_0 = 6927\) N. For a bar of the wall truss (\(EA = 1{,}96 \cdot 10^7\) N) at the same temperature change \(EA\,\varepsilon_0 = 8820\) N. For the post \(CD\) of length \(3\) m that is \(1\) mm too long, \(\varepsilon_0 = 1\ \text{mm}/3\ \text{m} = 3{,}33 \cdot 10^{-4}\) and \(EA\,\varepsilon_0 = 6533\) N.

7.9.2 Nodal forces of an initial strain (vozliščne sile začetne deformacije)

How does the initial strain enter the equations of the method? Imagine an element whose nodes are prevented from moving in any way. A heated element would like to lengthen, but the nodes do not let it, so it is compressed with the force \(-EA\,\varepsilon_0\), like the bar between walls. The nodes must therefore hold it with forces that compress it. By the law of action and reaction the element pushes the nodes outwards with the same force \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\). When the nodes are not fixed, these forces act on them as a load. The following lemma states this exactly. It holds for a bar on the axis \(x\) and for a bar of a truss, where \(\vek n\) is the unit vector from the first node of the bar to the second (section 7.5.1).

Lemma 7.49 Nodal forces of an initial strain (vozliščne sile začetne deformacije)

Let element \(e\) be a straight bar of length \(L_{\mathrm{e}}\) with linear shape functions (definition 7.2), constant axial stiffness \(EA\) and constant initial strain \(\varepsilon_0\) (definition 7.48). Let \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the column of displacements of its nodes, for a truss in global axes, and \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}} = \begin{bmatrix}\vek n^\T & \vek 0^\T \\ \vek 0^\T & \vek n^\T\end{bmatrix}\) the matrix from lemma 7.28, which gives the axial displacements of the nodes from the global ones, \(\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat T_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) (for a bar on the axis \(x\), \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the identity matrix and \(\vek n = 1\)). Then:

  1. The virtual displacement equation of the bar has, because of \(\varepsilon_0\), an additional term \(\int EA\,\varepsilon_0\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x\) on the right-hand side.
  2. The element contributes to the right-hand side the nodal forces \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-1, 1)^\T\) in the axis of the bar and \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-\vek n, \vek n)^\T\) in global axes.
  3. The equations of the structure are \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \vek f_0\), where \(\vek f_0 = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0}\) (theorem 7.13).
  4. The axial force of the element is \(N_{\mathrm{e}} = EA\,\big(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_2 - \vek u_1)/L_{\mathrm{e}} - \varepsilon_0\big)\), where \(\vek u_1\) and \(\vek u_2\) are the displacements of its nodes.
Proof

We must show that the initial strain changes only the right-hand side of the equations and the force of the element, not the matrix.

  1. By definition 7.48 the axial force of the bar is \(N = EA\,(u' - \varepsilon_0)\), because the strain of the bar is \(u'\).
  2. Theorem 4.76 requires only \(N' + q = 0\) and \(N(L) = F\) and does not use the relation between \(N\) and \(u\) (steps 2 to 5 of its proof). Hence for every virtual displacement \(w\) we have \(\int N\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x = \int q\,w\,\mathrm{d}x + F\,w(L)\). Where \(EA\) and \(\varepsilon_0\) are constant only piecewise, we apply the theorem on each piece separately and add the pieces, as for the weak form (definition 6.2).
  3. We insert \(N\) from step 1 and move the term with \(\varepsilon_0\) to the right:
    \[\int EA\,u'\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x = \int q\,w\,\mathrm{d}x + F\,w(L) + \int EA\,\varepsilon_0\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x . \tag{7.47}\]
    This is claim 1. The left side is the same as in equation (6.2), so the matrix of the element is the same. The solution of the method is the solution of this equation on the trial fields (equation (6.28)).
  4. On element \(e\) of length \(h = L_{\mathrm{e}}\) we have \(w' = \mat b_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek w_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(\mat b_{\mathrm{e}} = (-1, 1)/h\) (lemma 7.5). The additional term is therefore
    \[\int_{\mathrm{e}} EA\,\varepsilon_0\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x = \vek w_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,EA\,\varepsilon_0 \int_{\mathrm{e}} \mat b_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mathrm{d}x = \vek w_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-1, 1)^\T ,\]
    because \(\mat b_{\mathrm{e}}\) is constant and the integral of a constant function over the length \(h\) equals its value times \(h\) (theorem 3.40). The column next to \(\vek w_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\) is \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0}\), as for consistent nodal forces (definition 7.7).
  5. For a truss the axial displacements are \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), the test function has the axial displacements \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek w_{\mathrm{e}}\), so the additional term is \((\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}\vek w_{\mathrm{e}})^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = \vek w_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0}\). The columns of \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\) are \((\vek n, \vek 0)\) and \((\vek 0, \vek n)\), so \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T(a, b)^\T = (a\,\vek n, b\,\vek n)\) and \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-\vek n, \vek n)\). This is claim 2.
  6. Assembly (theorem 7.13) combines the contributions of the elements on the right-hand side term by term in the same way as it combined \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\). This is claim 3.
  7. Claim 4 is step 1 with the strain \(\varepsilon = \vek n\cdot(\vek u_2 - \vek u_1)/L_{\mathrm{e}}\), which is the axial elongation \(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_2 - \vek u_1)\) divided by the length (lemma 7.28, step 1).

Source: COOK §2.10 (2.10-1) to (2.10-8) (PDF pp. 66-68), FELIPPA §4.2.2 (4.19) to (4.24) (PDF p. 59), ONATE1 §4 (PDF p. 147). The proof from theorem 4.76 is not from a source.

What have we shown? The initial strain does not change the matrix \(\mat K\), but it changes the right-hand side of the equations and the force of the element. A bar with \(\varepsilon_0 > 0\) pushes its nodes apart with \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\) in the direction of its axis (the column \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-\vek n, \vek n)\) has \(+\vek n\) at the second node). COOK proceeds in the same way: he computes the initial stresses with prevented displacements, assembles them as a load and then solves (COOK, PDF p. 66, steps 1 to 3). Mechanical forces and forces of the initial strain are added on the same right-hand side (COOK, PDF p. 66).

The trap is the term \(-EA\,\varepsilon_0\) in claim 4. For loading with forces it was not there, so it is easy to forget. If we forget it, we get a force in a bar on which nobody acts. We test this on a free bar.

7.9.3 Bar between walls and free bar (palica med stenama in prosta palica)

Take the bar from section 3.3.3, of length \(L = 10\) m, with \(EA = 1{,}5394 \cdot 10^7\) N and \(\varepsilon_0 = 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}\), as one element with nodes at \(x = 0\) and \(x = L\). The nodal forces of the initial strain are

\[\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = EA\,\varepsilon_0\,(-1, 1)^\T = (-6927, 6927)^\T\ \text{N} .\]

If both nodes are fixed (the walls), there are no free unknowns and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is an empty matrix. The reactions from section 7.3.4 are the rows of the equations \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \vek f_0 + \vek r\) at \(\vek d = \vek 0\) and \(\vek f = \vek 0\), so \(\vek r = -\vek f_0 = (6927, -6927)^\T\) N. The first wall pushes the bar in the direction \(+x\) and the second in the direction \(-x\), each with a force of \(6927\) N. The axial force by claim 4 is \(N = EA\,(0 - \varepsilon_0) = -6927\) N, as in chapter 3.

We remove the right wall, so that node 2 is free. The equation of node 2 is \((EA/L)\,d_2 = EA\,\varepsilon_0\), where \(EA/L = 1{,}5394 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, hence \(d_2 = \varepsilon_0\,L = 4{,}5\) mm. This is the free elongation \(\alpha\,\Delta T\,L = 15 \cdot 10^{-6} \cdot 30 \cdot 10\ \text{m}\) from chapter 3. The axial force by claim 4 is \(N = EA\,(d_2/L - \varepsilon_0) = 0\), as it must be. But if we forget the term \(-EA\,\varepsilon_0\), the program prints \(N = EA\,d_2/L = +6927\) N, a tensile force in a bar that lengthens freely. A test on an example where we know the solution (there is no force in a free heated bar) finds such an error. Section 7.10.2 collects such tests.

7.9.4 Statically determinate truss: displacement without forces (statično določeno paličje: premik brez sil)

In the wall truss we heat the bar \(BC\) by \(30\) K and load nothing. What happens? Node \(C\) is held by the bar \(AC\), which lies horizontally, and by the bar \(BC\). Two bars and two coordinates of the node: we can choose the lengths of both bars independently, and the node adapts. The bar \(BC\) can lengthen, and node \(C\) moves so that the bar \(AC\) stays the same length. Therefore there are no forces. We take this first as a question about all such trusses, and then test it on the wall truss.

We write the equilibrium of the free nodes with the axial forces. A bar \(e\) with the unit vector \(\vek n\) from the first node to the second has the column \(\vek h_{\mathrm{e}} = (-\vek n, \vek n)^\T\) (this is the column from lemma 7.49). A tensile force \(N_{\mathrm{e}}\) pulls the first node in the direction \(+\vek n\) and the second in the direction \(-\vek n\), so with the force \(-N_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\). The column \(\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\) has components only in the displacements of the two nodes of the bar, and by assembly (theorem 7.13) we place it among all unknowns as \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\). Let \(\mat H\) be the matrix whose column \(e\) is \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\), restricted to the free unknowns (we omit the rows of the fixed unknowns), so that it has \(n_{\mathrm{f}}\) rows and \(m\) columns, and \(\vek N\) the column of the axial forces of the bars. The sum of the external force and the forces of the bars on the free nodes is zero, \(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} - \sum_{\mathrm{e}} N_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek h_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\), which in matrix form means

\[\mat H\,\vek N = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}} . \tag{7.48}\]

For the wall truss with the unknowns \((u_{\mathrm{C}}, v_{\mathrm{C}})\) and the bars \(AC\), \(BC\) in this order, \(\mat H = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0{,}8 \\ 0 & -0{,}6 \end{bmatrix}\), because nodes \(A\) and \(B\) are fixed and only the part of the column at node \(C\), the second node of both bars, remains, that is \(+\vek n_{\mathrm{AC}} = (1, 0)\) and \(+\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}} = (0{,}8, -0{,}6)\). The equation \(\mat H\,\vek N = (0, -F)^\T\) is the equation of the node from section 4.7.5 without the post. With the post, \(\mat H\) has a third column \((0, 1)^\T\).

When do the equilibrium equations alone determine the axial forces? The counting from section 7.5.3 says that this is possible only for \(m = n_{\mathrm{f}}\) (as many bars as free unknowns), but it is not sufficient, because it does not exclude a mechanism. Therefore we state static determinacy with equation (7.48) itself.

Theorem 7.50 Initial strains in a statically determinate truss (začetne deformacije v statično določenem paličju)

Let the truss have \(m\) bars and \(n_{\mathrm{f}}\) free unknowns, let \(\mat H\) be the \(n_{\mathrm{f}} \times m\) matrix from equation (7.48), let \(\mat D = \mathrm{diag}(EA_{\mathrm{e}}/L_{\mathrm{e}})\) and let \(\vek c\) be the column with entries \(c_{\mathrm{e}} = EA_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\). The truss is statically determinate (statično določeno) when the equation \(\mat H\,\vek N = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) has exactly one solution \(\vek N\) for every column \(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\), that is when the axial forces of the bars are determined by the equilibrium of the nodes alone, for every load. Let the truss be statically determinate, let no external force act on it, let the fixed displacements be zero and let bar \(e\) have the initial strain \(\varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\) (definition 7.48). Then \(m = n_{\mathrm{f}}\), the matrix \(\mat H\) is invertible, the equation of the method \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_{0,\mathrm{f}}\) has exactly one solution, the axial forces of all bars are zero, and the displacements of the free nodes are such that each bar lengthens by its free elongation \(\varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\).

Proof

We must show that under these assumptions \(\mat H\) is invertible, that the equation of the method is then solved by a displacement at which every axial force is zero, and that there is no other solution.

  1. For \(j = 1, \dots, n_{\mathrm{f}}\) let \(\vek b_{\mathrm{j}}\) be the only solution of the equation \(\mat H\,\vek b_{\mathrm{j}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\), where \(\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\) is column \(j\) of the identity matrix of order \(n_{\mathrm{f}}\) (by the definition of static determinacy it exists and is unique). The matrix \(\mat B\) with columns \(\vek b_{\mathrm{j}}\) has size \(m \times n_{\mathrm{f}}\) and \(\mat H\,\mat B = \mat I_{\mathrm{n}_\mathrm{f}}\), because column \(j\) of the product is \(\mat H\,\vek b_{\mathrm{j}}\) (definition 1.98).
  2. For every column \(\vek N\) of length \(m\), \(\vek N\) and \(\mat B\,\mat H\,\vek N\) are solutions of the equation \(\mat H\,\vek X = \mat H\,\vek N\), since \(\mat H\,(\mat B\,\mat H\,\vek N) = (\mat H\,\mat B)\,\mat H\,\vek N = \mat H\,\vek N\). The solution is unique, so \(\mat B\,\mat H\,\vek N = \vek N\). For \(\vek N\) equal to the columns of the identity matrix of order \(m\) we get \(\mat B\,\mat H = \mat I_{\mathrm{m}}\).
  3. We compute the sum of the diagonal entries in two ways: \(n_{\mathrm{f}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} (\mat H\,\mat B)_{\mathrm{ii}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \sum_{\mathrm{j}} H_{\mathrm{ij}}\,B_{\mathrm{ji}} = \sum_{\mathrm{j}} \sum_{\mathrm{i}} B_{\mathrm{ji}}\,H_{\mathrm{ij}} = \sum_{\mathrm{j}} (\mat B\,\mat H)_{\mathrm{jj}} = m\). The matrix \(\mat H\) is therefore square, and by steps 1 and 2 \(\mat B\) is its inverse matrix (definition 1.107). Also \(\mat H^\T\) is invertible, because by lemma 1.101 \(\mat H^\T\mat B^\T = (\mat B\,\mat H)^\T = \mat I\) and \(\mat B^\T\mat H^\T = (\mat H\,\mat B)^\T = \mat I\). The matrix \(\mat D\) is invertible, because it is diagonal with positive entries, \(\mat D^{-1} = \mathrm{diag}(L_{\mathrm{e}}/EA_{\mathrm{e}})\).
  4. By lemma 7.28 \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = (EA_{\mathrm{e}}/L_{\mathrm{e}})\,\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\). After assembly (theorem 7.13) and restriction to the free unknowns, entry \((i, k)\) of \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is the sum over the bars \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} H_{\mathrm{ie}}\,D_{\mathrm{e}}\,H_{\mathrm{ke}}\), which is entry \((i, k)\) of the product \(\mat H\,\mat D\,\mat H^\T\). Hence \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat H\,\mat D\,\mat H^\T\).
  5. By lemma 7.49, claim 3, the right-hand side without external forces is \(\vek f_0 = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0}\), and since \(\mat T_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek f_{\mathrm{e}0} = EA_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\,\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}\), its part for the free unknowns is \(\vek f_{0,\mathrm{f}} = \mat H\,\vek c\).
  6. Let \(\vek d^* = (\mat H^\T)^{-1}\mat D^{-1}\vek c\). Then \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d^* = \mat H\,\mat D\,\mat H^\T(\mat H^\T)^{-1}\mat D^{-1}\vek c = \mat H\,\vek c = \vek f_{0,\mathrm{f}}\), so \(\vek d^*\) solves the equation of the method.
  7. There is no other solution. If \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek y = \vek 0\), then \(\mat H\,\mat D\,\mat H^\T\vek y = \vek 0\). Multiplying by \(\mat H^{-1}\), then by \(\mat D^{-1}\) and then by \((\mat H^\T)^{-1}\) gives \(\vek y = \vek 0\). The difference of two solutions solves this equation, so \(\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek d^*\).
  8. By lemma 7.49, claim 4, with zero fixed displacements, \(N_{\mathrm{e}} = EA_{\mathrm{e}}\,\big(\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}/L_{\mathrm{e}} - \varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\big)\), because \(\vek h_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek n\cdot(\vek u_2 - \vek u_1)\). In a column this is \(\vek N = \mat D\,\mat H^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} - \vek c\). For \(\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek d^*\) we get \(\vek N = \mat D\,\mat H^\T(\mat H^\T)^{-1}\mat D^{-1}\vek c - \vek c = \vek c - \vek c = \vek 0\).
  9. From \(\mat H^\T\vek d^* = \mat D^{-1}\vek c\), for every bar \(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_2 - \vek u_1) = c_{\mathrm{e}}/D_{\mathrm{e}} = \varepsilon_{0,\mathrm{e}}\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\), which is the free elongation of the bar.

Source: The proof is not from a source. The converse, that in a statically indeterminate truss heating causes internal forces, is stated by FELIPPA (PDF p. 56, §4.2).

What have we shown? Because \(\vek N = \mat D\,\mat H^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} - \vek c\), we have \(\mat H\,\vek N = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} - \vek f_{0,\mathrm{f}}\), so the equations of the method are the equilibrium of the nodes \(\mat H\,\vek N = \vek 0\), in which the forces of the bars are computed from the displacements. In a truss that the equilibrium alone determines, this equilibrium has only the solution \(\vek N = \vek 0\), and heating shows itself only in the displacements.

We test it on the wall truss with the heated bar \(BC\) (figure 7.33). The unit vector from \(B\) to \(C\) is \(\vek n = (0{,}8, -0{,}6)\), and \(EA\,\varepsilon_0 = 8820\) N. The nodal force of the initial strain at \(C\) is

\[EA\,\varepsilon_0\,\vek n = 8820 \cdot (0{,}8, -0{,}6) = (7056, -5292)\ \text{N} ,\]

and the opposite one at \(B\), but \(B\) is fixed. The equation \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_{0,\mathrm{f}}\) with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) from section 7.5.2 is

\[\begin{bmatrix} 7{,}4088 & -1{,}8816 \\ -1{,}8816 & 1{,}4112 \end{bmatrix} \cdot 10^6\ \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}}\ \begin{pmatrix} u_{\mathrm{C}} \\ v_{\mathrm{C}} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 7056 \\ -5292 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{N} ,\]

and the solution is \(u_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\), \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = -3{,}75\) mm. Check in the first row: \(-1{,}8816 \cdot 10^6 \cdot (-3{,}75 \cdot 10^{-3}) = 7056\) N. The axial forces by claim 4 are

\[\begin{aligned} N_{\mathrm{AC}} &= EA\,\Big(\frac{(1, 0)\cdot(0, -3{,}75\ \text{mm})}{4\ \text{m}} - 0\Big) = 0 , \\ N_{\mathrm{BC}} &= EA\,\Big(\frac{(0{,}8, -0{,}6)\cdot(0, -3{,}75\ \text{mm})}{5\ \text{m}} - 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}\Big) \\ &= EA\,(4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4} - 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}) = 0 . \end{aligned}\]

The bar \(BC\) lengthened by \(2{,}25\) mm, which is \(\alpha\,\Delta T\,L = 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4} \cdot 5\ \text{m}\). The reactions are zero, because the axial forces are zero. Why did the node drop by exactly \(3{,}75\) mm? The bar \(AC\) stays \(4\) m long, so \(C\) moves perpendicular to it, vertically, and the vertical displacement \(v\) lengthens \(BC\) by \(0{,}6\,|v|\). The condition \(0{,}6\,|v| = 2{,}25\) mm gives \(|v| = 3{,}75\) mm.

The theorem holds in linear theory, and this has a limit. Node \(C\) in fact moves along a circular arc about \(A\) (figure 7.33), while linear theory moves it along the tangent to the arc, vertically. The bar \(AC\) therefore lengthens by \(v^2/(2 \cdot 4\ \text{m}) = 1{,}8\) µm, which is \(0{,}08\) % of the free elongation \(2{,}25\) mm. This is a term of second order in the displacement, which equation (4.48) neglects.

7.9.5 Statically indeterminate truss: forces without a load (statično nedoločeno paličje: sile brez bremena)

We add to the wall truss the post \(CD\) of length \(3\) m (section 4.7.5, figure 7.34). We take the bars in the order \(AC\), \(BC\) and \(DC\) (the post as an element with nodes \(D\) and \(C\) in this order), so that the post has the unit vector \(\vek n = (0, 1)\) from the ground to the node. The unknowns are \((u_{\mathrm{C}}, v_{\mathrm{C}})\), the matrix \(\mat H\) has two rows and three columns, \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0{,}8 & 0 \\ 0 & -0{,}6 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), and the equation \(\mat H\,\vek N = \vek 0\) has more solutions, so the truss is not statically determinate (in the sense above: \(m = 3 \ne 2 = n_{\mathrm{f}}\)). Section 4.7.5 wrote them with the unknown \(X = N_{\mathrm{CD}}\): without a load, \(\vek N = X\,(-\tfrac43, \tfrac53, 1)\). Theorem 7.50 therefore does not hold (the matrix \(\mat H\) is not square), and equilibrium does not say which \(X\) is the right one. This is told by the computation of the method, which requires that the elongations of the bars come from one displacement of the node.

We heat the post by \(30\) K, without a load. The nodal force of the initial strain at \(C\) is \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\,\vek n = (0, 8820)\) N: the heated post pushes node \(C\) upward. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is now

\[\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \begin{bmatrix} 7{,}4088 & -1{,}8816 \\ -1{,}8816 & 7{,}9445 \end{bmatrix} \cdot 10^6\ \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}} ,\]

where the post contributes \(EA/L_{\mathrm{CD}} = 1{,}96 \cdot 10^7/3 = 6{,}5333 \cdot 10^6\) N/m to the entry \((2, 2)\), \(1{,}4112 + 6{,}5333 = 7{,}9445\). The solution is \(u_{\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}300\) mm and \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 1{,}181\) mm, with axial forces

\[\begin{aligned} N_{\mathrm{AC}} &= 1{,}470\ \text{kN} , \qquad N_{\mathrm{BC}} = -1{,}838\ \text{kN} , \\ N_{\mathrm{CD}} &= EA\,\Big(\frac{1{,}18125\ \text{mm}}{3\ \text{m}} - 4{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}\Big) = -1{,}1025\ \text{kN} . \end{aligned}\]

These are indeed solutions \(\vek N = X\,(-\tfrac43, \tfrac53, 1)\) with \(X = -1{,}1025\) kN: \(-\tfrac43 \cdot (-1{,}1025) = 1{,}470\) and \(\tfrac53 \cdot (-1{,}1025) = -1{,}8375\). The method chose one of the solutions of equilibrium, and since no external force acts, the forces of the heated post balance only each other (figure 7.34, right): the bar \(AC\) pulls node \(C\) toward \(A\) with \(1470\) N, the bar \(BC\) pushes it with a force of \(1837{,}5\) N in the direction \((0{,}8, -0{,}6)\), that is with components \((1470, -1102{,}5)\) N, and the post pushes it upward with \(1102{,}5\) N. Horizontally \(-1470 + 1470 = 0\) and vertically \(-1102{,}5 + 1102{,}5 = 0\).

If the post were between two rigid bases, it would carry \(-EA\,\alpha\,\Delta T = -8{,}82\) kN (chapter 3, equation (3.18)). But it carries only \(1{,}1025\) kN, which is \(12{,}5\) % of this force. Why? The post cannot lengthen by its free elongation \(\varepsilon_0\,L_{\mathrm{CD}} = 1{,}35\) mm, because it must move node \(C\), which is held by the bars \(AC\) and \(BC\). The node moved upward by only \(1{,}181\) mm, the post lengthened by \(1{,}181\) mm of \(1{,}35\) mm, and the remaining \(0{,}169\) mm is compressed. How stiffly does the wall truss hold the node? From the first row of the node equation we express \(u_{\mathrm{C}} = (1{,}8816/7{,}4088)\,v_{\mathrm{C}}\) and insert it in the second row:

\[\Big(7{,}9445 - \frac{1{,}8816^2}{7{,}4088}\Big) \cdot 10^6\ v_{\mathrm{C}} = 8820\ \text{N} , \qquad 7{,}4667 \cdot 10^6\ \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}}\ v_{\mathrm{C}} = 8820\ \text{N} .\]

Of the vertical stiffness \(7{,}4667 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, \(k_{\mathrm{s}} = 6{,}5333 \cdot 10^6\) N/m belongs to the post, and to the wall truss without the post \(k_{\mathrm{p}} = 1{,}4112 - 1{,}8816^2/7{,}4088 = 0{,}9333 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, which is the second pivot of the elimination of the wall truss from section 7.5.2. The bar \(AC\) is horizontal, so the node can also adapt horizontally when it moves upward, and this adaptation is already counted in the pivot. The post and the truss are like two springs that both hold node \(C\), which moves by \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = EA\,\varepsilon_0/(k_{\mathrm{p}} + k_{\mathrm{s}})\). The force of the post is its stiffness times the difference between the displacement of the node and the free elongation:

\[\begin{aligned} N_{\mathrm{CD}} &= k_{\mathrm{s}}\,\big(v_{\mathrm{C}} - \varepsilon_0\,L_{\mathrm{CD}}\big) = k_{\mathrm{s}}\,EA\,\varepsilon_0\,\Big(\frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{p}} + k_{\mathrm{s}}} - \frac{1}{k_{\mathrm{s}}}\Big) \\ &= -EA\,\varepsilon_0\,\frac{k_{\mathrm{p}}}{k_{\mathrm{p}} + k_{\mathrm{s}}} = -8820\ \text{N} \cdot \frac{0{,}9333}{7{,}4667} = -1102{,}5\ \text{N} . \end{aligned}\]

The share \(k_{\mathrm{p}}/(k_{\mathrm{p}} + k_{\mathrm{s}}) = 0{,}125\) is the ratio of the stiffness of the truss to all the stiffness at the node. If the truss were infinitely stiff, the share would be \(1\) and the post would carry the whole force of the bar between walls. Because the truss is soft, the node gives way, and the post keeps an eighth.

The same computation holds if the post is \(\delta = 1\) mm too long and we fit it in. The initial strain is \(\varepsilon_0 = \delta/L_{\mathrm{CD}} = 3{,}33 \cdot 10^{-4}\), the nodal force of the initial strain at \(C\) is \(EA\,\varepsilon_0\,\vek n = (0, 6533)\) N, the displacements of the node are \(0{,}222\) mm and \(0{,}875\) mm and the forces are

\[\begin{aligned} N_{\mathrm{AC}} &= 1{,}089\ \text{kN} , \qquad N_{\mathrm{BC}} = -1{,}361\ \text{kN} , \\ N_{\mathrm{CD}} &= -6533\ \text{N} \cdot 0{,}125 = -0{,}817\ \text{kN} . \end{aligned}\]

The forces are in the ratio \(3{,}33/4{,}5 = 0{,}741\) to the heated post, because the equations are linear in \(\varepsilon_0\) (figure 7.35).

A load and a wrong length add up. The equation \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f + \vek f_0\) is linear in \(\vek f\) and \(\vek f_0\) and has exactly one solution, because \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite (theorem 7.33, theorem 6.29), so the solution for both loads is the sum of the solutions. For the load of \(10\) kN at \(C\) the forces are \(-1{,}667\), \(2{,}083\) and \(-8{,}750\) kN (section 4.7.5), and together with the post that is too long we get

\[\begin{aligned} N_{\mathrm{AC}} &= -1{,}667 + 1{,}089 = -0{,}578\ \text{kN} , \quad N_{\mathrm{BC}} = 2{,}083 - 1{,}361 = 0{,}722\ \text{kN} , \\ N_{\mathrm{CD}} &= -8{,}750 - 0{,}817 = -9{,}567\ \text{kN} , \end{aligned}\]

which agrees with the direct computation with both loads at once. The post is now compressed with \(9{,}567\) kN, that is with the stress \(95{,}7\) MPa, which is below the yield limit \(220\) MPa (buckling of the compressed post, as in chapter 4, we do not check). A millimetre of error in length loaded the post by \(9{,}3\) % more than the load alone.

What have we learned? Temperature and a wrong length enter the method as a right-hand side of the equations and as a term in the force of a bar. The matrix, assembly, supports and reactions are the same. In a statically determinate truss (theorem 7.50) the two effects are a displacement without forces, and in a statically indeterminate truss they are forces that the softer part takes up. Whether the computed forces are correct we check with the checks that the next section collects.
