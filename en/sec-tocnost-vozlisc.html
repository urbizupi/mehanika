Chapter 12 Beam and frame finite elements

12.3 Exactness at the nodes (točnost v vozliščih)

In chapter 6 we computed the cantilever under its own weight by the Ritz method with the trial functions \(x^2\) and \(x^3\) (section 6.4.3). The cantilever has length \(L = 1\) m, is clamped at \(x = 0\), has \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\) N m² and the load \(q = -61{,}21\) N/m, the weight of the steel flat bar \(20 \times 40\) mm, with the \(y\) axis pointing up. The true deflection is a polynomial of degree four. The tip displacement with two functions was nevertheless exact, \(-0{,}3660\) mm, while the clamp moment was 17 % too small in absolute value, \(-25{,}51\) instead of \(-30{,}61\) N m (section 6.5.2). The family \(\{x^2, x^3\}\) is exactly the family of fields of one finite element of a beam (definition 12.3) clamped at \(x = 0\): the field of the element is a polynomial of degree at most three, and the clamp requires \(v(0) = v'(0) = 0\), which removes the terms \(1\) and \(x\). Was the tip displacement exact by chance? Will it be exact for other loads and with more elements? And how do we obtain the right internal forces from the computation if the moment of the field does not give them?

The answer has four parts. First we need a tool that determines a beam with point loads and with a stiffness constant on each element uniquely (lemma 12.12). Then we show that for loads only at the nodes the finite element solution is exactly the true solution on the whole beam (theorem 12.13), as for a truss (corollary 7.31). Then we show that for any load with consistent nodal forces the deflection and the rotation at the nodes are still exact (theorem 12.14), which is the form of theorem 6.38 for a beam. Finally we obtain exact internal forces from the forces at the element ends, and the exact error inside an element (corollaries 12.15 and 12.16).

12.3.1 Uniqueness and virtual work (enoličnost in virtualno delo)

All statements of this section hold for an Euler-Bernoulli beam (definition 5.14) whose stiffness \(EI\) is constant on each element of a mesh and may change only at a node. This condition is essential, and we shall test it with numbers soon. Chapter 5 proved uniqueness for a constant stiffness and a continuous load (theorem 5.49), chapter 6 for kinks with a continuous shear force (corollary 6.14). Here we need both at once: jumps of the shear force under point forces (lemma 5.12) and jumps of the stiffness.

The break points \(0 = t_0 < t_1 < \dots < t_{\mathrm{m}} = L\) are all nodes of the mesh (definition 6.24), all points where point forces and couples act and all points where the load jumps. We call an interval \([t_{\mathrm{j}-1}, t_{\mathrm{j}}]\) a piece. The transverse load \(q\) is continuous on each piece and extends to a continuous function on the closed piece, and at the point \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) a given point force \(F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\) across the axis and a given couple with moment \(C_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\) act (both may be zero). Supports stand only at nodes and prevent the deflection, the slope or both. If a support at \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) prevents the deflection, an unknown reaction \(R_{\mathrm{j}}\) across the axis acts there as well, if it prevents the slope, an unknown reaction couple \(S_{\mathrm{j}}\), otherwise \(R_{\mathrm{j}}\) or \(S_{\mathrm{j}}\) is zero. The whole force at \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) is \(F_{\mathrm{j}} = F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}} + R_{\mathrm{j}}\), the whole couple \(C_{\mathrm{j}} = C_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}} + S_{\mathrm{j}}\). Signs are as in chapter 5: an upward force and a counterclockwise couple are positive.

When is a field \(v\) the solution of this problem? When it satisfies four conditions that we know from chapter 5:

(a) \(v\) and \(v'\) are continuous on \([0, L]\), \(v\) is zero where a support prevents the deflection, and \(v'\) is zero where a support prevents the slope,

(b) on each piece \(v\), \(v'\), \(v''\) and \(v'''\) are continuous on the closed piece (with one-sided values at its ends), and on the open piece the deflection equation \(EI\,v'''' = q\) holds (theorem 5.42), with \(M = EI\,v''\) and \(V = -M' = -EI\,v'''\) on each piece,

(c) at an interior break point the shear force jumps by \(-F_{\mathrm{j}}\) and the moment by \(-C_{\mathrm{j}}\), \(V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+) - V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-) = -F_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+) - M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-) = -C_{\mathrm{j}}\) (lemma 5.12), where \(V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^\pm)\) and \(M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^\pm)\) are the values from the piece to the right and to the left of \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\),

(d) at the ends \(V(L) = F_{\mathrm{m}}\), \(M(L) = C_{\mathrm{m}}\) and \(-V(0) = F_0\), \(-M(0) = C_0\).

We call such a field a regular field (pravilno polje) of the beam. In a component that a support prevents, conditions (c) and (d) require nothing of the field, because they contain the unknown reaction: they only say how large the reaction is. Let also \(B(v, w) = \int_0^L EI\,v''\,w''\,\mathrm{d}x\) be the bilinear form of the beam (definition 6.4) and

\[\ell(w) = \int_0^L q\,w\,\mathrm{d}x + \sum_{\mathrm{j}} \big(F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\,w(t_{\mathrm{j}}) + C_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\,w'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\big) \tag{12.7}\]

the work of the given loads on the field \(w\), without the support reactions.

Lemma 12.12 Virtual work and uniqueness for a beam with piecewise constant stiffness (virtualno delo in enoličnost nosilca z odsekoma stalno togostjo)

Let a beam of length \(L\) have stiffness \(EI > 0\) that is constant on each element of a mesh \(0 = x_0 < \dots < x_{\mathrm{n}} = L\) (definition 6.24), with break points \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\), load \(q\), given forces and couples and supports at nodes as described above. An admissible virtual displacement (dopustni virtualni poves) \(\delta v\) is a function on \([0, L]\) with continuous \(\delta v\) and \(\delta v'\), with a piecewise continuously differentiable \(\delta v'\) (definition 6.11), with \(\delta v = 0\) where a support prevents the deflection and with \(\delta v' = 0\) where a support prevents the slope. It is a virtual displacement of a beam (definition 5.60) that may have jumps of the second derivative, as in corollary 6.14. Then:

  1. Let \([c, d]\) be an interval on which \(EI\) is constant, \(p\) a continuous function on \([c, d]\) and \(u\) a function that together with \(u'\), \(u''\) and \(u'''\) is continuous on \([c, d]\) and has \(EI\,u'''' = p\) on \((c, d)\). With \(M = EI\,u''\) and \(V = -EI\,u'''\), for every function \(w\) with continuous \(w\), \(w'\) and a piecewise continuously differentiable \(w'\) on \([c, d]\)
    \[\int_{\mathrm{c}}^d M\,w''\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_{\mathrm{c}}^d p\,w\,\mathrm{d}x + \Big[V\,w + M\,w'\Big]_{\mathrm{c}}^d . \tag{12.8}\]
  2. For every regular field \(v\) and every admissible virtual displacement \(\delta v\), \(B(v, \delta v) = \ell(\delta v)\).
  3. If the only line \(a + b\,x\) that is zero where a support prevents the deflection and has zero slope where a support prevents the slope is the zero function, there is at most one regular field.
  4. With the supports of point 3 a regular field exists.
Proof

We have to show four points. The first two are a double integration by parts, the third is the zero energy argument, the fourth reduces existence to a square linear system that by point 3 has only the zero solution for zero load.

1. Work on a piece.

  1. The function \(M = EI\,u''\) is continuous on \([c, d]\) and has on \((c, d)\) the derivative \(M' = EI\,u''' = -V\), which is continuous on \([c, d]\). The function \(V = -EI\,u'''\) is continuous on \([c, d]\) and has on \((c, d)\) the derivative \(V' = -EI\,u'''' = -p\), which is continuous on \([c, d]\). Both are therefore piecewise continuously differentiable with a single partition (definition 6.11). The function \(w'\) is piecewise continuously differentiable by assumption, the function \(w\) because it has the continuous derivative \(w'\).
  2. By lemma 6.13 with \(M\) and \(w'\) on a common partition, \(\int_{\mathrm{c}}^d M\,w''\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[M\,w'\big]_{\mathrm{c}}^d - \int_{\mathrm{c}}^d M'\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[M\,w'\big]_{\mathrm{c}}^d + \int_{\mathrm{c}}^d V\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  3. Once more by lemma 6.13, with \(V\) and \(w\): \(\int_{\mathrm{c}}^d V\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[V\,w\big]_{\mathrm{c}}^d - \int_{\mathrm{c}}^d V'\,w\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[V\,w\big]_{\mathrm{c}}^d + \int_{\mathrm{c}}^d p\,w\,\mathrm{d}x\). We insert this into step 2 and obtain equation (12.8).

2. Work on the whole beam.

  1. A piece \([t_{\mathrm{j}-1}, t_{\mathrm{j}}]\) lies in one element, so \(EI\) is constant on it. A regular field \(v\) on the piece satisfies the assumptions of point 1 with \(p = q\) (condition (b), \(q\) extended to a continuous function on the closed piece), and \(\delta v\) the assumptions on \(w\). We add equation (12.8) over the pieces. By definition 6.9 and point 1 of lemma 6.10 the integrals combine into \(\int_0^L EI\,v''\,\delta v''\,\mathrm{d}x = B(v, \delta v)\) and \(\int_0^L q\,\delta v\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  2. At an interior point \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) the piece to its left gives the term \(V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-)\,\delta v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) + M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-)\,\delta v'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\), the piece to its right the term \(-V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+)\,\delta v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) - M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+)\,\delta v'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\), because \(\delta v\) and \(\delta v'\) are continuous. The sum is \(-\big(V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+) - V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-)\big)\,\delta v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) - \big(M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+) - M(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-)\big)\,\delta v'(t_{\mathrm{j}}) = F_{\mathrm{j}}\,\delta v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) + C_{\mathrm{j}}\,\delta v'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\) by condition (c).
  3. At the end \(x = L\) the term is \(V(L)\,\delta v(L) + M(L)\,\delta v'(L) = F_{\mathrm{m}}\,\delta v(L) + C_{\mathrm{m}}\,\delta v'(L)\), at the end \(x = 0\) it is \(-V(0)\,\delta v(0) - M(0)\,\delta v'(0) = F_0\,\delta v(0) + C_0\,\delta v'(0)\), by condition (d).
  4. In the sum of steps 5 and 6 the terms with reactions are zero: \(R_{\mathrm{j}}\) is nonzero only where a support prevents the deflection, so that \(\delta v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) = 0\), and \(S_{\mathrm{j}}\) only where a support prevents the slope, so that \(\delta v'(t_{\mathrm{j}}) = 0\). The given loads remain, and with step 4, \(B(v, \delta v) = \ell(\delta v)\) by equation (12.7).

3. Uniqueness.

  1. Let \(v_1\), \(v_2\) be regular fields of the same problem and \(w = v_1 - v_2\). The functions \(w\) and \(w'\) are continuous, \(w'\) is piecewise continuously differentiable with the break points \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) (condition (b)), \(w\) is zero where a support prevents the deflection, and \(w'\) where a support prevents the slope (condition (a)). So \(w\) is an admissible virtual displacement. By point 2, \(B(v_1, w) = \ell(w) = B(v_2, w)\), and since the form \(B\) is linear in its first argument, \(B(w, w) = \int_0^L EI\,w''^2\,\mathrm{d}x = 0\).
  2. The integrand is continuous and nonnegative on each closed piece, so by point 3 of lemma 6.10, \(EI\,w''^2 = 0\) and \(w'' = 0\) on each piece, because \(EI > 0\). By corollary 1.85 the function \(w'\) is constant on each piece, and since it is continuous, it is the same constant \(b\) on all pieces. The function \(w - b\,x\) has derivative zero on each piece and is continuous, so it is constant, \(w = a + b\,x\). This line is zero where a support prevents the deflection and has zero slope where a support prevents the slope, so by the assumption of point 3, \(w = 0\).

4. Existence.

  1. On the piece \([t_{\mathrm{k}-1}, t_{\mathrm{k}}]\), by theorem 5.45 with \(n = 4\) and \(f = q/EI\), every function of condition (b) has the form \(v = I_4^{(k)} + c_{\mathrm{k}0} + c_{\mathrm{k}1}\,x + c_{\mathrm{k}2}\,x^2 + c_{\mathrm{k}3}\,x^3\), where \(I_4^{(k)}\) is the function \(q/EI\) integrated four times in succession starting at \(t_{\mathrm{k}-1}\) (equation (5.43)). The field is thus given by \(4m\) numbers \(c_{\mathrm{ki}}\). The values of \(v\), \(v'\), \(M\) and \(V\) at the ends of the pieces are sums of linear functions of these numbers and of numbers determined by \(q\).
  2. Conditions (a), (c) and (d) are equations for these numbers. At an interior point \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) the continuity of \(v\) and the continuity of \(v'\) give two equations. The pair \((v, V)\) gives one more equation: if a support at \(t_{\mathrm{j}}\) prevents the deflection, it is \(v(t_{\mathrm{j}}) = 0\), and the jump of \(V\) in condition (c) only determines the reaction \(R_{\mathrm{j}}\), otherwise it is the jump \(V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^+) - V(t_{\mathrm{j}}^-) = -F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\). The pair \((v', M)\) likewise gives one equation, \(v'(t_{\mathrm{j}}) = 0\) or the jump of the moment. An interior point thus gives four equations.
  3. At each end the pair \((v, V)\) gives one equation, the essential condition \(v = 0\) if a support prevents the deflection, otherwise the natural condition of (d) with the given force. The pair \((v', M)\) likewise gives one equation. Altogether there are \(4\,(m - 1) + 2 + 2 = 4m\) equations, as many as numbers \(c_{\mathrm{ki}}\). This is a system \(\mat A\,\vek c = \vek b\) with a square matrix \(\mat A\) of order \(4m\) that depends only on the break points, the stiffness and the supports, and with a right side \(\vek b\) that depends on \(q\), \(F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}}\) and is zero if all three are zero.
  4. Let \(\mat A\,\vek c = \vek 0\). This is the system of the problem without loads, \(q = 0\) and \(F_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}} = C_{\mathrm{j}}^{\mathrm{d}} = 0\), for which \(I_4^{(k)} = 0\). The field with the numbers \(\vek c\), with the reactions determined by the jumps, is a regular field of this problem. The zero field is also a regular field of this problem, so by point 3 the field with the numbers \(\vek c\) is zero on each piece. The polynomial \(c_{\mathrm{k}0} + c_{\mathrm{k}1}x + c_{\mathrm{k}2}x^2 + c_{\mathrm{k}3}x^3\), which is zero on the piece, has all derivatives zero there: the third derivative gives \(6\,c_{\mathrm{k}3} = 0\), then the second \(2\,c_{\mathrm{k}2} = 0\), the first \(c_{\mathrm{k}1} = 0\) and the polynomial itself \(c_{\mathrm{k}0} = 0\). So \(\vek c = \vek 0\).
  5. By lemma 8.21 a square system whose homogeneous system has only the zero solution has exactly one solution for every right side. The field with the numbers of this solution of \(\mat A\,\vek c = \vek b\) satisfies conditions (a) to (d) if the reactions \(R_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(S_{\mathrm{j}}\) are determined from the jumps and the end conditions, and it is therefore a regular field.

Source: The proof is not from a source. Point 2 is the work of a beam with jumps from theorem 5.62 and corollary 5.64, uniqueness is the zero energy argument of corollary 6.14, and existence the argument on a square system of lemma 8.21.

With the supports of point 3 there is therefore exactly one regular field. We call it the true deflection of the beam. The lemma speaks of a regular field, not of the finite element solution. We want to know when the two are the same thing.

12.3.2 Loads only at the nodes (obremenitev samo v vozliščih)

Take the simplest loading: forces and couples only at the nodes, nothing along the elements. Each element is then unloaded. On an unloaded element the shear force is constant and the moment linear (corollary 5.11 with \(q = 0\)), and the deflection is a polynomial of degree three (theorem 5.45). This is exactly the form of the field of a finite element. Can the finite element field, made of such cubic pieces, differ from the true one at all?

Theorem 12.13 Nodal loads give the exact deflection (obremenitev v vozliščih da točen poves)

Let the beam of lemma 12.12 have the load \(q = 0\) and point forces and couples only at the nodes of the mesh, and let the supports satisfy the condition of point 3 of the lemma. Let \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) be the finite element field of corollary 12.11 with prescribed values zero and with the column \(\vek P\) of the given nodal forces and couples. Then \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a regular field of this problem, so it equals the true deflection \(v\) on the whole interval \([0, L]\).

Proof

We have to show that the field \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) satisfies conditions (a) to (d) of a regular field. Then by point 3 of lemma 12.12 it equals the only regular field.

  1. The field \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}} d_{\mathrm{I}}\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\) is continuously differentiable and has at the node \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) the value \(d_{2\mathrm{N}}\) and the slope \(d_{2\mathrm{N}+1}\) (lemma 12.8). The prescribed unknowns are zero, so \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is zero where a support prevents the deflection and \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'\) is zero where a support prevents the slope. This is condition (a).
  2. Point loads act only at the nodes and \(q = 0\) jumps nowhere, so the break points are the nodes and the pieces are the elements. On an element \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a polynomial of degree at most three (lemma 12.8, point 1), so \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'\), \(v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) and \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'''\) are continuous on the closed element and \(EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}'''' = 0 = q\). This is condition (b).
  3. On the element \(e\) with nodes \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(x_{\mathrm{b}}\) and with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d\), \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) (lemma 12.8, point 1). By point 2 of lemma 12.4 and step 9 of its proof, \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \big(-V(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), -M(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), V(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-), M(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-)\big)^\T\), where \(V = -EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}'''\) and \(M = EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) are the values of the field \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) from the element \(e\).
  4. Let \(I\) be a free unknown. By point 1 of theorem 7.13, which holds for every matrix of the form (12.6), \((\mat K\,\vek d)_{\mathrm{I}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} (\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{i}}\), the sum over the elements that have the unknown \(I\) as their local unknown \(i\). The elements carry no load, so \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\) (definition 12.6) and \(f_{\mathrm{I}} = P_{\mathrm{I}}\), the given force or couple at the node (theorem 12.9). Row \(I\) of the system therefore says that the sum of the forces of all elements at the node equals the given load of the node.
  5. At an interior node \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) between the elements \(N\) and \(N + 1\) the node is local node 2 for element \(N\) and local node 1 for element \(N + 1\). If the deflection at \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) is free, row \(2N\) gives by steps 3 and 4 the equation \(V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) - V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) = F_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{d}}\), and since \(R_{\mathrm{N}} = 0\) there, \(V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) - V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) = -F_{\mathrm{N}}\). If the slope is free, row \(2N + 1\) likewise gives \(M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) - M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) = -C_{\mathrm{N}}\). This is condition (c). At the node at \(x = L\) only the last element has the unknown, and the two rows give \(V(L) = F_{\mathrm{m}}\) and \(M(L) = C_{\mathrm{m}}\), at \(x = 0\) they give \(-V(0) = F_0\) and \(-M(0) = C_0\). This is condition (d).
  6. In a component that a support prevents, conditions (c) and (d) only determine the reaction (the paragraph before lemma 12.12). By steps 1 to 5, \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a regular field.

The proof showed that the rows of the assembled system are the equilibrium conditions of the nodes, and that the cubic pieces are the true deflection of unloaded elements.

Let us look at an example we know from chapter 5: the cantilever, \(L = 1\) m, \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\) N m², a force of \(500\) N downward at the free end. With one element, as in section 12.1, the tip displacement is \(-7{,}9719\) mm and the tip rotation \(-1{,}1958 \cdot 10^{-2}\), which is \(-F\,L^2/(2EI)\) (computed with SymPy and NumPy). With two elements, with a node at the middle, the tip displacement is again \(-7{,}9719\) mm and the rotation the same. The field at \(x = 0{,}5\) m, which is a node with two elements and an interior point of the element with one, is \(-2{,}4912\) mm in both cases, and the true deflection \(-F\,x^2(3L - x)/(6EI)\) gives the same number. The deflection is therefore exact on the whole beam, not only at the nodes, whatever the number of elements. By theorem 12.13 this is no coincidence. We see the same on the simply supported beam of length \(L = 2\) m with a force of \(1000\) N downward at the middle: two elements give \(v(L/2) = -7{,}97194\) mm, which is \(-F\,L^3/(48\,EI)\), and \(v(0{,}5\ \text{m}) = -5{,}48071\) mm, again equal to the true value. Half of this beam is a cantilever of length \(1\) m with a force of \(500\) N, so the first number is the same as for the cantilever.

The theorem has a condition we must not forget: the stiffness must be constant on each element. Let us test it. A cantilever with linearly increasing stiffness \(EI(x) = EI\,(1 + x/L)\) has the tip displacement \(-F\int_0^L (L - x)^2/EI(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = -6{,}5192\) mm. The finite element field in which the element matrix is computed as \(\int EI(x)\,\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}''^\T\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}''\,\mathrm{d}x\) (SymPy) gives with \(n = 1\), \(2\), \(4\) elements \(-6{,}4389\), \(-6{,}5124\), \(-6{,}5187\) mm. The error is not zero, because the deflection under a unit force with a variable stiffness is not a cubic polynomial on an element, but it drops at each halving by a factor of about 12 and then 14. With a jump of the stiffness that lies at a node (\(EI\) on the first half and \(EI/2\) on the second), the solution is exact by the theorem: the tip displacement is \(-8{,}9684\) mm for the true deflection and for the field computed with two elements. If the jump lies inside an element and we take the element with the mean stiffness, one element gives \(-10{,}629\) mm, 18,5 % too much. A jump of the stiffness must have a node, as we already saw for the bar with a step (section 6.5.2).

12.3.3 Any load, consistent forces and exactness at the nodes (poljubna obtežba, konsistentne sile in točnost v vozliščih)

The cantilever under its own weight has a load along the element, and the true deflection curve, which is of degree four, cannot be reached by a cubic field. Why is the tip displacement exact nevertheless? We asked the same question for the bar and answered it with a unit force (theorem 6.38). The tip displacement is the work of a unit force on the error. In the energy inner product the error is orthogonal to all fields of the family (lemma 6.36), so the displacement is exact if the field of the unit force is in the family. For the bar this held at every node, because the field of the unit force was piecewise linear there. For the beam we now know more: by theorem 12.13 the deflection of a beam under a unit force or couple at a node is cubic on each element and is therefore in the finite element family.

Let \(P\) be a node of the mesh. If the support at \(P\) does not prevent the deflection, \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) denotes the true deflection of the same beam, with the same supports and stiffness, under a unit upward force at the node \(P\) with no other load. If the support at \(P\) does not prevent the slope, \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^\varphi\) denotes the true deflection under a unit counterclockwise couple at \(P\) with no other load. By theorem 12.13 these are finite element fields of the same mesh, since they are loaded only at a node.

Theorem 12.14 Exactness of the deflection and rotation at the nodes (točnost povesa in zasuka v vozliščih)

Let the beam of lemma 12.12, with supports that satisfy the condition of point 3 of the lemma, carry any loading of the description before the lemma: a distributed load \(q\) and point forces and couples at any points. Let \(v\) be the true deflection and \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) the finite element field of corollary 12.11 with prescribed values zero, with consistent element forces (definition 12.6) for the load \(q\) and for the point loads inside elements, and with the column \(\vek P\) for the point loads at the nodes (theorem 12.9). Then at every node \(P\)

\[v_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = v(x_{\mathrm{P}}) , \qquad v_{\mathrm{h}}'(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = v'(x_{\mathrm{P}}) . \tag{12.9}\]
Proof

We have to show that the error at a node is the work of a unit force or couple on the error, and that this work is zero because the error is orthogonal to the family. If the support at \(P\) prevents the deflection, \(v(x_{\mathrm{P}})\) and \(v_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{P}})\) are both zero, and likewise for the slope. We therefore have to prove the equality only for a component that the support at \(P\) does not prevent.

  1. The true deflection \(v\) exists and is unique by points 4 and 3 of lemma 12.12. Let \(W_{\mathrm{h}}\) be the set of fields \(\sum_{\mathrm{I}} c_{\mathrm{I}}\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\) in which the prescribed unknowns are zero. Such a field is an admissible virtual displacement: it is continuously differentiable, a polynomial of degree at most three on each element, so its derivative is piecewise continuously differentiable, it has at the node \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) the value \(c_{2\mathrm{N}}\) and the slope \(c_{2\mathrm{N}+1}\) (lemma 12.8), and it is therefore zero, or has zero slope, where a support prevents this. By point 2 of lemma 12.12, \(B(v, w_{\mathrm{h}}) = \ell(w_{\mathrm{h}})\) for every \(w_{\mathrm{h}} \in W_{\mathrm{h}}\).
  2. For a free unknown \(I\), row \(I\) of the system is \(\sum_{\mathrm{J}} K_{\mathrm{IJ}}\,d_{\mathrm{J}} = f_{\mathrm{I}}\) with \(K_{\mathrm{IJ}} = B(H_{\mathrm{J}}, H_{\mathrm{I}})\) and \(f_{\mathrm{I}} = \ell(H_{\mathrm{I}})\) by point 2 of theorem 12.9, where \(\ell\) is the work of the given loads as in equation (12.7). Since the form \(B\) is linear in its first argument, this is \(B(v_{\mathrm{h}}, H_{\mathrm{I}}) = \ell(H_{\mathrm{I}})\) (corollary 12.11, point 2).
  3. Every \(w_{\mathrm{h}} \in W_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{I}} c_{\mathrm{I}}\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\) over the free unknowns. We multiply the equation of step 2 by \(c_{\mathrm{I}}\) and add over the free \(I\). Since the form \(B\) is linear in its second argument and the form \(\ell\) is linear, \(B(v_{\mathrm{h}}, w_{\mathrm{h}}) = \ell(w_{\mathrm{h}})\). We subtract this from the equation of step 1: for the error \(e = v - v_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(B(e, w_{\mathrm{h}}) = 0\) for every \(w_{\mathrm{h}} \in W_{\mathrm{h}}\). This is the orthogonality of lemma 6.36, proved by the same steps for admissible virtual displacements.
  4. Let the support at \(P\) not prevent the deflection. By theorem 12.13 the field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) is a finite element field with prescribed values zero, so \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v \in W_{\mathrm{h}}\).
  5. For the problem whose only load is a unit force at \(P\), the work of the given loads on a field \(w\) is \(1 \cdot w(x_{\mathrm{P}})\). By point 2 of lemma 12.12 for the regular field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) of this problem, therefore, \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v, w) = w(x_{\mathrm{P}})\) for every admissible virtual displacement \(w\).
  6. The error \(e\) is an admissible virtual displacement: \(v\) and \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) have a continuous deflection and slope, their slope is piecewise continuously differentiable on a common partition (condition (b) for \(v\), the cubic pieces for \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\)), and both are zero, or have zero slope, where a support prevents this. Step 5 with \(w = e\) gives \(e(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = B(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v, e)\).
  7. The form \(B\) is symmetric, since the integrand \(EI\,(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v)''\,e''\) is symmetric in the two fields (definition 6.4), so \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v, e) = B(e, G_{\mathrm{P}}^v)\), and this is zero by steps 3 and 4. So \(e(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = 0\).
  8. Let the support at \(P\) not prevent the slope. For the problem with a unit couple at \(P\) the work of the given loads is \(1 \cdot w'(x_{\mathrm{P}})\), so by point 2 of the lemma \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}^\varphi, w) = w'(x_{\mathrm{P}})\), by theorem 12.13 \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^\varphi \in W_{\mathrm{h}}\), and as in steps 6 and 7, \(e'(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = B(G_{\mathrm{P}}^\varphi, e) = B(e, G_{\mathrm{P}}^\varphi) = 0\).

Source: EP p. 553: »This element gives, for element-wise constant values of E_eI_e, the exact values of w_e and dw_e/dx at the nodes for any load q_e(x)«, and p. 320: with a constant stiffness on each part the deflection and the rotation at the ends of the parts are exact, »the solution is not exact, in general, at an interior point«. COOK PDF p. 61: »consistent nodal loading leads to computed nodal displacements that agree exactly with displacements at these locations in the mathematical model«, p. 66. The proof is not from a source, we assembled steps 1 to 8 from lemma 6.36 and theorem 12.13.

The proof showed that the error of a nodal value is the work of a unit force or couple on the error, and that this work is zero because the deflection under a unit load at the node is in the finite element family.

Figure 12.7 shows where this proof stops. On the left is \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) for the node at the middle of a cantilever with two elements: on each element it is a polynomial of degree at most three (on the second even a line), so it lies in the family and equals the field of the family with its own nodal values. On the right \(P\) is inside the second element. The field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) has a jump of the shear force there (lemma 5.12) and is made of a cubic and a linear piece on the element, so it is not cubic. The field of the family with the same values and slopes at the nodes (blue dashed) differs from it. At the point \(P\) the difference, drawn as a segment with end bars, is \(6{,}5 \cdot 10^{-4}\) against the value \(0{,}1406\) of the field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}^v\) (both multiplied by \(EI\), in m³), that is 0,46 %. The theorem therefore says nothing for a point inside an element. Exactness of the displacement at a point is a property of the nodes, not of every point.

The theorem says that the deflection and the rotation at a node are exact. Internal forces, however, must not be computed from the second derivative of the field. It jumps at a node, because each element has its own cubic polynomial, and as we shall see, it differs from the true moment inside the element as well. What do we get right, then? The forces with which the surroundings hold the element in the state \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) under its load.

Corollary 12.15 The end forces of an element are exact (sile na koncih elementa so točne)

Let the assumptions of theorem 12.14 hold. Let \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) be an element with nodal values \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and with the consistent forces \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) of the load and the point loads inside the element (definition 12.6). Let \(v_0\) be the true deflection of this element, clamped at both ends, under the load and the point loads inside the element (the regular field of the beam \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) with two clamps, which by points 4 and 3 of lemma 12.12 exists and is unique), \(V_0\) and \(M_0\) its shear force and moment, and \(\vek F^0 = \big(-V_0(x_{\mathrm{a}}), -M_0(x_{\mathrm{a}}), V_0(x_{\mathrm{b}}), M_0(x_{\mathrm{b}})\big)^\T\) the forces and couples with which the clamps hold it. Then:

  1. \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}} = \big(-V(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), -M(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), V(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-), M(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-)\big)^\T\), where \(V\) and \(M\) are the true shear force and moment (convention of chapter 5) with the values from the element,
  2. the true deflection on the element is \(v = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} + v_0\),
  3. \(\vek F^0 = -\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\). The column \(\vek F^0\) is called the fixed-end forces (sile vpetega elementa).
Proof

We have to show that the true deflection on the element differs from the Hermite field by the deflection of the clamped element, and that the forces at the ends of the two parts are \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(-\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\).

  1. By theorem 12.14, \(v\) and \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) have the same values and the same slopes at both ends of the element. The function \(w = v - \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) therefore has value zero and slope zero at both ends.
  2. The field \(\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is a polynomial of degree at most three, so its shear force and moment are continuous on the element and \(EI\,(\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}})'''' = 0\). The function \(w\) therefore has on each piece of the element the same equation \(EI\,w'''' = q\) as \(v\), at each interior break point of the element the same jumps of the shear force and the moment as \(v\), and \(w\) and \(w'\) are continuous. With step 1, \(w\) is a regular field of the beam \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) with two clamps. The clamps exclude every line, since a line with value zero and slope zero at \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\) is zero. By point 3 of lemma 12.12, \(w = v_0\), which is point 2.
  3. The shear force \(V = -EI\,v'''\) and the moment \(M = EI\,v''\) are linear in \(v\), so for \(v = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} + v_0\) they are sums of the contributions of the two parts. The column \(\big(-V(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), -M(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), V(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-), M(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-)\big)^\T\) is therefore the sum of the same column for \(\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and the column \(\vek F^0\). The first equals \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) by point 2 of lemma 12.4 and step 9 of its proof.
  4. We apply point 1 of lemma 12.12 to the field \(v_0\) on each piece of the element (\(p = q\)) with the function \(w = H_{\mathrm{i}}\), which is a polynomial. We add the equations over the pieces of the element as in steps 4 and 5 of the proof of the lemma. The interior break points of the element give the terms \(F_{\mathrm{j}}\,H_{\mathrm{i}}(t_{\mathrm{j}}) + C_{\mathrm{j}}\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\), where \(F_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{j}}\) are the given point loads inside the element, and the ends give the term \(\big[V_0\,H_{\mathrm{i}} + M_0\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\). We obtain
    \[\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} EI\,v_0''\,H_{\mathrm{i}}''\,\mathrm{d}x &= \int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} q\,H_{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x + \sum_{\mathrm{j}} \big(F_{\mathrm{j}}\,H_{\mathrm{i}}(t_{\mathrm{j}}) + C_{\mathrm{j}}\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'(t_{\mathrm{j}})\big) \\ &\quad + \big[V_0\,H_{\mathrm{i}} + M_0\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} . \end{aligned}\]
  5. Of the four end values \(H_{\mathrm{i}}(x_{\mathrm{a}})\), \(H_{\mathrm{i}}'(x_{\mathrm{a}})\), \(H_{\mathrm{i}}(x_{\mathrm{b}})\), \(H_{\mathrm{i}}'(x_{\mathrm{b}})\) of the function \(H_{\mathrm{i}}\), one equals \(1\) and the other three are zero (lemma 12.2, point 1, with unit numbers). The last term of step 4 is therefore \(-V_0(x_{\mathrm{a}})\) for \(i = 1\), \(-M_0(x_{\mathrm{a}})\) for \(i = 2\), \(V_0(x_{\mathrm{b}})\) for \(i = 3\) and \(M_0(x_{\mathrm{b}})\) for \(i = 4\), that is \(F_{\mathrm{i}}^0\). The first two terms together are \(f_{\mathrm{i}}\) by equation (12.4). So \(\int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} EI\,v_0''\,H_{\mathrm{i}}''\,\mathrm{d}x = f_{\mathrm{i}} + F_{\mathrm{i}}^0\).
  6. We apply point 1 of the lemma also to the field \(u = H_{\mathrm{i}}\) on the whole element (\(p = 0\), since \(EI\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'''' = 0\)), with \(M_{\mathrm{i}} = EI\,H_{\mathrm{i}}''\), \(V_{\mathrm{i}} = -EI\,H_{\mathrm{i}}'''\), and with the function \(w = v_0\), which has continuous \(v_0\) and \(v_0'\) and a piecewise continuously differentiable \(v_0'\): \(\int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} EI\,H_{\mathrm{i}}''\,v_0''\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[V_{\mathrm{i}}\,v_0 + M_{\mathrm{i}}\,v_0'\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} = 0\), because \(v_0\) and \(v_0'\) are zero at both ends. The left sides in steps 5 and 6 are the same, so \(f_{\mathrm{i}} + F_{\mathrm{i}}^0 = 0\) for every \(i\) and \(\vek F^0 = -\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\), which is point 3.
  7. With step 3 the column for \(v\) equals \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\), which is point 1.

Source: COOK §2.9 PDF p. 63 (consistent forces are the reactions of a beam fixed at both ends, reversed in direction), TIMO PDF p. 361 (»fixed-end moment«, the moment at the end of a bar with both ends fixed under lateral load, in classical frame analysis). The proof is not from a source.

The proof showed that the forces of the surroundings on the element are the sum of the forces of the Hermite field and the fixed-end forces, and that the fixed-end forces are exactly the consistent forces with the opposite sign. ONATE2 (PDF pp. 42 and 47) calls this agreement for a uniform load »fortuitous« and writes that it cannot be generalized to other loadings. For an element with constant stiffness point 3 shows that it holds for every load. For a force of \(500\) N downward at \(a_1 = 0{,}3\) m on an element with \(h = 1\) m, the deflection of the clamped element gives (SymPy) the fixed-end forces \((392\ \text{N},\ 73{,}5\ \text{N m},\ 108\ \text{N},\ -31{,}5\ \text{N m})\), exactly \(-\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) of section 12.2. Point 1 is what we need for internal forces. Point 2 says that an element whose nodal values are exact is particularly simple: its deflection is the Hermite field plus a correction that we compute on a single element. This correction is the error of the field.

Corollary 12.16 Error inside an element for a uniform load (napaka v elementu pri stalni obtežbi)

Let theorem 12.14 hold, and let the element \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) of length \(h\) carry a constant load \(q\) and no point load inside it. With \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}}\),

\[\begin{aligned} v - v_{\mathrm{h}} &= v_0 = \frac{q\,s^2\,(h - s)^2}{24\,EI} , \\ &\qquad M - EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = EI\,v_0'' = \frac{q\,(h^2 - 6\,h\,s + 6\,s^2)}{12} . \end{aligned} \tag{12.10}\]

The error of the deflection \(v - v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is largest in absolute value at the middle of the element, where it is \(q\,h^4/(384\,EI)\). The error of the field moment \(M - EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) is \(q\,h^2/12\) at the ends of the element and \(-q\,h^2/24\) at the middle. If the element is halved, the error of the deflection drops by a factor of 16, the error of the field moment by a factor of 4.

Proof

We have to show that \(v_0\) of corollary 12.15 is indeed this field, then we differentiate it twice and find its largest value.

  1. The field \(q\,s^2(h - s)^2/(24\,EI) = q\,(s^4 - 2h\,s^3 + h^2 s^2)/(24\,EI)\) is a polynomial with fourth derivative \(q/EI\), so it satisfies \(EI\,v'''' = q\) on the whole element, which has no interior break points. At \(s = 0\) and \(s = h\), \(s^2(h - s)^2\) and its derivative \(2s(h - s)(h - 2s)\) are zero, so the value and the slope are zero at both ends. The field is therefore a regular field of the element with two clamps, and by point 3 of lemma 12.12 it is \(v_0\). By point 2 of corollary 12.15, \(v - v_{\mathrm{h}} = v_0\).
  2. We have \(s\,(h - s) = h^2/4 - (s - h/2)^2\), so \(0 \le s\,(h - s) \le h^2/4\) on the element, with equality on the right only at \(s = h/2\). Squaring gives \(s^2(h - s)^2 \le h^4/16\), so \(|v_0|\) is largest at \(s = h/2\), where \(v_0 = q\,h^4/(384\,EI)\).
  3. We differentiate twice: \((s^4 - 2h\,s^3 + h^2 s^2)'' = 12\,s^2 - 12\,h\,s + 2\,h^2\), so \(EI\,v_0'' = q\,(6\,s^2 - 6\,h\,s + h^2)/12\). Since \(M = EI\,v''\) and \(v = v_{\mathrm{h}} + v_0\), \(M - EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = EI\,v_0''\).
  4. At \(s = 0\) and \(s = h\), \(EI\,v_0'' = q\,h^2/12\), at \(s = h/2\) it is \(q\,(1{,}5\,h^2 - 3\,h^2 + h^2)/12 = -q\,h^2/24\).
  5. The error of the deflection is proportional to \(h^4\), the error of the moment to \(h^2\). Under halving \(h \to h/2\) they decrease by \(2^4 = 16\) and \(2^2 = 4\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. COOK §2.9 PDF p. 63 describes the same correction: the moment field of the fixed element (»fixed-end element moment fields«) is added to the moment produced by the nodal displacements.

12.3.4 Internal forces after the computation and lumped loads (notranje sile po računu in zbrane obremenitve)

The results give a procedure for internal forces after the computation, post-processing (notranje sile po računu). For each element we compute \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) and by point 1 of corollary 12.15 we obtain the exact \(V\) and \(M\) at the ends of the element. Inside the element we use the statics of the element with its own load, or the correction \(v_0\). The field moment \(EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) we do not use, except as an approximation that tends to the true one as the mesh is refined.

A point force inside an element shows this well. A cantilever with a single force of \(500\) N downward at \(a_1 = 0{,}3\) m has by definition 12.6 the consistent forces \(F_1\,H_{\mathrm{i}}^e(a_1)\), that is \((-392\ \text{N},\ -73{,}5\ \text{N m},\ -108\ \text{N},\ 31{,}5\ \text{N m})\). The tip displacement is \(-0{,}96859\) mm, exact, equal to \(-F\,a_1^2(3L - a_1)/(6EI)\) with \(F = 500\) N. The forces and couples at the ends of the element are \((500\ \text{N},\ 150\ \text{N m},\ 0,\ 0)\), so \(V(0) = -500\) N and \(M(0) = -150\) N m, as statics gives (\(500\ \text{N} \cdot 0{,}3\) m). The field moment, however, is \(EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''(0) = -76{,}5\) N m, only 51 % of the true one. The displacements at the nodes are exact, while the field moment at the clamp is 49 % too small in absolute value.

For a uniform load the consistent nodal loads contain, besides the forces \(q\,h/2\), the two couples \(\pm q\,h^2/12\) (equation (12.5)). What happens if we omit the couples? Lumped loads (zbrana obtežba) give the nodes only forces, for a uniform load \(q\,h/2\) at each end of an element, without couples. COOK (PDF p. 65) calls such a column »reduced« or »lumped«, and for a cantilever with two elements finds that, with the field moment omitted, it gives »approximate nodal displacements but exact bending moments at nodes« (PDF p. 66). The forces are the same in both systems, the lumped load omits only the couples. Why is this enough to spoil the displacements but not the moments?

The lumped system is a beam with loads at the nodes, so by theorem 12.13 the finite element field is exactly the deflection of this other beam. The deflection at the middle is therefore different from the true one, because the load is different. The simply supported beam, however, is statically determinate, so its internal forces follow from equilibrium. On each element the two forces \(q\,h/2\) at its ends have the same resultant \(q\,h\) and the same moment about any point as the uniform load on the element, because their centre is at the middle of the element. The whole vertical force at a support, the reaction together with the lumped force acting at the support, is determined by the moment equilibrium of the whole beam about the other support, so it is the same in both systems. The moment at the node \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the moment of these forces and of the load to the left of \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) about \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) (the force at the node itself has lever arm zero). To the left of \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) lie whole elements, so the moment is the same in both systems, \(M(x_{\mathrm{i}}) = -q\,x_{\mathrm{i}}\,(L - x_{\mathrm{i}})/2\), and \(M(L/2) = 500\) N m. The deflection, however, differs:

2 \(-7{,}97194\) 0,8000 500,00
4 \(-9{,}46668\) 0,9500 500,00

The rotation at the support for \(n = 2\) is \(-1{,}1958 \cdot 10^{-2}\) instead of \(-1{,}5944 \cdot 10^{-2}\). Figure 12.10 shows both loadings and the ratios.

What have we shown? For a beam with a stiffness constant on each element, the finite element field for loads only at the nodes is exact on the whole beam, because the rows of the system are the equilibrium of the nodes and the true deflection of an unloaded element is cubic (theorem 12.13). For any load with consistent forces the deflection and the rotation at the nodes are exact, because the deflection under a unit force or couple at a node is in the family and the error is orthogonal to it (theorem 12.14). The forces at the ends of an element, \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\), are exact internal forces, because the true deflection of an element differs from the Hermite field only by the deflection of the clamped element, whose forces are \(-\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) (corollary 12.15). This deflection is the error of the field inside the element, in closed form for a uniform load (corollary 12.16). The results for the cantilever under its own weight in chapter 6 are now explained: the tip displacement is exact because the tip is a node, and the clamp moment was 17 % too small in absolute value because we computed it from the second derivative of the field, which differs from the true moment by \(q\,h^2/12\).

In this section we did not use a general error estimate for interpolation with Hermite polynomials. For a uniform load we obtained the error of the deflection and of the moment in closed form, for other loads this chapter shows it only by numbers.
