Chapter 18 Plates

18.3 Energy, boundary conditions and uniqueness of a plate

We obtained the plate equation \(D\,\nabla^4 w = q\) (theorem 18.12) from the equilibrium of a finite piece of the middle plane. The solution of a fourth-order equation is determined only when we say what happens at the edge of the plate, and the equilibrium of a piece in the interior does not say that. The beam was the same: the fourth-order equation had four free constants, the conditions at the ends fixed them, and theorem 5.70 showed that the first variation of the energy gives the equation and the conditions at once (section 5.11.4). Chapter 5 promised for plates that the divergence theorem replaces integration by parts. We ask three questions. Which energy does a plate have, and is it always non-negative? Which equation and which edge conditions follow from the stationarity of the energy? And do these conditions determine the deflection uniquely?

The answer will have the form the beam already knows, with one novelty. The edge of a plate is a curve and not a point, so there is also a rotation in the direction of the edge and with it a twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), which the beam does not have. We shall therefore have to find that at a free edge the transverse force and the twisting moment merge into one force, and that point forces appear at the corners.

18.3.1 Model and energy

Take a plate (definition 18.1) with a rectangular plan \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) of the middle plane, with the axis \(x\) along the side \(a\) and the axis \(y\) along the side \(b\), of constant thickness \(h\), of a homogeneous isotropic material with modulus of elasticity \(E\) and Poisson's ratio \(\nu\) (definitions 3.1 and 3.8), where \(-1 < \nu < 1\). The deflection of the middle plane is \(w(x, y)\) and the load \(q(x, y)\) per unit area points in the direction of \(w\). The idealizations of the Kirchhoff plate hold (definition 18.2): the plate is thin, the deflection is small, the normals stay straight and perpendicular to the middle plane, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\) is neglected and every layer is in plane stress. The boundary of the rectangle consists of four edges \(x = 0\), \(x = a\), \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\), which meet at four corners. For now there is no load on the edges, so the edges are either fixed or free (loaded edges are described by the remark after theorem 18.21). As an example we take the steel plate of the running example of the chapter: \(a = b = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm, \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\), \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m (definition 18.6) and \(q = 10\) kPa.

Let us write down what we know from section 18.1, in the same sign convention as for the beam (\(M = EI\,v''\), equation (5.24)). By theorem 18.7 the moments per unit length are

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{y}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{yy}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}} , \tag{18.20}\]

and \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) are by definition 18.5 the integrals \(-\int z\,\sigma\,\mathrm{d}z\) through the thickness, with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\). Below we also need the transverse forces \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = -(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}})\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = -(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}})\) (definition 18.9, the relation to the moments is the moment balance in the proof of theorem 18.12, steps 2 and 3), where an index after the comma means a partial derivative. The moments and transverse forces of a plate are per unit length of the edge (\(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) in N m/m, \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) in N/m), so they differ from \(M\) and \(V\) of a beam in N m and N.

The energy of a beam was the integral of the energy density over the body (definition 5.76). For a plate we take it from the same definition for the body \(V = R \times [-h/2, h/2]\) that the plate occupies (\(z\) is the coordinate perpendicular to the middle plane), and we write it with the deflection. That the integral below is indeed this energy is shown by lemma 18.15.

In the plan the plate is the rectangle \(R\). Take a small piece of area \(\mathrm{d}A\). Its curvature is measured in three directions: \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) along the axis \(x\), \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) along the axis \(y\) and the twist \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\). The square of the sum \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) is the first term of the density, the difference of the product \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and the square of the twist is the second. The sum of the density over all pieces is the energy.

Definition 18.14 Strain energy of a plate (deformacijska energija plošče)

Let the plate be as above and \(w\) its deflection. The strain energy of the plate is the number

\[U(w) = \frac12 \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\big[ (\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2\,(1 - \nu)\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2) \big]\,\mathrm{d}A , \tag{18.21}\]

where \(\nabla^2 w = w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(w\) has continuous derivatives up to second order on \(R\). It is a functional of the field \(w\): the input is a function \(w\) on \(R\) and the output is a number in joules.

Source: EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431, written there with the moments). The form with \((1 - \nu)\) follows from it (lemma 18.15).

Why the energy has this form is shown by the next lemma. It also shows that it equals half the integral of the products of moments and curvatures, as for the beam \(\tfrac12\int M\,v''\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Lemma 18.15 Strain energy of a plate in terms of the moments (energija plošče iz momentov)

Under the idealizations of the Kirchhoff plate the strain energy of the body \(V = R \times [-h/2, h/2]\) with the displacement field \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{y}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{z}} = w\) (theorem 18.3) is

\[U = \frac12 \int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}} \big)\,\mathrm{d}A , \tag{18.22}\]

which equals (18.21): every moment is multiplied by its curvature.

Proof

We must show that the integral of the energy density through the thickness gives the moments, and that inserting the relations (18.20) gives (18.21).

  1. The normal stays straight and turns by the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\), so the point at height \(z\) above the middle plane moves along \(x\) by \(-z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\). The strains from the displacement field (equation (1.71)) are therefore \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\), the others are zero.
  2. The energy density in plane stress (half the product of stress and strain) is \(\tfrac12\,(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}})\), since \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yz}}\) are by the idealizations zero or multiplied by a zero strain (definition 18.2, theorem 3.19).
  3. By the theorem on the order of integration (theorem 4.9) \(U = \int_{\mathrm{R}} \int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} (\dots)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}A\). The strains of step 1 are linear in \(z\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) do not depend on \(z\), so we take them out (lemma 3.54). What remains are the integrals \(\int (-z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}})\,\mathrm{d}z = M_{\mathrm{x}}\), which is the moment of the stress arrows \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\) about the middle plane, and the same for \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) (definition 18.5). This is (18.22).
  4. Insert (18.20): \(M_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2 M_{\mathrm{xy}} w_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,[\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\,]\). Since \((\nabla^2 w)^2 = w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + 2 w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2\), the same expression equals \((\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2(1 - \nu)(w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2)\). SymPy checked the difference of the two forms as an equality of polynomials in the second derivatives.

Source: EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431) for equation (18.22). The proof by steps is not from the source.

Is the energy always non-negative, and when is it zero? The energy of a beam \(\tfrac12\int EI\,v''^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) is a square and therefore non-negative. For a plate the square bracket is a difference of two expressions, and a difference is not necessarily non-negative. Let us test it on a twist, which we make with a piece of cardboard (figure 18.25).

We lay a square plate on a table, the axes \(x\) and \(y\) lie in the table and the deflection \(w\) is drawn upward. We leave the corner \((0, 0)\) on the table and hold the corners \((a, 0)\) and \((0, a)\) on it, and we raise the corner \((a, a)\) by \(1\) mm. Three corners determine a plane and the fourth is out of it, so the plate cannot merely tilt, it must twist. The edges \(x = a\) and \(y = a\) stay straight lines that rise to the raised corner, and all the other lines of the grid are straight too: the deflection is \(w = c\,x\,y\), linear in \(x\) at constant \(y\) and the other way round, with \(c = 1\ \text{mm}/a^2 = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\). Therefore \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) and \(\nabla^2 w = 0\), while the mixed derivative is \(w_{\mathrm{xy}} = c\). For the plate to stay so, four forces \(F\) must hold it, upward at \((0, 0)\) and \((a, a)\), downward at \((a, 0)\) and \((0, a)\). How large they are we compute in section 18.3.6.

Why \(\nabla^2 w = 0\) although the plate is twisted is shown by the diagonals. The diagonal \(s\) from \((0, 0)\) to \((a, a)\) lies below its chord, so it curves upward: along it \(w = c\,s^2/2\), where \(s\) is the distance from \((0, 0)\), and the curvature is \(w_{\mathrm{ss}} = c\). The diagonal \(t\) from \((a, 0)\) to \((0, a)\) lies above its chord and has the curvature \(w_{\mathrm{tt}} = -c\). The sum of the curvatures in two perpendicular directions does not depend on the choice of the axes (section 18.2.4), and in the axes of the diagonals \(\nabla^2 w = c + (-c) = 0\), as in the axes \(x\), \(y\). Germain's energy sees only this sum, so it does not see the twist. The term \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\) in the square bracket, however, is a product: in the axes \(x\), \(y\) it is \(0 \cdot 0 - c^2\), in the axes of the diagonals \(c \cdot (-c) - 0^2\), both times \(-c^2\). Curvatures of opposite signs cancel in the sum, but not in the product.

The first term in the square bracket is therefore zero, the second is \(-2(1 - \nu)(0 - c^2) = 2(1 - \nu)\,c^2\), hence positive. For the square \(a = 500\) mm with a lift of \(1\) mm and \(\nu = 0{,}3\) the energy is

\[U = \tfrac12\,D \cdot 2(1 - \nu)\,c^2 \cdot a^2 = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c^2 a^2 = 17\,948{,}7 \cdot 0{,}7 \cdot 0{,}004^2 \cdot 0{,}5^2\ \text{J} = 50{,}26\ \text{mJ} .\]

The energy \(\tfrac12\int D\,(\nabla^2 w)^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) would be zero for this field, although the plate is twisted. What happens with this energy and why it matters here is told by lemma 18.18.

For three cases of curvature at \(\nu = 0{,}3\) the density of the energy is always to the right of zero and at least as long as the lower bound. For a single curvature the bound is shorter, for opposite curvatures and for the twist the two are equal. The next lemma says this.

Lemma 18.16 Non-negativity of the plate energy (nenegativnost energije plošče)

Let \(D > 0\) and \(-1 < \nu < 1\). The energy density in (18.21) satisfies the inequality

\[\tfrac12\,D\,\big[ (\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2(1 - \nu)(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2) \big] \ \ge\ \tfrac12\,D\,(1 - |\nu|)\,\big( w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \big) \ \ge\ 0\]

at every point (density above the bound), hence \(U(w) \ge 0\). If \(U(w) = 0\) and \(w\) is twice continuously differentiable on \(R\), then \(w = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) on \(R\) with constants \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\gamma\) (rigid motion of the plate).

Proof

We must show the estimate of the density, then derive from zero energy that all second derivatives are zero, and from that the form of the field. Let us look at the inequalities and at the step to the form of the field on a figure.

  1. By lemma 18.15, step 4, the expression in the square bracket equals \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\).
  2. For any numbers \(s\), \(t\) we have \(2\,|s\,t| \le s^2 + t^2\). Let \(|s| \ge |t|\), otherwise we exchange the roles. A square of side \(|s|\) and above it a square of side \(|t|\) have the area \(s^2 + t^2\) together. From them we put together two rectangles with sides \(|s|\) and \(|t|\): the bottom strip of the first square, and the column made of the left part of the first square above the strip and the whole second square. What is left is a square of side \(|s| - |t|\), whose area is non-negative, so \(s^2 + t^2 = 2\,|s|\,|t| + (|s| - |t|)^2 \ge 2\,|s\,t|\). For \(|s| = |t|\) the leftover vanishes and equality holds. With \(s = w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(t = w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and the factor \(|\nu|\) we get \(2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}} \ge -2\,|\nu|\,|w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}}| \ge -|\nu|\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2)\). Therefore \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}} \ge (1 - |\nu|)(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2)\).
  3. Since \(\nu \le |\nu| < 1\), we have \(1 - \nu \ge 1 - |\nu| > 0\): for \(\nu = 0{,}3\) the segments \(1 - \nu\) and \(1 - |\nu|\) are equally long, \(0{,}7\), for \(\nu = -0{,}3\) the segment \(1 - \nu = 1{,}3\) is longer than \(1 - |\nu| = 0{,}7\). Therefore \(2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \ge 2\,(1 - |\nu|)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\). Steps 2 and 3 together give the estimate of the density.
  4. The density is continuous and non-negative. If it were positive at some point, it would by continuity be positive on a small square around it, as in step 3 of the proof of lemma 18.11, and the integral would be positive, which would mean \(U > 0\). For \(U = 0\) therefore \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} = w_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) at every point.
  5. By step 4 the function \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) has zero partial derivatives \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\). From \((0, 0)\) we go parallel to the \(x\) axis to \((x, 0)\), where the derivative of \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the path is \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\), then parallel to the \(y\) axis to \((x, y)\), where this derivative is \(w_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\). By the fundamental theorem of calculus (theorem 3.55) \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) does not change on either segment, so \(w_{\mathrm{x}}(x, y) = w_{\mathrm{x}}(0, 0) = \beta\) at every point of the rectangle. Likewise \(w_{\mathrm{y}} = \gamma\). The function \(w - \beta\,x - \gamma\,y\) has zero derivatives and is therefore constant, \(\alpha\).

Source: The estimate is our own reasoning. For the energy EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431), positivity for the beam and the body is lemma 6.7. The proof is not from the source.

The lemma has two consequences. The energy is non-negative, so a minimum makes sense (theorem 4.91). Zero energy, however, does not mean rest: the plane \(\alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) is a rigid motion: a lift \(\alpha\), a tilt \(\beta\) in the direction \(x\) and a tilt \(\gamma\) in the direction \(y\), and the curvature stays zero. When zero energy does mean rest is the question of uniqueness in section 18.3.6.

The integrals by parts that we need below are done in the plane by the divergence theorem. For a rectangle lemma 18.11 proved it (figure 18.16). Here we write it in components, as we need it, with the outward normals of the four edges.

Lemma 18.17 Divergence theorem in the plane for a rectangle (izrek o divergenci v ravnini za pravokotnik)

Let \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) and \(f\) a continuously differentiable function on an open set containing \(R\). Then

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^b f(a, y)\,\mathrm{d}y - \int_0^b f(0, y)\,\mathrm{d}y , \qquad \int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^a f(x, b)\,\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^a f(x, 0)\,\mathrm{d}x . \tag{18.23}\]

With the outward normal \((n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}})\) of the edges this reads \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} f\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} f\,n_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s\), where the integral over \(\partial R\) means the sum of the integrals over the four sides, with the variable \(s\), the arc length along the side.

Proof

We must show the first equation, the second is the same with the axes exchanged.

  1. Lemma 18.11, point 1, with the outward normal of the edge and the field \(\vek v = (f, 0)\) gives \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} f\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\).
  2. On the edge \(x = a\) we have \(\vek n = \vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), hence \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\), on the edge \(x = 0\) we have \(\vek n = -\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = -1\), and on the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) we have \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\). What remains are the integrals \(\int_0^b f(a, y)\,\mathrm{d}y - \int_0^b f(0, y)\,\mathrm{d}y\), which is the first equation.

Source: A special case of lemma 18.11. For a general edge the same statement is a consequence of 4.40 for a body cut into pieces.

For a rectangle the lemma is all we need. For plates with oblique or curved edges we would take corollary 4.40 instead of the lemma and describe the edge by the normal \(\vek n\) and the arc length \(s\), as EP (7.2.101)-(7.2.108) (p. 433-435) does. The rectangle is chosen as the simplest example, not as a limit of the method. For the same reason only, we keep to edges parallel to the axes in what follows.

With the lemma let us estimate what the term with \((1 - \nu)\) adds. The energy that Germain used for a plate with the principal curvatures (section 18.2) is \(U_{\mathrm{G}}(w) = \tfrac12\int D\,(\nabla^2 w)^2\,\mathrm{d}A\). It gives the same plate equation, and the difference lies in the boundary conditions.

Lemma 18.18 The determinant term and the edge (člen determinante in rob)

Let \(w\) have continuous derivatives up to third order on an open set containing \(R\). Then

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \big)\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^b \big[\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\big]_{\mathrm{x}=0}^{x=a}\,\mathrm{d}y - \int_0^a \big[\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\big]_{\mathrm{y}=0}^{y=b}\,\mathrm{d}x , \tag{18.24}\]

so \(U - U_{\mathrm{G}} = -D\,(1 - \nu)\) times this boundary expression. It is zero if the edge of \(R\) is clamped (\(w_{\mathrm{x}} = w_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) on the whole edge) or simply supported (\(w = 0\) on the whole edge), but in general it is not zero on a free edge.

Proof

We must show an identity between derivatives and integrate it with lemma 18.17.

  1. By the product rule \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}) = w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{y}}(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}) = w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\). The difference is \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\) (the order of the derivatives may be exchanged because the third derivatives are continuous). SymPy confirms this.
  2. Lemma 18.17 with \(f = w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(f = w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) gives (18.24).
  3. On a clamped edge \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = w_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) along the whole edge, so the boundary terms are zero. On a simply supported edge \(w = 0\) along the edge, so the derivatives along the edge are zero: on an edge \(x = \text{const}\) we have \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\), on an edge \(y = \text{const}\) we have \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\). The terms \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) therefore have a zero factor \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) on the first edges and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) on the second. On a free edge nothing requires this. For the twist \(w = c\,xy\) (figure 18.25) all edges are straight lines, so \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) on the edges \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\) and the first integral is zero. On the edge \(y = 0\), which stays on the table, \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\). On the edge \(y = a\), which rises from \(0\) to \(1\) mm, the slope is \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = c\,a\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}} = c\), so the second term is \(-\int_0^a c\,a \cdot c\,\mathrm{d}x = -c^2 a^2 \ne 0\), which agrees with the integral \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} (-c^2)\,\mathrm{d}A\).

Source: The proof is not from the source. EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431) uses this term in the energy, TIMO (PDF p. 128-129) describes Germain's energy.

The lemma tells why Germain's energy is right for clamped and simply supported edges and wrong for free ones. Both energies give the same fourth-order equation, because they differ only by a boundary expression. At a free edge, however, the boundary expression changes the natural conditions. The energy \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) gives by the same computation as in theorem 18.21, without the mixed term, the conditions \(\nabla^2 w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}(\nabla^2 w) = 0\) at a free edge. The twist \(w = c\,xy\) satisfies them, so by \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) a free plate would stay twisted without forces. This is not true: it needs forces \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) at the corners (section 18.3.4).

18.3.2 Edges: clamped, simply supported, free

Before we derive the conditions from the energy, let us say what kinds of edges we know and what is prescribed on each. We look at the edge of the plate from the side (figure 18.29). The middle plane is drawn as a thick line that ends at the edge \(x = a\), the undeformed position as a dashed line. The first edge is clamped: a solid wall holds it, so the plate must reach the wall with a horizontal tangent, on the dashed line. The deflection and the rotation are zero there, \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\), where \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\) is the derivative in the direction of the outward normal \(n\) of the edge lying in the middle plane. The second edge is simply supported, like a beam on a pin support: the deflection is zero and the tangent is free, which the inclined line shows. The rotation is free because a pin support gives no moment, so the moment at the edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) must be zero. The third edge is free. Nothing holds it, so it moves and turns as the plate demands, and as at the free end of a beam (section 5.11.4) both the moment and the transverse force at it must be zero.

At the edge \(x = a\) the moment at the edge is \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = M_{\mathrm{x}}\), the transverse force is \(Q_{\mathrm{n}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), and the edge also carries the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), the moment of the shear stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\), which point in opposite directions above and below. For a beam there were one force and one moment at the end, here there are three quantities, and a fourth-order equation accepts only two conditions at each edge (as the beam, section 5.11.4). How three quantities shrink to two we show in two ways, with figure 18.30 for the definition and with the derivation in theorem 18.21. The quantity that replaces \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) at a free edge is the transverse force increased by the effect of the twisting moment, and we call it the effective shear force (nadomestna prečna sila). We write its form down in advance.

Definition 18.19 Boundary conditions of a plate (robni pogoji plošče)

Let a rectangular plate (section 18.3.1) have an edge with outward normal \(\vek n = (n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}}) \in \{\pm\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}, \pm\vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\}\), and let \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = n_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{x}} + n_{\mathrm{y}}\,w_{\mathrm{y}}\). On this edge we define the moment \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) and the effective shear force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), both per unit length of the edge:

\[M_{\mathrm{n}} = n_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,M_{\mathrm{x}} + n_{\mathrm{y}}^2\,M_{\mathrm{y}} , \qquad V_{\mathrm{n}} = -\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\big(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\big) - n_{\mathrm{y}}\,\big(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\big) . \tag{18.25}\]

With (18.20) on the edge \(x = a\) (\(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\)) we have \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{yy}})\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = -D\,\big[\,w_{\mathrm{xxx}} + (2 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\,\big]\), on the edge \(y = b\) the same with \(x \leftrightarrow y\). An edge is

  • clamped (vpet), if \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\) on it,
  • simply supported (prostoležeč), if \(w = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on it,
  • free (prost), if \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on it.

The conditions \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\) are conditions on the field \(w\) (essential, as for the beam), the conditions \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) are conditions on its derivatives (natural).

Source: EP (7.2.99)-(7.2.108) (p. 433-435), VK (PDF p. 31-36), ONATE2 §5.2.7 (PDF p. 274-276). The sign of \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) is derived in the proof of theorem 18.21, EP has the sign of \(M_{\mathrm{ns}}\) by another convention.

In the lectures the essential conditions are called kinematic (kinematična) and the natural ones static (statična). The names say what a condition prescribes. A kinematic condition prescribes the motion of the edge, the deflection \(w\) and the slope \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\) in the direction of the normal. A static condition prescribes what the edge carries, the moment \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) and the effective shear force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), in the lectures efektivna prečna sila. The quantities come in two pairs, \(w\) with \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\) with \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\), and on every edge we prescribe exactly one of each pair. Why not both? The support that holds the edge in place carries whatever force the plate demands: if we prescribe the deflection, the force is a reaction that the solution gives, and if we prescribe the force, the deflection is what we compute. The same holds for the rotation and the moment. The prescribed value need not be zero. A support may settle, \(w = \bar w\), and along the edge a given moment \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar M\) in N m/m or a given line force \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar V\) in N/m may act. The example is the circular plate of radius \(a = 250\) mm and thickness \(h = 10\) mm of section 18.6, simply supported and loaded along its edge by a uniform moment \(M_{\mathrm{a}} = 100\) N m/m. The conditions on the edge \(r = a\) are the kinematic \(w = 0\) and the static \(M_{\mathrm{r}} = M_{\mathrm{a}}\). By equation (18.45) the solution is \(w = -M_{\mathrm{a}}\,(a^2 - r^2)/(2D\,(1 + \nu))\) with \(M_{\mathrm{r}} = M_\theta = M_{\mathrm{a}}\) at every point. The centre moves by \(-M_{\mathrm{a}}\,a^2/(2D\,(1 + \nu)) = -0{,}134\) mm and the edge rotates by \(M_{\mathrm{a}}\,a/(D\,(1 + \nu)) = 1{,}07\cdot 10^{-3}\) (NumPy, \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m). The deflection on the edge we prescribed, the rotation the solution gave.

At the edge \(x = a\) we have \(-(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}) = Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), so \(V_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\): the effective force is the transverse force reduced by the derivative of the twisting moment along the edge. Why exactly so we show first on figure 18.30, and the computation follows in theorem 18.21.

Let us see how the effective force arises. We look at the edge from the side. The twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) per unit length of the edge is the moment of pairs of horizontal shear forces in the plate, and a piece of edge of length \(\Delta s\) has the moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\Delta s\). We replace the pair of horizontal forces by a pair of vertical forces \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) at a distance \(\Delta s\) along the edge. This pair also has the moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\Delta s\), and the moment vectors of both pairs point in the direction of the normal of the edge, so the pairs are equivalent (VK, PDF p. 33-34). In the figure \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) grows along the edge, so the arrows on the right pieces are longer. At the joints of the pieces the lower force of one piece and the upper force of the neighbour add up, and their difference \(\Delta M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) remains. The difference divided by \(\Delta s\) is a force per unit length of the edge and tends to the derivative \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{s}}\) as \(\Delta s \to 0\). This force joins the transverse force \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\), and the sum is the effective force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), whose sign the derivation in theorem 18.21 fixes. At the ends of the edge one whole force \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) remains. At a corner two edges meet and each gives its force \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), together \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), with the sign of the corner (section 18.3.4).

This replacement of a moment by a pair of vertical forces, which Kirchhoff used to reduce the conditions, is statically equivalent (definition 11.48): both pairs have zero resultant and the same moment. It is an assumption of the model, so let us say why it holds here, when it does not and what it costs. It holds because by the idealization of the Kirchhoff plate the normals stay straight, so a small piece of the edge is rigid, and on a rigid body a pair of forces may be replaced by another pair with the same moment without changing the equilibrium (VK, PDF p. 34). It does not hold in a real plate right at the edge. There the horizontal stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) of the twisting moment and the vertical stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) of the transverse force are distributed differently through the thickness, their difference is a self-balanced load of the edge and causes an additional stress field that dies out quickly with the distance from the edge (VK, PDF p. 36-37), over a distance of about two thicknesses (VK, PDF p. 216-217). So it costs accuracy in a band of width about \(2h\) along the edge, for the plate of the example \(2 \cdot 10\ \text{mm} = 20\) mm, which is \(4\,\%\) of the side \(a = 500\) mm. Farther from the edge the additional field practically vanishes (VK, PDF p. 217), and for plates of a solid isotropic material the corrections are as a rule negligible (VK, PDF p. 37).

18.3.3 First variation of a plate

For the beam we used integration by parts twice so that the variation \(\eta\) came out of the derivatives. For the plate we do the same in the plane, along the edge of the rectangle, with lemma 18.17. First we compute what integration by parts gives for the term that will appear in the first variation.

Lemma 18.20 Green's identity for a plate (Greenova enačba plošče)

Let \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\), \(D\) constant, \(w\) a function with continuous partial derivatives up to fourth order and \(\eta\) a function with continuous derivatives up to second order, both on an open set containing \(R\). Let \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) be the moments of the field \(w\) by (18.20) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) by (18.25). Then

\[\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}} \big)\,\mathrm{d}A &= \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\nabla^4 w\;\eta\,\mathrm{d}A \\ &\quad + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta \big)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c) , \end{aligned} \tag{18.26}\]

where \(\nabla^4 w = w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + 2\,w_{\mathrm{xxyy}} + w_{\mathrm{yyyy}}\). The sum runs over the four corners \(c\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}}\) is the component of the normal of the edge parallel to the axis \(y\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\) the component of the normal of the edge parallel to the axis \(x\), and the two edges meet at \(c\).

Proof

We must show that each of the three terms of the left side gives its part of the right side. We apply lemma 18.17 with a function that is a product of two fields, and the product rule.

  1. For \(f = M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\) we have \(f_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\). By the lemma \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). Once more with \(f = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). Together \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,(M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  2. With \(y\) instead of \(x\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,(M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}} - M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{yy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  3. The mixed term: \(\eta_{\mathrm{xy}} = \eta_{\mathrm{yx}}\), because the second derivatives are continuous. First by \(x\) with \(f = M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\), then by \(y\) with \(f = M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). The same term in the opposite order: \(\int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). Twice the mixed term is the sum of both.
  4. The terms \(\int n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) on the edges \(x = \text{const}\) and \(\int n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) on the edges \(y = \text{const}\) contain the derivative of \(\eta\) along the edge. By integration by parts (corollary 3.56) on an edge that runs from \(s_0\) to \(s_1\) in the direction of the larger coordinate we have \(\int M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{s}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}s = \big[M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\big]_{\mathrm{s}_0}^{s_1} - \int M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{s}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s\). On the edge \(x = a\) this gives \(\int_0^b M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}y = \big[M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\big]_0^b - \int_0^b M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}y\), and the same on the other edges.
  5. On an edge \(x = \text{const}\) with the normal \(\pm\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\) we collect the terms with \(\eta\): \(-n_{\mathrm{x}}\,(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}})\), on an edge \(y = \text{const}\) \(-n_{\mathrm{y}}\,(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}})\), which is \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) from (18.25). The terms with the derivatives \(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) give \(n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\) on an edge \(x = \text{const}\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}} = M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\) on an edge \(y = \text{const}\).
  6. The point terms from step 4: the end \(s_1\) of an edge gets \(+M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) and its start \(s_0\) gets \(-M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\), both multiplied by \(n_{\mathrm{x}}\) or \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\) of that edge. The corner \(c = (a, b)\) gets \(+M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(x = a\) (upper end, \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\)) and \(+M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(y = b\) (right end, \(n_{\mathrm{y}} = 1\)), together \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). The corner \((a, 0)\) gets \(-M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(x = a\) (lower end) and \(-M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(y = 0\) (\(n_{\mathrm{y}} = -1\), right end), together \(-2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). Similarly \((0, b)\) and \((0, 0)\) give \(-2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) and \(+2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). In all four cases this is \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) with the normals of both edges at the corner.
  7. The integrals over \(R\) in steps 1 to 3 have the factor \(\eta\) with the sum \(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}} + M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{yy}}\), which by (18.20) equals \(D\,\nabla^4 w\) (checked with SymPy). The sum of steps 1 to 7 is (18.26).

Source: The same procedure is in EP (7.2.96)-(7.2.108) (p. 432-435), where the term with \(\partial\delta w/\partial s\) is integrated by parts along the edge and the point forces at the corners are \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = -2M_{\mathrm{ns}}\) (7.2.106) (p. 434). The proof by steps is not from the source.

The energy of a plate is quadratic, a parabola in \(\lambda\): \(U(w + \lambda\,\eta) = U(w) + \lambda\,B(w, \eta) + \lambda^2\,U(\eta)\) with \(B(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A\) (from (18.22), since \(U\) is half of the symmetric form \(B(w, w)\), as in lemma 6.7). The total potential energy (definition 4.89) is \(\Pi(w) = U(w) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\).

For the trial function \(w + \lambda\,\eta\) the total energy as a function of \(\lambda\) is a parabola. At \(\lambda = 0\) it has the value \(\Pi(w)\), the slope of the tangent is the first variation \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta)\), defined below, and the lowest point is the minimum over \(\lambda\). If the first variation is zero, the tangent is horizontal, as for the beam.

Admissible fields \(w\) are those with continuous derivatives up to fourth order on an open set containing \(R\) that satisfy at each edge the essential conditions of its kind (definition 18.19). An admissible variation \(\eta\) has continuous derivatives up to second order on an open set containing \(R\) and satisfies the same essential condition with zero: \(\eta = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\) on a clamped edge, \(\eta = 0\) on a simply supported edge, nothing on a free edge. The first variation is \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = B(w, \eta) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\), the derivative of the function \(\lambda \mapsto \Pi(w + \lambda\,\eta)\) at \(\lambda = 0\).

For the steps with the bumps we need functions that have a continuous second derivative (figure 18.32).

The bump \(\psi_{\alpha\beta}(t) = (t - \alpha)^4\,(\beta - t)^4\) for \(\alpha \le t \le \beta\) and zero elsewhere (lemma 4.123, point 1) is different from zero only on the interval \([\alpha, \beta]\). The function \(g_1(x) = (x/a)^2\,(3 - 2x/a)\) has \(g_1(0) = g_1'(0) = 0\), \(g_1(a) = 1\) and \(g_1'(a) = 0\): it lifts the end \(x = a\) by \(1\) without tilting it. The function \(g_2(x) = x^2\,(x - a)/a^2\) has \(g_2(0) = g_2'(0) = 0\), \(g_2(a) = 0\) and \(g_2'(a) = 1\): it tilts the end \(x = a\) without lifting it.

A bump also singles out the sign of a function, which the proof needs on the edge. Let us see how.

Take a continuous function \(h\) along the edge that is positive at the point \(y_0\). On a short enough interval a continuous function changes by less than half the value \(h(y_0)\), so it stays on the whole interval \([\alpha, \beta]\) around \(y_0\) above the dashed line at the height \(\tfrac12\,h(y_0)\). On this interval we place the bump \(\psi_{\alpha\beta}\), which is positive inside the interval and zero outside. The product \(h\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}\) is therefore zero outside \([\alpha, \beta]\) and positive inside, and the area under it, the integral \(\int h\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{d}y\), is positive. What \(h\) does elsewhere, also where it is negative, does not change the integral, since the bump is zero there. If the integral with every such bump is zero, \(h\) can therefore be positive nowhere, by the mirror image negative nowhere either, and \(h = 0\).

Theorem 18.21 First variation of a plate (prva variacija plošče)

Let the plate be as in section 18.3.1 with a load \(q\) continuous on \(R\) and with edges of the clamped, simply supported or free kind, and let \(w\) be an admissible function. Then \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = 0\) for every admissible variation \(\eta\) exactly when

\[D\,\nabla^4 w = q \quad \text{on } R , \tag{18.27}\]

on every simply supported edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on every free edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), and at every corner where two free edges meet \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).

Proof

We must show the first variation with Green's identity, then with each kind of variation single out one condition.

  1. By the definition of the first variation and lemma 18.20, equation (18.26), for every admissible \(\eta\) (contributions of the edges, the corners and the interior)
    \[\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} (D\,\nabla^4 w - q)\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c) . \tag{18.28}\]
  2. If equation (18.27) and the conditions of the theorem hold, then \(\delta\Pi = 0\). The first term is zero. On a clamped edge \(\eta = \partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\), on a simply supported one \(\eta = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on a free one \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), so every edge contributed zero. At a corner with a clamped or simply supported edge \(\eta(c) = 0\) (along the whole such edge \(\eta = 0\)), at a corner of two free edges \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).
  3. Conversely: let \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = 0\) for every admissible \(\eta\). Let \(\varrho = D\,\nabla^4 w - q\), a continuous function on \(R\). For numbers \(0 < \alpha_1 < \beta_1 < a\) and \(0 < \alpha_2 < \beta_2 < b\) let \(\eta = \psi_{\alpha_1\beta_1}(x)\,\psi_{\alpha_2\beta_2}(y)\), a bump on an interior rectangle. By lemma 4.123, points 1 and 2, it has continuous derivatives up to third order and is zero with all derivatives on the edge of \(R\), so it is admissible whatever the kinds of the edges, and the boundary terms in (18.28) are zero. Hence \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} \varrho\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\). Let \(\tilde\varrho(x, y, s) = \varrho(x, y)\), continuous on \((0, a) \times (0, b) \times (0, 1)\), and \(\varphi = \psi_{\alpha_1\beta_1}(x)\,\psi_{\alpha_2\beta_2}(y)\,\psi_{\alpha_3\beta_3}(s)\). For every box \(K\) in this set \(\int_{\mathrm{K}} \tilde\varrho\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}V = \int \psi_{\alpha_3\beta_3}\,\mathrm{d}s \cdot \int_{\mathrm{R}} \varrho\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) (theorem 4.9), so by lemma 4.123, point 3, \(\varrho = 0\) in the interior of \(R\), and by continuity on all of \(R\). This is (18.27).
  4. Let the edge \(x = a\) be free, and \(\eta = g_1(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\) with \(0 < \alpha < \beta < b\). This variation lifts the piece of the edge \(x = a\) between \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) without tilting it, since \(g_1(a) = 1\) and \(g_1'(a) = 0\). It has continuous derivatives up to second order. On the edge \(x = 0\) we have \(\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), on the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) it is zero with all derivatives, and on the edge \(x = a\) the free edge prescribes nothing. So it is admissible. The first term in (18.28) is zero by step 3, there are no point terms (\(\eta\) is zero at the corners), on the edge \(x = a\) we have \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) and \(\eta = \psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\), the other edges contribute zero. What remains is \(0 = \int_0^b V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\). If the continuous \(V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, \cdot)\) were positive at some point, it would be positive on an interval \([\alpha, \beta]\) around it, and the integral with a positive bump would be positive. The same for a negative value. Therefore \(V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y) = 0\) for every \(y \in (0, b)\). Lifting a piece of the edge does work only with the force that holds the edge vertically, and so this variation singles out exactly \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\).
  5. Let the edge \(x = a\) be free or simply supported and \(\eta = g_2(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\). This variation tilts the same piece of the edge without lifting it, and the tilt does work only with the moment \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\). It is admissible (on the edge \(x = a\) we have \(\eta = 0\), which the simply supported edge requires and the free one requires nothing), and \(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}(a, y) = \psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\). The same conclusion as in step 4 gives \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y) = 0\). The edges \(x = 0\), \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) are treated in the same way, with the reflection \(x \to a - x\) or an exchange of the axes, since lemma 18.20 and (18.20) stay the same under it.
  6. Let the corner \(c = (a, b)\) be the meeting of two free edges. By steps 4 and 5 \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) are zero on both edges, and in (18.28) there remains \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c)\). The function \(\eta = g_1(x)\,g_1(y)\) is admissible (the edges \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) have \(\eta = \partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\), the edges \(x = a\) and \(y = b\) are free) and lifts the corner \(c\) by \(1\), \(\eta(c) = 1\), so \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\).

Source: EP (7.2.96)-(7.2.108) (p. 432-435) for the same general form with Hamilton's principle, EP p. 146-147 for the natural conditions of the beam, VK (PDF p. 33-35) for the effective force from the equilibrium of forces. The choice of the bumps and the proof by steps are not from the source.

What does the theorem say? Equation (18.27) is the same as in theorem 18.12, but we obtained it from the energy and not from the equilibrium of a piece. Both ways lead to the same equation, as for the beam (theorem 13.37 and lemma 13.38). New are the boundary conditions and the form of \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\). They arose in step 4 of the proof of lemma 18.20: the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) appeared in the energy with the derivative \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) along the edge, and integration by parts along the edge turned it into the derivative \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) beside \(\eta\), which joined the transverse force. This is the computation for the differences of the forces at the joints of the pieces of the edge of figure 18.30. Therefore \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) contains twice \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) (one part from \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) and one from \(\eta_{\mathrm{xy}}\)), and therefore we wrote \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and not \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\). At the edge only two functions of the edge, \(\eta\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\), appeared in the first variation, hence two conditions and not three.

Loaded edge: if a given force \(\bar V\) and moment \(\bar M\) per unit length act on the edge \(x = a\), the potential of the load has the additional terms \(-\int \bar V\,w\,\mathrm{d}s - \int \bar M\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\,\mathrm{d}s\), and the conditions of the theorem become \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar V\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar M\), as for the beam (theorem 5.70). If a given force \(P_{\mathrm{c}}\) in the direction of \(w\) acts at a corner \(c\) of two free edges, the potential of the load has the additional term \(-P_{\mathrm{c}}\,w(c)\). Then (18.28) contains \((2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) - P_{\mathrm{c}})\,\eta(c)\), and step 6 of the proof gives the condition \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = P_{\mathrm{c}}\) instead of \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\). We do not need loaded edges here, but we use forces at the corners for the free plate (section 18.3.6).

18.3.4 Corner forces

The term \(\sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c)\) in equation (18.26) is the work of a point force \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\) on the virtual displacement \(\eta(c)\). At a free corner the theorem requires it to be zero. What does it mean at a corner of a supported plate, where \(w(c) = 0\) and the theorem is silent about it? Take a simply supported square \(a = b = 500\) mm loaded with \(q = 10\) kPa, that is with the force \(q\,a^2 = 2500\) N, which the section on Navier's solution solves with a double sine series (figure 18.34). Here we use the solution only for the numbers, the proof that the series gives it is there: \(w = \sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}\ \text{odd}} \dfrac{16\,q\,a^4}{\pi^6 D\,m\,n\,(m^2 + n^2)^2}\,\sin\dfrac{m\pi x}{a}\,\sin\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\). The twisting moment at the corner \((a, a)\) is \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}(a, a) = 0{,}03248\,q\,a^2 = 81{,}21\) N m/m, so \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0{,}06496\,q\,a^2 = 162{,}4\) N, although \(w = 0\) along the whole edge. What is this force and why did two edges contribute \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) each?

We already saw the answer on figure 18.30: at the ends of the edge one whole force \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) remains, and at a corner two edges meet, each with its force.

From history

How three conditions shrink to two. For an edge on which given forces act, Poisson required three conditions: the shear force, the twisting moment and the bending moment, computed from the molecular forces, must be in equilibrium with the corresponding quantities of the external forces on the edge. For a supported and a clamped edge his conditions are the same as today's (TIMO, PDF p. 120). The reduction of the number of conditions from three to two was made later by Kirchhoff, and the physical explanation was given by Lord Kelvin (TIMO, PDF p. 121). Boussinesq treated the Poisson-Kirchhoff conditions with a local disturbance caused by replacing the forces on the edge by statically equivalent ones, and reached the same conclusions as Kelvin (TIMO, PDF p. 271). This is the replacement of figure 18.30. The reason that there are only two conditions is our reading: a fourth-order equation accepts two conditions, and the twisting moment is included in the force after the replacement. The page of Kelvin's own work (TIMO, p. 266) we have not read and do not cite.

Let us return to the corner force \(F_{\mathrm{c}}\). Is it really the force with which the support holds the corner? Virtual work tells. The supports give forces that the plate would not carry at a displacement \(\eta\), and the principle of virtual displacements (section 4.5.4) for a displacement that violates the supports equates the work of the load and of the reactions with the internal work \(B(w, \eta)\). Section 4.5.4 proves it for a body with surface forces. It does not know the line and point reactions of the plate model, so we define them by this equation.

Corollary 18.22 Reactions of a simply supported plate (reakcije prostoležeče plošče)

Let a rectangular plate be simply supported along all four edges and \(w\) its solution (theorem 18.21). Then the support holds the edge with the force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) per unit length and the corner \(c\) with the force \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\), both in the direction of \(w\), and the force equilibrium holds

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} F_{\mathrm{c}} = 0 . \tag{18.29}\]
Proof

We must show that the work of the reactions matches the terms of Green's identity, then the special case \(\eta = 1\).

  1. Let \(\eta\) be any function with continuous derivatives up to second order that does not respect the supports, for example the lift of a piece of the edge or the lift of the whole plate. Let the reactions of the edges and corners be \(r\) per unit length of the edge and \(R_{\mathrm{c}}\) at the corners. The principle of virtual displacements, carried over to the plate model, is the definition of the reactions: \(B(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} r\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} R_{\mathrm{c}}\,\eta(c)\).
  2. By lemma 18.20, the plate equation and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on simply supported edges \(B(w, \eta) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} F_{\mathrm{c}}\,\eta(c)\).
  3. Steps 1 and 2 give \(\int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} (r - V_{\mathrm{n}})\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} (R_{\mathrm{c}} - F_{\mathrm{c}})\,\eta(c) = 0\) for every \(\eta\). With \(\eta = g_1(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\), which lifts only a piece of the edge, and similar functions for the other edges (as in step 4 of theorem 18.21) it follows with the bump that singles out the sign, if \(r\) is continuous, that \(r = V_{\mathrm{n}}\) on every edge, then with \(\eta = g_1(x)\,g_1(y)\), which lifts the corner, and similar ones for the other corners \(R_{\mathrm{c}} = F_{\mathrm{c}}\).
  4. For \(\eta = 1\) the plate only rises and bends nowhere, so all derivatives are zero and \(B(w, 1) = 0\). Every force does on the displacement \(1\) a work equal to the force itself, and step 1 gives (18.29) with \(r = V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{c}} = F_{\mathrm{c}}\).

Source: VK (PDF p. 35-36) for the corner forces \(S = 2M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) and their directions, EP (7.2.106) (p. 434). The proof is not from the source.

A test on the example (figure 18.34). The load of the plate is \(q\,a^2 = 2500\) N. The effective shear force \(V_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge \(x = a\) has \(-0{,}4204\,q\,a\) in the middle and \(-0{,}3548\,q\,a\) at \(y = a/4\), integrated along the edge \(-787{,}3\) N. The sum over the four edges is \(-3149{,}1\) N, and the four corner forces are \(+649{,}6\) N, and \(2500 - 3149{,}1 + 649{,}6 = 0\) within \(0{,}02\,\%\) (the series is taken to \(4000\) terms, num-03.out, 3d). Without the corner forces \(650\) N would be missing, a quarter of the load. The edges act on the plate against the load with \(3149\) N, the corners in the direction of the load with \(650\) N. For a load downward the support must therefore hold the corners down, otherwise they would lift off the support. If the support cannot hold the plate at the corner, the corner lifts off and the solution is different.

18.3.5 Poisson's three conditions: a near miss

What if the twisting moment remained an independent condition, as Poisson required? At a free edge we would have \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), \(Q_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\). Let us test on the twist of section 18.3.1, \(w = c\,xy\) on a free plate. For it \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), but the twisting moment is \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c = 50{,}26\) N m/m at \(c = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\). The first two conditions hold and the third does not, so the third condition does not follow from the first two. Three independent conditions are too many for a fourth-order equation. A deflection that satisfies all three has \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) along the edge, so the derivative of \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) along the edge is zero too. Since \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) differs from \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) only by this derivative (at the edge \(x = a\), \(V_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\), section 18.3.2), \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), and by continuity \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\) at the corners. By the remark on corner forces below theorem 18.21 such a deflection carries no force at a corner. For a plate with four forces \(F = 100{,}5\) N at the corners, which the twist \(w = c\,xy\) needs (section 18.3.6), Poisson's conditions therefore admit no solution. Kirchhoff merged \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) into \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), which is a statically equivalent replacement (definition 11.48), and two conditions remained at each edge.

How different are the forces \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\)? For the simply supported square \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge \(x = a\) equals \(-0{,}3376\,q\,a\) in the middle and \(-0{,}2813\,q\,a\) at \(y = a/4\), so \(20\,\%\) and \(21\,\%\) less in absolute value than \(V_{\mathrm{x}}\) (\(-0{,}4204\) and \(-0{,}3548\)). The integral of \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge is \(-624{,}9\) N and the sum over the four edges \(-2499{,}6\) N, which is, up to the error of the truncated series, the whole load \(2500\) N without the corner forces, as equation (18.12) for the whole \(R\) requires. The two ways of supporting, \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) without corner forces and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) with them, thus have the same resultant and are statically equivalent (definition 11.48). They differ in the distribution along the edge: \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) puts \(162{,}4\) N more on the edge, which the corner forces balance. Boussinesq showed, according to TIMO (PDF p. 271), that the difference concerns only the region near the edge. We do not give a proof for the plate here (lemma 11.49 proves it for a strip in plane stress, not for a plate).

18.3.6 Uniqueness of the solution and the free plate

We have the equation and the conditions. Do they determine \(w\) uniquely? For a body we answered this with the energy (lemma 4.134), and we do the same for the plate. First let us see which supports prevent a rigid motion.

We start with a single simply supported edge \(x = 0\). The plane \(\alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) in the coordinates \(x\) and \(y\) must be zero along it, so \(\alpha + \gamma\,y = 0\) for every \(y\), which gives \(\alpha = \gamma = 0\). What remains is \(w = \beta\,x\): the plate rotates about the edge like a lid about its hinge, and a simple support allows this. If the edge \(x = 0\) is clamped, the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = \beta\) must be zero too, and the rotation is gone. In the same way it is stopped by a second simply supported edge \(x = a\), which the rotation would lift by \(\beta\,a\), so \(\beta = 0\). Also the adjacent simply supported edge \(y = 0\) stops it, since the rotation would lift each of its points \(x > 0\) by \(\beta\,x\). For these three choices \(0\) is the only plane that respects the supports, for a single simply supported edge it is not.

Theorem 18.23 Uniqueness of the plate solution (enoličnost rešitve plošče)

Let a rectangular plate \(R\) be as in section 18.3.1 with \(-1 < \nu < 1\) and edges of the clamped, simply supported or free kind, and let \(0\) be the only function \(\alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) that satisfies the essential conditions of the edges (for example one clamped edge, or two simply supported edges that are not parallel, or two opposite simply supported edges). Then two solutions \(w_1\), \(w_2\) of equation (18.27) with the same load \(q\), continuous derivatives up to fourth order on an open set containing \(R\) and the same conditions give the same deflection, \(w_1 = w_2\).

Proof

We must show that the difference \(f = w_1 - w_2\) has zero energy and that this means \(f = 0\).

  1. The equation and all conditions are linear, so \(f\) satisfies \(D\,\nabla^4 f = 0\) and the homogeneous conditions: \(f = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} f = 0\) on a clamped edge, \(f = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = 0\) on a simply supported one, \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = V_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = 0\) on a free one, and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(f) = 0\) at the corners of two free edges.
  2. Lemma 18.20 with \(w = \eta = f\) gives \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{x}}\,f_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,f_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,f_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\nabla^4 f\;f\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}}(M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} f + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,f)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,f(c)\). The left side is \(2\,U(f)\) by equation (18.22).
  3. The right side is zero. The first term is zero by step 1. On every edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} f\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\,f\) are zero by the kind of the edge (on a clamped one \(f = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} f = 0\), on a simply supported one \(f = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on a free one \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\)). At a corner with a clamped or simply supported edge \(f(c) = 0\), at a corner of two free edges \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).
  4. So \(U(f) = 0\) and by lemma 18.16 \(f = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\). By the assumption this is \(0\), as for supports that do not even allow a rotation about an edge.

Source: The procedure is the same as for the body in lemma 4.134. The proof is not from the source.

What happens when the assumption is missing? Take a free plate with four free edges (figure 18.36) and the deflection \(w = c\,xy\). For it \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) everywhere, the equation \(\nabla^4 w = 0\) holds and the edges are not loaded. At the corners \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c \ne 0\), so by theorem 18.21, with corner forces by the remark below it, this deflection exists only if forces \(F = 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) act at the corners: upward at \((0, 0)\) and \((a, a)\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} = +1\), and downward at \((a, 0)\) and \((0, a)\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} = -1\). The forces \(F\) at opposite corners act in the same direction and the sum of forces and moments is zero, the plate is in equilibrium. Every deflection \(w + \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) is a solution with the same forces, because a plane changes no moment or higher derivative. So the deflection is determined only up to a rigid motion. The lower edge is for \(c\,xy\) a straight line, which the deflection along the edge seen from the side shows.

The numbers: for \(a = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm and the corner \((a, a)\) 1 mm above the other three (\(c = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\)) we have \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 50{,}26\) N m/m and \(F = 100{,}5\) N. The work of the forces on the displacements, half of the product (Clapeyron) \(\tfrac12 \cdot 100{,}5\ \text{N} \cdot 1\ \text{mm} = 50{,}26\) mJ, equals the energy \(D\,(1 - \nu)\,c^2 a^2 = 50{,}26\) mJ from equation (18.21), as it must. Here we see why the energy has the term with \((1 - \nu)\): Germain's energy \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) would be zero for this field, although the forces do work and the plate is twisted (lemma 18.18). The uniqueness theorem does not hold, because a free plate has no essential condition, and \(f = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) are three independent arbitrary numbers. The solution is determined only up to a rigid motion, and therefore in finite elements of free plates we shall find three zero eigenvalues of the stiffness matrix.

18.3.7 What we got

The energy of a plate is non-negative (lemma 18.16) and from its first variation (theorem 18.21) the plate equation and two conditions at each edge followed, which the kind of support determines: clamped \(w = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\), simply supported \(w = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), free \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) (definition 18.19). At a free edge the transverse force and the twisting moment merge into \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), and at the corners point forces \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) arise (corollary 18.22), which the simply supported square confirms with the number \(162{,}4\) N. The solution is unique if the supports prevent rigid motion (theorem 18.23), and the free plate is an example where they do not. The section on Navier's solution uses the simply supported edge for a rectangle, and the section on energy methods uses the energy for Ritz's method, in which the trial fields must satisfy only the essential conditions.
