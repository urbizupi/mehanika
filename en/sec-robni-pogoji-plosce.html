Chapter 18 Plates

18.3 Energy, boundary conditions and uniqueness of a plate

We obtained the plate equation \(D\,\nabla^4 w = q\) (theorem 18.12) from the equilibrium of a finite piece of the middle plane. The solution of a fourth-order equation is determined only when we say what happens at the edge of the plate, and the equilibrium of a piece in the interior does not say that. The beam was the same: the fourth-order equation had four free constants, the conditions at the ends fixed them, and theorem 5.69 showed that the first variation of the energy gives the equation and the conditions at once (section 5.11.4). Chapter 5 promised for plates that the divergence theorem replaces integration by parts. We ask three questions. Which energy does a plate have, and is it always non-negative? Which equation and which edge conditions follow from the stationarity of the energy? And do these conditions determine the deflection uniquely?

The answer will have the form the beam already knows, with one novelty. The edge of a plate is a curve and not a point, so there is also a rotation in the direction of the edge and with it a twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), which the beam does not have. We shall therefore have to find that at a free edge the transverse force and the twisting moment merge into one force, and that point forces appear at the corners.

18.3.1 Model and energy

Take a plate (definition 18.1) with a rectangular plan \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) of the middle plane, of constant thickness \(h\), of a homogeneous isotropic material with modulus of elasticity \(E\) and Poisson's ratio \(\nu\) (definitions 3.1 and 3.2), where \(-1 < \nu < 1\). The deflection of the middle plane is \(w(x, y)\) and the load \(q(x, y)\) per unit area points in the direction of \(w\). The idealizations of the Kirchhoff plate hold (definition 18.2): the plate is thin, the deflection is small, the normals stay straight and perpendicular to the middle plane, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\) is neglected and every layer is in plane stress. The rectangle has four edges and four corners. For now there is no load on the edges, so the edges are either fixed or free (loaded edges are described by the remark after theorem 18.21). As an example we take the steel plate of the running example of the chapter: \(a = b = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm, \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\), \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m (definition 18.6) and \(q = 10\) kPa.

Let us write down what we know from section 18.1, in the same sign convention as for the beam (\(M = EI\,v''\), equation (5.24)). By theorem 18.7 the moments per unit length are

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{y}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{yy}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}} , \tag{18.20}\]

and \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) are by definition 18.5 the integrals \(-\int z\,\sigma\,\mathrm{d}z\) through the thickness, with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\). Below we also need the transverse forces \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = -(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}})\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = -(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}})\) (definition 18.9, the relation to the moments is the moment balance in the proof of theorem 18.12, steps 2 and 3), where an index after the comma means a partial derivative. The moments and transverse forces of a plate are per unit length of the edge (\(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) in N m/m, \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) in N/m), so they differ from \(M\) and \(V\) of a beam in N m and N.

The energy of a beam was the integral of the energy density over the body (definition 5.75). For a plate we take it from the same definition for the body \(V = R \times [-h/2, h/2]\) that the plate occupies (\(z\) is the coordinate perpendicular to the middle plane), and we write it with the deflection. That the integral below is indeed this energy is shown by lemma 18.15.

Definition 18.14 Strain energy of a plate (deformacijska energija plošče)

Let the plate be as above and \(w\) its deflection. The strain energy of the plate is the number

\[U(w) = \frac12 \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\big[ (\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2\,(1 - \nu)\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2) \big]\,\mathrm{d}A , \tag{18.21}\]

where \(\nabla^2 w = w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(w\) has continuous derivatives up to second order on \(R\). It is a functional of the field \(w\): the input is a function \(w\) on \(R\) and the output is a number in joules.

Source: EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431, written there with the moments). The form with \((1 - \nu)\) follows from it (lemma 18.15).

Why the energy has this form is shown by the next lemma. It also shows that it equals half the integral of the products of moments and curvatures, as for the beam \(\tfrac12\int M\,v''\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Lemma 18.15 Strain energy of a plate in terms of the moments (energija plošče iz momentov)

Under the idealizations of the Kirchhoff plate the strain energy of the body \(V = R \times [-h/2, h/2]\) with the displacement field \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{y}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{z}} = w\) (theorem 18.3) is

\[U = \frac12 \int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}} \big)\,\mathrm{d}A , \tag{18.22}\]

which equals (18.21).

Proof

We must show that the integral of the energy density through the thickness gives the moments, and that inserting the relations (18.20) gives (18.21).

  1. The strains from the displacement field (equation (1.63)) are \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\), the others are zero.
  2. The energy density in plane stress is \(\tfrac12\,(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}})\), since \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yz}}\) are by the idealizations zero or multiplied by a zero strain (definition 18.2, theorem 3.11).
  3. By the theorem on the order of integration (theorem 4.9) \(U = \int_{\mathrm{R}} \int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} (\dots)\,\mathrm{d}z\,\mathrm{d}A\). The strains of step 1 are linear in \(z\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) do not depend on \(z\), so we take them out (lemma 3.39). What remains are the integrals \(\int (-z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}})\,\mathrm{d}z = M_{\mathrm{x}}\) and the same for \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) (definition 18.5). This is (18.22).
  4. Insert (18.20): \(M_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2 M_{\mathrm{xy}} w_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,[\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\,]\). Since \((\nabla^2 w)^2 = w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + 2 w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2\), the same expression equals \((\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2(1 - \nu)(w_{\mathrm{xx}} w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2)\). SymPy checked the difference of the two forms as an equality of polynomials in the second derivatives.

Source: EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431) for equation (18.22). The proof by steps is not from the source.

Is the energy always non-negative, and when is it zero? The energy of a beam \(\tfrac12\int EI\,v''^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) is a square and therefore non-negative. For a plate the square bracket is a difference of two expressions, and a difference is not necessarily non-negative. Let us test on the field \(w = c\,xy\) (a twist), for which \(\nabla^2 w = 0\). The first term is then zero, the second is \(-2(1 - \nu)(0 - c^2) = 2(1 - \nu)\,c^2\), hence positive. For a square \(a = 500\) mm, \(\nu = 0{,}3\) and \(c = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\) (the corner \((a, a)\) is 1 mm above the other three) the energy is \(D\,(1 - \nu)\,c^2 a^2 = 50{,}26\) mJ. The energy \(\tfrac12\int D\,(\nabla^2 w)^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) would be zero for this field, although the plate is twisted. What happens with this energy and why it matters here is told by lemma 18.18.

Lemma 18.16 Non-negativity of the plate energy (nenegativnost energije plošče)

Let \(D > 0\) and \(-1 < \nu < 1\). The energy density in (18.21) satisfies the inequality

\[\tfrac12\,D\,\big[ (\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2(1 - \nu)(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2) \big] \ \ge\ \tfrac12\,D\,(1 - |\nu|)\,\big( w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \big) \ \ge\ 0\]

at every point, hence \(U(w) \ge 0\). If \(U(w) = 0\) and \(w\) is twice continuously differentiable on \(R\), then \(w = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) on \(R\) with constants \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), \(\gamma\) (rigid motion of the plate).

Proof

We must show the estimate of the density, then derive from zero energy that all second derivatives are zero, and from that the form of the field.

  1. By lemma 18.15, step 4, the expression in the square bracket equals \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\).
  2. For any numbers \(s\), \(t\) we have \(2\,|s\,t| \le s^2 + t^2\), hence \(2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}} \ge -|\nu|\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2)\). Therefore \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + 2\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}} \ge (1 - |\nu|)(w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yy}}^2)\).
  3. Since \(1 - \nu \ge 1 - |\nu| > 0\), we have \(2(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \ge 2\,(1 - |\nu|)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\). Steps 2 and 3 together give the estimate of the density.
  4. The density is continuous and non-negative. If it were positive at some point, it would be positive on a small rectangle around it and the integral would be positive, which would mean \(U > 0\). For \(U = 0\) therefore \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} = w_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) at every point.
  5. By step 4 the function \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) has zero partial derivatives \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\). By the fundamental theorem of calculus (theorem 3.40) along segments parallel to the axes, \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) is constant on the rectangle, \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = \beta\). Likewise \(w_{\mathrm{y}} = \gamma\). The function \(w - \beta\,x - \gamma\,y\) has zero derivatives and is therefore constant, \(\alpha\).

Source: The estimate is our own reasoning. For the energy EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431), positivity for the beam and the body is lemma 6.7. The proof is not from the source.

The lemma has two consequences. The energy is non-negative, so a minimum makes sense (theorem 4.91). Zero energy, however, does not mean rest: the plane \(\alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) is a rigid motion and does not change the curvature. When zero energy does mean rest is the question of uniqueness in section 18.3.6.

The integrals by parts that we need below are done in the plane by the divergence theorem. For a rectangle we already used it in section 18.2. Here we write it in components, as we need it, and derive it from the theorem for bodies (theorem 4.14), applied to a body of unit thickness.

Lemma 18.17 Divergence theorem in the plane for a rectangle (izrek o divergenci v ravnini za pravokotnik)

Let \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) and \(f\) a continuously differentiable function on an open set containing \(R\). Then

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^b f(a, y)\,\mathrm{d}y - \int_0^b f(0, y)\,\mathrm{d}y , \qquad \int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^a f(x, b)\,\mathrm{d}x - \int_0^a f(x, 0)\,\mathrm{d}x . \tag{18.23}\]

With the outward normal \((n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}})\) of the edges this reads \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} f\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} f\,n_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s\), where the integral over \(\partial R\) means the sum of the integrals over the four sides, with the variable \(s\), the arc length along the side.

Proof

We must show the first equation, the second is the same with the axes exchanged.

  1. The body \(K = R \times [0, 1]\) is a box. The function \(f(x, y)\), which does not depend on \(z\), is continuously differentiable on an open set containing \(K\), and by the divergence theorem (theorem 4.14, point 1) \(\int_{\mathrm{K}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}V = \int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) over the surface \(S\) of the box.
  2. The faces \(z = 0\), \(z = 1\) and \(y = 0\), \(y = b\) have \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\). The faces \(x = a\) and \(x = 0\) have \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\) and \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = -1\) and are rectangles \([0, b] \times [0, 1]\), on which \(f\) is independent of \(z\), so by theorem 4.9 the integral over the first equals \(\int_0^b f(a, y)\,\mathrm{d}y\) and over the second \(\int_0^b f(0, y)\,\mathrm{d}y\).
  3. Since \(f_{\mathrm{x}}\) is independent of \(z\) and \(\int_0^1 \mathrm{d}z = 1\), we have \(\int_{\mathrm{K}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}V = \int_{\mathrm{R}} f_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) by theorem 4.9. Steps 2 and 3 give the first equation.

Source: The proof is not from the source. For a general edge the same statement is a consequence of 4.40 for a body cut into pieces.

For a rectangle the lemma is all we need. For plates with oblique or curved edges we would take corollary 4.40 instead of the lemma and describe the edge by the normal \(\vek n\) and the arc length \(s\), as EP (7.2.101)-(7.2.108) (p. 433-435) does. The rectangle is chosen as the simplest example, not as a limit of the method. For the same reason only, we keep to edges parallel to the axes in what follows.

With the lemma let us estimate what the term with \((1 - \nu)\) adds. The energy that Germain used for a plate with the principal curvatures (section 18.2) is \(U_{\mathrm{G}}(w) = \tfrac12\int D\,(\nabla^2 w)^2\,\mathrm{d}A\). It gives the same plate equation, and the difference lies in the boundary conditions.

Lemma 18.18 The determinant term and the edge (člen determinante in rob)

Let \(w\) have continuous derivatives up to third order on an open set containing \(R\). Then

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 \big)\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_0^b \big[\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\big]_{\mathrm{x}=0}^{x=a}\,\mathrm{d}y - \int_0^a \big[\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\big]_{\mathrm{y}=0}^{y=b}\,\mathrm{d}x , \tag{18.24}\]

so \(U - U_{\mathrm{G}} = -D\,(1 - \nu)\) times this boundary expression. It is zero if the edge of \(R\) is clamped (\(w_{\mathrm{x}} = w_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) on the whole edge) or simply supported (\(w = 0\) on the whole edge), but in general it is not zero on a free edge.

Proof

We must show an identity between derivatives and integrate it with lemma 18.17.

  1. By the product rule \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}) = w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{y}}(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}) = w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\). The difference is \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\) (the order of the derivatives may be exchanged because the third derivatives are continuous). SymPy confirms this.
  2. Lemma 18.17 with \(f = w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(f = w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) gives (18.24).
  3. On a clamped edge \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = w_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) along the whole edge, so the boundary terms are zero. On a simply supported edge \(w = 0\) along the edge, so the derivatives along the edge are zero: on an edge \(x = \text{const}\) we have \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\), on an edge \(y = \text{const}\) we have \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\). The terms \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) therefore have a zero factor \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) on the first edges and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) on the second. On a free edge nothing requires this. For the twist \(w = c\,xy\) on a square the boundary expression is \(-c^2 a^2 \ne 0\), which agrees with the integral \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} (-c^2)\,\mathrm{d}A\).

Source: The proof is not from the source. EP (7.2.90) (p. 430-431) uses this term in the energy, TIMO (PDF p. 128-129) describes Germain's energy.

The lemma tells why Germain's energy is right for clamped and simply supported edges and wrong for free ones. Both energies give the same fourth-order equation, because they differ only by a boundary expression. At a free edge, however, the boundary expression changes the natural conditions. The energy \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) gives by the same computation as in theorem 18.21, without the mixed term, the conditions \(\nabla^2 w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}(\nabla^2 w) = 0\) at a free edge. The twist \(w = c\,xy\) satisfies them, so by \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) a free plate would stay twisted without forces. This is not true: it needs forces \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) at the corners (section 18.3.4).

18.3.2 Edges: clamped, simply supported, free

Before we derive the conditions from the energy, let us say what kinds of edges we know and what is prescribed on each. We look at the edge of the plate from the side (figure 18.8). The middle plane is drawn as a thick line that ends at the edge \(x = a\), the undeformed position as a dashed line. The first edge is clamped: a solid wall holds it, so the plate must reach the wall with a horizontal tangent, on the dashed line. The deflection and the rotation are zero there, \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\), where \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\) is the derivative in the direction of the outward normal \(n\) of the edge lying in the middle plane. The second edge is simply supported, like a beam on a pin support: the deflection is zero and the tangent is free, which the inclined line shows. The rotation is free because a pin support gives no moment, so the moment at the edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) must be zero. The third edge is free. Nothing holds it, so it moves and turns as the plate demands, and as at the free end of a beam (section 5.11.4) both the moment and the transverse force at it must be zero.

At the edge \(x = a\) the moment at the edge is \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = M_{\mathrm{x}}\), the transverse force is \(Q_{\mathrm{n}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), and the edge also carries the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\). For a beam there were one force and one moment at the end, here there are three quantities, and a fourth-order equation accepts only two conditions at each edge (as the beam, section 5.11.4). How three quantities shrink to two is shown by the derivation in theorem 18.21: the quantity that replaces \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) at a free edge is the transverse force increased by the effect of the twisting moment, and we call it the effective shear force (nadomestna prečna sila). We write its form down in advance.

Definition 18.19 Boundary conditions of a plate (robni pogoji plošče)

Let a rectangular plate (section 18.3.1) have an edge with outward normal \(\vek n = (n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}}) \in \{\pm\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}, \pm\vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\}\), and let \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = n_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{x}} + n_{\mathrm{y}}\,w_{\mathrm{y}}\). On this edge we define the moment \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) and the effective shear force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), both per unit length of the edge:

\[M_{\mathrm{n}} = n_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,M_{\mathrm{x}} + n_{\mathrm{y}}^2\,M_{\mathrm{y}} , \qquad V_{\mathrm{n}} = -\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\big(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\big) - n_{\mathrm{y}}\,\big(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\big) . \tag{18.25}\]

With (18.20) on the edge \(x = a\) (\(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\)) we have \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = D\,(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,w_{\mathrm{yy}})\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = -D\,\big[\,w_{\mathrm{xxx}} + (2 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xyy}}\,\big]\), on the edge \(y = b\) the same with \(x \leftrightarrow y\). An edge is

  • clamped (vpet), if \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\) on it,
  • simply supported (prostoležeč), if \(w = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on it,
  • free (prost), if \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on it.

The conditions \(w = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\) are conditions on the field \(w\) (essential, as for the beam), the conditions \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) are conditions on its derivatives (natural).

Source: EP (7.2.99)-(7.2.108) (p. 433-435), VK (PDF p. 31-36), ONATE2 §5.2.7 (PDF p. 274-276). The sign of \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) is derived in the proof of theorem 18.21, EP has the sign of \(M_{\mathrm{ns}}\) by another convention.

At the edge \(x = a\) we have \(-(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}) = Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), so \(V_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\): the effective force is the transverse force reduced by the derivative of the twisting moment along the edge. Why exactly so is told by the theorem.

18.3.3 First variation of a plate

For the beam we used integration by parts twice so that the variation \(\eta\) came out of the derivatives. For the plate we do the same in the plane with lemma 18.17. First we compute what integration by parts gives for the term that will appear in the first variation.

Lemma 18.20 Green's identity for a plate (Greenova enačba plošče)

Let \(R = [0, a] \times [0, b]\), \(D\) constant, \(w\) a function with continuous partial derivatives up to fourth order and \(\eta\) a function with continuous derivatives up to second order, both on an open set containing \(R\). Let \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) be the moments of the field \(w\) by (18.20) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) by (18.25). Then

\[\begin{aligned} \int_{\mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}} \big)\,\mathrm{d}A &= \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\nabla^4 w\;\eta\,\mathrm{d}A \\ &\quad + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} \big( M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta \big)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c) , \end{aligned} \tag{18.26}\]

where \(\nabla^4 w = w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + 2\,w_{\mathrm{xxyy}} + w_{\mathrm{yyyy}}\). The sum runs over the four corners \(c\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}}\) is the component of the normal of the edge parallel to the axis \(y\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\) the component of the normal of the edge parallel to the axis \(x\), and the two edges meet at \(c\).

Proof

We must show that each of the three terms of the left side gives its part of the right side. We apply lemma 18.17 with a function that is a product of two fields, and the product rule.

  1. For \(f = M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\) we have \(f_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\). By the lemma \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). Once more with \(f = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). Together \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,(M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  2. With \(y\) instead of \(x\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,(M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}} - M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{yy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  3. The mixed term: \(\eta_{\mathrm{xy}} = \eta_{\mathrm{yx}}\), because the second derivatives are continuous. First by \(x\) with \(f = M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\), then by \(y\) with \(f = M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\): \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). The same term in the opposite order: \(\int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_{\mathrm{R}} M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\). Twice the mixed term is the sum of both.
  4. The terms \(\int n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) on the edges \(x = \text{const}\) and \(\int n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) on the edges \(y = \text{const}\) contain the derivative of \(\eta\) along the edge. By integration by parts (corollary 3.41) on an edge running from \(s_0\) to \(s_1\) we have \(\int M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{s}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}s = \big[M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\big]_{\mathrm{s}_0}^{s_1} - \int M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{s}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s\). On the edge \(x = a\) this gives \(\int_0^b M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}y = \big[M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\big]_0^b - \int_0^b M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}y\), and the same on the other edges.
  5. On an edge \(x = \text{const}\) we collect the terms with \(\eta\): \(-n_{\mathrm{x}}\,(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}})\), on an edge \(y = \text{const}\) \(-n_{\mathrm{y}}\,(M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}})\), which is \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) from (18.25). The terms with the derivatives \(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) give \(n_{\mathrm{x}}\,M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\) on an edge \(x = \text{const}\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{y}} = M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\) on an edge \(y = \text{const}\).
  6. The point terms from step 4: the corner \(c = (a, b)\) gets \(+M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(x = a\) (upper end, \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 1\)) and \(+M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(y = b\) (right end, \(n_{\mathrm{y}} = 1\)), together \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). The corner \((a, 0)\) gets \(-M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(x = a\) (lower end) and \(-M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) from the edge \(y = 0\) (\(n_{\mathrm{y}} = -1\), right end), together \(-2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). Similarly \((0, b)\) and \((0, 0)\) give \(-2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) and \(+2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\). In all four cases this is \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta\) with the normals of both edges at the corner.
  7. The integrals over \(R\) in steps 1 to 3 have the factor \(\eta\) with the sum \(M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{xx}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{xy}} + M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{yy}}\), which by (18.20) equals \(D\,\nabla^4 w\) (checked with SymPy). The sum of steps 1 to 7 is (18.26).

The energy of a plate is quadratic: \(U(w + \lambda\,\eta) = U(w) + \lambda\,B(w, \eta) + \lambda^2\,U(\eta)\) with \(B(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A\) (from (18.22), since \(U\) is half of the symmetric form \(B(w, w)\), as in lemma 6.7). The total potential energy (definition 4.89) is \(\Pi(w) = U(w) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\).

Admissible fields \(w\) are those with continuous derivatives up to fourth order on an open set containing \(R\) that satisfy at each edge the essential conditions of its kind (definition 18.19). An admissible variation \(\eta\) has continuous derivatives up to second order on an open set containing \(R\) and satisfies the same essential condition with zero: \(\eta = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\) on a clamped edge, \(\eta = 0\) on a simply supported edge, nothing on a free edge. The first variation is \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = B(w, \eta) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\), the derivative of the function \(\lambda \mapsto \Pi(w + \lambda\,\eta)\) at \(\lambda = 0\).

For the step with the bumps we need functions that have a continuous second derivative. Let \(\psi_{\alpha\beta}(t) = (t - \alpha)^4\,(\beta - t)^4\) for \(\alpha \le t \le \beta\) and zero elsewhere (lemma 4.122, point 1), \(g_1(x) = (x/a)^2\,(3 - 2x/a)\) and \(g_2(x) = x^2\,(x - a)/a^2\). The function \(g_1\) has \(g_1(0) = g_1'(0) = 0\), \(g_1(a) = 1\), \(g_1'(a) = 0\), and the function \(g_2\) has \(g_2(0) = g_2'(0) = 0\), \(g_2(a) = 0\), \(g_2'(a) = 1\).

Theorem 18.21 First variation of a plate (prva variacija plošče)

Let the plate be as in section 18.3.1 with a load \(q\) continuous on \(R\) and with edges of the clamped, simply supported or free kind, and let \(w\) be an admissible function. Then \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = 0\) for every admissible variation \(\eta\) exactly when

\[D\,\nabla^4 w = q \quad \text{on } R , \tag{18.27}\]

on every simply supported edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on every free edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), and at every corner where two free edges meet \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).

Proof

We must show the first variation with Green's identity, then with each kind of variation single out one condition.

  1. By the definition of the first variation and lemma 18.20, equation (18.26), for every admissible \(\eta\)
    \[\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} (D\,\nabla^4 w - q)\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c) . \tag{18.28}\]
  2. If equation (18.27) and the conditions of the theorem hold, then \(\delta\Pi = 0\). The first term is zero. On a clamped edge \(\eta = \partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\), on a simply supported one \(\eta = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on a free one \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), so every edge contributed zero. At a corner with a clamped or simply supported edge \(\eta(c) = 0\) (along the whole such edge \(\eta = 0\)), at a corner of two free edges \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).
  3. Conversely: let \(\delta\Pi(w, \eta) = 0\) for every admissible \(\eta\). Let \(\varrho = D\,\nabla^4 w - q\), a continuous function on \(R\). For numbers \(0 < \alpha_1 < \beta_1 < a\) and \(0 < \alpha_2 < \beta_2 < b\) let \(\eta = \psi_{\alpha_1\beta_1}(x)\,\psi_{\alpha_2\beta_2}(y)\). By lemma 4.122, points 1 and 2, it has continuous derivatives up to third order and is zero with all derivatives on the edge of \(R\), so it is admissible whatever the kinds of the edges, and the boundary terms in (18.28) are zero. Hence \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} \varrho\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\). Let \(\tilde\varrho(x, y, s) = \varrho(x, y)\), continuous on \((0, a) \times (0, b) \times (0, 1)\), and \(\varphi = \psi_{\alpha_1\beta_1}(x)\,\psi_{\alpha_2\beta_2}(y)\,\psi_{\alpha_3\beta_3}(s)\). For every box \(K\) in this set \(\int_{\mathrm{K}} \tilde\varrho\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}V = \int \psi_{\alpha_3\beta_3}\,\mathrm{d}s \cdot \int_{\mathrm{R}} \varrho\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) (theorem 4.9), so by lemma 4.122, point 3, \(\varrho = 0\) in the interior of \(R\), and by continuity on all of \(R\). This is (18.27).
  4. Let the edge \(x = a\) be free, and \(\eta = g_1(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\) with \(0 < \alpha < \beta < b\). It has continuous derivatives up to second order. On the edge \(x = 0\) we have \(\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), on the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) it is zero with all derivatives, and on the edge \(x = a\) the free edge prescribes nothing. So it is admissible. The first term in (18.28) is zero by step 3, there are no point terms (\(\eta\) is zero at the corners), on the edge \(x = a\) we have \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) and \(\eta = \psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\), the other edges contribute zero. What remains is \(0 = \int_0^b V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\). If the continuous \(V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, \cdot)\) were positive at some point, it would be positive on an interval \([\alpha, \beta]\) around it, and the integral with a positive bump would be positive. The same for a negative value. Therefore \(V_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y) = 0\) for every \(y \in (0, b)\).
  5. Let the edge \(x = a\) be free or simply supported and \(\eta = g_2(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\). It is admissible (on the edge \(x = a\) we have \(\eta = 0\), which the simply supported edge requires and the free one requires nothing), and \(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}(a, y) = \psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\). The same conclusion as in step 4 gives \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(a, y) = 0\). The edges \(x = 0\), \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) are treated in the same way, with the reflection \(x \to a - x\) or an exchange of the axes, since lemma 18.20 and (18.20) stay the same under it.
  6. Let the corner \(c = (a, b)\) be the meeting of two free edges. By steps 4 and 5 \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) are zero on both edges, and in (18.28) there remains \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c)\). The function \(\eta = g_1(x)\,g_1(y)\) is admissible (the edges \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) have \(\eta = \partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta = 0\), the edges \(x = a\) and \(y = b\) are free) and has \(\eta(c) = 1\), so \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\).

Source: EP (7.2.96)-(7.2.108) (p. 432-435) for the same general form with Hamilton's principle, EP p. 146-147 for the natural conditions of the beam, VK (PDF p. 33-35) for the effective force from the equilibrium of forces. The choice of the bumps and the proof by steps are not from the source.

What does the theorem say? Equation (18.27) is the same as in theorem 18.12, but we obtained it from the energy and not from the equilibrium of a piece. Both ways lead to the same equation, as for the beam (theorem 13.28 and lemma 13.29). New are the boundary conditions and the form of \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\). They arose in step 4 of the proof of lemma 18.20: the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) appeared in the energy with the derivative \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) along the edge, and integration by parts along the edge turned it into the derivative \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) beside \(\eta\), which joined the transverse force. Therefore \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) contains twice \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) (one part from \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\) and one from \(\eta_{\mathrm{xy}}\)), and therefore we wrote \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and not \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\). At the edge only two functions of the edge, \(\eta\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{n}}\eta\), appeared in the first variation, hence two conditions and not three.

Loaded edge: if a given force \(\bar V\) and moment \(\bar M\) per unit length act on the edge \(x = a\), the potential of the load has the additional terms \(-\int \bar V\,w\,\mathrm{d}s - \int \bar M\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} w\,\mathrm{d}s\), and the conditions of the theorem become \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar V\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = \bar M\), as for the beam (theorem 5.69). If a given force \(P_{\mathrm{c}}\) in the direction of \(w\) acts at a corner \(c\) of two free edges, the potential of the load has the additional term \(-P_{\mathrm{c}}\,w(c)\). Then (18.28) contains \((2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) - P_{\mathrm{c}})\,\eta(c)\), and step 6 of the proof gives the condition \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = P_{\mathrm{c}}\) instead of \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\). We do not need loaded edges here, but we use forces at the corners for the free plate (section 18.3.6).

18.3.4 Corner forces

The term \(\sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\,\eta(c)\) in equation (18.26) is the work of a point force \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\) on the virtual displacement \(\eta(c)\). At a free corner the theorem requires it to be zero. What does it mean at a corner of a supported plate, where \(w(c) = 0\) and the theorem is silent about it? Take a simply supported square \(a = b = 500\) mm loaded with \(q = 10\) kPa, which the section on Navier's solution solves with a double sine series. Here we use the solution only for the numbers, the proof that the series gives it is there: \(w = \sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}\ \text{odd}} \dfrac{16\,q\,a^4}{\pi^6 D\,m\,n\,(m^2 + n^2)^2}\,\sin\dfrac{m\pi x}{a}\,\sin\dfrac{n\pi y}{a}\). The twisting moment at the corner \((a, a)\) is \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}(a, a) = 0{,}03248\,q\,a^2 = 81{,}21\) N m/m, so \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0{,}06496\,q\,a^2 = 162{,}4\) N, although \(w = 0\) along the whole edge. What is this force and why did two edges contribute \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) each?

Let us see how it arises (figure 18.9). We look at the edge from the side. The twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) per unit length of the edge is the moment of pairs of horizontal shear forces in the plate, and a piece of edge of length \(\Delta s\) has the moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\Delta s\). We replace the pair of horizontal forces by a pair of vertical forces \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) at a distance \(\Delta s\) along the edge. This pair also has the moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\Delta s\), and the moment vectors of both pairs point in the direction of the normal of the edge, so the pairs are equivalent (VK, PDF p. 33-34). In the figure \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) grows along the edge, which the longer arrows on the right pieces show. At the joints of the pieces the lower force of one piece and the upper force of the neighbour add up, and their difference \(\Delta M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) remains. The difference divided by \(\Delta s\) is a force per unit length of the edge and tends to the derivative \(M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{s}}\) as \(\Delta s \to 0\). At the ends of the edge one whole force \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) remains. At a corner two edges meet and each gives its force \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\), together \(2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\). This replacement of a moment by an equivalent pair of forces, which Kirchhoff used to reduce the conditions, is statically equivalent (definition 11.44). It is allowed because it is only another way to write the same load of the edge (VK, PDF p. 34).

From history

How three conditions shrink to two. For an edge on which given forces act, Poisson required three conditions: the shear force, the twisting moment and the bending moment, computed from the molecular forces, must be in equilibrium with the corresponding quantities of the external forces on the edge. For a supported and a clamped edge his conditions are the same as today's (TIMO, PDF p. 120). The reduction of the number of conditions from three to two was made later by Kirchhoff, and the physical explanation was given by Lord Kelvin (TIMO, PDF p. 121). Boussinesq treated the Poisson-Kirchhoff conditions with a local disturbance caused by replacing the forces on the edge by statically equivalent ones, and reached the same conclusions as Kelvin (TIMO, PDF p. 271). This is the replacement of figure 18.9. The reason that there are only two conditions is our reading: a fourth-order equation accepts two conditions, and the twisting moment is included in the force after the replacement. The page of Kelvin's own work (TIMO, p. 266) we have not read and do not cite.

Let us return to the corner force \(F_{\mathrm{c}}\). Is it really the force with which the support holds the corner? Virtual work tells. The supports give forces that the plate would not carry at a displacement \(\eta\), and the principle of virtual displacements (section 4.5.4) for a displacement that violates the supports equates the work of the load and of the reactions with the internal work \(B(w, \eta)\). Section 4.5.4 proves it for a body with surface forces. It does not know the line and point reactions of the plate model, so we define them by this equation.

Corollary 18.22 Reactions of a simply supported plate (reakcije prostoležeče plošče)

Let a rectangular plate be simply supported along all four edges and \(w\) its solution (theorem 18.21). Then the support holds the edge with the force \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) per unit length and the corner \(c\) with the force \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c)\), both in the direction of \(w\), and the force equilibrium holds

\[\int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} F_{\mathrm{c}} = 0 . \tag{18.29}\]
Proof

We must show that the work of the reactions matches the terms of Green's identity, then the special case \(\eta = 1\).

  1. Let \(\eta\) be any function with continuous derivatives up to second order that does not respect the supports. Let the reactions of the edges and corners be \(r\) per unit length of the edge and \(R_{\mathrm{c}}\) at the corners. The principle of virtual displacements, carried over to the plate model, is the definition of the reactions: \(B(w, \eta) = \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} r\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} R_{\mathrm{c}}\,\eta(c)\).
  2. By lemma 18.20, the plate equation and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on simply supported edges \(B(w, \eta) - \int_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} V_{\mathrm{n}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} F_{\mathrm{c}}\,\eta(c)\).
  3. Steps 1 and 2 give \(\int_{\partial \mathrm{R}} (r - V_{\mathrm{n}})\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} (R_{\mathrm{c}} - F_{\mathrm{c}})\,\eta(c) = 0\) for every \(\eta\). With \(\eta = g_1(x)\,\psi_{\alpha\beta}(y)\) and similar functions for the other edges (as in step 4 of theorem 18.21) it follows, if \(r\) is continuous, that \(r = V_{\mathrm{n}}\) on every edge, then with \(\eta = g_1(x)\,g_1(y)\) and similar ones for the other corners \(R_{\mathrm{c}} = F_{\mathrm{c}}\).
  4. For \(\eta = 1\) all derivatives are zero and \(B(w, 1) = 0\). Step 1 gives (18.29) with \(r = V_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{c}} = F_{\mathrm{c}}\).

Source: VK (PDF p. 35-36) for the corner forces \(S = 2M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) and their directions, EP (7.2.106) (p. 434). The proof is not from the source.

A test on the example. The load of the plate is \(q\,a^2 = 2500\) N. The effective shear force \(V_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge \(x = a\) has \(-0{,}4204\,q\,a\) in the middle and \(-0{,}3548\,q\,a\) at \(y = a/4\), integrated along the edge \(-787{,}3\) N. The sum over the four edges is \(-3149{,}1\) N, and the four corner forces are \(+649{,}6\) N, and \(2500 - 3149{,}1 + 649{,}6 = 0\) within \(0{,}02\,\%\) (the series is taken to \(4000\) terms, num-03.out, 3d). Without the corner forces \(650\) N would be missing, a quarter of the load. The edges pull the plate against the load with \(3149\) N, the corners push it in the direction of the load with \(650\) N. For a load downward this means that the corners of the plate lift off the support and the support must hold them. If the support cannot hold the plate at the corner, the corner lifts off and the solution is different.

18.3.5 Poisson's three conditions: a near miss

What if the twisting moment remained an independent condition, as Poisson required? At a free edge we would have \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), \(Q_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\). Let us test on the twist of section 18.3.1, \(w = c\,xy\) on a free plate. For it \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), but the twisting moment is \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c = 50{,}26\) N m/m at \(c = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\). The first two conditions hold and the third does not, so the third condition does not follow from the first two. Three independent conditions are too many for a fourth-order equation. A deflection that satisfies all three has \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) along the edge, so the derivative of \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) along the edge is zero too. Since \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) differs from \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) only by this derivative (at the edge \(x = a\), \(V_{\mathrm{x}} = Q_{\mathrm{x}} - M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\), section 18.3.2), \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), and by continuity \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(c) = 0\) at the corners. By the remark on corner forces below theorem 18.21 such a deflection carries no force at a corner. For a plate with four forces \(F = 100{,}5\) N at the corners, which the twist \(w = c\,xy\) needs (section 18.3.6), Poisson's conditions therefore admit no solution. Kirchhoff merged \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) into \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), which is a statically equivalent replacement (definition 11.44), and two conditions remained at each edge.

How different are the forces \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\)? For the simply supported square \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge \(x = a\) equals \(-0{,}3376\,q\,a\) in the middle and \(-0{,}2813\,q\,a\) at \(y = a/4\), so \(20\,\%\) and \(21\,\%\) less in absolute value than \(V_{\mathrm{x}}\) (\(-0{,}4204\) and \(-0{,}3548\)). The integral of \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\) along the edge is \(-624{,}9\) N and the sum over the four edges \(-2499{,}6\) N, which is, up to the error of the truncated series, the whole load \(2500\) N without the corner forces, as equation (18.12) for the whole \(R\) requires. The two ways of supporting, \(Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) without corner forces and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) with them, thus have the same resultant and are statically equivalent (definition 11.44). They differ in the distribution along the edge: \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) puts \(162{,}4\) N more on the edge, which the corner forces balance. Boussinesq showed, according to TIMO (PDF p. 271), that the difference concerns only the region near the edge. We do not give a proof for the plate here (lemma 11.45 proves it for a strip in plane stress, not for a plate).

18.3.6 Uniqueness of the solution and the free plate

We have the equation and the conditions. Do they determine \(w\) uniquely? For a body we answered this with the energy (lemma 4.133), and we do the same for the plate.

Theorem 18.23 Uniqueness of the plate solution (enoličnost rešitve plošče)

Let a rectangular plate be as in section 18.3.1 with \(-1 < \nu < 1\) and edges of the clamped, simply supported or free kind, and let \(0\) be the only function \(\alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) that satisfies the essential conditions of the edges (for example one clamped edge, or two simply supported edges that are not parallel, or two opposite simply supported edges). Then two solutions \(w_1\), \(w_2\) of equation (18.27) with the same load \(q\), continuous derivatives up to fourth order on an open set containing \(R\) and the same conditions give the same deflection, \(w_1 = w_2\).

Proof

We must show that the difference \(f = w_1 - w_2\) has zero energy and that this means \(f = 0\).

  1. The equation and all conditions are linear, so \(f\) satisfies \(D\,\nabla^4 f = 0\) and the homogeneous conditions: \(f = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} f = 0\) on a clamped edge, \(f = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = 0\) on a simply supported one, \(M_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = V_{\mathrm{n}}(f) = 0\) on a free one, and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(f) = 0\) at the corners of two free edges.
  2. Lemma 18.20 with \(w = \eta = f\) gives \(\int_{\mathrm{R}} (M_{\mathrm{x}}\,f_{\mathrm{xx}} + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,f_{\mathrm{yy}} + 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,f_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{R}} D\,\nabla^4 f\;f\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\partial \mathrm{R}}(M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} f + V_{\mathrm{n}}\,f)\,\mathrm{d}s + \sum_{\mathrm{c}} 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,f(c)\). The left side is \(2\,U(f)\) by equation (18.22).
  3. The right side is zero. The first term is zero by step 1. On every edge \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{n}} f\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\,f\) are zero by the kind of the edge (on a clamped one \(f = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} f = 0\), on a simply supported one \(f = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), on a free one \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\)). At a corner with a clamped or simply supported edge \(f(c) = 0\), at a corner of two free edges \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).
  4. So \(U(f) = 0\) and by lemma 18.16 \(f = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\). By the assumption this is \(0\).

Source: The procedure is the same as for the body in lemma 4.133. The proof is not from the source.

What happens when the assumption is missing? Take a free plate with four free edges (figure 18.10) and the deflection \(w = c\,xy\). For it \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) everywhere, the equation \(\nabla^4 w = 0\) holds and the edges are not loaded. At the corners \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c \ne 0\), so by theorem 18.21, with corner forces by the remark below it, this deflection exists only if forces \(F = 2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) act at the corners: upward at \((0, 0)\) and \((a, a)\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} = +1\), and downward at \((a, 0)\) and \((0, a)\), where \(n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} = -1\). The forces \(F\) at opposite corners act in the same direction and the sum of forces and moments is zero, the plate is in equilibrium. Every deflection \(w + \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) is a solution with the same forces, because a plane changes no moment or higher derivative. So the deflection is determined only up to a rigid motion. The lower edge is for \(c\,xy\) a straight line, which the deflection along the edge seen from the side shows.

The numbers: for \(a = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm and the corner \((a, a)\) 1 mm above the other three (\(c = 0{,}004\ \text{m}^{-1}\)) we have \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = 50{,}26\) N m/m and \(F = 100{,}5\) N. The work of the forces on the displacements, half of the product (Clapeyron) \(\tfrac12 \cdot 100{,}5\ \text{N} \cdot 1\ \text{mm} = 50{,}26\) mJ, equals the energy \(D\,(1 - \nu)\,c^2 a^2 = 50{,}26\) mJ from equation (18.21), as it must. Here we see why the energy has the term with \((1 - \nu)\): Germain's energy \(U_{\mathrm{G}}\) would be zero for this field, although the forces do work and the plate is twisted (lemma 18.18). The uniqueness theorem does not hold, because a free plate has no essential condition, and \(f = \alpha + \beta\,x + \gamma\,y\) are three independent arbitrary numbers. The solution is determined only up to a rigid motion, and therefore in finite elements of free plates we shall find three zero eigenvalues of the stiffness matrix.

18.3.7 What we got

The energy of a plate is non-negative (lemma 18.16) and from its first variation (theorem 18.21) the plate equation and two conditions at each edge followed, which the kind of support determines: clamped \(w = \partial_{\mathrm{n}} w = 0\), simply supported \(w = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), free \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) (definition 18.19). At a free edge the transverse force and the twisting moment merge into \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\), and at the corners point forces \(2\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) arise (corollary 18.22), which the simply supported square confirms with the number \(162{,}4\) N. The solution is unique if the supports prevent rigid motion (theorem 18.23), and the free plate is an example where they do not. The section on Navier's solution uses the simply supported edge for a rectangle, and the section on energy methods uses the energy for Ritz's method, in which the trial fields must satisfy only the essential conditions.
