Chapter 19 Symmetry, periodicity and constraints

19.5 Elimination of constraints and the inclined support

In chapter 12 we supported the L-frame at \(C\) on a surface inclined at \(30°\) and imposed the constraint \(-\sin 30°\,u_{\mathrm{C}} + \cos 30°\,v_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\) in two ways (section 12.6). Multipliers (theorem 12.26) add an unknown to the system, and its matrix is no longer positive definite. The penalty (theorem 12.28) keeps the size of the system, but it is approximate and the matrix becomes worse conditioned. We only opened a third way (figure 19.26): in steps 1 and 2 of the proof of theorem 12.26 we expressed the dependent unknowns by the free ones. Here we follow it to the end and ask how much choice we have in doing so, whether every choice gives the same answer, when a choice fails and what it costs. We call the way elimination (izločanje), because the constraint removes a dependent unknown from the system, as the equation \(y = 2x\) removes \(y\) from a calculation. The letter \(\mat T\) means here what it meant in that proof, the matrix that expands the free numbers into all the unknowns.

First we draw the support once more, now as something that chooses two axes.

We draw the frame: the column clamped at \(A\) and the beam with nodes \(B\) and \(D\), ending at \(C\). At \(D\) the force \(F = 500\) N acts downward. At \(C\) the frame rests on a roller support on a surface that makes the angle \(\beta = 30°\) with the horizontal (definition 5.5). The roller of this support carries only a force perpendicular to the surface. If the surface acted on the roller also with a force along itself, this force at the radius of the roller would make a moment about the roller's centre, the pin at the centre carries no moment, and the sum of moments on a roller that does not spin up must be zero. Along the surface the support therefore holds nothing, the roller rolls. So we choose the axis \(\vek t = (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)\) along the surface and the axis \(\vek n = (-\sin\beta, \cos\beta)\) perpendicular to it, both of unit length. The force of the support on the frame is \(\lambda\,\vek n\) with an unknown \(\lambda\), as in figure 12.54. The support is idealized: with a real roller, friction in the bearing would add a small force along the surface that the model does not know. That force is bounded by \(\mu\lambda\), so with \(\mu = 0{,}01\) and the \(\lambda = 213{,}765\) N computed below it would be at most \(2{,}1\) N, which is \(1\) % of \(\lambda\), and it would change \(\lambda\) and the displacements by no more than that. On a rough surface with a large \(\mu\) the model no longer holds.

We split the displacement of the point \(C\) in figure 19.25 into two numbers: \(u_{\mathrm{t}} = \vek t\cdot\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) along the surface and \(u_{\mathrm{n}} = \vek n\cdot\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) perpendicular to it. The surface is hard, so \(u_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\). This is the same constraint as before, only written in the axes that the surface chose, and in these axes the constraint is a prescribed unknown. A prescribed unknown we know how to eliminate (theorem 7.18). Is this a peculiarity of a surface, or can we generalize it to every constraint?

Let us look at the unknowns \(u\) and \(v\) of the node \(C\) as a point in a plane.

We draw the axes \(u\) and \(v\): \(u\) is the displacement of the node \(C\) in the \(x\) direction, \(v\) the displacement in the \(y\) direction. The constraint \(-\sin\beta\,u + \cos\beta\,v = 0\) lets through only the points on a line through the origin, along the surface. Take an allowed point \(P\). To fix it we do not need both numbers, one is enough, in three ways. With the vertical dashed line we read off \(u\) and compute \(v = \tan\beta\,u\) from it, so \(v\) is dependent and \(u\) is master. With the horizontal dashed line we read off \(v\) and compute \(u = \cot\beta\,v\). With the length \(u_{\mathrm{t}}\) along the line we take a new number that is none of the original unknowns. Each of the three numbers can be the master unknown, because it determines all the rest. It is called master (glavna, "chief") because its value fixes the values of the others. These are dependent (odvisne), because the constraint determines them from the masters. We write the general form for all constraints at once.

Definition 19.25 Transformation of unknowns (transformacija neznank)

Let \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) be linear constraints (definition 12.25, figure 19.26) for a column \(\vek d\) of \(n\) unknowns, \(\mat C\) a matrix with \(m\) rows and \(n\) columns. A transformation of unknowns (transformacija neznank) is a representation

\[\vek d = \mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} + \vek d_0 , \tag{19.6}\]

where \(\mat T\) is a real matrix with \(n\) rows and \(n - m\) columns that has linearly independent columns (\(\mat T\vek w = \vek 0\) implies \(\vek w = \vek 0\)) and satisfies \(\mat C\,\mat T = \mat 0\), and \(\vek d_0\) is a column of \(n\) elements with \(\mat C\,\vek d_0 = \vek g\). The column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}\) of \(n - m\) numbers holds the master unknowns (glavne neznanke). When the master unknowns are some of the original unknowns (a partition, as in step 1 of the proof of theorem 12.26), the remaining original unknowns are dependent (odvisne).

Source: COOK §8.3, equation (8.3-3) and the text below it (PDF p. 290): the matrix \([T]\) "must be known but need have no special form", and §13.1, equations (13.1-1) to (13.1-3) (PDF pp. 503-504), where the master unknowns are "retained" and the dependent ones "eliminated" or "condensed out". The general form with any matrix \(\mat T\) with the conditions \(\mat C\,\mat T = \mat 0\) and independent columns is ours. ONATE1 §9.4.1 (PDF pp. 337-338) writes the same with a partition.

In figure 19.26 we have \(n = 2\), \(m = 1\) and \(\mat T\) is a column: \((1, \tan\beta)^\T\) for the master \(u\), \((\cot\beta, 1)^\T\) for the master \(v\) and \(\vek t = (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)^\T\) for the master \(u_{\mathrm{t}}\). Each of them lies along the line, so \(\mat C\,\mat T = (-\sin\beta, \cos\beta)\,\mat T = 0\), because \(\vek n\cdot\vek t = 0\). In all three \(\vek d_0 = \vek 0\), because \(g = 0\). For \(g \ne 0\) (\(\vek d_0\) is then one point of the line, for instance a support that has settled) the line moves parallel to itself. In the L-frame we have nine unknowns altogether, \(m = 1\) and \(\mat T\) is a matrix with 9 rows and 8 columns that leaves the unknowns \(u_{\mathrm{B}}\) to \(\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{D}}\) to \(\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{C}}\) unchanged and in the column for \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\) inserts one of the three above. Step 1 of the proof of theorem 12.26 knew only the form in which the unknowns split into free and dependent ones, that is \(\mat T\) with the column \((1, \tan\beta)^\T\) or \((\cot\beta, 1)^\T\). The definition allows every \(\mat T\). Let us see what this brings and what breaks. The letter \(\mat M\) in the theorem is, as in the proof of theorem 12.26, the matrix \(\mat T^\T\mat K\,\mat T\) and not the mass matrix of section 19.4.

Theorem 19.26 Elimination of constraints (izločanje vezi)

Let \(\mat K\), \(\vek f\), \(\mat C\), \(\vek g\) be as in theorem 12.26 (figure 19.26, \(\mat K\) symmetric positive semidefinite of order \(n\), \(\mat C\) with \(m\) pivots and \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d > 0\) for every \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\)), \(\vek d^*\) and \(\vek\lambda\) its solution and multipliers, and \(\vek d = \mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} + \vek d_0\) any transformation of unknowns (definition 19.25). Then:

  1. Every column \(\vek d\) with \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) has the form (19.6) with exactly one \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}\).

  2. The matrix \(\mat M = \mat T^\T\mat K\,\mat T\) of order \(n - m\) is symmetric and positive definite, the system

    \[\mat M\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat T^\T(\vek f - \mat K\,\vek d_0) \tag{19.7}\]

    has exactly one solution \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}^*\) and \(\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}^* + \vek d_0 = \vek d^*\), for every choice of the transformation.

  3. The column \(\vek r = \mat K\,\vek d^* - \vek f\) equals \(\mat C^\T\vek\lambda\) and

    \[\vek\lambda = (\mat C\,\mat C^\T)^{-1}\,\mat C\,\vek r . \tag{19.8}\]

    If the dependent unknowns are last, \(\vek d = (\vek y, \vek z)\), \(\mat C = [\mat C_{\mathrm{y}}\ \mat C_{\mathrm{z}}]\) and \(\mat T = \begin{pmatrix} \mat I \\ -\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^{-1}\mat C_{\mathrm{y}} \end{pmatrix}\), then \(\vek\lambda = \mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^{-\mathsf T}\vek r_{\mathrm{z}}\), where \(\vek r_{\mathrm{z}}\) is the last \(m\) elements of the column \(\vek r\).

  4. The matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\) of \(m\) chosen columns of \(\mat C\) is invertible exactly when the chosen unknowns are determined by the others: if \(\vek d\) and \(\vek d'\) are solutions of \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) with the same free unknown \(\vek y\), then \(\vek d = \vek d'\).

    For one constraint, \(m = 1\), with the row \(\vek c^\T\), the unknown \(d_{\mathrm{k}}\) can be dependent exactly when \(c_{\mathrm{k}} \ne 0\).

  5. If \(\mat T\) has orthonormal columns (\(\mat T^\T\mat T = \mat I\)) and \(\mat K\) is positive definite, then \(\kappa(\mat M) \le \kappa(\mat K)\) (definition 6.31).

Idea

The constraint defines a flat line (plane) of allowed columns, as in figure 19.26. Every matrix \(\mat T\) is a coordinate system on it, and the energy in these coordinates is a quadratic function with a positive definite matrix. Its minimum is the same, because the line is the same, and the multipliers come from the equilibrium off the line.

Proof

We must show (figure 19.26) that the columns of \(\mat T\) cover all allowed columns (item 1), that the energy in these coordinates is strictly convex and that its minimum is \(\vek d^*\) (item 2), then connect the multipliers with the residuals (item 3), check when the partition works (item 4) and bound the conditioning (item 5).

  1. We number the unknowns so that the chosen pivot columns of \(\mat C\) are last, as in step 1 of the proof of theorem 12.26, \(\vek d = (\vek y, \vek z)\).

    The matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\) is invertible (lemma 8.22) and every \(\vek w\) with \(\mat C\,\vek w = \vek 0\) has the form \(\vek w = \mat T_0\vek w_{\mathrm{y}}\) with \(\mat T_0 = \begin{pmatrix} \mat I \\ -\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^{-1}\mat C_{\mathrm{y}} \end{pmatrix}\), because \(\mat C_{\mathrm{y}}\vek w_{\mathrm{y}} + \mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\vek w_{\mathrm{z}} = \vek 0\) and \(\vek w_{\mathrm{z}} = -\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^{-1}\mat C_{\mathrm{y}}\vek w_{\mathrm{y}}\).

  2. The columns of \(\mat T\) satisfy \(\mat C\,\mat T = \mat 0\), so \(\mat T = \mat T_0\,\mat B\), where \(\mat B\) is the top \(n - m\) rows of \(\mat T\). If \(\mat B\,\vek w = \vek 0\), then \(\mat T\vek w = \mat T_0\mat B\,\vek w = \vek 0\) and by the independence of the columns \(\vek w = \vek 0\). The square matrix \(\mat B\) is therefore invertible (lemma 8.22).

  3. For every \(\vek d\) with \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) we have \(\mat C(\vek d - \vek d_0) = \vek 0\), so by step 1 \(\vek d - \vek d_0 = \mat T_0(\vek y - \vek y_0) = \mat T\,\mat B^{-1}(\vek y - \vek y_0)\), where \(\vek y_0\) is the upper part of the column \(\vek d_0\), and \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat B^{-1}(\vek y - \vek y_0)\). Another \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}'\) with the same \(\vek d\) would give \(\mat T(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} - \vek d_{\mathrm{m}}') = \vek 0\) and by the independence of the columns it would be equal.

  4. The matrix \(\mat M\) is symmetric, because \(\mat K\) is symmetric. For \(\vek w \ne \vek 0\) we have \(\mat T\vek w \ne \vek 0\) (independent columns) and \(\mat C\,\mat T\vek w = \vek 0\), so \(\vek w^\T\mat M\vek w = (\mat T\vek w)^\T\mat K(\mat T\vek w) > 0\) by the hypothesis of theorem 12.26. The matrix \(\mat M\) is positive definite and the system (19.7) has exactly one solution (theorem 6.29).

  5. The expansion from step 2 of the proof of theorem 12.26 gives \(\Pi(\mat T\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} + \vek d_0) = \tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}^\T\mat M\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} - \vek h^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} + \Pi(\vek d_0)\) with \(\vek h = \mat T^\T(\vek f - \mat K\vek d_0)\), which has its minimum exactly at the solution of the system (19.7) (lemma 6.30).

    By item 1 every allowed point belongs to one \(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}\), so \(\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}^* + \vek d_0\) is the point of least energy on the set of constraints, and by item 1 of theorem 12.26 this is \(\vek d^*\).

  6. By item 2 of theorem 12.26 \(\vek r = \mat C^\T\vek\lambda\). For \(\vek x \ne \vek 0\) we have \(\mat C^\T\vek x \ne \vek 0\) (step 3 of the proof of theorem 12.26) and \(\vek x^\T\mat C\,\mat C^\T\vek x = |\mat C^\T\vek x|^2 > 0\).

    The matrix \(\mat C\,\mat C^\T\) is symmetric and positive definite, so invertible (theorem 6.29). Multiplying the equation \(\vek r = \mat C^\T\vek\lambda\) by \(\mat C\) from the left gives (19.8). For the partition the last \(m\) rows of \(\mat C^\T\) are \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^\T\), so \(\vek r_{\mathrm{z}} = \mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^\T\vek\lambda\), and \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}^\T\) is invertible (lemma 8.22).

  7. Let \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\) be invertible. From \(\mat C\,\vek d = \mat C\,\vek d' = \vek g\) and \(\vek y = \vek y'\) it follows that \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}(\vek z - \vek z') = \vek 0\), so \(\vek z = \vek z'\).

    Let \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\) be not invertible. By lemma 8.22 there is \(\vek w \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}}\vek w = \vek 0\). Then \(\mat C\,(\vek 0, \vek w) = \vek 0\) and \(\vek d' = \vek d + (\vek 0, \vek w)\) is another solution with the same \(\vek y\). For \(m = 1\) we have \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}} = (c_{\mathrm{k}})\), a square matrix of order 1, invertible exactly when \(c_{\mathrm{k}} \ne 0\).

  8. For \(\vek w \ne \vek 0\) we have \(|\mat T\vek w|^2 = \vek w^\T\mat T^\T\mat T\vek w = |\vek w|^2\), so the Rayleigh quotient of \(\mat M\) at \(\vek w\) is the quotient of \(\mat K\) at \(\mat T\vek w\). By theorem 10.42 with \(\mat M = \mat I\) it lies between \(\lambda_{\min}(\mat K)\) and \(\lambda_{\max}(\mat K)\).

    This holds for every \(\vek w\), also for the eigencolumns of \(\mat M\), so \(\lambda_{\max}(\mat M) \le \lambda_{\max}(\mat K)\) and \(\lambda_{\min}(\mat M) \ge \lambda_{\min}(\mat K)\), and the ratio is smaller.

Source: COOK §13.1, equations (13.1-3) and (13.1-4) (PDF p. 504): \([T]^\T[K][T]\{D_{\mathrm{r}}\} = [T]^\T\{R\}\) for a partition, and "For multipoint constraints, the choice of which d.o.f. to place in {D_c} is not unique, but different choices should lead to the same final results". ONATE1 §9.4.1 (PDF p. 338): "The selection of \(a_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{e}}\) is not unique". The proof gives this claim, items 3 to 5 are not from the sources.

The proof showed (figure 19.26) that any matrix \(\mat T\) with independent columns and \(\mat C\,\mat T = \mat 0\) gives the same solution \(\vek d^*\), the same as the multipliers, and that we get the multipliers from the residual \(\mat K\,\vek d^* - \vek f\), as in equilibrium. The partition of chapter 12 is only one of the choices. We return to both: what the choice of \(\mat T\) does in numbers and what happens when the partition fails.

We eliminate the constraint \(u_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) in the L-frame in three ways, with a \(\mat T\) that leaves \(u_{\mathrm{B}}\) to \(\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}\), \(u_{\mathrm{D}}\) to \(\varphi_{\mathrm{D}}\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{C}}\) unchanged. The eight master unknowns in each are these seven and one of the three from figure 19.26. The frame has the same \(E = 196\) GPa, \(A = 800\) mm² and \(I = 106\,667\) mm⁴ as in chapter 12, \(F = 500\) N and \(h = 0{,}5\) m for the elements \(BD\) and \(DC\). The multipliers of theorem 12.26 give \(\lambda = 213{,}765\) N, \(u_{\mathrm{C}} = -0{,}1533\) mm, \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = -0{,}0885\) mm, \(\varphi_{\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}000891\).

Now let us see what breaks when the partition is impossible. Item 4 of the theorem says that \(d_{\mathrm{k}}\) can be dependent only if \(c_{\mathrm{k}} \ne 0\). Take the horizontal surface of section 12.4.5. The constraint is \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\) with the row \(\vek c^\T = \vek e_{\mathrm{v}_\mathrm{C}}^\T\), at the place of \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\) we have \(c = 0\). If we choose \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\) as dependent, we would have to compute \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\) from \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\), but the constraint says nothing about \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\). In the right half of figure 19.26 the points \(P\) and \(Q\) on the horizontal line of the constraint have the same \(v\) and different \(u\), as the dashed lines on both axes show. The matrix \(\mat T_0\) does not exist, because we would have to divide by \(\mat C_{\mathrm{z}} = c_{\mathrm{u}} = 0\). The master unknown must be \(u_{\mathrm{C}}\), the dependent one \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\), and this is the prescribed unknown that chapter 7 eliminated.

We used the local axes (figure 19.25) at \(C\) without proving that such a \(\mat T\) is a transformation in the sense of the definition. We write it in general.

Lemma 19.27 Inclined support by local axes (nagnjena podpora z lokalnimi osmi)

Let \(\mat K\) be a positive definite matrix of order \(n\), \(\vek f\) a column of \(n\) elements and let the node \(C\) (figure 19.25) have the unknowns \(u\) and \(v\) at the places \(i\) and \(j\). The support does not allow the node to move in the direction \(\vek n = (-\sin\beta, \cos\beta)\), the constraint is \(\vek c^\T\vek d = 0\) with \(c_{\mathrm{i}} = -\sin\beta\), \(c_{\mathrm{j}} = \cos\beta\) and \(0\) elsewhere. Let \(\mat R\) be the matrix of order \(n\) that is the identity except in the block at the places \(i\) and \(j\), where it is \(\mat Q = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\beta & \sin\beta \\ -\sin\beta & \cos\beta \end{pmatrix}\), the new unknowns \(\vek d' = \mat R\,\vek d\) (among them \(u_{\mathrm{t}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) at the places \(i\) and \(j\)), \(\mat K' = \mat R\,\mat K\,\mat R^\T\) and \(\vek f' = \mat R\,\vek f\). Then:

  1. The matrix \(\mat R\) is orthogonal (definition 2.46), \(\mat K'\) is positive definite, \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) is equivalent to \(\mat K'\vek d' = \vek f'\) and the constraint is \(d'_{\mathrm{j}} = u_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\).
  2. We eliminate the constraint by deleting row and column \(j\) of \(\mat K'\) and \(\vek f'\) (theorem 7.18). What remains is a system of order \(n - 1\) with the matrix \(\mat M\), which is the elimination of theorem 19.26 with \(\mat T = \mat R^\T\) without the column \(j\).
  3. The column \(\mat K'\vek d' - \vek f'\) has its only nonzero element at the place \(j\) and it is the multiplier \(\lambda\). The support acts on the frame with the force \(\lambda\,\vek n\) and none along \(\vek t\).
  4. \(\kappa(\mat M) \le \kappa(\mat K)\).
Idea

We put the axis \(\vek n\) (figure 19.25) on a coordinate axis of the new unknowns. The constraint is then one of them, and a rotation of the coordinates, which does not change lengths, does not change the eigenvalues.

Proof

We must show (figure 19.25) that the rotation of the node's coordinates keeps the system and the constraint, that the constraint is a prescribed unknown and that the residual in row \(j\) carries the multiplier.

  1. The block \(\mat Q\) is orthogonal, \(\mat Q^\T\mat Q = \mat I\) (\(\cos^2\beta + \sin^2\beta = 1\) and the mixed terms cancel), so \(\mat R\) is orthogonal too and \(\mat R^\T\mat R = \mat I\). For \(\vek w \ne \vek 0\) we have \(\vek w^\T\mat K'\vek w = (\mat R^\T\vek w)^\T\mat K(\mat R^\T\vek w) > 0\), because \(\mat R^\T\vek w \ne \vek 0\).

    Multiplying the equation \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) by \(\mat R\) from the left and using \(\vek d = \mat R^\T\vek d'\) gives \(\mat K'\vek d' = \vek f'\), and back with \(\mat R^\T\). The second row of the block \(\mat Q\) gives \(d'_{\mathrm{j}} = -\sin\beta\,u + \cos\beta\,v = \vek c^\T\vek d\).

  2. The constraint \(d'_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\) is a prescribed unknown, and by theorem 7.18 the solution of the other unknowns is the solution of the system with row and column \(j\) deleted.

    The columns of \(\mat R^\T\) except the column \(j\) are \(\mat T\) with \(\mat C\,\mat T = \vek c^\T\mat R^\T = \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}^\T\mat R\mat R^\T\) without the column \(j\), so zero, \(\mat C\,\mat T = \mat 0\).

  3. By item 3 of theorem 19.26 \(\mat K\,\vek d - \vek f = \vek c\,\lambda\), because \(\mat C = \vek c^\T\). Multiplying by \(\mat R\) gives \(\mat K'\vek d' - \vek f' = \mat R\,\vek c\,\lambda\) and \(\mat R\,\vek c = \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\), because \(\vek c = \mat R^\T\vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\).

  4. The matrix \(\mat K'\) is orthogonally similar to \(\mat K\) and has the same eigenvalues (if \(\mat K\vek v = \lambda\vek v\), then \(\mat K'\mat R\vek v = \lambda\mat R\vek v\)).

    The matrix \(\mat M\) is \(\mat K'\) without row and column \(j\), that is \(\mat T^\T\mat K\,\mat T\), where \(\mat T\) (the columns of \(\mat R^\T\) without column \(j\)) has orthonormal columns. So by item 5 of theorem 19.26 \(\kappa(\mat M) \le \kappa(\mat K) = \kappa(\mat K')\).

Source: COOK §8.4, equation (8.4-4) (PDF pp. 290-291): \([T]^\T[K'][T]\) with \([T_3]\) for node 3 and "It is preferable to transform individual element matrices before assembly". ONATE1 §9.2, equations (9.1) to (9.4) (PDF pp. 332-333): local direction \(x'\), \(y'\) at the support, "The reactions at the inclined boundary nodes are directly computed in the local boundary system". The proof, the bound on the condition number and the form of \(\mat K'\vek d' - \vek f'\) are not from the sources.

Item 3 of the lemma is the same conclusion as the argument about the roller. In the L-frame the residual at \(C\) along \(\vek t\) is \(-2 \cdot 10^{-12}\) N (rounding), along \(\vek n\) it is \(213{,}765\) N, as the multiplier. The support really carries nothing along the surface (figure 19.25). The penalty with \(\alpha s = 10^4\) on the other hand satisfies the constraint only approximately, \(\vek c^\T\vek d = -1{,}6 \cdot 10^{-6}\) m instead of zero.

We put the three ways side by side, on the same frame and with the same constraint.

The rows on multipliers and penalty are from chapter 12 (figure 12.54), the row on elimination is new. COOK (PDF p. 503) says the same in words: multipliers "add to the number of equations but require less manipulation", the penalty leaves the number of unknowns and "may produce an ill-conditioned set of equations", and elimination requires "considerable bookkeeping in software and rearrangement of coefficients, especially for multipoint constraints". This last point holds for the general partition. For a single support with local axes there is little rearranging, because only the \(2 \times 2\) block of the node changes, as COOK states on PDF p. 291 (element matrices before assembly). For constraints between several nodes, which section 19.6 needs, the partition \(\mat T_0\) changes rows and columns of several nodes at once.

The section showed three things (figures 19.25 and 19.26). Elimination gives for every transformation the same solution as the multipliers (item 2), with no extra unknown and with a positive definite matrix. The choice of dependent unknowns is not indifferent: the partition is possible only for \(c_{\mathrm{k}} \ne 0\) and for a small \(\sin\beta\) it already loses conditioning, the local axes do not. The inclined support is therefore elimination, which chapter 7 already knew for a horizontal surface, with a rotation of the axes at the node. A matrix \(\mat T\) that is not orthogonal comes into its own with rigid links, where a constraint ties the displacements of several nodes.
