Chapter 18 Plates

18.5 The simply supported rectangle: Navier's solution

A steel plate with sides \(a = b = 500\) mm and thickness \(h = 10\) mm lies on all four edges and carries a uniform pressure \(q = 10\) kPa. For steel we take \(E = 196\) GPa and \(\nu = 0{,}3\), so the bending rigidity is \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m (definition 18.6). How much does the plate deflect at the centre, what is the bending moment there and what is the stress? What forces must the supports carry, and what happens in the corners? In section 18.4 we answered for a plate so long in one direction that it bends as a strip, and then the equation is ordinary. A square bends in both directions at once, the function \(w(x, y)\) has two variables and the equation is partial.

For the beam we had the same difficulty in one variable and solved it by modes (theorem 13.46): the sine \(\sin(n\pi x/L)\) has a fourth derivative proportional to itself, so a load in the form of a sine gives a deflection in the form of the same sine. In two variables a product of two sines does the same, and a load that is not a sine is expanded into a sum of such products. This idea is Navier's, so the solution is called Navier's (the reason is in the historical frame below).

Model. The plate (definition 18.1) has the middle plane \(P = [0, a] \times [0, b]\), thickness \(h\) and rigidity \(D\). The Kirchhoff assumptions hold (definition 18.2): the plate is thin, \(h\) is much less than \(a\) and \(b\), the deflection \(w\) is much smaller than \(h\), the normals stay straight and perpendicular to the middle plane. In this section we measure the deflection \(w\), the load \(q(x, y)\) (a force per unit area) and the shear forces \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(Q_{\mathrm{y}}\) downward, in the direction of the load, so that they are positive for a plate under pressure. This is opposite to the axis \(z\) of section 18.1, so all four quantities change sign, while the moments stay the same (positive with compression on top). The plate equation stays \(D\nabla^4 w = q\) (theorem 18.12, \(\nabla^4 w = w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + 2w_{\mathrm{xxyy}} + w_{\mathrm{yyyy}}\)), because both sides change sign. The moments of theorem 18.7 get a minus, \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = -D(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu w_{\mathrm{yy}})\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = -D(w_{\mathrm{yy}} + \nu w_{\mathrm{xx}})\), and the twisting moment is \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = -D(1 - \nu)w_{\mathrm{xy}}\). The shear forces are \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{x}} + M_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}}\), and equation (18.16) keeps its form, \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = -D\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(\nabla^2 w)\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = -D\,\partial_{\mathrm{y}}(\nabla^2 w)\), because \(Q\) and \(w\) both change sign.

All four edges are simply supported (definition 18.19): the edge cannot move down, \(w = 0\), but it can rotate freely, so the bending moment perpendicular to the edge is zero, \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\). This is an idealization. A real edge has friction and the stiffness of the frame, which clamp it a little and reduce the deflection. How much, section 18.6 shows: a clamped circular plate deflects \(4{,}08\) times less than a simply supported one. On the straight edge \(x = 0\) we have \(w(0, y) = 0\) for every \(y\), so also \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}(0, y) = 0\) (we differentiate twice along the edge). The condition \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = -D(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu w_{\mathrm{yy}}) = 0\) is therefore equivalent to \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\). The same holds on the other edges, and the conditions are

\[\begin{aligned} w = 0 \ \text{and}\ w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0 \ &\text{at } x = 0 \text{ and } x = a , \\ w = 0 \ \text{and}\ w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0 \ &\text{at } y = 0 \text{ and } y = b . \end{aligned} \tag{18.32}\]

Notation: \(w\) is the deflection here, as in chapter 13 (for the beam in chapter 5 the deflection was \(v\)). The load \(q\) is per unit area, for the beam per unit length. The moments \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) are per unit length of edge (N m/m), for the beam \(M\) was in N m. The shear force \(Q\) is per unit length, for the beam it was called \(V\). The numbers \(m\) and \(n\) in this section count the half-waves of the sine, they are not a mass or a normal, \(N\) is the upper bound \(m, n \le N\) of the indices of a partial sum, not an axial force, and \(K\) is a constant.

Let us draw the plate from above. The middle plane is a rectangle, long \(a\) in the direction \(x\) and \(b\) in the direction \(y\). The axes \(x\) and \(y\) start in the lower left corner. Along all four edges the edge is supported, which the hatching shows, and so the conditions (18.32) hold.

A single sine load tells us what the sum will do. Let the plate be seen from the side, along the line \(y = b/2\). The supports hold both ends of the middle plane. The load presses on it, zero at the ends and largest in the middle, \(q_{11}\sin(\pi x/a)\) with a number \(q_{11}\) in the unit of pressure. The plate deflects down, in the middle by \(W_{11}\), in the shape of the same sine. For terms with larger \(m\) and \(n\) the load and the deflection change sign, and the plate is cut by nodal lines on which the deflection is zero. The figure shows the term \(m = n = 2\) with the nodal lines \(x = a/2\) and \(y = b/2\).

From history

The first satisfactory solution of plate bending is Navier's. Before him, Sophie Germain, in the Academy's competition on the theory of plates, assumed that the energy of a plate is an integral of the principal radii of curvature, following Euler's elastic curves. She made an error in the variational calculation, Lagrange corrected it. At the second entry the judges demanded a physical justification of her energy, at the third, in 1816, they awarded her the prize, although the justification was still missing (TIMO PDF p. 128). Poisson derived the equation from a system of particles that all lie in the middle plane, so the constant in his equation is proportional to the square of the thickness and not to the third power (TIMO PDF p. 129). Navier distributed the particles over the whole thickness and assumed that their displacements during bending are parallel to the middle plane and proportional to the distance from it (TIMO PDF p. 129). This is the same as what follows from straight normals in definition 18.2: a point at distance \(z\) from the middle plane moves horizontally by \(-z\) times the slope. Because the particles are over the whole thickness, the third power of the thickness appears in the rigidity, as in definition 18.6. So he obtained the correct equation for an arbitrary transverse load, and his results are expressed with a single elastic constant: his rigidity \(D\) agrees with the modern value at \(\nu = 1/4\), so his model has \(\nu = 1/4\) built in (TIMO PDF p. 129). He presented the memoir to the Academy on 14 August 1820, it was published in 1823. For the simply supported rectangle he wrote the correct boundary conditions and the solution as a double trigonometric series, which he used for a uniform load and for a load concentrated at the centre. These were the first satisfactory solutions of plate bending (TIMO PDF pp. 129-130). The method is named after him because he was the first to write the correct solution, and we shall test both of his examples too, the second as an example that does not satisfy the requirement of the theorem.

We watched how Navier solved the simply supported rectangle by expanding load and deflection into products of sines. First let us show that a single product solves the equation and the boundary conditions.

Lemma 18.28 Sine term of the plate (sinusni člen plošče)

Let \(m\) and \(n\) be natural numbers, \(q_{\mathrm{mn}}\) a real number and

\[\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}} = \pi^2\Big(\frac{m^2}{a^2} + \frac{n^2}{b^2}\Big) , \qquad W_{\mathrm{mn}} = \frac{q_{\mathrm{mn}}}{D\,\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2} . \tag{18.33}\]

The function \(s_{\mathrm{mn}}(x, y) = \sin\dfrac{m\pi x}{a}\sin\dfrac{n\pi y}{b}\) is infinitely often continuously differentiable, and \(w_{\mathrm{mn}} = W_{\mathrm{mn}}\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) solves \(D\nabla^4 w = q_{\mathrm{mn}}\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and satisfies the boundary conditions (18.32). For the shear forces \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{y}} w_{\mathrm{mn}}\).

Proof

We must show that \(s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) is an eigenfunction of the operator \(\nabla^2\), from this \(\nabla^4\) follows, then the boundary conditions and the shear forces.

  1. By the theorem on derivatives of sine and cosine (8.12), \(\partial_{\mathrm{xx}}s_{\mathrm{mn}} = -(m\pi/a)^2 s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{yy}}s_{\mathrm{mn}} = -(n\pi/b)^2 s_{\mathrm{mn}}\). The sum is \(\nabla^2 s_{\mathrm{mn}} = -\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}\).
  2. The operator is linear, so \(\nabla^4 s_{\mathrm{mn}} = \nabla^2(-\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}s_{\mathrm{mn}}) = \lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}\). For \(w_{\mathrm{mn}} = W_{\mathrm{mn}}s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) we get \(D\nabla^4 w_{\mathrm{mn}} = D\,W_{\mathrm{mn}}\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,s_{\mathrm{mn}} = q_{\mathrm{mn}}\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}\).
  3. At \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\), \(\sin(m\pi x/a) = 0\) (lemma 8.10, point 2), so \(w_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{mn}} = -(m\pi/a)^2 w_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\). At \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) likewise \(w_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) and \(\partial_{\mathrm{yy}}w_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\). These are the conditions (18.32).
  4. By equation (18.16) (in the form of the model above), \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = -D\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}}\nabla^2 w_{\mathrm{mn}} = -D\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(-\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}w_{\mathrm{mn}}) = D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{mn}}\), and likewise for \(Q_{\mathrm{y}}\).

Source: EP section 7.2.4 (printed pp. 436-438) inserts the double sine series into the plate equation and obtains the equation for the coefficients \(W_{\mathrm{mn}}\) (7.2.116-7.2.119). The proof by the eigenfunction of \(\nabla^2\) is ours, the idea is the same as for the beam in theorem 13.46.

The lemma holds for each term separately, but the load is usually a sum of infinitely many terms. For the beam we took the derivative of the series from the expansion theorem. Here we need a smaller theorem that says when we may differentiate a series term by term.

Lemma 18.29 Term by term differentiation of a series (odvajanje vrste člen za členom)

Let \(I\) be an interval, \(J\) an interval and \((u_{\mathrm{k}})\) a sequence of continuous functions \(I \times J \to \mathbb{R}\) whose partial derivative \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}} u_{\mathrm{k}}\) is continuous. Let \(M_{\mathrm{k}} = \sup|u_{\mathrm{k}}|\) and \(M_{\mathrm{k}}' = \sup|\partial_{\mathrm{x}} u_{\mathrm{k}}|\) with \(\sum M_{\mathrm{k}} < \infty\) and \(\sum M_{\mathrm{k}}' < \infty\). Then the series \(F = \sum u_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(G = \sum \partial_{\mathrm{x}} u_{\mathrm{k}}\) converge uniformly, \(F\) and \(G\) are continuous, the derivative \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}} F\) exists and equals \(G\). The same holds for \(\partial_{\mathrm{y}}\). For double indices \(k = (m, n)\) the sum does not depend on the order of the terms.

Proof

We must show uniform convergence, continuity of the limit and the exchange of derivative and sum. The first is Weierstrass's criterion, the second an estimate with \(\eta/3\), the third the fundamental theorem of calculus.

  1. By theorem 9.85 (points 1 and 2) the series converge absolutely and uniformly, so independently of the order. The sums are \(F\) and \(G\), the partial sums \(F_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{N}}\).
  2. Let \(\eta > 0\). Choose \(N\) with \(\sup|F - F_{\mathrm{N}}| < \eta/3\). The function \(F_{\mathrm{N}}\) is continuous at the point \(p\), so there is \(\delta > 0\) with \(|F_{\mathrm{N}}(p') - F_{\mathrm{N}}(p)| < \eta/3\) for \(|p' - p| < \delta\). Then \(|F(p') - F(p)| \le |F(p') - F_{\mathrm{N}}(p')| + |F_{\mathrm{N}}(p') - F_{\mathrm{N}}(p)| + |F_{\mathrm{N}}(p) - F(p)| < \eta\). So \(F\) is continuous, and likewise \(G\).
  3. For every \(y\) and numbers \(x_0, x\), by the fundamental theorem of calculus (3.40, point 1) \(u_{\mathrm{k}}(x, y) - u_{\mathrm{k}}(x_0, y) = \int_{\mathrm{x}_0}^{x}\partial_{\mathrm{x}} u_{\mathrm{k}}(s, y)\,\mathrm{d}s\). Summing the first \(N\) terms, \(F_{\mathrm{N}}(x, y) - F_{\mathrm{N}}(x_0, y) = \int_{\mathrm{x}_0}^{x}G_{\mathrm{N}}(s, y)\,\mathrm{d}s\). By the integral estimate lemma (9.31), \(\big|\int_{\mathrm{x}_0}^{x}(G - G_{\mathrm{N}})\,\mathrm{d}s\big| \le |x - x_0|\sup|G - G_{\mathrm{N}}| \to 0\), so \(F(x, y) - F(x_0, y) = \int_{\mathrm{x}_0}^{x}G(s, y)\,\mathrm{d}s\).
  4. The function \(s \mapsto G(s, y)\) is continuous (step 2), so by the fundamental theorem (3.40, point 2) \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}} F(x, y) = G(x, y)\) exists.

Source: The theorem is standard, the proof is not from a source. The same idea with the fundamental theorem of calculus.

Now we can state the main theorem. The coefficient \(q_{\mathrm{mn}}\) is the average of the load with the sine: the factor \(4/(ab)\) is the reciprocal of \(\iint s_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A = ab/4\), which follows from the orthogonality of sines (lemma 9.87 with \(T_{\mathrm{p}} = 2a\), the product \(\sin^2\) is an even function). Navier's load is not every function. The coefficients \(q_{\mathrm{mn}}\) must not decay more slowly than \(1/(mn)\), which holds for a uniform load (the reason is in the corollary below), and the load must be the sum of its series in the sense of integrals over rectangles. For a double series this is the condition that for a single series is guaranteed by lemma 13.10, and here we prove it only for the uniform load.

Theorem 18.30 Navier's solution of the simply supported rectangular plate (Navierova rešitev prosto podprte pravokotne plošče)

Let \(P = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) be a plate with rigidity \(D\) and \(0 \le \nu < 1\), simply supported on all edges (conditions (18.32)), and \(q\) a load on \(P\). Let

\[q_{\mathrm{mn}} = \frac{4}{ab}\int_0^a\!\!\int_0^b q(x, y)\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}(x, y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x \tag{18.34}\]

and let (H1) \(|q_{\mathrm{mn}}| \le K/(mn)\) for all natural \(m, n\) and some constant \(K\), (H2) \(\displaystyle\iint_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\mathrm{d}A = \sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}}q_{\mathrm{mn}}\iint_{\mathrm{R}} s_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) for every rectangle \(R = [x_1, x_2] \times [y_1, y_2] \subset P\). Let \(w_{\mathrm{mn}} = W_{\mathrm{mn}}s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) with \(W_{\mathrm{mn}}\) from equation (18.33). Then:

  1. The series \(w = \sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}}w_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and all its partial derivatives up to third order, obtained term by term, converge uniformly on \(P\) and absolutely. The function \(w\) is three times continuously differentiable on \(P\), the moments are continuous and
    \[M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}}W_{\mathrm{mn}}\Big[\Big(\frac{m\pi}{a}\Big)^2 + \nu\Big(\frac{n\pi}{b}\Big)^2\Big]s_{\mathrm{mn}} , \qquad M_{\mathrm{xy}} = -D(1 - \nu)\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}}W_{\mathrm{mn}}\frac{m\pi}{a}\frac{n\pi}{b}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{a}\cos\frac{n\pi y}{b} , \tag{18.35}\]
    and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) is like \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) with \(m\pi/a\) and \(n\pi/b\) exchanged.
  2. The function \(w\) satisfies the boundary conditions (18.32).
  3. For every rectangle \(R \subset P\) the equilibrium of the piece holds,
    \[\int_{\mathrm{y}_1}^{y_2}\big[Q_{\mathrm{x}}(x_2, y) - Q_{\mathrm{x}}(x_1, y)\big]\mathrm{d}y + \int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2}\big[Q_{\mathrm{y}}(x, y_2) - Q_{\mathrm{y}}(x, y_1)\big]\mathrm{d}x + \iint_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\mathrm{d}A = 0 . \tag{18.36}\]
Idea

Each term solves the equation for its part of the load (lemma 18.28). The sum may be differentiated term by term if the series of derivatives converge uniformly, and this we show by estimating the terms. The equilibrium (18.36) holds for every partial sum, and in the limit for the sum.

Proof

We must show the estimate of the terms, the differentiation, the boundary conditions and the equilibrium of the piece.

  1. Let \(\rho = \max(m, n)\). We have \(\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}} \ge c\,\rho^2\) with \(c = \pi^2/\max(a, b)^2\), so by hypothesis (H1) \(|W_{\mathrm{mn}}| \le K/(mn\,D\,c^2\rho^4)\). The term of a partial derivative of order \(i + j \le 3\) of \(w_{\mathrm{mn}}\) is in absolute value at most \(|W_{\mathrm{mn}}|(m\pi/a)^i(n\pi/b)^j \le C_1\rho^3|W_{\mathrm{mn}}| \le C\,/(mn\,\rho)\) with constants \(C_1\), \(C\) determined by \(a\), \(b\), \(D\), \(K\). Since \(\rho^2 \ge mn\), this is \(\le C\,(mn)^{-3/2}\).
  2. The sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{k} \ge 1}k^{-3/2}\) is finite: for \(k \ge 2\), \(k^{-3/2} \le \int_{\mathrm{k}-1}^{k}s^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{d}s\), so the sum is \(\le 1 + \int_1^\infty s^{-3/2}\,\mathrm{d}s = 3\). Hence \(\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}}(mn)^{-3/2} \le 9\), and by theorem 9.85 the series of all derivatives up to third order converge uniformly and absolutely. By lemma 18.29, applied successively to derivatives of order 0, 1 and 2, \(w\) is three times continuously differentiable and the derivatives are the sums of the derivative series. From \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = -D(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu w_{\mathrm{yy}})\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -\sum W_{\mathrm{mn}}(m\pi/a)^2 s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) formula (18.35) follows, likewise for \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = -D(1 - \nu)w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\).
  3. Every term is zero at \(x = 0, a\) and \(y = 0, b\) together with \(\partial_{\mathrm{xx}}\) or \(\partial_{\mathrm{yy}}\) respectively (step 3 of lemma 18.28). Since the series for \(w\), \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) converge, these values are zero also for the sum, and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) because \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) on the edge.
  4. Let \(w_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n} \le \mathrm{N}}w_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and \(q_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n} \le \mathrm{N}}q_{\mathrm{mn}}s_{\mathrm{mn}}\). By lemma 18.28, \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}}Q_{\mathrm{x}} + \partial_{\mathrm{y}}Q_{\mathrm{y}} = -D\nabla^4 w_{\mathrm{N}} = -q_{\mathrm{N}}\) for the partial sum. By the fundamental theorem of calculus and the theorem on the order of integration (4.9), the sum of the first two integrals of equation (18.36) for \(w_{\mathrm{N}}\) equals \(\iint_{\mathrm{R}}(\partial_{\mathrm{x}}Q_{\mathrm{x}} + \partial_{\mathrm{y}}Q_{\mathrm{y}})\,\mathrm{d}A = -\iint_{\mathrm{R}} q_{\mathrm{N}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). So for \(w_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(q_{\mathrm{N}}\) the equilibrium holds.
  5. As \(N \to \infty\), \(Q_{\mathrm{x}} = -D\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{yy}})\) (a sum of derivatives of third order) tends uniformly to its limit, so by the integral estimate lemma (9.31) the integrals over the sides of \(R\) converge. The right side: \(\iint_{\mathrm{R}} s_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) has absolute value at most \(\frac{2a}{m\pi}\cdot\frac{2b}{n\pi}\), so by (H1) \(\sum|q_{\mathrm{mn}}|\,\big|\iint_{\mathrm{R}} s_{\mathrm{mn}}\big| \le \frac{4abK}{\pi^2}\sum(mn)^{-2} < \infty\), and by (H2) \(\iint_{\mathrm{R}} q_{\mathrm{N}}\,\mathrm{d}A \to \iint_{\mathrm{R}} q\,\mathrm{d}A\). The limit of the equilibrium is equation (18.36).
Explanation

Equation (18.36) is the force equilibrium of every rectangular piece of the middle plane (lemma 18.10), which by the same argument as in that lemma (vanishing integrand, section 18.2) is replaced by the differential equation at points where the derivatives of fourth order are continuous. That \(w\) is the only solution this theorem does not claim: uniqueness is proved by theorem 18.23 for solutions in its class. Without it the theorem says that this \(w\) is a solution.

Source: EP section 7.2.4 (printed pp. 435-440): expansion of \(w\) and \(q\) into a double sine series, moments and shear forces (p. 439), formula (7.2.119). VK section 3.3 (PDF pp. 52-54) the same procedure, on convergence it says only that the series is estimated with \(|\sin| \le 1\) (PDF p. 54). The proof of convergence of derivatives, of term by term differentiation and of the equilibrium of the piece is ours. (H2) for a general load means completeness of the double sines and we do not prove it (assumption of completeness), we prove it for the uniform load.

How is the theorem used? From the load we compute the coefficients \(q_{\mathrm{mn}}\) by a double integral (equation (18.34)), divide each by \(D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\), and put the result in front of the sine. For a uniform load all three steps are written in formulas.

Corollary 18.31 Uniform load on the simply supported rectangle (enakomerna obtežba prosto podprtega pravokotnika)

Let \(q = q_0\) be constant on \(P\). Then the hypotheses (H1) and (H2) are satisfied, \(q_{\mathrm{mn}} = 16q_0/(\pi^2 mn)\) for odd \(m\), \(n\) and \(q_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) otherwise, and

\[w(x, y) = \frac{16\,q_0}{\pi^6 D}\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}\ \text{odd}}\frac{1}{mn\big(m^2/a^2 + n^2/b^2\big)^2}\,s_{\mathrm{mn}}(x, y) . \tag{18.37}\]

At the centre of the square \(a = b\), \(w_{\mathrm{c}} = 0{,}004062\,q_0a^4/D\) and \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0{,}04789\,q_0a^2\). The number \(0{,}004062\) does not depend on \(\nu\) (\(\nu\) enters only through \(D\)), the number \(0{,}04789\) is computed at \(\nu = 0{,}3\).

Proof

We must show the coefficients, hypothesis (H1) and hypothesis (H2), the rest is theorem 18.30.

  1. For natural \(m\), \(\int_0^a\sin(m\pi x/a)\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{a}{m\pi}(1 - \cos m\pi)\), which is \(\frac{2a}{m\pi}\) for odd \(m\) and \(0\) for even. From formula (18.34), \(q_{\mathrm{mn}} = \frac{4}{ab}q_0\cdot\frac{2a}{m\pi}\cdot\frac{2b}{n\pi} = \frac{16q_0}{\pi^2 mn}\) for odd \(m\), \(n\), otherwise zero. Hypothesis (H1) holds with \(K = 16q_0/\pi^2\).
  2. For (H2) let \(h(x) = x(a - x)/2\) on \([0, a]\). The function is piecewise continuously differentiable and \(h(0) = h(a) = 0\), so by lemma 13.10 \(h = \sum_{\mathrm{m}} h_{\mathrm{m}}\sin(m\pi x/a)\) uniformly, with \(h_{\mathrm{m}} = \frac{2}{a}\int_0^a h\sin\frac{m\pi x}{a}\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{4a^2}{\pi^3m^3}\) for odd \(m\) and \(0\) for even (SymPy).
  3. The series of derivatives \(\sum h_{\mathrm{m}}\frac{m\pi}{a}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{a} = \sum_{\mathrm{m}\ \text{odd}}\frac{4a}{\pi^2m^2}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{a}\) has terms of absolute value at most \(\frac{4a}{\pi^2m^2}\), and the series of sines \(\frac{4a^2}{\pi^3m^3}\), and both series of maxima converge, so by lemma 18.29 \(h'(x) = \frac{a - 2x}{2} = \sum_{\mathrm{m}\ \text{odd}}\frac{4a}{\pi^2m^2}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{a}\).
  4. For \(0 \le x_1 \le x_2 \le a\) we therefore have \(x_2 - x_1 = h'(x_1) - h'(x_2) = \sum_{\mathrm{m}\ \text{odd}}\frac{4a}{\pi^2m^2}\Big(\cos\frac{m\pi x_1}{a} - \cos\frac{m\pi x_2}{a}\Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{m}\ \text{odd}}\frac{4}{m\pi}\int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2}\sin\frac{m\pi x}{a}\,\mathrm{d}x\), because \(\int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2}\sin(m\pi x/a)\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{a}{m\pi}(\cos\frac{m\pi x_1}{a} - \cos\frac{m\pi x_2}{a})\). The same for \(y\) with \(b\).
  5. The product of two absolutely convergent series is a double series, and by theorem 4.9 \(\iint_{\mathrm{R}} s_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2}\sin\frac{m\pi x}{a}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\mathrm{y}_1}^{y_2}\sin\frac{n\pi y}{b}\,\mathrm{d}y\). Hence \(\sum q_{\mathrm{mn}}\iint_{\mathrm{R}} s_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A = q_0\big(\sum\frac{4}{m\pi}\int\sin\big)\big(\sum\frac{4}{n\pi}\int\sin\big) = q_0(x_2 - x_1)(y_2 - y_1) = \iint_{\mathrm{R}} q_0\,\mathrm{d}A\). This is (H2).
  6. Theorem 18.30 gives \(w = \sum q_{\mathrm{mn}}s_{\mathrm{mn}}/(D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2)\), which is equation (18.37), since \(\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 = \pi^4(m^2/a^2 + n^2/b^2)^2\) and \(16q_0/(\pi^2mn)\cdot 1/\pi^4 = 16q_0/(\pi^6mn)\). At the centre \(s_{\mathrm{mn}} = \sin(m\pi/2)\sin(n\pi/2) = \pm 1\). The sum with \(a = b\), computed up to \(N = 399\) (NumPy), gives \(w_{\mathrm{c}} = 0{,}004062\,q_0a^4/D\) and \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 0{,}04789\,q_0a^2\) with \(\nu = 0{,}3\).

Source: EP section 7.2.4 (printed p. 437, table 7.2.3) gives the coefficients for the uniform load, example 7.2.9 (pp. 441-442) and table 9.5.1 (p. 628) the same values \(0{,}4062\cdot 10^{-2}\) and \(0{,}4789\cdot 10^{-1}\) for the square at \(\nu = 0{,}3\). The proof of (H2) by the series for \(h(x) = x(a - x)/2\) is ours.

Let us test the corollary on the steel plate from the beginning. At \(q = 10\) kPa, \(q\,a^4/D = 10^4\cdot 0{,}5^4/17\,948{,}7 = 0{,}03482\) m, so the deflection at the centre is \(w_{\mathrm{c}} = 0{,}004062\cdot 0{,}03482\) m \(= 0{,}1415\) mm. This is \(w_{\mathrm{c}}/h = 0{,}014\) of the thickness. The moment is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 0{,}04789\cdot 10^4\cdot 0{,}25 = 119{,}72\) N m/m and the stress at the surface \(\sigma = 6M_{\mathrm{x}}/h^2 = 7{,}18\) MPa (corollary 18.8). The assumption \(w \ll h\) about the smallness of the deflection is needed because for a deflection comparable to the thickness the middle plane stretches: the stretch is of order \((w/a)^2\), the bending strain at the surface is of order \(h\,w/a^2\), the ratio of the two is of order \(w/h\), here \(0{,}014\), so the stretch is of the order of one percent of the bending. For deflections comparable to \(h\) only the theory of large deflections helps, which section 18.10 merely names.

A uniform load on a simply supported plate gives more than deflection and moment. The corners show something that the beam does not have.

Corollary 18.32 Corner forces of the simply supported square (vogalne sile prosto podprtega kvadrata)

Under a uniform load \(q_0\) of a simply supported square with side \(a\), every corner carries the force \(R = 2(1 - \nu)\,D\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0)\), and

\[R = \frac{32(1 - \nu)}{\pi^4}\,q_0a^2\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n}\ \text{odd}}\frac{1}{(m^2 + n^2)^2} = 0{,}06496\,q_0a^2 . \tag{18.38}\]

The support must pull it down. At \(q_0 = 10\) kPa and \(a = 500\) mm, \(R = 162{,}4\) N, the four corners together \(649{,}6\) N, which is \(26{,}0\) % of the total load \(q_0a^2 = 2500\) N.

Proof

We must show the value of \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) in the corner, then the force by the corollary on the corner force and the equilibrium of the whole plate.

  1. By theorem 18.30, \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is the sum of a series with terms \(W_{\mathrm{mn}}\frac{m\pi}{a}\frac{n\pi}{b}\cos\frac{m\pi x}{a}\cos\frac{n\pi y}{b}\). In the corner \((0, 0)\) both cosines are \(1\) and all terms are positive. For \(a = b\) and \(q_{\mathrm{mn}} = 16q_0/(\pi^2mn)\) the term is \(\frac{16q_0}{\pi^2 mn D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2mn}{a^2} = \frac{16q_0}{D\,a^2\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2}\) with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}} = \pi^2(m^2 + n^2)/a^2\). The sum is \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0) = \frac{16q_0a^2}{\pi^4D}\sum\frac{1}{(m^2 + n^2)^2}\), and \(D\) cancels in \(R\).
  2. By corollary 18.22 the support holds the corner \((0, 0)\) with the force \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}}\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0)\) in the direction of the axis \(z\), that is upward. The outward normals of the edges \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) have \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = -1\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}} = -1\), so \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = 2M_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0)\). By formula (18.35), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0) = -D(1 - \nu)w_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0)\), and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0) > 0\) by step 1. So \(F_{\mathrm{c}} = -2(1 - \nu)Dw_{\mathrm{xy}}(0, 0) = -R\): a force of magnitude \(R\) pointing down. Hence formula (18.38). The series \(\sum(m^2 + n^2)^{-2}\) over odd \(m\), \(n\) has the sum \(0{,}2825\) (NumPy up to \(N = 3999\)), so \(R = 32\cdot 0{,}7/\pi^4\cdot 0{,}2825\,q_0a^2 = 0{,}06496\,q_0a^2\).
  3. The check is the equilibrium of vertical forces of the whole plate. The load is \(q_0a^2\) downward. By the same corollary the support holds the edge \(x = 0\) (\(n_{\mathrm{x}} = -1\)) with the force \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = M_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} + 2M_{\mathrm{xy},\mathrm{y}}\) per unit length in the direction of the axis \(z\) (definition 18.19), with our moments \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = -D\,[w_{\mathrm{xxx}} + (2 - \nu)w_{\mathrm{xyy}}]\), and by theorem 18.30 this is \(D\sum W_{\mathrm{mn}}\frac{m\pi}{a}\big[(m\pi/a)^2 + (2 - \nu)(n\pi/a)^2\big]\sin\frac{n\pi y}{a}\). Its integral along the edge is \(0{,}3149\,q_0a^2\) (NumPy, \(N = 3999\)), over the four edges \(1{,}2597\,q_0a^2\), and this is \(q_0a^2 + 4R = 1{,}2599\,q_0a^2\) (the difference \(0{,}0002\) is the remainder of the truncated series), as equation (18.29) requires with the load \(-q_0\) in the direction of the axis \(z\). The sum is positive, so the support pushes the edge up. The edges together carry more than the load, and the corners pull down: if the corners were free, they would lift.

Source: EP section 7.2.4 (printed pp. 439-440) gives the reactions \(V_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(V_{\mathrm{y}}\) and the corner force \(2M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) of the same forms. The value \(0{,}0650\,q_0a^2\) for the square and the equilibrium are ours, checked with NumPy.

A force must be seen. Let us look at the edge \(x = 0\) from the side.

The edge is drawn as a horizontal line, of length \(a\). Under a uniform load the support pushes it up, so the arrows end on the edge. We divide the edge into ten pieces of length \(a/10\) and each piece carries its own force, the integral of the reaction \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) over the piece. The force is largest at the middle of the edge (\(0{,}0417\,q_0a^2\)) and smallest at the corner (\(0{,}0131\,q_0a^2\)): the load near the corner is shared by the two edges that meet there, while the corner force carries none of it, since it pulls down like the load. If each edge carried only a quarter of the load, the force per piece would be \(0{,}025\,q_0a^2\), the middle carries \(1{,}7\) times more. In every corner the support pulls down with the force \(0{,}0650\,q_0a^2\), the arrow starts in the corner. By corollary 18.22 this force is the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) along the edge, replaced by pairs of vertical forces (section 18.3).

So far all examples satisfied (H1). Navier tested one more case, a load concentrated at a point.

Lemma 18.33 Point load at the centre of the square: the series of the moment diverges (točkovna sila v sredini kvadrata: vrsta za moment ne konvergira)

Let the simply supported square \([0, a]^2\) carry a force \(P\) at the centre, which is the limit of a load spread over an ever smaller circle. Its coefficients are \(q_{\mathrm{mn}} = \frac{4P}{a^2}\sin\frac{m\pi}{2}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\), so \(|q_{\mathrm{mn}}| = 4P/a^2\) for odd \(m\), \(n\), which does not satisfy (H1). Then:

  1. The series for \(w\) in equation (18.33) converges uniformly, \(w\) is continuous and \(w_{\mathrm{c}} = 0{,}011601\,Pa^2/D\).
  2. The partial sums \(\sum_{\mathrm{m},\mathrm{n} \le \mathrm{N}}\) of the series for \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) at the centre are positive and grow beyond every bound as \(N \to \infty\).
Proof

We must show an estimate for \(w\) and a lower bound for the sum of the moment by blocks.

  1. For \(w\) the term is \(|W_{\mathrm{mn}}| = 4P/(a^2D\lambda_{\mathrm{mn}}^2) = 4Pa^2/(\pi^4D(m^2 + n^2)^2) \le 4Pa^2/(4\pi^4Dm^2n^2)\) because \((m^2 + n^2)^2 \ge 4m^2n^2\). The sum \(\sum(mn)^{-2}\) is finite, and by theorem 9.85 the series converges uniformly, its sum is continuous (step 2 of lemma 18.29). The sum at the centre is \(0{,}011601\,Pa^2/D\) (NumPy up to \(N = 1199\)).
  2. At the centre \(s_{\mathrm{mn}} = \pm1\), so the term of the series for \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) by formula (18.35) for odd \(m\), \(n\) is \(\frac{4P}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{m^2 + \nu n^2}{(m^2 + n^2)^2}\). The sign is plus because \(W_{\mathrm{mn}}\) has the sign of \(q_{\mathrm{mn}}\), that is \(\sin\frac{m\pi}{2}\sin\frac{n\pi}{2}\), and at the centre it is multiplied by \(s_{\mathrm{mn}}\) with the same sign. So the partial sums grow with \(N\). The indices \(m, n \le N\) form a square that the swap \(m \leftrightarrow n\) maps onto itself, so the sum with the numerator \(n^2 + \nu m^2\) has the same value. The average of both gives the partial sum \(\frac{2(1 + \nu)P}{\pi^2}\sum\frac{1}{m^2 + n^2}\) over odd \(m, n \le N\) (NumPy at \(N = 41\) gives \(0{,}4406\,P\) both ways).
  3. For \(k \ge 1\) let the block \(B_{\mathrm{k}}\) contain the odd \(m\), \(n\) from \((2^k, 2^{k+1}]\). There are \(2^{k-1}\) odd numbers in \((2^k, 2^{k+1}]\), so \(4^{k-1}\) pairs, and each has \(m^2 + n^2 \le 2\cdot 4^{k+1}\) and \(\frac{1}{m^2 + n^2} \ge \frac{1}{2\cdot4^{k+1}}\). Every block contributes to the sum \(\sum 1/(m^2 + n^2)\) at least \(\frac{4^{k-1}}{2\cdot 4^{k+1}} = \frac{1}{32}\). The blocks are disjoint and all in \(m, n \le 2^K\) for \(k < K\), so the partial sum of the moment at \(N = 2^K\) is at least \(\frac{2(1 + \nu)P}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{K - 1}{32} = (K - 1)\,\frac{(1 + \nu)P}{16\pi^2}\), which tends to infinity, also at \(\nu = 0\).

Source: VK section 3.4 (PDF p. 61) says that the series for moments, shear forces and stresses under a point load diverge at the singular point. Navier treated this case (TIMO PDF p. 130). The proof by blocks is ours.

The numbers show how slowly. At \(a = 500\) mm the partial sums of \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) at \(N = 11\), \(41\), \(161\), \(641\) are \(0{,}311\,P\), \(0{,}441\,P\), \(0{,}580\,P\), \(0{,}723\,P\). Every fourfold increase of \(N\) adds about \(0{,}13\) to \(0{,}14\,P\), that is \(0{,}093\), \(0{,}101\) and \(0{,}103\,P\) per \(\ln 4\), and the coefficient \((1 + \nu)/(4\pi) = 0{,}1035\) from section 18.6, where we treat the point load on the circular plate, has the same growth. The moment therefore grows as \(\ln N\). The deflection, on the other hand, converges fast: the partial sum at \(N = 3\) is \(0{,}967\) of the limit, at \(N = 11\) \(0{,}996\). Let us look at the right part of figure 18.15. On the right axis is again \(N\) on a logarithmic scale, vertically \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) in the unit \(P\). The points lie on an almost straight line, every multiple of four of \(N\) raises them by the same step, and the line does not reach the horizontal.

Why does the plate itself not have this? A series with \(N\) terms distinguishes positions at a distance of about \(a/N\): at \(N = 41\) this is \(12\) mm, at \(N = 161\) \(3\) mm. A real plate with \(h = 10\) mm is under the Kirchhoff assumptions only where \(w\) changes over lengths much larger than \(h\). A point load is an idealization of a force acting on a small area, and the plate theory under it does not hold at a distance of a few \(h\), because of \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\), which it neglects, and because of the straight normals. At \(h = 10\) mm about \(N \approx 10\) terms make sense (\(a/N = 50\) mm, that is \(5h\)), at which the partial sum lies between \(0{,}29\,P\) (\(N = 9\)) and \(0{,}31\,P\) (\(N = 11\)). A sum with more terms is a pretty mathematical object, not a prediction of stress. The deflection is therefore a good result of a point load, the moment only at a distance from the force.

Navier thus solved both cases correctly, but the second demanded more than the theorem: the deflection at the point of the load is finite, the moment under the force is infinite for an ideal force. We shall find the same \(\ln\) for the circular plate (section 18.6), where we can sum it in closed form.

If only two opposite edges are simply supported, for example \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\), Navier's series does not fit the other two edges, because \(\sin(n\pi y/b)\) cannot satisfy a clamped edge \(w_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\). A solution with a single series, in which the factor in \(y\) has a form adapted to the boundary condition, is Lévy's solution (EP section 7.2.5, printed pp. 449-454, VK section 3.5, PDF p. 65, where the year is 1900). We do not prove this solution and do not need it in this book.

What we have shown. We expanded the deflection and the load into products of sines, which satisfy the boundary conditions of the simply supported rectangle by themselves and are eigenfunctions of \(\nabla^4\) (lemma 18.28), so every term solves the equation for its part of the load. That the sum solves the equation as the equilibrium of every rectangular piece (theorem 18.30) we proved by estimating the terms, which guarantees uniform convergence of derivatives up to third order if the coefficients of the load do not decay more slowly than \(1/(mn)\). For the uniform load we proved this to the end (corollary 18.31) and computed the deflection, moment and stress of the steel plate, the corner forces (corollary 18.32) and the test against the strip from section 18.4. For the point load the hypothesis fails, and with it the series for the moment (lemma 18.33). Double sines will come once more in section 18.7, where they will be the modes of vibration of the simply supported plate, and the theorem on term by term differentiation will be needed there again.
