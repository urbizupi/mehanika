Chapter 21 Modal reduction and eigenvalue solvers

21.2 Effective modal mass (efektivna modalna masa)

Section 21.1 answered how much of the response to a force acting on one coordinate is lost when modes are cut off. Here we ask the same question for a load that is not a force on a coordinate but a motion of the support: an earthquake that shakes a building, ground vibration that reaches a machine. Such a load acts on every mass at once, so the answer is different. Which modes receive anything from it at all, and how many modes must we keep to describe the structure under this load? The section shows that every mode receives a "share of mass", that all the shares together give the mass of the structure (theorem 21.9), and that this share is the measure for the number of modes to keep (GR section 2.9.5, PDF p. 128). The measure is proved only for slow motion of the support. At the end of the section two examples show where it stops holding and why. The section runs in order: first the equation of motion in coordinates relative to the support (theorem 21.5), then the participation factor (definition 21.6), the effective modal mass (definition 21.8) with its sum (theorem 21.9), the force on the support (corollary 21.10) and the limits of the measure. The additional mass method, which solves the same task without partitioning the coordinates, is the subject of section 21.3.

21.2.1 The support moves (podlaga se giblje)

The first model is the frame with three floors of chapter 10: each floor has mass \(m = 500\) kg, each storey stiffness \(k = 501\,760\) N/m, the floors move only horizontally and there is no damping. The natural frequencies of the frame with a fixed support are \(f_{\mathrm{r}} = 2{,}2438\), \(6{,}2870\) and \(9{,}0850\) Hz, the natural circular frequencies \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} = 14{,}098\), \(39{,}502\) and \(57{,}082\) 1/s. The second model is bar C of section 21.1 (steel, \(A = 100\) mm², \(L = 1\) m, sixteen equal elements, clamped at \(x = 0\)), whose mass is \(m_{\mathrm{T}} = \rho A L = 0{,}78\) kg and whose first natural circular frequency is \(\omega_1 = 7877{,}26\) 1/s. The support is the ground for the frame and the clamped node for the bar. Both supports move as a rigid body with prescribed displacement \(u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\) and acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\).

We build the figure part by part. The frame stands on the ground, which the support moves to the right by the displacement \(u_{\mathrm{g}}\) and gives the acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\). The original position of the axis of the ground is dashed, the motion is measured by the distance \(u_{\mathrm{g}}\). If the frame were completely rigid, all floors would move by the same \(u_{\mathrm{g}}\) and stand on the dashed axis that moves with the support. The frame is flexible, so the floors and columns deform and the floors lag behind the axis. The displacement of the third floor relative to the ground is \(y_3\), its displacement from the original axis is \(q_3\), and \(q_3 = u_{\mathrm{g}} + y_3\). Since \(y_3\) is negative, \(q_3\) is smaller than \(u_{\mathrm{g}}\). What pushes the floors? For an observer at rest with respect to the ground, each floor seems to carry an inertia force \(-m\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) against the acceleration of the ground, equal on all floors because they have equal masses.

Now the same with matrices. We split the coordinates into a column \(\boldsymbol q_1\) of structure coordinates (three floor displacements for the frame, the displacements of the sixteen free nodes for the bar) and one support displacement \(q_2\) (the ground for the frame, the clamped node for the bar). Section 21.3 will use the same partition. The mass and stiffness matrices are

\[\mat M = \begin{bmatrix} \mat M_{11} & \mat M_{12} \\ \mat M_{21} & M_{22} \end{bmatrix} , \qquad \mat K = \begin{bmatrix} \mat K_{11} & \mat K_{12} \\ \mat K_{21} & K_{22} \end{bmatrix} , \tag{21.7}\]

with \(\mat M_{21} = \mat M_{12}^\T\) and \(\mat K_{21} = \mat K_{12}^\T\). The frame has \(\mat M_{11} = m\,\mat I\), \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\), \(M_{22} = 0\) (we do not count the mass of the ground in the structure), \(\mat K_{12} = (-k, 0, 0)^\T\) and \(K_{22} = k\), with

\[\mat K_{11} = k\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 \\ 0 & -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} . \tag{21.8}\]

For the bar \(M_{22} = m_{\mathrm{T}}/48\) (the diagonal entry \(\rho A h/3\) of the consistent mass matrix of the element at the wall, with the element length \(h = L/16\)) and \(\mat M_{12}\) has the single nonzero entry \(m_{\mathrm{T}}/96\) in the row of the first free node, because the consistent mass matrix (lemma 10.31) couples neighbouring nodes. This coupling will matter: for the frame \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\), for the bar it is not.

The motion of the support is a rigid motion. A displacement of the support by \(1\) moves all structure coordinates by a column \(\vek s\), and the whole column \(\vek r = (\vek s,\ 1)\) has no strain, hence no elastic forces: \(\mat K\,\vek r = \vek 0\), that is

\[\mat K_{11}\,\vek s + \mat K_{12} = \vek 0 , \qquad \mat K_{21}\,\vek s + K_{22} = 0 . \tag{21.9}\]

The frame has \(\vek s = (1, 1, 1)^\T\), the bar \(\vek s = (1, \dots, 1)^\T\), and we check both conditions with the matrices (frame: \(k\,(2 - 1,\ -1 + 2 - 1,\ -1 + 1) + (-k, 0, 0) = \vek 0\), bar: num-02.py, largest deviation \(0\)). In every system where the support holds the structure with a prescribed rigid motion, condition (21.9) holds, because a rigid motion creates no strain energy (theorem 10.23, item 2).

Theorem 21.5 Equation of motion relative to the support (gibalna enačba glede na podlago)

Let \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) be symmetric matrices, partitioned as in (21.7), with \(\mat M_{11}\) and \(\mat K_{11}\) positive definite (definition 6.27), and let (21.9) hold. Let \(q_2(t) = u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\) be a twice continuously differentiable prescribed displacement of the support and \(\vek q_1(t)\) a twice differentiable column of structure displacements. Let no other load act on the structure, that is \(\vek F = (\vek 0,\ F_2)\) in the system \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek F\) (theorem 10.2 without damping) with \(\vek q = (\vek q_1,\ q_2)\). Put

\[\vek h = \mat M_{11}\,\vek s + \mat M_{12} , \qquad \boldsymbol y = \vek q_1 - \vek s\,u_{\mathrm{g}} . \tag{21.10}\]

We call \(\vek h\) the inertia column (vztrajnostni stolpec), and \(\boldsymbol y\) is the displacement of the structure relative to the support. Then \(\vek q\) satisfies the structure rows of this system exactly when \(\boldsymbol y\) satisfies

\[\mat M_{11}\,\ddot{\boldsymbol y} + \mat K_{11}\,\boldsymbol y = -\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} . \tag{21.11}\]

For the frame \(\vek h = m\,\vek s = (m, m, m)^\T\), so each floor carries the load \(-m\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\), as in figure 21.5.

Proof

We must show that the first row of the system turns into (21.11) under the substitution \(\vek q_1 = \boldsymbol y + \vek s\,u_{\mathrm{g}}\). The substitution is invertible, so the converse holds too.

  1. The first row of the system, without load, is \(\mat M_{11}\ddot{\vek q}_1 + \mat M_{12}\,\ddot q_2 + \mat K_{11}\vek q_1 + \mat K_{12}\,q_2 = \vek 0\).
  2. Insert \(\vek q_1 = \boldsymbol y + \vek s\,u_{\mathrm{g}}\) and \(q_2 = u_{\mathrm{g}}\) (the column \(\vek s\) is constant, so \(\ddot{\vek q}_1 = \ddot{\boldsymbol y} + \vek s\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\)): \(\mat M_{11}\ddot{\boldsymbol y} + \mat K_{11}\boldsymbol y + (\mat M_{11}\vek s + \mat M_{12})\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} + (\mat K_{11}\vek s + \mat K_{12})\,u_{\mathrm{g}} = \vek 0\).
  3. By the first condition (21.9) the last term is zero, and \(\mat M_{11}\vek s + \mat M_{12} = \vek h\) is (21.10). What remains is (21.11).
  4. For the frame \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(\mat M_{11} = m\,\mat I\), so \(\vek h = m\,\vek s\).

Source: The partition into structure and support and the load by inertia forces follow GR section 2.9.4 (PDF p. 126, text read, equations (2.179)-(2.183) are images). The theorem and the proof are ours, the derivation is a direct consequence of theorem 10.2.

The column \(\vek h\) has an interpretation that we need later. If the whole system moves with a rigid acceleration \(1\) (the column \(\vek r\)), the forces that cause this acceleration are \(\mat M\,\vek r\), and the inertia forces are \(-\mat M\,\vek r\). The upper block of the column \(\mat M\,\vek r\), the rows of the structure, is \(\mat M_{11}\vek s + \mat M_{12} = \vek h\). So \(\vek h\) is the column of forces with which we would have to hold the structure if we moved it rigidly with acceleration \(1\). For bar C, \(h_{\mathrm{i}}\) is therefore the sum of row \(i\) of the full mass matrix, which is \(\rho A h\) at every node except the last, where it is \(\rho A h/2\). Motion of the support is thus, for the structure, the same as the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) with a fixed support. Viscous damping would add a term \(\mat C_{11}\dot{\boldsymbol y}\) if \(\mat C\,\vek r = \vek 0\), that is if the dampers do not feel the rigid motion (theorem 10.2). Damping is the subject of chapter 20.

21.2.2 The participation factor (faktor udeležbe)

Equation (21.11) is the equation of theorem 10.2 with the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\), so we decouple it with the modes of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\) (modal matrix \(\mat\Phi\), definition 10.15). The load on mode \(r\) is its projection on that mode.

Definition 21.6 Participation factor (faktor udeležbe)

Let \(\mat K_{11}\), \(\mat M_{11}\) and \(\vek h\) be as in theorem 21.5 and let \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\), \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) (\(r = 1, \dots, n_1\)) be the eigenpairs of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\), \(\mat M_{11}\)-normalized (theorem 10.13). The participation factor of mode \(r\) is the number

\[\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} = \boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\,\vek h . \tag{21.12}\]

Source: GR (2.186), PDF p. 127, the equation is an image. The name is GR's ("modal participation factors", PDF p. 127). The choice of \(\vek h\) instead of \(\mat M_{11}\vek s\) and the symbol \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) for our partition are ours.

The name is literal: \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) tells how strongly the support motion excites mode \(r\). Since \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is normalized by \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M_{11}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\) and \(\vek h\) is in kg, \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) has the unit kg\(^{1/2}\). Its sign depends on the sign we chose for the mode, since \(-\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) serves equally well. The symbol \(\Gamma\) here means the participation factor and has nothing to do with the broken line \(\Gamma\) of chapter 4, and \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is a mode here, not the nodal rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) of chapter 12.

Lemma 21.7 Modal equation under support motion (modalna enačba pri gibanju podlage)

Let the hypotheses of theorem 21.5 hold, \(\mat\Phi\) the modal matrix of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\), \(\boldsymbol\eta = \mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\,\boldsymbol y\) the modal coordinates relative to the support (definition 10.19) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) as in (21.12). Then for \(r = 1, \dots, n_1\)

\[\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} , \qquad \boldsymbol y = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n_1}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} . \tag{21.13}\]
Proof

We must show that the decoupling of theorem 10.2 also holds for the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) and that the right-hand side of the \(r\)-th equation is \(-\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\).

  1. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat\Phi^{-1} = \mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\), so \(\boldsymbol y = \mat\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\) (expansion theorem, corollary 10.14).
  2. Insert \(\boldsymbol y = \mat\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta\) in (21.11) and multiply from the left by \(\mat\Phi^\T\): \(\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\mat\Phi\,\ddot{\boldsymbol\eta} + \mat\Phi^\T\mat K_{11}\mat\Phi\,\boldsymbol\eta = -\mat\Phi^\T\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\).
  3. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\mat\Phi = \mat I\) and \(\mat\Phi^\T\mat K_{11}\mat\Phi = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}})\), and the \(r\)-th component of the column \(\mat\Phi^\T\vek h\) is \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek h = \Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\). Row \(r\) is (21.13).

Source: The proof is not from a source. It is the same step as in the proof of the decoupling theorem (theorem 10.2 and lemma 10.16), with the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\).

So each mode is a one-degree-of-freedom equation driven by the ground acceleration multiplied by \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\). A mode with \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) does not feel the support motion at all. For the frame the modes, normalized by \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M_{11}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\), are in kg\(^{-1/2}\):

\(r\) \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r},1}\) \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r},2}\) \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r},3}\) \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) (kg\(^{1/2}\))
1 \(0{,}014668\) \(0{,}026431\) \(0{,}032959\) \(37{,}0286\)
2 \(-0{,}032959\) \(-0{,}014668\) \(0{,}026431\) \(-10{,}5979\)
3 \(-0{,}026431\) \(0{,}032959\) \(-0{,}014668\) \(-4{,}0700\)

The first mode has all three components of the same sign, so the uniform load \(-m\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) excites it with all floors at once. The second and third modes have components of different signs, which mostly cancel in the sum \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} = m\,(\varphi_{\mathrm{r},1} + \varphi_{\mathrm{r},2} + \varphi_{\mathrm{r},3})\) with \(m = 500\) kg. The computation is in num-02.py.

21.2.3 The effective modal mass

How much of the mass of the structure does a single mode cover? To answer we need a number with the unit of mass that does not depend on the choice of sign and scale of the mode. The participation factor \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) has the unit kg\(^{1/2}\) and depends on the sign, but its square has the unit kg.

Definition 21.8 Effective modal mass (efektivna modalna masa)

Let \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) be the participation factors (definition 21.6). The effective modal mass of mode \(r\) is

\[m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = \Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2 = \big(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek h\big)^2 . \tag{21.14}\]

Source: GR (2.187)-(2.191), PDF pp. 127-128, the notion is GR's ("effective modal mass", Imbert 1984, Preumont 1994). The expression \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) is our definition, GR writes the sum over modes.

The name is so because \(m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\) tells how much mass mode \(r\) actually contributes ("effectively") to the reaction of the support in slow motion. This is shown by corollary 21.10 (item 3): the support force per unit ground acceleration, computed with modes \(1\) to \(N\) only, tends to the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\) as the support oscillates more and more slowly, and for some structures the mass felt by the support itself is added to it (theorem 21.9). The sign of the mode disappears, since \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2 \ge 0\) and does not change under \(-\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\). If the mode is not normalized by \(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M_{11}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\), the mass stays the same in the form \((\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek h)^2/(\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M_{11}\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}})\). For the frame \(m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = 1371{,}12\), \(112{,}32\) and \(16{,}57\) kg, that is \(91{,}41\), \(7{,}49\) and \(1{,}10\) % of the mass \(m_\Sigma = 3m = 1500\) kg (figure 21.6, bars on the left).

Why would the sum of these numbers give the mass of the structure? By the expansion in modes every displacement of the structure is a sum of modes, and if none is cut off, no mass can stay unaccounted for. The exact statement is the next theorem, in which one more quantity appears, the one felt by the support itself.

Theorem 21.9 Sum of effective modal masses (vsota efektivnih modalnih mas)

Let the hypotheses of theorem 21.5 hold and \(\mat M\) be positive semidefinite. Put the total mass \(m_\Sigma = \vek r^\T\mat M\,\vek r\) and the mass of the support

\[m_{\mathrm{S}} = M_{22} - \mat M_{21}\,\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12} . \tag{21.15}\]

Then:

  1. \(m_{\mathrm{S}} \ge 0\), and \(m_{\mathrm{S}} = 0\) when \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(M_{22} = 0\).
  2. The sum over all modes is
    \[\sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n_1} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = \vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h = m_\Sigma - m_{\mathrm{S}} . \tag{21.16}\]
Proof

We must show that the sum of the squares of the participation factors equals \(\vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h\) and that this expression can be written with \(m_\Sigma\) and \(m_{\mathrm{S}}\).

  1. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat\Phi^{-1} = \mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\), so \(\mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11} = \mat I\) and \(\mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T = \mat M_{11}^{-1}\) (multiply by \(\mat M_{11}^{-1}\) from the right).
  2. The sum is \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} \Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2 = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}\vek h^\T\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek h = \vek h^\T\,\mat\Phi\mat\Phi^\T\,\vek h = \vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h\) by step 1.
  3. Insert \(\vek h = \mat M_{11}\vek s + \mat M_{12}\) into \(\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h = \vek s + \mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12}\). So \(\vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h = \vek h^\T\vek s + \vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12}\), and the last term is \((\vek s^\T\mat M_{11} + \mat M_{21})\,\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12} = \vek s^\T\mat M_{12} + \mat M_{21}\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12}\).
  4. The total mass is \(m_\Sigma = \vek r^\T\mat M\,\vek r = \vek s^\T\mat M_{11}\vek s + \vek s^\T\mat M_{12} + \mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22} = \vek s^\T\vek h + \mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22}\), so \(\vek s^\T\vek h = m_\Sigma - \mat M_{21}\vek s - M_{22}\).
  5. Insert step 4 in step 3 and use \(\vek s^\T\mat M_{12} = \mat M_{21}\vek s\) (symmetry): \(\vek h^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h = m_\Sigma - \mat M_{21}\vek s - M_{22} + \mat M_{21}\vek s + \mat M_{21}\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12} = m_\Sigma - m_{\mathrm{S}}\). This is item 2.
  6. Let \(\vek v = (-\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12},\ 1)\). Expanding, \(\vek v^\T\mat M\,\vek v = \mat M_{21}\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12} - 2\,\mat M_{21}\mat M_{11}^{-1}\mat M_{12} + M_{22} = m_{\mathrm{S}}\). Since \(\mat M\) is positive semidefinite, \(m_{\mathrm{S}} \ge 0\). For \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(M_{22} = 0\) equation (21.15) gives \(m_{\mathrm{S}} = 0\). This is item 1.

Source: The sum over modes and the notion \(m_{\mathrm{S}}\) ("the part of the structural mass attached to the support") follow GR section 2.9.5 (PDF pp. 127-128, text read, (2.188)-(2.193) are images). GR neglects the last term of its equation (2.189) as second order in \(\mat M_{12}\). Here that term is kept and the sum is exact. The proof is ours.

The sum (21.16) is simple: if all modes are kept, the effective masses add up to the mass of the structure, except the mass of the support. For the frame \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(M_{22} = 0\), so \(m_{\mathrm{S}} = 0\) and the sum is exactly \(m_\Sigma = 1500\) kg: \(1371{,}1192 + 112{,}3155 + 16{,}5653 = 1500{,}0000\) kg (num-02.py).

We read the figure from the left. The vertical axis shows percent of the total mass, one grey horizontal line is at \(90\) %, the other at \(100\) %. On the left is the frame, its horizontal axis counts the modes \(r = 1, 2, 3\). The three bars are the shares \(91{,}4\), \(7{,}5\) and \(1{,}1\) %, and the cumulative sum (\(91{,}4\), \(98{,}9\), \(100{,}0\) %) reaches \(100\) % at the third mode. One mode already covers \(91{,}4\) %, which exceeds the lower limit of the measure (\(90\) %), on the measure at the end of the section. On the right is bar C with modes \(1\) to \(16\) on its own horizontal axis. Its sixteen bars fall quickly: the first mode \(81{,}06\) % of the mass \(m_{\mathrm{T}}\), the second \(9{,}01\), the third \(3{,}24\), the fourth \(1{,}65\) and the fifth \(0{,}99\) %, cumulatively \(81{,}06\), \(90{,}06\), \(93{,}30\), \(94{,}95\) and \(95{,}95\) %. The first ten modes give \(97{,}85\) %. The sum of all sixteen is \(98{,}20\) %, the orange dashed line, and the cumulative sum does not exceed this line however many modes we take.

Why does the bar not stop at \(100\) %? This is the first near miss of the theorem. The bar has a consistent mass matrix, in which the clamped node has the diagonal entry \(M_{22} = m_{\mathrm{T}}/48 = 2{,}08\) % of the mass and is coupled with the mass element of the first free node. The computation by (21.15) gives \(m_{\mathrm{S}}/m_{\mathrm{T}} = 0{,}018042\), which by theorem 21.9 is exactly the difference \(100 - 98{,}196 = 1{,}804\) %, the gap between the orange dashed line and the \(100\) % line. This much mass is felt by the support itself: the support has a prescribed acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) whatever the structure does, and this part of the mass enters no modal equation (21.13). What would we get if we left out \(\mat M_{12}\)? With \(\vek h' = \mat M_{11}\vek s\) (inertia forces from the motion of the structure only, without the coupling to the support) we would get \(80{,}80\) % for the first mode (instead of \(81{,}06\) %) and the sum \(95{,}83\) % (instead of \(98{,}20\) %). Step 2 of the proof of the theorem explains the difference: the sum is then \(\vek h'^\T\mat M_{11}^{-1}\vek h' = \vek s^\T\mat M_{11}\vek s = 0{,}9583\,m_{\mathrm{T}}\), the mass of the free nodes without the coupling to the support, and not \(m_\Sigma - m_{\mathrm{S}} = 0{,}9820\,m_{\mathrm{T}}\). For the frame with point masses there is no such error, since \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\).

We can compare the values for the bar with the continuous bar (theorem 13.18, case (b)): its modes are \(\sin k_{\mathrm{r}} x\) with \(k_{\mathrm{r}} = (2r - 1)\pi/(2L)\) and \(\rho A\int_0^L \sin^2 k_{\mathrm{r}} x\,\mathrm dx = \rho A L/2\), so the normalized mode is \(\sqrt{2/(\rho A L)}\,\sin k_{\mathrm{r}} x\) and the analogous participation factor is \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} = \rho A\int_0^L\sqrt{2/(\rho AL)}\sin k_{\mathrm{r}} x\,\mathrm dx = \sqrt{2\rho A/L}\,/k_{\mathrm{r}}\) (since \(\cos k_{\mathrm{r}} L = 0\)). The effective mass is \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2 = 8\rho A L/((2r-1)^2\pi^2)\). For \(r = 1, \dots, 5\) this is \(0{,}81057\), \(0{,}09006\), \(0{,}03242\), \(0{,}01654\) and \(0{,}01001\) of the mass \(m_{\mathrm{T}}\), and bar C has \(0{,}81057\), \(0{,}09006\), \(0{,}03240\), \(0{,}01650\) and \(0{,}00994\). The first two agree to five decimal places, the third differs by \(0{,}06\) %, the fourth by \(0{,}23\) % and the fifth by \(0{,}64\) %. This is a comparison of two computations and not a proof that the discrete value converges to the continuous one.

21.2.4 The force on the support (sila na podlago)

The effective mass tells how much mass the kept modes cover when the support moves slowly. What about faster motion? The answer is in the force that the structure demands from the support. This force matters in itself, since by Newton's third law it is the opposite of the force with which the structure loads the support (for a building, the foundation). Let \(F\) be the force that the support must give to the structure, that is the support row of the system \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek F\), so \(F = \mat M_{21}\ddot{\vek q}_1 + M_{22}\,\ddot q_2 + \mat K_{21}\vek q_1 + K_{22}\,q_2\). For a harmonic ground acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} = \mathrm{Re}(A\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\Omega t})\) this force is \(\mathrm{Re}\big(R(\Omega)\,A\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\Omega t}\big)\) with a number \(R(\Omega)\) in kg, which we get as follows.

Corollary 21.10 Support force by modes (sila na podlago po načinih)

Let the hypotheses of theorem 21.5 hold, \(\mat M\) positive semidefinite, \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} = \mathrm{Re}(A\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\Omega t})\), \(\Omega > 0\) and \(\Omega^2 \ne \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\) for all \(r\). Let the solution have amplitude \(\boldsymbol Q_1\) on the structure and let \(F = \mathrm{Re}(R\,A\,\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\Omega t})\) be the support force. Then:

  1. The support force is
    \[R(\Omega) = m_\Sigma + \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n_1} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\frac{\Omega^2}{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2} = m_{\mathrm{S}} + \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n_1} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2} . \tag{21.17}\]
  2. Let \(N < n_1\). If we keep only modes \(1\) to \(N\), the two approximations are
    \[R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}} = m_{\mathrm{S}} + \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{N} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2} , \qquad R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}} = m_\Sigma + \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{N} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\frac{\Omega^2}{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2} , \tag{21.18}\]
    the first with the modes (and the mass of the support), the second with the modes and a static remainder, which we get with the total mass.
  3. As \(\Omega \to 0\), \(R \to m_\Sigma\) and \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}} \to m_\Sigma\), while \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}} \to m_{\mathrm{S}} + \sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = m_\Sigma - \mu_{\mathrm{N}}\) with the missing mass \(\mu_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r} > \mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\).
  4. If \(\Omega^2 < \lambda_{\mathrm{N}+1}\), then
    \[0 \le R - R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}} \le \frac{\Omega^2}{\lambda_{\mathrm{N}+1} - \Omega^2}\,\mu_{\mathrm{N}} , \qquad R - R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r} > \mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\frac{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2} \ge \mu_{\mathrm{N}} . \tag{21.19}\]
Proof

We must show that computing the force in the harmonic response gives (21.17), then truncate only the sum and estimate the remainder.

  1. The harmonic solution has \(q_2 = u_{\mathrm{g}}\), so the amplitude of the support displacement is \(U = -A/\Omega^2\), and \(\boldsymbol Q_1 = \boldsymbol Y + \vek s\,U\) with \(\boldsymbol Y\) the amplitude of the vector \(\boldsymbol y\). From (21.11), \((\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11})\,\boldsymbol Y = -\vek h\,A\). The matrix \(\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11}\) is invertible because \(\Omega^2\) is not an eigenvalue (step 6 below), so \(\boldsymbol Y = -\mat H\,\vek h\,A\) with \(\mat H = (\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11})^{-1}\).
  2. By the support row, the amplitude of the force is \(R\,A = (\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\boldsymbol Q_1 + (K_{22} - \Omega^2 M_{22})\,U\). Insert \(\boldsymbol Q_1\) and use \(\mat K_{21}\vek s + K_{22} = 0\) from (21.9): \(R\,A = (\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\boldsymbol Y - \Omega^2\,(\mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22})\,U\).
  3. By symmetry and the first condition (21.9), \(\mat K_{21} = -\vek s^\T\mat K_{11}\), and by (21.10), \(\mat M_{21} = \vek h^\T - \vek s^\T\mat M_{11}\). So \(\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21} = -\vek s^\T(\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11}) - \Omega^2\vek h^\T\).
  4. By steps 1 and 3, \((\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\boldsymbol Y = \vek s^\T\vek h\,A + \Omega^2\,\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h\,A\).
  5. With \(U = -A/\Omega^2\) and steps 2 and 4, \(R = \vek s^\T\vek h + \mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22} + \Omega^2\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h\). We expressed the first three terms by \(m_\Sigma\) in step 4 of the proof of theorem 21.9, so \(R = m_\Sigma + \Omega^2\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h\).
  6. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11} = \mat M_{11}\mat\Phi\,(\mat\Lambda - \Omega^2\mat I)\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\) (as in step 2 of the proof of lemma 10.50), the diagonal entries \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2\) are nonzero and \(\mat H = \mat\Phi\,(\mat\Lambda - \Omega^2\mat I)^{-1}\mat\Phi^\T\). Hence \(\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2)\) and the first expression of (21.17) follows.
  7. The second expression: \(\Omega^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2) = \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2) - 1\) and \(m_\Sigma - \sum_{\mathrm{r}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = m_{\mathrm{S}}\) (theorem 21.9). This is item 1.
  8. As \(\Omega \to 0\) every term \(\Omega^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2)\) of the sum in (21.17) tends to \(0\), so \(R \to m_\Sigma\) and \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}} \to m_\Sigma\). The term \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2) \to 1\), so \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}} \to m_{\mathrm{S}} + \sum_{\mathrm{r}\le \mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} = m_\Sigma - \mu_{\mathrm{N}}\) by theorem 21.9. This is item 3.
  9. By (21.17) and (21.18) the difference \(R - R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}}\) is the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}>\mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\Omega^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2)\), and for \(r > N\) we have \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} \ge \lambda_{\mathrm{N}+1} > \Omega^2\), so \(0 \le \Omega^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2) \le \Omega^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{N}+1} - \Omega^2)\). Multiplying by \(m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}} \ge 0\) and summing gives the first inequality of (21.19). The difference \(R - R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}}\) is the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}>\mathrm{N}} m_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathrm{ef}}\,\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2)\), and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2) \ge 1\) for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > \Omega^2\). This is item 4, and item 2 is a definition.

Source: The proof is ours. The form of the sum with the mass \(m_{\mathrm{S}}\) and with the mass completed to \(m_\Sigma\) follows GR section 2.9.4-2.9.5 (reactions on the support, PDF pp. 126-128, equations are images). The approximation \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}}\) is the same idea as the correction in theorem 21.4.

The approximation \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}}\) is nothing new: it is the same correction as in theorem 21.4, only that the static part of the cut-off modes is now simply the whole remaining mass \(\mu_{\mathrm{N}}\). The first approximation, \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}}\), needs the modes and \(m_{\mathrm{S}}\), the second only the modes and \(m_\Sigma\) (the total mass can be read from the mass matrix).

21.2.5 What the measure says and what it does not

From the sum (21.16) GR derives a measure for the number of modes: keep so many modes that their share of the total mass is at least \(P\), usually \(90\) to \(95\) % (GR section 2.9.5, PDF p. 128, equation (2.194) is an image). The measure is sensible because the missing mass \(\mu_{\mathrm{N}}\) is, by item 3 of corollary 21.10, exactly the error of the support force in slow motion if we only add up the modes. For the frame one mode suffices for \(91{,}41\) %, for bar C two give \(90{,}06\) % and five give \(95{,}95\) %. For bar C we can never reach \(99\) %, since \(m_{\mathrm{S}}/m_{\mathrm{T}} = 1{,}80\) % (theorem 21.9): the limit \(P\) must lie below \(98{,}20\) %.

The measure has two further restrictions. First, it holds for the direction of support motion for which it was computed (GR says so explicitly, PDF p. 128: for several directions the measure is applied in each). Second, it vouches only for slow motion, which the second near miss of this section shows: the frame with one mode meets \(90\) %, but at \(\Omega = 0{,}9\,\omega_2\) the error of the support force with \(R_1^{\mathrm{pop}}\) is \(-135\) % (figure 21.7). The measure answers the question of how much mass moves together with the support. It does not answer whether the frequencies of the load come close to the natural frequency of the first cut-off mode, since that requires comparing \(\Omega^2\) with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{N}+1}\) (bound (21.19)). So we check both.

In short: the motion of the support is, for the structure, the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) (theorem 21.5), mode \(r\) feels it with the factor \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}\) (definition 21.6) and contributes the effective mass \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) (definition 21.8), and the sum of all is the mass of the structure without the mass of the support (theorem 21.9). The support force has the form (21.17), which with cut-off modes we approximate by \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{nač}}\) or by \(R_{\mathrm{N}}^{\mathrm{pop}}\) and bound the error (corollary 21.10). What we have not proved is an error estimate for arbitrary time dependence \(u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\), which would need a Fourier decomposition, and the response for several support coordinates at once (GR takes a matrix instead of a number). The additional mass method of section 21.3 solves the same task without partitioning the coordinates, and later we pose the same question of which coordinates to keep for the coordinates of the structure itself (section 21.4).
