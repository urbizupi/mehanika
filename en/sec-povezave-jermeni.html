Chapter 26 Belts as axially moving continua

26.10 Connections

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19. In this chapter we let the string known from chapter 13 run through two fixed eyelets (očesci) at speed \(v\) and took it as the model of a belt span (razpon). Now we ask where the results come back from and where they go: what of the earlier chapters remained at \(v = 0\), what is proved here for the first time, and what we leave to the chapters that follow. The numbers are for a steel band \(0{,}9 \times 50\) mm, span length \(L = 1\) m and tension \(100\) MPa (\(\mu = 0{,}3510\) kg/m, \(T = 4500\) N, \(c = 113{,}23\) m/s). The band, the length and the tension are our choice, we have no data on real belts in the books (section 26.9).

  • The string at speed zero (VD, DSK, chapter 13): at \(v = 0\) the equation of theorem 26.4 becomes the wave equation of theorem 13.3, the modes of theorem 26.13 become the modes of theorem 13.7, and the natural frequencies \(\omega_{\mathrm{n}} = \dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\,(1 - v^2/c^2)\) become \(n\pi c/L\). For \(v \ne 0\) the standing wave of section 13.2 is no longer a solution, as lemma 26.11 of section 26.3 proves, so the promise of chapter 13 is kept and the strings of that chapter are the case \(v = 0\). The factor \(1 - v^2/c^2\) multiplies all frequencies equally, the first drops from \(56{,}61\) Hz at \(v = 0\) to \(54{,}85\) Hz at \(20\) m/s, \(49{,}55\) Hz at \(40\) m/s and \(28{,}35\) Hz at \(80\) m/s. It reaches half of the starting value at \(v = c/\sqrt{2} = 80{,}06\) m/s.
  • Travelling waves (VD, chapter 13): lemma 13.5 showed only that \(F(x - ct)\) and \(G(x + ct)\) are solutions, and the converse, that every solution is a sum of such waves, we did not prove for the string. Lemma 26.6 proves it for every \(v \ge 0\), so also for \(v = 0\). At \(v = 40\) m/s the speed of waves downstream is \(c + v = 153{,}23\) m/s and upstream \(c - v = 73{,}23\) m/s, at \(v = 0\) both are \(113{,}23\) m/s.
  • The column and the critical speed (DSK, chapter 8): the static equation of the moving beam (section 26.7) is the column equation of theorems 8.134 and 8.135 with the compressive force \(P = \mu v^2 - T\). The critical speed (kritična hitrost) is the speed at which the equilibrium position turns into a family of positions: for the string it is \(v = c\), where \(P = 0\) and every shape with the two ends held is in equilibrium (lemma 26.19), and for the beam \(v_{\mathrm{c}}^2 = (T + \pi^2 E I/L^2)/\mu\) with pinned supports and \((T + 4\pi^2 E I/L^2)/\mu\) with clamped ends (theorem 26.37). It has its name because the way of moving breaks at it: below it the frequencies fall, at it a natural frequency is zero. For our band the bending stiffness is \(E I = 0{,}59535\) N m\(^2\) and the Euler loads are \(5{,}876\) N with pinned supports and \(23{,}503\) N with clamped ends, which is \(0{,}13\) % and \(0{,}52\) % of the force \(T = 4500\) N. So bending raises the critical speed by only \(0{,}0653\) % and \(0{,}2608\) %. A thin band is therefore a string, as the model of section 26.1 assumes.
  • The conserved quantity (DSK, chapter 8): the functional \(\mathcal{E}^*\) of theorem 26.21 is the energy integral \(T_2 - T_0 + V\) of theorem 8.109. The kinetic energy of the transverse motion of the material per unit length of the band, \(\tfrac12\mu(w_{\mathrm{t}} + v w_{\mathrm{x}})^2\), splits by theorem 8.86 into the quadratic part \(T_2\), the linear part \(T_1\) and the zeroth part \(T_0\). The part \(T_1\) is the one that gives the gyroscopic forces, and the part \(T_0\) lowers the stiffness from \(T\) to \(T - \mu v^2\). The energy of the span \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{r}}\) changes by lemma 26.23, because the material carries energy through the eyelets, and only \(\mathcal{E}^*\) is conserved. At \(v = 40\) m/s, \(\mu v^2 = 561{,}6\) N, which is \(12{,}5\) % of the tension \(T\), and at \(80\) m/s it is \(2246{,}4\) N, which is \(49{,}9\) %. Exactly half is reached at \(v = c/\sqrt{2}\), where \(\mu v^2 = \mu c^2/2 = T/2\).
  • Gyroscopic matrices and rotors (DSK, chapter 24): chapter 8 called the coefficients linear in the velocities with a skew-symmetric matrix Coriolis or gyroscopic forces and postponed them to rotating systems. Here we write them for the first time in the equation \(\mat M \ddot{\vek q} + \mat G \dot{\vek q} + \mat K \vek q = \vek 0\) (definition 26.25) and prove that they do no work (lemma 26.26) and that for positive definite \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) all eigenvalues are purely imaginary (theorem 26.27). In a belt \(\mat G\) comes from the motion of the material along the band, in a rotor from rotation (chapter 24). The letter \(\mat G\) is a matrix, the shear modulus \(G\) is a scalar, so they do not meet. Gyroscopic stabilization is a case where \(\mat K\) is not positive definite and the eigenvalues are still purely imaginary (section 26.5). For a pinned moving beam with \(\bar T = T L^2/(E I) = 100\) and the dimensionless speed \(\bar v = v\sqrt{\mu L^2/(E I)}\) the system is unstable for \(10{,}482 < \bar v < 11{,}810\) (lemma 26.38), and for \(11{,}810 < \bar v < 12{,}177\) all eigenvalues are purely imaginary again. This window is computed with \(24\) sines and not proved (section 26.7). Theorem 8.119 of chapter 8 does not apply here, because it requires \(\mat G = \mat 0\).
  • Several degrees of freedom and damping (VD, DSK, chapters 10 and 20): the decoupling of theorem 10.20 needs real modes, which theorem 10.13 gives only for \(\mat G = \mat 0\). For the moving string the modes are complex, orthogonality holds in the state-space form (theorem 26.32), and the plain mass product of the first and the second mode is not zero (section 26.6, its real part is \(0{,}09701\) at \(v/c = \tfrac12\), \(L = 1\) and \(\mu = 1\)). Complex modes and the first-order form for systems with damping are treated by chapter 20, and the two systems share this apparatus. This chapter does not derive how the frequencies of a belt change with damping.
  • Finite elements (AK, chapters 7 and 10): the element stiffness matrix of lemma 7.5 and the consistent mass matrix of lemma 10.31 give, for the moving string, also the element gyroscopic matrix (lemma 26.40), and assembly is theorem 7.13. After the held nodes are removed, the assembled matrix \(\mat G\) is skew-symmetric (corollary 26.41). At \(v/c = \tfrac12\) with \(n = 4, 8, 16, 32\) elements, \(\omega_1 = 2{,}51476\), \(2{,}39523\), \(2{,}36591\) and \(2{,}35862\) against the exact value \(2{,}35619\) (for \(L = c = \mu = 1\)). The Ritz upper bound of theorem 10.42 needs \(\mat G = \mat 0\), so for a belt it is not proved. In all computed cases (Galerkin and elements) the frequency is above the exact one, but this is an observation.
  • Fourier series and completeness (VD, chapter 13): the completeness of the sines of theorem 13.11 holds for \(v = 0\). For the modes of the moving string, which are a product of a sine and a phase factor, the expansion is theorem 26.34 of section 26.6. For the moving beam the completeness of the complex modes is assumed after a source (WM1990, PDF p. 3), not proved.
  • Nonlinear and excited belts (AK, DSK, chapters 17 and 24): the model has the speed \(v\) and the tension \(T\) constant, a small slope \(|w_{\mathrm{x}}| \le 0{,}005\) and fixed eyelets (section 26.1). If the tension changes through stretching of the band or through excitation, nonlinear vibration and parametric excitation arise, which we only name here (CHEN2005, sections 3.1 to 3.5, PDF p. 7-14). Numerical solution of nonlinear equations of motion is in chapter 17. The coupling of a belt with the pulleys and the tensioner is in the source only a literature review (CHEN2005, sections 2.4.2 and 3.6, PDF p. 4-6 and 14-15) and we do not derive it.
  • Limits of the chapter: everything we did for the string is proved, up to and including orthogonality and natural frequencies, under the assumptions of section 26.1. Cited without proof are: the completeness of the complex modes of the moving beam (WM1990, PDF p. 3), Hamilton's principle for moving materials (WM1990, PDF p. 2, only named), the Green's function (WM1990, PDF p. 3-4), nonlinear and parametric vibration, the coupling with the pulleys. Also without proof is that the frequencies of Galerkin and of elements are above the exact ones (an observation). Above the critical speed, \(v > c\), the model with two fixed eyelets does not hold and we claim nothing about the string there (section 26.4). A theory of belt friction, centrifugal tension on the pulleys and data on real belts are not in our books, so all numbers of the chapter are a calculation for our band and not a measurement.
