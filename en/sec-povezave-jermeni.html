Chapter 26 Belts as axially moving continua

26.10 Connections

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19. In this chapter we let the string known from chapter 13 run at speed \(v\) through two fixed eyelets (očesci) and took it as the model of a belt span (razpon). Now we ask where the results come back from and where they go: what of the earlier chapters remained at \(v = 0\), what is proved here for the first time, and what we leave to the chapters that follow. The numbers are for a steel band \(0{,}9 \times 50\) mm, span length \(L = 1\) m and tension \(100\) MPa (\(\mu = 0{,}3510\) kg/m, \(T = 4500\) N, \(c = 113{,}23\) m/s). The band, the length and the tension are our choice, we have no data on real belts in the books (section 26.9).

  • The string at speed zero (VD, DSK, chapter 13): at \(v = 0\) the equation of theorem 26.4 becomes the wave equation of theorem 13.3, the modes of theorem 26.13 become the modes of theorem 13.7, and the natural frequencies \(\omega_{\mathrm{n}} = \dfrac{n\pi c}{L}\,(1 - v^2/c^2)\) become \(n\pi c/L\). For \(v \ne 0\) the standing wave of section 13.2 is no longer a solution, as lemma 26.11 of section 26.3 proves, so the promise of chapter 13 is kept and the strings of that chapter are the case \(v = 0\). The factor \(1 - v^2/c^2\) multiplies all frequencies equally, so the drop at \(40\) m/s is the same for \(f_1\) and for \(f_3\), \(12{,}5\) %. The first drops from \(56{,}61\) Hz at \(v = 0\) to \(54{,}85\) Hz at \(20\) m/s, \(49{,}55\) Hz at \(40\) m/s and \(28{,}35\) Hz at \(80\) m/s. The curve \(f_1\) reaches half of the starting value at \(v = c/\sqrt{2} = 80{,}06\) m/s, just to the right of the last dot.
  • Travelling waves (VD, chapter 13): lemma 13.5 showed only that \(F(x - ct)\) and \(G(x + ct)\) are solutions, and the converse, that every solution is a sum of such waves, we did not prove for the string. Lemma 26.6 proves it for every \(v \ge 0\), so also for \(v = 0\). At \(v = 40\) m/s the speed of waves is downstream \(c + v = 153{,}23\) m/s and upstream \(c - v = 73{,}23\) m/s, at \(v = 0\) both are \(113{,}23\) m/s. This is where the factor of the previous item comes from: the way downstream takes \(6{,}526\) ms, the way upstream \(13{,}656\) ms, so the round trip takes \(20{,}182\) ms instead of \(17{,}664\) ms. The slow way back loses more time than the fast way forward gains, the difference is \(2{,}518\) ms, and one round trip per \(20{,}182\) ms is the frequency \(49{,}55\) Hz.
  • The column and the critical speed (DSK, chapter 8): the static equation of the moving beam (section 26.7) is the column equation of theorems 8.150 and 8.151 with the compressive force \(P = \mu v^2 - T\). The critical speed (kritična hitrost) is the speed at which the equilibrium position turns into a family of positions: for the string it is \(v = c\), where \(P = 0\) and every shape with the two ends held is in equilibrium (lemma 26.19), because the force of the tension on a piece is then, at every curvature, exactly the force the piece needs to run along the curve, also for the other two shapes, and for the beam \(v_{\mathrm{c}}^2 = (T + \pi^2 E I/L^2)/\mu\) with pinned supports and \((T + 4\pi^2 E I/L^2)/\mu\) with clamped ends (theorem 26.37). It has its name because the way of moving breaks at it: below it the frequencies fall, at it a natural frequency is zero. For our band the bending stiffness is \(E I = 0{,}59535\) N m\(^2\) and the Euler loads are \(5{,}876\) N with pinned supports and \(23{,}503\) N with clamped ends, which is \(0{,}13\) % and \(0{,}52\) % of the force \(T = 4500\) N. So bending raises the critical speed by only \(0{,}0653\) % and \(0{,}2608\) %: the parabola reaches both dashed lines already within the magnified neighbourhood of \(v = c\), which is \(0{,}36\) m/s wide. A thin band is therefore a string, as the model of section 26.1 assumes.
  • The conserved quantity (DSK, chapter 8): the functional \(\mathcal{E}^*\) of theorem 26.21 is the energy integral \(T_2 - T_0 + V\) of theorem 8.124. The kinetic energy of the transverse motion of the material per unit length of the band, \(\tfrac12\mu(w_{\mathrm{t}} + v w_{\mathrm{x}})^2\), splits by theorem 8.97 into the quadratic part \(T_2\), the linear part \(T_1\) and the zeroth part \(T_0\). The part \(T_1\) is the one that gives the gyroscopic forces, and the part \(T_0\) lowers the stiffness from \(T\) to \(T - \mu v^2\). The energy of the span \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{r}}\) changes by lemma 26.23 (in example A between \(25{,}6\) and \(30{,}6\) mJ), because the material brings energy in through the first eyelet and carries it out through the second, and only \(\mathcal{E}^*\), in example A \(21{,}9\) mJ, is conserved. At \(v = 40\) m/s, \(\mu v^2 = 561{,}6\) N, which is \(12{,}5\) % of the tension \(T\), and \(3938\) N is left for the stiffness. At \(80\) m/s the orange part is four times longer, \(2246{,}4\) N, which is \(49{,}9\) %. Exactly half is reached at \(v = c/\sqrt{2}\), where \(\mu v^2 = \mu c^2/2 = T/2\).
  • Gyroscopic matrices and rotors (DSK, chapter 24): chapter 8 called the coefficients linear in the velocities with a skew-symmetric matrix Coriolis or gyroscopic forces and postponed them to rotating systems. Here we write them for the first time in the equation \(\mat M \ddot{\vek q} + \mat G \dot{\vek q} + \mat K \vek q = \vek 0\) (definition 26.25) and prove that they do no work (lemma 26.26) and that for positive definite \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) all eigenvalues are purely imaginary (theorem 26.27). In a belt \(\mat G\) comes from the motion of the material along the band, in a rotor from rotation (chapter 24). The letter \(\mat G\) is a matrix, the shear modulus \(G\) is a scalar, so they do not meet. Gyroscopic stabilization is a case where \(\mat K\) is not positive definite and the eigenvalues are still purely imaginary (section 26.5): with \(\mat K = -\mat I\) the eigenvalues have a real part for \(g < 2\), they lie on the orange circle, and for \(g \ge 2\) they are all on the imaginary axis. For a pinned moving beam with \(\bar T = T L^2/(E I) = 100\) and the dimensionless speed \(\bar v = v\sqrt{\mu L^2/(E I)}\) the system is unstable for \(10{,}482 < \bar v < 11{,}810\), between the two orange dashed lines (lemma 26.38), and for \(11{,}810 < \bar v < 12{,}177\) all eigenvalues are purely imaginary again, the curve of the largest real part is at zero there. This window is computed with \(24\) sines and not proved (section 26.7). Theorem 8.135 of chapter 8 does not apply here, because it requires \(\mat G = \mat 0\).
  • Several degrees of freedom and damping (VD, DSK, chapters 10 and 20): the decoupling of theorem 10.28 needs real modes, which theorem 10.21 gives only for \(\mat G = \mat 0\). For the moving string the modes are complex, orthogonality holds in the state-space form (theorem 26.32), and the plain mass product of the first and the second mode is not zero (section 26.6, its real part is \(0{,}09701\) at \(v/c = \tfrac12\), \(L = 1\) and \(\mu = 1\)). Only the sum of two parts is zero: the mass part with area \(1{,}0771\), which is \(0{,}09701\) times \(\mu\omega_1\omega_2 = 11{,}103\), and the stiffness part with area \(-1{,}0771\) add up to area zero. Complex modes and the first-order form for systems with damping are treated by chapter 20, and the two systems share this apparatus. This chapter does not derive how the frequencies of a belt change with damping.
  • Finite elements (AK, chapters 7 and 10): the element stiffness matrix of lemma 7.5 and the consistent mass matrix of lemma 10.47 give, for the moving string, also the element gyroscopic matrix (lemma 26.40), and assembly is theorem 7.13. After the held nodes are removed, the assembled matrix \(\mat G\) is skew-symmetric (corollary 26.41). At \(v/c = \tfrac12\) with \(n = 4, 8, 16, 32\) elements, \(\omega_1 = 2{,}51476\), \(2{,}39523\), \(2{,}36591\) and \(2{,}35862\) against the exact value \(2{,}35619\) (for \(L = c = \mu = 1\)). The error \(0{,}15857\), \(0{,}03904\), \(0{,}00972\) and \(0{,}00243\) is divided by about \(4\) at every doubling of \(n\), so the blue dots lie parallel to the grey line of slope \(-2\). The Ritz upper bound of theorem 10.59 needs \(\mat G = \mat 0\), so for a belt it is not proved. In all computed cases (Galerkin and elements) the frequency is above the exact one, but this is an observation.
  • Fourier series and completeness (VD, chapter 13): the completeness of the sines of theorem 13.11 holds for \(v = 0\). For the modes of the moving string, which are a product of a sine, the grey dashed envelope, and a phase factor, which makes the point of largest deflection travel, the expansion is theorem 26.34 of section 26.6. For the moving beam the completeness of the complex modes is assumed after a source (WM1990, PDF p. 3), not proved.
  • Nonlinear and excited belts (AK, DSK, chapters 17 and 24): the model has the speed \(v\) and the tension \(T\) constant, a small slope \(|w_{\mathrm{x}}| \le 0{,}005\) (an angle below \(0{,}29^\circ\)) and fixed eyelets (section 26.1). If the tension changes through stretching of the band or through excitation, nonlinear vibration and parametric excitation arise, which we only name here (CHEN2005, sections 3.1 to 3.5, PDF p. 7-14). Numerical solution of nonlinear equations of motion is in chapter 17. The coupling of a belt with the pulleys and the tensioner is in the source only a literature review (CHEN2005, sections 2.4.2 and 3.6, PDF p. 4-6 and 14-15) and we do not derive it.
  • Limits of the chapter: everything we did for the string is proved, up to and including orthogonality and natural frequencies, under the assumptions of section 26.1. Cited without proof are: the completeness of the complex modes of the moving beam (WM1990, PDF p. 3), Hamilton's principle for moving materials (WM1990, PDF p. 2, only named), the Green's function (WM1990, PDF p. 3-4), nonlinear and parametric vibration, the coupling with the pulleys. Also without proof is that the frequencies of Galerkin and of elements are above the exact ones (an observation). A theory of belt friction, centrifugal tension on the pulleys and data on real belts are not in our books, so all numbers of the chapter are a calculation for our band and not a measurement.
  • The speed limit (section 26.4): above the critical speed, \(v > c\), the model with two fixed eyelets does not hold and we claim nothing about the string there. Figure 26.67 shows why in the plane of position \(x\) and time \(t\). At \(v = 40\) m/s the wave along the flow reaches the eyelet at \(x = L\), where the condition \(w(L, t) = 0\) fixes the reflected wave against the flow, which runs back into the span at the speed \(c - v = 73{,}23\) m/s. At \(v = 1{,}2\,c = 135{,}87\) m/s the speed \(c - v\) is negative, so the flow carries forward both the wave along the flow at the speed \(c + v = 249{,}1\) m/s and the other wave at the speed \(v - c = 22{,}6\) m/s. There is no way back from the eyelet at \(x = L\): the displacement there is already fixed by what flows in from the left, so the condition \(w(L, t) = 0\) can no longer be prescribed. On the speed axis the band \(0 \le v < c\) is therefore where the model holds, and about the hatched part \(v > c\) we claim nothing.
