Chapter 29 Structural optimization

29.8 The stiffest rod (najtogejša palica)

So far a design variable (projektna spremenljivka) was a list of numbers, for example the areas \(A_{\mathrm{AC}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{BC}}\) of two bars of a truss. Let us ask what happens when a single rod has its own area in every cross-section. Then the area is not a number but a function \(A(x)\) of the distance \(x\) from the support, and each of its infinitely many values may be chosen separately. When the design variable is a function, we speak of a distributed parameter system (sistem s porazdeljenimi parametri), because the decision is spread along the whole length and not collected in a few numbers. A truss is by nature a discrete system, because there are as many variables as there are bars (CHK §1.5, PDF p. 17). The stiffest (najtogejša) rod is the one with the greatest stiffness among all those with the same volume of material. We measure stiffness by compliance (podajnost, section 29.5): the more stiffness, the less compliance.

The model is this. A straight rod of length \(L\) is clamped into a wall at \(x = 0\), is free at \(x = L\), and on every unit of length of the rod acts a force \(b(x)\) in the direction of the \(x\) axis. We call such a force a distributed load (porazdeljena obtežba), because it is not concentrated at one point but spread over the whole rod. Its unit is N/m. In chapter 11 the letter \(b\) means force per unit volume, here it is force per unit length. There are three idealizations. The material is linearly elastic with modulus \(E\) (definition 3.1), displacements are small, and the load \(b\) does not depend on the area, as the weight of the rod would. The weight of a rod of volume \(100\) cm³ is \(7{,}65\) N, which is \(0{,}08\) % of the load \(10\) kN we shall take below, so we neglect it. For \(b\) we assume that it is continuous and that there is a number \(m > 0\) with \(b(x) \ge m\) everywhere, so the rod is pulled everywhere (tension, section 2.1).

Let us draw the rod as on a board. The wall is on the left, because the rod is clamped there. The rod reaches from it to the free end. We draw the load as a row of arrows on the axis of the rod, because the axis is the line along which the forces act, and each arrow pulls in the direction of \(x\). The forces act along the axis, so in a cross-section only the axial force matters (definition 2.5). How large is it in the cross-section at \(x\)? We cut the rod in thought at \(x\), at the distance \(x\) from the wall, and look at the piece from the cut to the free end, which we draw separately. Only two forces act on it. The first is the load on the piece, whose sum is \(b\,(L - x)\) if \(b\) is constant. The second is the axial force across the cut. By definition 2.5 the part on the side of larger \(x\) acts on the part on the side of smaller \(x\) with the force \(N\), so the part on the side of smaller \(x\) acts on our piece with the force \(-N(x)\) in the direction of \(x\), that is, it pulls the piece to the left, and the arrow is drawn from the cut to the left. The piece is in equilibrium (definition 2.3), the sum of the forces on it is zero, and the load on the piece is the integral of the load over it. Therefore

\[N(x) = \int_{\mathrm{x}}^L b(s)\,\mathrm{d}s . \tag{29.47}\]

For a constant load \(b\) we have \(N(x) = b\,(L - x)\), from \(bL\) at the wall to \(0\) at the free end. Always \(N(x) \ge m\,(L - x) \ge 0\) (lemma 3.39, item 3). It matters that we obtained \(N\) from equilibrium alone, without the area and the material. We call such a rod statically determinate (statično določena): the forces in it are determined by statics, that is, by equilibrium, hence the name. What would be different is shown by the example of a rod clamped at both ends, at the end of the section.

Now we know where the force is large. At the wall the cross-section carries the whole load, at the free end almost nothing. Let us test what that means on a steel rod with \(E = 196\) GPa (section 3.1.5), length \(L = 1\) m, load \(b = 10\) kN/m and constant area \(A_0 = 100\) mm². The volume is \(V = A_0 L = 100\) cm³. The stress (definition 2.6) is \(bL/A_0 = 100\) MPa at the wall, zero at the free end, and in between it falls linearly. The material near the free end hardly carries anything, and the material at the wall carries the most. How could we redistribute the same material so that the rod is as stiff as possible?

In section 29.5 the compliance is the number \(F\cdot u\), the force times its displacement. We cut the load into pieces: the piece at \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) of length \(\Delta x_{\mathrm{i}}\) has the force \(b(x_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta x_{\mathrm{i}}\) and its displacement is \(u(x_{\mathrm{i}})\). The sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} b(x_{\mathrm{i}})\,u(x_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta x_{\mathrm{i}}\) is a Riemann sum (definition 3.32) and approaches an integral.

Definition 29.57 Compliance of a rod under a distributed load (podajnost palice pod porazdeljeno obtežbo)

Let a rod be clamped at \(x = 0\), with the load \(b\) and the displacement \(u(x)\) of the cross-section at \(x\), continuously differentiable on \([0, L]\). The compliance (podajnost) of the rod is the number

\[C = \int_0^L b(x)\,u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x . \tag{29.48}\]

Source: CHK §8.3 (PDF pp. 168 and 173): compliance is the value \(\ell(U)\) of the linear form of the load at the equilibrium displacement and by (8.26) equals \(a(U, U)\). For a rod \(\ell(v) = \int b\,v\,\mathrm{d}x\) (PDF p. 173). For point forces it is the \(F\cdot u\) of section 29.5.

Compliance depends on the area, because the displacement \(u\) depends on it, so we write \(C(A)\) when we stress this. Which areas may we choose? Those that nowhere break the rod where there is force, and that near the free end, where there is hardly any force, may be arbitrarily small.

Definition 29.58 Admissible area function (dopustna funkcija ploščine)

Let a rod be as above, with the axial force \(N\) from equation (29.47). A function \(A : [0, L] \to [0, \infty)\) is an admissible area function (dopustna funkcija ploščine) if it satisfies three conditions:

  1. \(A\) is continuous on \([0, L]\),
  2. \(A(x) > 0\) for every \(x \in [0, L)\),
  3. the stress \(\sigma(x) = N(x)/A(x)\) on \([0, L)\) has a limit as \(x\) tends to \(L\) (definition 1.5).

We take the value of this limit as \(\sigma(L)\).

Source: The notion is not from the source. CHK §8.3 (PDF p. 169) requires only \(\rho \ge 0\) and a given volume.

The name admissible says that the function may take part in the contest for the best, like the elements of the feasible set in section 29.1. Condition 2 does not allow the rod to be broken anywhere, because the elongation there would be infinite. Condition 3 does not allow infinite stress at the free end. Two properties follow from it that we shall need. First, \(\sigma\) is continuous on \([0, L]\): on \([0, L)\) as a quotient of continuous functions with a denominator that is not zero (lemma 4.108, item 2), and at \(L\) by the choice of \(\sigma(L)\) (definition 1.58). Second, \(N = \sigma A\) on \([0, L]\): on \([0, L)\) by the definition of \(\sigma\), and at \(L\) the left side is zero, and so is the right, because either \(A(L) = 0\), or \(A(L) > 0\) and \(\sigma(L) = \lim N/A = 0/A(L) = 0\).

How do we compute the compliance without computing the displacement? The load is known, the axial force is given by equation (29.47), and the displacement follows from the strain. With integration by parts we move the derivative from \(u\) to \(N\).

Lemma 29.59 Compliance of a statically determinate rod (podajnost statično določene palice)

Let a rod be as above with an admissible area function \(A\) and displacement \(u\), \(u(0) = 0\). Then

\[C(A) = \frac1E \int_0^L N(x)\,\sigma(x)\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac1E \int_0^L \frac{N(x)^2}{A(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x , \tag{29.49}\]

where \(N^2/A\) at \(x = L\) is taken as the limit \(N\sigma = 0\).

Proof

We must show that the integral of the work of the load passes into the integral of the square of the axial force. We express the displacement through the strain and integrate by parts.

  1. In every cross-section the stress is \(\sigma = N/A\) by definition 2.6 and the strain is \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E\) by definition 3.1. The law measured on a specimen we apply in every cross-section separately, as for the hanging rod in section 3.1.5. The function \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E\) is continuous on \([0, L]\).
  2. By definition 1.10 \(u' = \varepsilon\), and \(u(0) = 0\), because the rod is clamped. The displacement is therefore continuously differentiable on \([0, L]\).
  3. The function \(N(x) = \int_0^L b\,\mathrm{d}s - \int_0^x b\,\mathrm{d}s\) is by the fundamental theorem of calculus 3.40, item 2, differentiable with continuous derivative \(N' = -b\). From equation (29.47), \(N(L) = 0\).
  4. Corollary 3.41 with \(f = N\) and \(g = u\) on \([0, L]\) gives
    \[\int_0^L N\,u'\,\mathrm{d}x = N(L)\,u(L) - N(0)\,u(0) - \int_0^L N'\,u\,\mathrm{d}x = 0 - 0 + \int_0^L b\,u\,\mathrm{d}x = C .\]
  5. By step 2, \(N\,u' = N\varepsilon = N\sigma/E\), which is the first equality in equation (29.49). For \(x < L\), \(N\sigma = N^2/A\), which is the second.

Source: CHK (8.26) (PDF p. 168), \(a(U, U) = \ell(U)\), called Clapeyron's theorem. The proof by parts is not from the source.

So we compute compliance from the axial force and the area, without displacements. Because \(N = \sigma A\), we have \(N\sigma = \sigma^2 A\), and by definition 4.2 the strain energy of the rod is \(U = \int_0^L \frac{\sigma^2}{2E}\,A\,\mathrm{d}x\), since the energy density is \(\tfrac12 E\varepsilon^2 = \sigma^2/(2E)\). Hence \(C = 2U\), as in theorem 4.3, where the work of the force is half the product of force and displacement. Let us check on the uniform rod. By the fundamental theorem of calculus \(\frac{1}{EA_0}\int_0^L b^2 (L - x)^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{b^2 L^3}{3EA_0}\), and for our numbers \(1{,}7007\) J. With the displacement \(u(x) = \dfrac{b}{EA_0}\big(Lx - \tfrac12 x^2\big)\) and equation (29.48) we get the same number (num-08.out, E1).

Now we set the problem. The material is as much as the volume \(V\), and the volume of the rod is \(\int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x\), like a Riemann sum of the volumes of pieces \(A(x_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta x_{\mathrm{i}}\). We look for an admissible function \(A\) with \(\int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x = V\) for which \(C(A)\) is smallest. We denote \(S = \int_0^L N\,\mathrm{d}x\) (unit N m, \(S\) as the sum of axial forces). In the uniform rod the cross-section at the wall is loaded the most, the cross-section at the end almost not at all. We suspect that the area must be moved to where \(N\) is large. The theorem says how much exactly.

Theorem 29.60 The stiffest statically determinate rod (najtogejša statično določena palica)

Let a rod with modulus \(E > 0\) and load \(b\) be as above, let \(N\) be from equation (29.47), \(S = \int_0^L N\,\mathrm{d}x\) and \(V > 0\). For every admissible area function \(A\) with \(\int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x = V\):

  1. \[C(A) \ge \frac{S^2}{E\,V} , \tag{29.50}\]
  2. equality holds exactly for \(A^*(x) = V\,N(x)/S\), which is an admissible area function with \(\int_0^L A^*\,\mathrm{d}x = V\),
  3. for \(A^*\) the stress in every cross-section equals \(S/V\).
Proof

We must show the bound for every admissible \(A\), that \(A^*\) reaches it and that no other function does. We get the bound from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the inner product with the weight \(A\).

  1. Let \(X\) be the vector space of continuous functions on \([0, L]\) and \((f, g)_{\mathrm{A}} = \int_0^L f\,g\,A\,\mathrm{d}x\). The product of continuous functions is continuous (corollary 1.64), so the integral exists (theorem 3.38).
  2. Properties 1 to 3 of definition 6.16 follow from \(f g A = g f A\), \((f + g)\,h A = f h A + g h A\) and \((k f)\,g A = k\,(f g A)\) at every \(x\) and from the linearity of the integral (lemma 3.39, item 1).
  3. Property 4: let \(f \ne 0\), that is \(f(x_0) \ne 0\). Since \(f\) is continuous, \(f \ne 0\) on an interval around \(x_0\), which contains a point \(x_1 < L\). There \(A(x_1) > 0\), and \(f^2 A\) is continuous, nonnegative and positive at \(x_1\). As in step 4 of the proof of lemma 6.17, item 1, we therefore have \((f, f)_{\mathrm{A}} > 0\).
  4. The function \(\sigma\) is continuous and \(N = \sigma A\) (by definition 29.58), so \((\sigma, 1)_{\mathrm{A}} = \int_0^L \sigma A\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^L N\,\mathrm{d}x = S\). By lemma 29.59, \((\sigma, \sigma)_{\mathrm{A}} = \int_0^L \sigma^2 A\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^L N\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x = E\,C(A)\). The volume is \((1, 1)_{\mathrm{A}} = \int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x = V\).
  5. Lemma 6.19 gives \(|S| \le \sqrt{E\,C(A)}\,\sqrt{V}\). Both sides are nonnegative, so by lemma 1.28 \(S^2 \le E\,C(A)\,V\), and dividing by the positive number \(EV\) we get the bound (29.50). This is item 1.
  6. Let \(A^* = VN/S\). From \(b \ge m > 0\) it follows by lemma 3.39, item 3, that \(N(x) \ge m\,(L - x) > 0\) for \(x < L\), so \(S > 0\) and \(A^* > 0\) on \([0, L)\). The function \(A^*\) is continuous, because \(N\) is continuous (step 3 of the proof of lemma 29.59), and \(A^*(L) = 0\). The stress \(N/A^* = S/V\) is constant, so it has a limit, and \(A^*\) is admissible. By the linearity of the integral \(\int_0^L A^*\,\mathrm{d}x = (V/S)\int_0^L N\,\mathrm{d}x = V\). The stress \(S/V\) is item 3.
  7. By lemma 29.59, \(C(A^*) = \frac1E \int_0^L N\,\frac SV\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac{S^2}{EV}\). So \(A^*\) reaches the bound. This is the direction "if" of item 2.
  8. Let \(A\) be admissible with \(\int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x = V\) and \(C(A) = S^2/(EV)\). By step 4, \((\sigma, 1)_{\mathrm{A}}^2 = S^2 = E\,C(A)\,V = (\sigma, \sigma)_{\mathrm{A}}\,(1, 1)_{\mathrm{A}}\), which is equality in lemma 6.19. By step 3, \((\cdot, \cdot)_{\mathrm{A}}\) is an inner product and \(1 \ne 0\), so \(\sigma\) is a multiple of the function \(1\), hence constant, \(\sigma = c\). From \(N = \sigma A\) we get \(A = N/c\) for \(x < L\), where \(c \ne 0\), because \(N > 0\) for \(x < L\). The functions \(A\) and \(N/c\) are continuous and equal on \([0, L)\), so they have the same limit at \(L\) and are equal there as well (definition 1.58), hence \(A = N/c\) on \([0, L]\). The condition \(\int_0^L A\,\mathrm{d}x = V\) gives by the linearity of the integral \(S/c = V\), so \(c = S/V\) and \(A = VN/S = A^*\). This is the direction "only if" of item 2.

Source: CHK §8.3.2 (PDF pp. 173-174): from the condition of constant energy density \(E\,(u')^2 = \lambda\) (8.33), the volume (8.32) and equilibrium it derives the wedge \(\rho = 2V\,(1 - x)\) for constant \(b\) and \(L = 1\). The proof by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, general \(b\), the bound (29.50) and uniqueness are not from the source.

What does the theorem say? Among all admissible areas with volume \(V\) the compliance is at least \(S^2/(EV)\), and we reach it with exactly one, with the area \(A^*\) proportional to the axial force. In it the stress is everywhere equal, \(\sigma = S/V\). Such a rod is fully stressed (polno napeta), like the truss in section 29.2, and every bit of material is used equally. Equal stress means equal \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E\) everywhere, so the energy density \(\tfrac12 E\varepsilon^2\) is constant as well, and with it its double \(E\varepsilon^2 = E\,(u')^2\). This is the condition \(E\,(u')^2 = \lambda\) with a constant \(\lambda\) from CHK (8.33), PDF p. 173. The same condition is, in section 29.5, the condition of theorem 29.37 for the bars of a truss, here for infinitely many bars. Why the result is so, the proof tells: compliance is the sum \(\int \sigma\cdot N\), the volume is the sum \(\int A\), and the axial force is the product \(N = \sigma A\). The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality is an equality exactly when the factors are proportional, that is, when \(\sigma\) is constant.

For our rod \(N = b\,(L - x)\), \(S = bL^2/2 = 5000\) N m and \(A^*(x) = V N/S = 2A_0\,(1 - x/L)\). The area is \(2A_0 = 200\) mm² at the wall and falls linearly to zero. Its shape is a wedge (klin), after the splitting wedge that narrows to an edge. At the top is the uniform rod with the area \(A_0\) everywhere. Below them is the axial force, the same for both rods, falling linearly from \(bL\) to zero, because it depends only on the load and not on the area. At the bottom is the stress \(\sigma = N/A\): in the uniform rod it falls from \(100\) to \(0\) MPa, in the wedge it is constant, \(S/V = 50\) MPa, as item 3 of the theorem says. The compliances are (num-08.out, E1)

\[C(A_0) = 1{,}7007\ \text{J} , \qquad C(A^*) = \frac{S^2}{EV} = \frac{5000^2}{196 \cdot 10^9 \cdot 10^{-4}} = 1{,}2755\ \text{J} . \tag{29.51}\]

The ratio is \(3/4\): the wedge is less compliant by a quarter. The uniform rod is a factor \(4/3\) above the lower bound of the theorem and the bound (29.50) is strict for it, because \(A_0\) is not proportional to \(N\). The displacement of the free end is the same for both rods, \(0{,}2551\) mm. In the wedge the strain is \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E = 0{,}2551 \cdot 10^{-3}\) everywhere, so \(u(x) = \varepsilon x\) and \(u(L) = 0{,}2551\) mm. In the uniform rod \(u(x) = \varepsilon_0\,(x - x^2/(2L))\) with \(\varepsilon_0 = bL/(EA_0) = 0{,}5102 \cdot 10^{-3}\) and also \(u(L) = \varepsilon_0 L/2 = 0{,}2551\) mm. The difference of displacements is \(\varepsilon_0\,x\,(L - x)/(2L) \ge 0\), in the interior the displacement of the wedge is smaller. Compliance is the work of the load on the displacements of all points, not only of the free end, so it is smaller for the wedge. The displacement of the free end is therefore not a measure of stiffness under a distributed load.

Example 29.61 The wedge as the rod of least volume at an allowed stress (klin kot palica z najmanjšo prostornino pri dovoljeni napetosti)

Let us limit the stress by the allowed stress \(\sigma_0 = 220\) MPa, without a safety factor (the assumption of section 29.2), and ask how much material the cheapest rod needs. The condition \(N/A \le \sigma_0\) is the condition \(A(x) \ge N(x)/\sigma_0\) in every cross-section separately, because \(N\) is known from equilibrium. The volume \(\int A\,\mathrm{d}x\) is the smaller the smaller \(A\) is, so it is smallest when \(A = N/\sigma_0\) everywhere. This is a wedge with \(\sigma = \sigma_0\), with the volume \(V = S/\sigma_0 = 5000/(220 \cdot 10^6) = 22{,}73\) cm³ and the area \(A(0) = bL/\sigma_0 = 45{,}45\) mm² at the wall. This is the area \(A^*\) from theorem 29.60 for \(V = S/\sigma_0\), since by item 3 the stress in it is \(S/V = \sigma_0\). A uniform rod with the same volume has \(A = 22{,}73\) mm² and at the wall the stress \(bL/A = 440\) MPa, twice what is allowed (num-08.out, E2). The rod of least compliance at a given volume and the rod of least volume at a given stress are therefore the same rod, the wedge.

The wedge has area zero at the free end. This is an idealization. A real rod would have a smallest area \(A_{\min} > 0\) there, which we would have to add as the condition \(A \ge A_{\min}\), and with such a condition \(A^*\) is not admissible, so the theorem in this form does not hold. CHK requires only \(\rho \ge 0\) (PDF p. 169), and so do we, and we do not treat a rod with \(A_{\min}\). The rod is in tension everywhere (\(N \ge 0\)), so buckling does not threaten even at small area, while for compressed rods a condition would have to be added, as in section 29.2. The rod model requires that the area changes slowly, because in it the stress acts only along the axis, while the surface of the wedge is inclined to the axis. For a round cross-section of the wedge the radius at the wall is \(7{,}98\) mm and the slope of the surface is \(\mathrm{d}r/\mathrm{d}x = -0{,}0040\) (num-08.out, E1). From \(\pi r^2 = 2A_0\,(1 - x/L)\) the size of the slope is \(0{,}0040/\sqrt{1 - x/L}\), which grows towards the free end and reaches \(0{,}01\) at \(1 - x/L = 0{,}4^2 = 0{,}16\). Along the first \(84\) % of the length the slope is therefore smaller than \(0{,}01\). The last \(16\) % of the length contributes little to the compliance. In the wedge \(\sigma\) is constant, so the share of this piece in the compliance (29.49) equals its share in \(S = \int_0^L N\,\mathrm{d}x\), that is \(0{,}16^2 = 0{,}0256\), less than \(3\) %.

The theorem holds for a statically determinate rod, because the proof of lemma 29.59 and step 4 of the proof of the theorem used that \(N\) is known before we choose \(A\). What would happen if \(N\) depended on \(A\)? Let us look at a rod clamped at both ends.

Example 29.62 A rod fixed at both ends (palica, vpeta na obeh koncih)

A steel rod of length \(L = 1\) m with \(E = 196\) GPa and volume \(V = 100\) cm³ is clamped at \(x = 0\) and at \(x = L\) (supports). In the joint at the distance \(a = 0{,}3L\) from the left wall it is pulled by the force \(P = 10\) kN in the direction of \(x\) (force). The left piece has length \(a\) and constant area \(A_1\), the right piece length \(L - a\) and constant area \(A_2\), and \(s = A_1 a/V\) is the share of the volume in the left, shorter piece.

How much does each piece carry? The joint moves by \(u\) in the direction of the force. The left piece stretches by \(u\), the right one shortens by \(u\). For a rod, force \(=\) stiffness \(\times\) elongation with the stiffness \(EA/L\) (step 3 of the proof of theorem 4.3), so in the left piece the tension is \(k_1 u\) with \(k_1 = EA_1/a\) and in the right one the compression is \(k_2 u\) with \(k_2 = EA_2/(L - a)\). On the joint act the force \(P\) in the direction of \(x\), the left piece pulls it to the left with the force \(k_1 u\) and the right one pushes it to the left with the force \(k_2 u\). The joint is in equilibrium, so \(P = (k_1 + k_2)\,u\). The forces in the pieces are therefore

\[N_1 = P\,\frac{k_1}{k_1 + k_2} , \qquad |N_2| = P\,\frac{k_2}{k_1 + k_2} ,\]

and they depend on the areas. Equilibrium alone is one equation, \(N_1 + |N_2| = P\), for two unknowns, so the rod is statically indeterminate (statično nedoločena): we need Hooke's law as well. The stiffness of the rod as a whole, force over displacement of the joint, is \(k = k_1 + k_2 = (EV/L^2)\,\big(s/0{,}3^2 + (1 - s)/0{,}7^2\big)\) and the compliance \(C = P\,u = P^2/k\).

The graph shows \(k\) in the unit \(EV/L^2 = 19{,}60\) MN/m against \(s\) (num-08.out, E3). It runs from \(2{,}04\) at \(s = 0\) (all material in the longer piece) to \(11{,}11\) at \(s = 1\) (all material in the shorter piece). The uniform rod has \(s = a/L = 0{,}3\), \(k = 4{,}76\) units, \(93{,}33\) MN/m, and \(C = 1{,}0714\) J. At \(s = 1\), \(k = 217{,}8\) MN/m and \(C = 0{,}4592\) J. Then the right piece has in the limit area zero and carries nothing, \(N_2 = 0\), and the left piece is a statically determinate rod of length \(a\) with the axial force \(P\), constant along the piece, for which the proportional area is constant. Its compliance is \(P^2 a/(EA_1) = P^2 a^2/(EV) = 0{,}4592\) J, as the graph says.

We would like to use the theorem by taking the areas proportional to the forces. In the uniform rod the forces are \(N_1 = 0{,}7P\) and \(|N_2| = 0{,}3P\), so \(A_1 : A_2 = 7 : 3\), which gives \(A_1 a : A_2 (L - a) = 0{,}7 \cdot 0{,}3 : 0{,}3 \cdot 0{,}7 = 1 : 1\), so \(s = 0{,}5\). The stiffness is \(6{,}58\) units (the fourth point), \(C = 0{,}7759\) J, better than \(1{,}0714\) J, but worse than \(0{,}4592\) J at \(s = 1\). In this new rod the forces are no longer \(0{,}7P\) and \(0{,}3P\), but \(0{,}8448P\) and \(0{,}1552P\), the ratio is \(5{,}44\) instead of \(2{,}33\). The areas no longer follow the forces that these areas cause. Is there a plan in which the areas follow the forces in that same plan?

Lemma 29.63 The areas cannot be proportional to their own forces (ploščini ne moreta biti sorazmerni lastnima silama)

Let the rod be as in example 29.62 with \(a \ne L/2\). There is no plan with positive areas \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) in which \(A_1 : A_2 = N_1 : |N_2|\), where \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) are the forces in this plan.

Proof

We must show that proportionality would require \(a = L/2\).

  1. By example 29.62 the forces in a plan are \(N_1 = P\,k_1/(k_1 + k_2)\) and \(|N_2| = P\,k_2/(k_1 + k_2)\), so \(N_1/|N_2| = k_1/k_2\).
  2. From \(k_1 = EA_1/a\) and \(k_2 = EA_2/(L - a)\) we get \(k_1/k_2 = \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}\cdot\dfrac{L - a}{a}\).
  3. The requirement \(A_1/A_2 = N_1/|N_2|\) and steps 1 and 2 give \(\dfrac{A_1}{A_2} = \dfrac{A_1}{A_2}\cdot\dfrac{L - a}{a}\). Since \(A_1/A_2 > 0\), we must have \((L - a)/a = 1\), so \(a = L/2\).

Source: The proof is not from the source.

The reason is Hooke's law. At full stress the elongation per unit length \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E\) would have to be equal in size in both pieces, but the left piece stretches by \(u\) over the length \(a\), the right one shortens by \(u\) over the length \(L - a\), and \(u/a = u/(L - a)\) only for \(a = L/2\). If we correct the areas by the rule "the new area is proportional to the present force", the ratio of the areas is multiplied in every step by \((L - a)/a = 7/3\) (step 2 of the lemma), and all the material moves into the shorter piece, to which the computation has already brought us. Theorem 29.60 does not hold for a rod clamped at both ends, because the proof assumes that the axial force \(N\) is determined by the load alone. The best plan in the family \(s\) is \(s = 1\), but we do not know whether it is the best among all plans. The condition of constant energy density is derived by CHK for an abstract system with an energy that is linear in the design function, and it does not require static determinacy (§8.3.1, theorem 8.1, PDF pp. 168 and 172).

The same problem for a beam, where the variable is the width of the cross-section, is in CHK §8.3.3 (PDF pp. 175-178). First, for a given cross-section area, one looks for the shape that gives the largest second moment of area, and shows that the material must be put in the flanges (the I profile, PDF p. 176). Then, for a simply supported beam with constant load, the width along the beam is computed from the condition of constant energy density (PDF pp. 177-178). We do not repeat this here, because it needs the beam equation of chapter 5 and the calculus of variations with a second derivative.

A summary of how each claim is shown. Equation (29.47) is the equilibrium of the piece, with the sign from the definition of the axial force. Lemma 29.59 is derived by integration by parts from the definitions of stress and strain. Theorem 29.60 is proved by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the inner product with the area function as the weight. Example 29.62 is computed from the equilibrium of the joint and Hooke's law, and lemma 29.63 from the same two equations. Notation: \(C\) is compliance, and in chapter 10 the damping matrix. The stress \(\sigma_0\) is the allowed stress without a safety factor, which would only multiply all the areas by the same number. The letter \(S\) denotes the sum of axial forces, because \(I\) in chapter 5 is the second moment of area.
