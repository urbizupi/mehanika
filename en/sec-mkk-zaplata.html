Chapter 15 Continuum finite elements

15.7 The patch test and incompatible modes (preizkus z zaplato in nekompatibilni načini)

We have the triangle (section 15.4) and the quadrilateral (section 15.5), Gauss rules for their stiffness matrix (section 15.6) and the problem on which we compared them: the cantilever of chapter 11. Such a comparison is expensive, since the mesh \(40 \times 8\) cells has \(720\) unknowns, and it does not tell whether the error lies in the element, in the integration or in the mesh. We look for the smallest test that a new element must pass before we trust it with the cantilever, an engine housing or anything whose solution we do not know. That test is in this section, and it will answer a second question as well: why the four-node quadrilateral fails in bending and how we repair it without spoiling what it can do.

The model is a body of steel (\(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\)), linearly elastic, with small strains (chapter 1), plane stress (theorem 11.33), without body forces. All computations are in SymPy and NumPy (num-07.out).

15.7.1 The patch (zaplata)

The test is called the patch test (preizkus z zaplato). In sewing a patch is a piece of cloth that covers a small part of a surface, and the name is used here for the same reason: a small piece of the body, covered by a few elements, at least one of which has a node in the interior, so that not the whole mesh lies on the boundary.

From history

The patch test was devised by Irons. COOK (PDF p. 252) writes that since then it has been analysed, explained and understood anew at length. The question it answers is the following (PDF p. 253): if we solve a problem again and again with an ever finer mesh, does the sequence of solutions converge to the true displacements, strains and stresses? The answer is yes for stable elements that pass the patch test (PDF pp. 253-254). The test asks whether an assembly of elements can represent a constant state of strain.

Let us return to the patch and to the question why constant strain is the right thing to test. Take the cantilever of chapter 11 (\(L = 1\) m, strain \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \kappa y\) with a stress that decreases linearly with \(L - x\)). On a cell of length \(100\) mm at the clamping the strain changes along the length by \(100/1000 = 10\) %, on a cell of length \(25\) mm by \(2{,}5\) %. With every doubling of the number of cells the departure from constant strain within a cell is halved. A mesh that cannot represent constant strain therefore cannot reach the true solution even with fine subdivision, since it cannot even do what will come ever closer to the true field on every cell. We test this on the simplest field with constant strains.

Take a rectangle \(240 \times 120\) mm and divide it into five quadrilaterals, as figure 15.26 shows.

The mesh has four boundary nodes (with numbers \(1\) to \(4\)) at the corners of the rectangle and four interior ones at the points \((40, 20)\), \((180, 30)\), \((160, 80)\) and \((80, 80)\) mm. The elements are deliberately distorted: the ratio of the smallest to the largest value of \(J\) at the corners of an element runs from \(0{,}17\) to \(0{,}50\) (num-07.out, part 2), and none is a rectangle. At the boundary nodes we prescribe the displacements of the linear field

\[u = 10^{-3}\Big(x + \frac{y}{2}\Big) , \qquad v = 10^{-3}\Big(\frac{x}{2} + y\Big) , \tag{15.35}\]

which has strains \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = 10^{-3}\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = 10^{-3}\) everywhere. By Hooke's law (theorem 11.33) the stresses are equal everywhere, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = 280{,}0\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = 75{,}38\) MPa. The equilibrium equations (theorem 2.28) without body forces are satisfied, since the derivatives of constant stresses are zero. The field (15.35) is therefore the true solution of the problem in which the displacement of this field is prescribed on the whole boundary. The mesh receives no force, only boundary displacements, and the question is whether it computes the true field at the interior nodes. The solution of the element Q4 with \(2 \times 2\) Gauss points (section 15.6) is the orange mesh: the largest relative error of the displacements of the interior nodes is \(2{,}7 \cdot 10^{-16}\), which is the rounding of the computer, and the same with a single point (\(5{,}6 \cdot 10^{-16}\)). The same holds for each of the six basic linear fields separately: two translations, a rotation and three strains (errors from \(10^{-16}\) to \(10^{-15}\), num-07.out, part 1). Is this an accident of the distorted mesh or a theorem?

15.7.2 The patch theorem (izrek o zaplati)

The precise question is as follows. Take a mesh of elements and the space \(V_{\mathrm{h}}\) of displacement fields that the family of elements gives on this mesh. The solution of the patch problem is a field \(u_{\mathrm{h}} \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\) that has the prescribed values of the field \(\vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\) at the boundary nodes (a constant vector \(\vek a\) and a constant matrix \(\mat G\)) and satisfies the weak equation \(B(u_{\mathrm{h}}, w) = 0\) for every field \(w \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\) that is zero at all boundary nodes. This is the problem of chapter 6 with the bilinear form \(B\) of the body (definition 6.6) and without load, \(\ell = 0\). The theorem says when the solution is exactly the field \(\vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\).

Theorem 15.47 Patch theorem (izrek o zaplati)

Let the mesh of elements be connected through shared edges (in space through shared faces) and let a body of isotropic material (\(E > 0\), \(-1 < \nu < \tfrac12\)) fill the patch. Let \(V_{\mathrm{h}}\) be the space of displacement fields that the family of elements gives, and let the following hold:

  1. Conformity (skladnost): every field of \(V_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a piecewise smooth field (definition 15.13), and on every edge of an element it is determined by the values at the nodes of that edge (lemma 15.7).
  2. Completeness for linear fields: for every constant vector \(\vek a\) and constant matrix \(\mat G\) the field \(\vek x \mapsto \vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\) is an element of the space \(V_{\mathrm{h}}\).
  3. Support: the only rigid motion that is zero at all boundary nodes is zero (as in lemma 6.7, item 3).

Then the patch problem has exactly one solution, and it is \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\) on the whole body. The displacements, strains and stresses are exact at every point.

Proof

We must show that the field \(u_{\mathrm{lin}} = \vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\) is admissible, that it satisfies the weak equation and that the solution is unique.

  1. By assumption 2, \(u_{\mathrm{lin}} \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\), and at the boundary nodes it has the prescribed values. It is therefore admissible.
  2. The strain is \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}}(u_{\mathrm{lin}}) = \tfrac12(G_{\mathrm{ij}} + G_{\mathrm{ji}})\) (definition 1.115), constant. By Hooke's law (theorem 3.11) the stress \(\sigma^0_{\mathrm{ij}}\) is constant. Hence \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \partial\sigma^0_{\mathrm{ij}}/\partial x_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\), and the equilibrium equations (theorem 2.28) without body forces hold. The field \(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is therefore the true solution of the problem without load and with this boundary displacement.
  3. Let \(w \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\) be zero at all boundary nodes. By assumption 1, \(w\) is zero on the whole boundary of the body, because the edge of an element is determined by the nodes of that edge, and it is piecewise smooth. By theorem 15.15 the true solution satisfies the weak equation with every piecewise smooth field: when we cut by elements the terms on shared faces cancel, because \(w\) is continuous and the stress \(\sigma^0\) is constant, and on the boundary of the body \(w = 0\). Hence \(B(u_{\mathrm{lin}}, w) = \ell(w) = 0\).
  4. Let \(u_1\) and \(u_2\) be solutions and \(d = u_1 - u_2\). The field \(d \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\) is zero at the boundary nodes, so by step 3 and bilinearity \(B(d, w) = B(u_1, w) - B(u_2, w) = 0\) for every such \(w\), also for \(w = d\): \(B(d, d) = 0\).
  5. By lemma 15.14, \(B(d, d)\) is the sum over elements of the energies \(2\int_{\mathrm{e}} \Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\), and the density \(\Phi\) is positive for every strain that is not zero (theorem 3.48, item 1). Hence the strain of the field \(d\) is zero on every element, and by theorem 1.134 the field \(d\) is a rigid motion on every element.
  6. Two elements with a shared edge have the same rigid motion. The field \(d\) is continuous, so the rigid motions \(\vek b + \mat W\,\vek x\) (antisymmetric \(\mat W\)) agree on the whole shared edge, that is in the plane at two different points, in space at three points not on a line, and with that they are equal (the difference \(\vek b' + \mat W'\,\vek x\) is zero at these points and by the same argument as in the proof of lemma 6.7 zero everywhere). The mesh is connected through shared edges, so \(d\) is a single rigid motion on the whole body. It is zero at all boundary nodes, so by assumption 3 it equals zero. The solution is unique and is \(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\).

Source: ONATE1 section 6.10 (PDF pp. 237-238) describes test A (a known linear field prescribed at all nodes of the patch) and test B (only the boundary nodes prescribed) and says that passing them is necessary for convergence of the element. COOK section 6.13 (PDF p. 253) describes the procedure with a patch and an interior node. The theorem and the proof in steps are not from the sources: the sources state the test, they do not prove it.

The theorem says more than we tested: the test need not be made for every patch separately, because two properties of the element suffice, conformity and completeness for linear fields. We check them for the quadrilateral.

Lemma 15.48 The four-node quadrilateral passes the patch test (štirikotnik s štirimi vozlišči opravi zaplato)

Let the four-node quadrilateral (definition 15.30) be on a mesh with \(J > 0\) at all corners of all elements. Then it satisfies assumptions 1 and 2 of theorem 15.47. The force on the nodes under a constant stress \(\sigma^0\) is given exactly by every Gauss product rule with at least one point in each direction, so \(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\) satisfies the discrete equations also under numerical integration with \(1 \times 1\) and \(2 \times 2\) points. That the solution is unique is, under numerical integration, only checked on the patch (num-07.out, part 1).

Proof

We must show conformity, completeness and exactness of the integration of the force.

  1. Let the edge be \(\eta = -1\) of the element. On it \(N_3 = N_4 = 0\), so the position and the displacement are \(\vek x = (1 - t)\,\vek x_1 + t\,\vek x_2\) and \(\vek u = (1 - t)\,\vek u_1 + t\,\vek u_2\) with \(t = (1 + \xi)/2\). The edge is a segment, the field on it is linear and is determined by the two nodes of the edge. The neighbouring element has the same node values on the same edge and the same position function, so the two fields agree. The same holds for the other three edges.
  2. On the element the field is a polynomial in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), and the map is injective for \(J > 0\) at the corners (theorem 15.32) and has a smooth inverse by theorem 4.51, so the field is continuously differentiable on the element up to the edge. With step 1 this is assumption 1.
  3. By lemma 15.35, \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\vek x_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek x\), hence \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,(\vek a + \mat G\,\vek x_{\mathrm{i}}) = \vek a + \mat G\,\vek x\). This is assumption 2.
  4. For the force \(\vek f_{\mathrm{i}} = \int \nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\sigma^0\,\mathrm{d}A = \iint_{[-1,1]^2} J\,\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\sigma^0\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta\) (theorem 4.59) we have \(J\,\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x = y_\eta N_{\mathrm{i},\xi} - y_\xi N_{\mathrm{i},\eta}\) and \(J\,\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y = -x_\eta N_{\mathrm{i},\xi} + x_\xi N_{\mathrm{i},\eta}\) (by the chain rule \((N_{,\xi}, N_{,\eta})^\T = \mat F^\T\,(N_{,\mathrm{x}}, N_{,\mathrm{y}})^\T\) with the Jacobian matrix \(\mat F = \begin{bmatrix} x_\xi & x_\eta \\ y_\xi & y_\eta\end{bmatrix}\) and \(J = \det\mat F\), and the inverse of a \(2 \times 2\) matrix gives these two equalities). The derivative \(N_{\mathrm{i},\xi}\) is a polynomial of degree \(0\) in \(\xi\) and \(1\) in \(\eta\), and \(y_\eta\) of degree \(1\) in \(\xi\) and \(0\) in \(\eta\). The product therefore has degree at most \(1\) in each variable. A Gauss rule with \(q \ge 1\) points is exact up to degree \(2q - 1 \ge 1\) (theorem 15.41), and the product rule in each variable separately (corollary 15.42). With \(u_{\mathrm{lin}} \in V_{\mathrm{h}}\) we have \(\mat B\,\vek u_{\mathrm{e}} = \{\varepsilon^0\}\) at every Gauss point, so the numerical force \(\sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,J_{\mathrm{g}}\,\mat B_{\mathrm{g}}^\T\,\sigma^0\) equals the exact one. The discrete equations at \(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\) are then the same as the exact ones, and steps 1 to 3 of theorem 15.47 hold. Unique solvability (steps 4 to 6) is not proved under numerical integration, but it is checked on the patch (num-07.out, part 1).

Source: COOK section 6.12 (PDF p. 252, end of the section) shows that the field of an isoparametric element contains a complete linear polynomial. The conformity of the edges and the exactness of the integration are ours, not from the source.

The patch of figure 15.26 is thereby explained: the error \(10^{-16}\) is expected, not accidental. With one integration point it is the same, but such an element has spurious modes (definition 15.45, lemma 15.46) that the patch does not show: COOK (PDF p. 254) warns that an element can pass the test although it is unstable, because it has too few Gauss points. The test is therefore necessary for convergence (ONATE1, PDF p. 238), and by COOK (PDF pp. 253-254) it suffices if a stable element passes it (stated, not proved in this book). About accuracy on a coarse mesh it says nothing, as the next example shows.

15.7.3 The quadrilateral and bending

The cantilever of chapter 11, divided into \(10 \times 2\) quadrilaterals Q4 (cell \(100 \times 20\) mm), gives \(0{,}292\) of the true deflection of the end. The triangles of section 15.4 gave \(0{,}115\), and we knew the cause: constant strain cannot follow \(\kappa y\). The quadrilateral has in the displacement also the term \(\xi\eta\), its strain is not constant. Why is it still too stiff?

Look at a single cell \(2a \times 2b\) in pure bending with curvature \(\kappa\) (figure 15.27). The exact field is the one we used for the triangle, \(u = \kappa x y\) and \(v = -\kappa(x^2 + \nu y^2)/2\). Its strains are \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \kappa y\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\nu\kappa y\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \kappa x - \kappa x = 0\), the stress is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\kappa y\) and the others zero, which agrees with equilibrium and Hooke's law (theorems 2.28 and 11.33).

The grey rectangle is the cell, the measures \(2a\) and \(2b\) are its size. The exact field bends the horizontal lines downward on both sides (the term \(-\kappa x^2/2\) in \(v\)) and tilts the vertical ones, because \(u = \kappa x y\). The quadrilateral Q4 has on each side a field that is linear along it (lemma 15.48, step 1), so its horizontal lines stay straight. The four corners have the same value \(v = -\kappa(a^2 + \nu b^2)/2\) (all have the same \(x^2\) and \(y^2\)), so \(v\) is constant: all points of the cell move in \(y\) by the same \(v\). This is a rigid shift, which does not deform the cell, so it is subtracted in both parts of the figure, and the horizontal sides stay horizontal. The field \(u = \kappa x y\) is bilinear, so the quadrilateral reproduces it exactly, and the vertical sides tilt as in the true field. But the horizontal lines are straight and the vertical ones tilted, so the right angle between them changes by \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \kappa x\), which at \(x = a\) equals \(\kappa a\). This is the angle between the tilted side and the vertical at the upper right corner. The exact field has shear zero.

Lemma 15.49 Energy of the Q4 element in pure bending (energija štirikotnika Q4 v čistem upogibu)

Let the cell \([-a, a] \times [-b, b]\) be of isotropic material in plane stress (\(E\), \(\nu\)). Let \(u_{\mathrm{h}}\) be the field of the quadrilateral Q4 with the node values of the exact field of pure bending \(u = \kappa x y\), \(v = -\kappa(x^2 + \nu y^2)/2\). Then the ratio of the energy of the field \(u_{\mathrm{h}}\) to the energy of the exact field on the cell is

\[\frac{U_{\mathrm{h}}}{U} = \frac{1}{1 - \nu^2} + \frac{a^2}{2\,(1 + \nu)\,b^2} . \tag{15.36}\]
Proof

We must find the strains of the field \(u_{\mathrm{h}}\), express the energy and compare it with the exact one.

  1. The corners \((\pm a, \pm b)\) have the same value of \(v\), so \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is constant (\(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\), lemma 7.3). With the map of the rectangle \(x = a\xi\), \(y = b\eta\) we have \(u = \kappa ab\,\xi\eta\) with the node values \(\kappa ab\,\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\). With \(N_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi)(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta)\) we get \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14\big(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} + \xi\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \eta_{\mathrm{i}} + \eta\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}} + 4\,\xi\eta\big) = \xi\eta\), since \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}^2 = \eta_{\mathrm{i}}^2 = 1\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \eta_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\). Hence \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = \kappa x y\). The strains are \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \kappa y\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \kappa x\).
  2. By lemma 15.23 the energy density is \(\tfrac12\{\varepsilon\}^\T\mat C\{\varepsilon\}\). With \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) it is \(\tfrac12\big(\tfrac{E}{1 - \nu^2}\,\kappa^2 y^2 + \tfrac{E}{2(1 + \nu)}\,\kappa^2 x^2\big)\).
  3. The integral over the cell gives, with \(\int y^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 4ab^3/3\) and \(\int x^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 4a^3 b/3\) (theorem 4.9), the energy \(U_{\mathrm{h}} = \tfrac12 E\kappa^2\,\tfrac{4ab^3}{3}\big(\tfrac{1}{1 - \nu^2} + \tfrac{a^2}{2(1 + \nu)b^2}\big)\).
  4. The exact field has \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\kappa y\), so \(U = \tfrac12 E\kappa^2\,\tfrac{4ab^3}{3}\). The quotient is equation (15.36).

Source: The computation is ours, SymPy and NumPy (num-07.out, part 7). COOK (PDF p. 233) says that the four-node plane element shows the bending mode too stiffly because of parasitic shear.

The ratio has two parts, each with its own cause. The first, \(1/(1 - \nu^2) = 1{,}099\), comes from \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\): Q4 cannot show the transverse contraction \(-\nu\kappa y\), because \(v\) is constant, and the element is therefore \(9{,}9\) % too stiff. The second, \(a^2/(2(1+\nu)b^2)\), is the energy of the parasitic shear \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \kappa x\): a quantity that is zero in the true field. At \(a/b = 1\) it is \(0{,}385\), in total \(1{,}484\), at \(a/b = 2{,}5\) it is \(2{,}404\), in total \(3{,}503\), at \(a/b = 5\) it is \(9{,}615\), in total \(10{,}714\) (num-07.out, part 7). The cell of the cantilever \(100 \times 20\) mm has \(a/b = 5\): the field that Q4 assembles from the node values of true bending stores \(10{,}7\) times too much energy, so the mesh does not choose such a field and bends less. This is the cause that the lemma shows, not a derivation of the number \(0{,}292\), which the whole mesh gives. The shear is the larger the longer the cell relative to its height, which the square of the ratio \(a/b\) in equation (15.36) shows.

The quadrilateral is better than the triangle (\(0{,}292\) against \(0{,}115\)), because it has the term \(\xi\eta\), with which it shows \(u = \kappa x y\) exactly. The true field has in \(v\) also the quadratic terms \(x^2\) and \(y^2\), and precisely these two are missing from both.

15.7.4 Incompatible modes (nekompatibilni načini)

We add the terms \(x^2\) and \(y^2\) to the element as extra functions. In an isoparametric element these are \(1 - \xi^2\) and \(1 - \eta^2\): on the edges \(\xi = \pm 1\) the first vanishes, on the edges \(\eta = \pm 1\) the second, and at the corners both are zero.

Definition 15.50 Element with incompatible modes Q6 and QM6 (element z nekompatibilnimi načini Q6 in QM6)

Let the four-node quadrilateral (definition 15.30) have node displacements \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}}\). The element Q6 has the displacement

\[\vek u(\xi, \eta) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{4} N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi, \eta)\,\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} + (1 - \xi^2)\,\vek c_1 + (1 - \eta^2)\,\vek c_2 , \tag{15.37}\]

where \(\vek c_1\) and \(\vek c_2\) are vectors of two numbers each, the internal degrees of freedom of this element (four numbers, no node). The strain is \(\{\varepsilon\} = \mat B\,\vek u_{\mathrm{e}} + \mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\vek c\) with \(\vek c = (\vek c_1, \vek c_2)\), where \(\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\) is obtained from the derivatives of the functions \(1 - \xi^2\) and \(1 - \eta^2\) with respect to \(x\) and \(y\) using the inverse of the Jacobian matrix \(\mat F\) of the map (by the chain rule, as for \(\mat B\)). We eliminate the internal degrees of freedom on the element, so \(\partial\Pi/\partial\vek c = \vek 0\) and

\[\vek c = -\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}^{-1}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{cu}}\,\vek u_{\mathrm{e}} , \qquad \mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat K_{\mathrm{uu}} - \mat K_{\mathrm{uc}}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}^{-1}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{cu}} , \tag{15.38}\]

where \(\mat K_{\mathrm{uu}}\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{uc}}\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\) are blocks of the stiffness matrix of the element with the vector \((\vek u_{\mathrm{e}}, \vek c)\). The element QM6 is Q6 in which in \(\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\) we use everywhere the Jacobian matrix \(\mat F_0\) and the determinant \(J_0\) at the centre of the element \(\xi = \eta = 0\) instead of \(\mat F\) and \(J\): \(\mat B_{\mathrm{c}} = (J_0/J)\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}|_{\mat{F} = \mat{F}_0}\).

Source: COOK section 6.6 (PDF pp. 233-234): the field of the element Q6, the internal ("nodeless") degrees of freedom and the correction that gives QM6. The elimination is by COOK section 6.7 (PDF p. 235). The notation with \(\vek c\) is ours.

The name Q6 is COOK's (PDF p. 233). We read the letter Q as quadrilateral and the number six as the number of functions in each displacement component, four shape functions and two extra ones, but this reading is ours, the source does not explain it. The letter M in QM6 means modified (PDF p. 234). The modes are called incompatible because on the edge of the element they are not determined by the nodes of the edge: the extra function \((1 - \eta^2)\,\vek c_2\) is zero on the edge \(\xi = 1\) only at the corners and not in between, and the neighbouring element has its own \(\vek c_2\). COOK (PDF p. 233) says that we call them incompatible or nonconforming because at places other than the nodes they allow overlaps or gaps between neighbouring elements.

We integrate the elements Q6 and QM6 like Q4 with \(2 \times 2\) Gauss points (section 15.6), and all computations of this section are done so. The elimination (15.38) needs the inverse of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\). We show more: the matrix is positive definite, so the elimination is not a division by zero.

Lemma 15.51 The matrix of the incompatible modes is positive definite (matrika \(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\) nekompatibilnih načinov je pozitivno definitna)

Let a quadrilateral have \(J > 0\) at all corners and let \(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\) be the block of the stiffness matrix of the element Q6 or QM6 (definition 15.50), integrated exactly or with \(2 \times 2\) Gauss points. Then \(\vek c^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\,\vek c > 0\) for every \(\vek c \ne \vek 0\).

Proof

We must show that the extra field \(\varphi_1\,\vek c_1 + \varphi_2\,\vek c_2\) with \(\varphi_1 = 1 - \xi^2\) and \(\varphi_2 = 1 - \eta^2\) is free of strain at two Gauss points only for \(\vek c = \vek 0\).

  1. The quantity \(\vek c^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\,\vek c\) is the sum over the Gauss points of the terms \(w_{\mathrm{g}}\,J_{\mathrm{g}}\,\{\varepsilon\}_{\mathrm{g}}^\T\mat C\{\varepsilon\}_{\mathrm{g}}\), where \(\{\varepsilon\}_{\mathrm{g}} = (\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\vek c)_{\mathrm{g}}\) is the strain of the extra field. The weights \(w_{\mathrm{g}}\) and \(J_{\mathrm{g}}\) are positive (theorem 15.32, item 1), and the density is positive for every strain that is not zero (theorem 3.48, item 1). If the sum is zero, the strain of the extra field is zero at every Gauss point. Under exact integration it is then zero everywhere, hence also at these points.
  2. The derivative of the extra field with respect to \((\xi, \eta)\) is the matrix with the columns \(-2\xi\,\vek c_1\) and \(-2\eta\,\vek c_2\). The derivative with respect to \((x, y)\) is this matrix multiplied from the right by \(\mat F^{-1}\), for QM6 by \(\mat F_0^{-1}\) and by the positive number \(J_0/J\), which does not change a zero. The strain is the symmetric part of the derivative (definition 1.115), so it is zero exactly when the derivative is antisymmetric. An antisymmetric \(2 \times 2\) matrix is \(\omega\mat R\) with \(\mat R = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\) and some number \(\omega\). Multiplying by \(\mat F\) (for QM6 by \(\mat F_0\)) from the right gives \(-2\xi\,\vek c_1 = \omega\,\mat R\,\vek x_{,\xi}\) and \(-2\eta\,\vek c_2 = \omega\,\mat R\,\vek x_{,\eta}\), where \(\vek x_{,\xi}\) and \(\vek x_{,\eta}\) are the columns of the matrix \(\mat F\) (for QM6 the constant columns of the matrix \(\mat F_0\)).
  3. For the bilinear map, \(\vek x_{,\eta} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \tfrac14\eta_{\mathrm{i}}(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi)\,\vek x_{\mathrm{i}}\) depends only on \(\xi\), and \(\vek x_{,\xi} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \tfrac14\xi_{\mathrm{i}}(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta)\,\vek x_{\mathrm{i}}\) only on \(\eta\), in which it is affine. Take the points \((g, g)\) and \((g, -g)\) with \(g = 1/\sqrt3\) and their numbers \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_2\). Both have the same \(\vek x_{,\eta}\), and the second equation of step 2 gives \(-2g\,\vek c_2 = \omega_1\mat R\,\vek x_{,\eta}\) and \(2g\,\vek c_2 = \omega_2\mat R\,\vek x_{,\eta}\). The sum is \((\omega_1 + \omega_2)\,\mat R\,\vek x_{,\eta} = \vek 0\). The column \(\vek x_{,\eta}\) is not zero, since \(J \ne 0\), and \(\mat R\) is invertible, so \(\omega_2 = -\omega_1\).
  4. The first equation of step 2 gives \(-2g\,\vek c_1 = \omega_1\mat R\,\vek x_{,\xi}(g)\) and \(-2g\,\vek c_1 = \omega_2\mat R\,\vek x_{,\xi}(-g) = -\omega_1\mat R\,\vek x_{,\xi}(-g)\), where the value in brackets is that of \(\eta\). The difference of the equations is \(\omega_1\mat R\,\big(\vek x_{,\xi}(g) + \vek x_{,\xi}(-g)\big) = \vek 0\). Since \(\vek x_{,\xi}\) is affine in \(\eta\), the sum in brackets is \(2\,\vek x_{,\xi}(0)\), the first column of the matrix \(\mat F_0\), which is not zero, since \(J_0 > 0\). Hence \(\omega_1 = \omega_2 = 0\), and the equations of step 2 give \(\vek c_1 = \vek c_2 = \vek 0\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. On the patch the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\) is from \(2{,}1 \cdot 10^{11}\) to \(4{,}6 \cdot 10^{11}\) N/m for Q6 and from \(6{,}3 \cdot 10^{10}\) to \(3{,}5 \cdot 10^{11}\) N/m for QM6 (num-07.out, part 9).

Back to pure bending. Does the new element contain the true field at all?

Lemma 15.52 The Q6 element contains the pure bending field (element Q6 vsebuje polje čistega upogiba)

On the rectangle \([-a, a] \times [-b, b]\) with \(x = a\xi\) and \(y = b\eta\) the field \(u = \kappa x y\), \(v = -\kappa(x^2 + \nu y^2)/2\) is a field of the element Q6 with the node values of this field and \(\vek c_1 = (0, \kappa a^2/2)\), \(\vek c_2 = (0, \nu\kappa b^2/2)\).

Proof

We must show that each component has the form (15.37).

  1. The component \(u = \kappa a b\,\xi\eta\) is bilinear and the node values are \(\kappa x_{\mathrm{i}} y_{\mathrm{i}}\), so \(u = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} u_{\mathrm{i}}\) (step 1 of lemma 15.49) with \(\vek c = \vek 0\) in the component \(u\).
  2. The component \(v = -\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(a^2\xi^2 + \nu b^2\eta^2) = -\tfrac{\kappa}{2}(a^2 + \nu b^2) + \tfrac{\kappa a^2}{2}(1 - \xi^2) + \tfrac{\nu\kappa b^2}{2}(1 - \eta^2)\). The constant is the value of \(v\) at all corners, and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\) (lemma 7.3), so it equals \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} v_{\mathrm{i}}\). The other two terms are the extra functions with \(c_{1\mathrm{y}} = \kappa a^2/2\) and \(c_{2\mathrm{y}} = \nu\kappa b^2/2\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. Computation of the energy (num-07.out, part 7): the ratio \(U_{\mathrm{h}}/U\) for the element Q6 is \(1{,}000000\) at \(a/b = 1\), \(2{,}5\) and \(5\). The elimination chooses the \(\vek c\) with the least energy at given node displacements (\(\mat K_{\mathrm{cc}}\) is positive definite, lemma 15.51), so the energy is \(\le U\), because the exact field is in the family. Equality holds because the exact field is stationary in \(\vek c\): \(\int \sigma : \varepsilon(\varphi\,\vek c')\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) for every \(\vek c'\), since \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\kappa y\) is odd in \(y\), the derivatives \(\partial\varphi_1/\partial x\) and \(\partial\varphi_2/\partial x\) do not depend on \(y\) or are zero, and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) (checked with SymPy).

Is Q6 then the solution? The extra functions are zero at the corners but not in between, and every element has its own \(\vek c_1\), \(\vek c_2\). Conformity is lost, so theorem 15.47 no longer holds. We test the patch of figure 15.26 with the element Q6 instead of Q4.

Figure 15.28 shows the true field and the edge of each element Q6, drawn with its extra functions. The elements \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) have a shared edge \(2\)-\(6\), but each bends it additionally in its own way, because each has its own \(\vek c_2\). The edge has the same node values on both sides, so the difference of the fields is only the difference of the extra terms, \((1 - \eta^2)\,(\vek c_2^{(1)} - \vek c_2^{(2)})\), a parabola with the peak \((-9{,}92;\,-8{,}99)\) µm, that is \(13{,}4\) µm, when the largest displacement component in the patch is \(300\) µm (node \(3\), num-07.out, part 4). The field jumps across the edge and is not an element of the class of piecewise smooth fields, in which theorem 15.15 holds.

And the computer shows what happens. For the field (15.35) Q6 gives a relative error of the displacements of the interior nodes \(0{,}142\), that is 14,2 %, and QM6 \(2{,}3 \cdot 10^{-16}\). For the individual fields: the translations and the rotation Q6 reproduces (errors from \(2 \cdot 10^{-16}\) to \(6 \cdot 10^{-16}\)), the three strains it does not: \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\) with an error \(0{,}022\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}}\) with \(0{,}271\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) with \(0{,}061\) (num-07.out, part 1). The stress at the centre of the element \(e_1\) is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = 373\) MPa instead of \(280\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = 512\) instead of \(280\) MPa, the element \(e_3\) has \(357\) and \(448\) MPa (part 4). The extra degrees of freedom, which should be zero, have values up to \(142{,}5\) µm at \(e_1\). A rigid motion does not excite the extra functions, because it has zero strain, and therefore Q6 is exact for translations and rotation.

We find the cause with the same computation as in the patch theorem. If \(\vek u = u_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\vek c = \vek 0\) were a solution, the condition \(\partial\Pi/\partial\vek c = \vek 0\) would have to hold at constant stress \(\sigma^0\): \(\sigma^0 : \int_{\mathrm{e}} \varepsilon(\varphi\,\vek c)\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) for the extra functions \(\varphi_1 = 1 - \xi^2\) and \(\varphi_2 = 1 - \eta^2\). We compute this integral.

Lemma 15.53 Integrals of the extra functions over the quadrilateral (integrali dodatnih funkcij po štirikotniku)

Let the quadrilateral with nodes \(\vek p_{\mathrm{k}} = (x_{\mathrm{k}}, y_{\mathrm{k}})\), \(k = 1, \dots, 4\), at the corners \((\xi, \eta) = (-1, -1), (1, -1), (1, 1), (-1, 1)\) be isoparametrically mapped with \(J > 0\). Let \(\vek q = \vek p_1 - \vek p_2 + \vek p_3 - \vek p_4 = (q_{\mathrm{x}}, q_{\mathrm{y}})\) and \(\varphi_1 = 1 - \xi^2\), \(\varphi_2 = 1 - \eta^2\). Then

\[\int \frac{\partial\varphi_1}{\partial x}\,\mathrm{d}A = -\tfrac23\,q_{\mathrm{y}} , \quad \int \frac{\partial\varphi_1}{\partial y}\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac23\,q_{\mathrm{x}} , \quad \int \frac{\partial\varphi_2}{\partial x}\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac23\,q_{\mathrm{y}} , \quad \int \frac{\partial\varphi_2}{\partial y}\,\mathrm{d}A = -\tfrac23\,q_{\mathrm{x}} , \tag{15.39}\]

and all four are zero exactly when \(\vek q = \vek 0\), that is \(\vek p_2 - \vek p_1 = \vek p_3 - \vek p_4\): the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

Proof

We must show that \(J\,\varphi_{,\mathrm{x}}\) and \(J\,\varphi_{,\mathrm{y}}\) are polynomials in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), integrate them over the square and read off the condition.

  1. As in step 4 of lemma 15.48, \(\varphi_{,\mathrm{x}} = (y_\eta\,\varphi_{,\xi} - y_\xi\,\varphi_{,\eta})/J\) and \(\varphi_{,\mathrm{y}} = (-x_\eta\,\varphi_{,\xi} + x_\xi\,\varphi_{,\eta})/J\).
  2. The bilinear map has \(y_\eta = \tfrac14[-(1 - \xi)y_1 - (1 + \xi)y_2 + (1 + \xi)y_3 + (1 - \xi)y_4]\), which is \(\tfrac14(y_3 + y_4 - y_1 - y_2) + \tfrac14\,\xi\,(y_1 - y_2 + y_3 - y_4)\). The coefficient of \(\xi\) is \(q_{\mathrm{y}}/4\). The same computation shows that in \(y_\xi\) the coefficient of \(\eta\) is \(q_{\mathrm{y}}/4\), in \(x_\eta\) the coefficient of \(\xi\) is \(q_{\mathrm{x}}/4\) and in \(x_\xi\) the coefficient of \(\eta\) is \(q_{\mathrm{x}}/4\).
  3. For \(\varphi_1\) we have \(\varphi_{1,\xi} = -2\xi\) and \(\varphi_{1,\eta} = 0\), so \(J\,\varphi_{1,\mathrm{x}} = -2\xi\,y_\eta\). By theorem 4.59, \(\int \varphi_{1,\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \iint_{[-1,1]^2}J\,\varphi_{1,\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta\), and by theorem 4.9 this is \(-2\int_{-1}^{1}\xi\,(c_0 + \tfrac{q_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi\cdot 2 = -2\cdot\tfrac{q_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\cdot\tfrac23\cdot 2 = -\tfrac23 q_{\mathrm{y}}\), since \(\int_{-1}^{1}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\xi = 0\) and \(\int_{-1}^{1}\xi^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi = \tfrac23\).
  4. In the same way: \(J\,\varphi_{1,\mathrm{y}} = 2\xi\,x_\eta\) gives \(\tfrac23 q_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(J\,\varphi_{2,\mathrm{x}} = 2\eta\,y_\xi\) gives \(\tfrac23 q_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(J\,\varphi_{2,\mathrm{y}} = -2\eta\,x_\xi\) gives \(-\tfrac23 q_{\mathrm{x}}\).
  5. \(\vek q = \vek 0\) is equivalent to \(\vek p_2 - \vek p_1 = \vek p_3 - \vek p_4\), which is the condition for the quadrilateral to be a parallelogram.

Theorem 15.54 Patch theorem with incompatible modes (zaplata z nekompatibilnimi načini)

Let the Q4 part of the elements satisfy assumptions 1 to 3 of theorem 15.47 and let all elements of the patch be Q6 or QM6 with \(J > 0\).

  1. If on every element \(\int_{\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \mat 0\), the pair (\(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\), \(\vek c = \vek 0\) on all elements) is a solution of the patch problem with the elimination (15.38), and the only one if the system for the node displacements is uniquely solvable.
  2. The condition holds for QM6 on every quadrilateral with \(J > 0\) and for Q6 on every parallelogram.
  3. For Q6 on a quadrilateral that is not a parallelogram, \(\int_{\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}A \ne \mat 0\), and for a general constant stress \(\sigma^0\) the pair (\(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\), \(\vek 0\)) is not a solution.
Proof

We must show that the pair (\(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\), \(\vek 0\)) solves the discrete equations if the condition is satisfied, then compute the condition and finally show that without it the pair is not a solution.

  1. The discrete equations are \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} \int_{\mathrm{e}} \sigma_{\mathrm{e}} : \varepsilon(w + \varphi\,\vek c_{\mathrm{e}}')\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) for every field \(w\) from the Q4 part that is zero at the boundary nodes, and for all vectors \(\vek c_{\mathrm{e}}'\) of the elements. The elimination (15.38) is equal to these equations, since the equations at \(\vek c_{\mathrm{e}}'\) give the first equality in (15.38), and substituting it into the equations at \(w\) gives the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) of the second.
  2. At \(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\vek c = \vek 0\) we have \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}} = \sigma^0\) on every element (step 2 of theorem 15.47). The left side is the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} \int_{\mathrm{e}} \sigma^0 : \varepsilon(w)\,\mathrm{d}A + \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \sigma^0 : \int_{\mathrm{e}} \varepsilon(\varphi\,\vek c_{\mathrm{e}}')\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  3. The first part is \(B(u_{\mathrm{lin}}, w) = 0\) by step 3 of theorem 15.47, since \(w\) is a Q4 field. The second part is zero by the assumption \(\int_{\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \mat 0\). The pair is therefore a solution, and if the system is uniquely solvable, the only one. This is item 1.
  4. For QM6, \(\mat B_{\mathrm{c}} = (J_0/J)\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}^0\), where \(\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}^0\) is the matrix computed with \(\mat F_0\) (derivatives with respect to \(x\) and \(y\) as in step 1 of lemma 15.53, with \(\mat F_0\) instead of \(\mat F\)): its entries are constant coefficients multiplied by \(\varphi_{1,\xi} = -2\xi\) or \(\varphi_{2,\eta} = -2\eta\) or zero. By theorem 4.59, \(\int_{\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \iint_{[-1,1]^2}(J_0/J)\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}^0\,J\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta = J_0\iint_{[-1,1]^2}\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}^0\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta = \mat 0\), since \(J\) drops out and \(\int_{-1}^{1}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\xi = 0\). For Q6 this is lemma 15.53: all four integrals vanish exactly at \(\vek q = \vek 0\). This is item 2.
  5. If for Q6 \(\vek q \ne \vek 0\), then by equation (15.39) for example \(\sigma^0 : \int \varepsilon(\varphi_1\,\vek e_{\mathrm{x}})\,\mathrm{d}A = \sigma^0_{\mathrm{xx}}\int\varphi_{1,\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A + \sigma^0_{\mathrm{xy}}\int\varphi_{1,\mathrm{y}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac23\,(-\sigma^0_{\mathrm{xx}}\,q_{\mathrm{y}} + \sigma^0_{\mathrm{xy}}\,q_{\mathrm{x}})\), which for a general stress is different from zero. With \(\vek c_{\mathrm{e}}' = \vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\) for this mode the equation of step 1 is not satisfied, so the pair (\(u_{\mathrm{lin}}\), \(\vek 0\)) is not a solution. This is item 3.
  6. The \(2 \times 2\) rule, with which the computations of this section are done, gives the integrals of the extra functions in steps 3 to 5 exactly. For Q6, \(J\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(2\) in each variable (step 3 of lemma 15.53), for QM6 \(J\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}} = J_0\,\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}^0\) is linear, and the rule is exact up to degree \(3\) (corollary 15.42). The Q4 part is integrated exactly by lemma 15.48, step 4. Items 1 to 3 therefore hold also under this integration.

Source: COOK section 6.6 (PDF p. 234): Q6 does not represent constant strain unless it is a rectangle (by the lemma it holds for all parallelograms), and the correction QM6 satisfies the condition that the loads belonging to the internal degrees of freedom vanish at constant stress. The proof in steps is ours and does not prove that QM6 converges: COOK (PDF p. 234) claims it, we do not prove it.

The theorem summarises the example: Q6 fails where the condition does not hold, that is on the distorted quadrilaterals of the patch, and QM6 passes. QM6 remains incompatible. The difference of the fields of neighbouring elements on a shared edge is still \((1 - \eta^2)\,(\vek c_2^{(1)} - \vek c_2^{(2)})\) and for a general problem it is different from zero. The test does not detect it, because in the patch \(\vek c = \vek 0\). Why convergence nevertheless holds is not proved in this book, COOK (PDF p. 234) claims that QM6 passes the test and therefore converges.

The patch test thus separates the quadrilaterals that we may use from those that we may not: Q4 (with \(2 \times 2\) points) and QM6 passed it, Q6 did not. It does not say how well an element works in other problems (COOK, PDF p. 254): Q4 passes it, yet in bending it gives \(0{,}292\) of the true value. COOK (PDF p. 254) mentions also a weak test: an element that passes it only on shapes that arise by ever repeated subdivision (parallelograms, \(J\) nearly constant) can still converge, because subdivision brings every quadrilateral ever closer to a parallelogram. Q6 would pass such a test. With a finite number of elements, however, the error of Q6 on distorted meshes is still large, as figure 15.28 shows.

Let us summarise the section. The patch test is a problem with a few elements and a linear field prescribed on the boundary: the solution must be that field. Theorem 15.47 proves that every conforming family of elements that contains the linear fields passes it, whatever the shape of the elements (lemma 15.48 for Q4). Q4 is too stiff in bending because of parasitic shear and the absent transverse contraction (lemma 15.49, energy ratio \(1{,}48\) to \(10{,}7\)). The extra functions of the element Q6 make bending possible (lemma 15.52), but they break conformity and the patch on distorted quadrilaterals (lemma 15.53, error \(0{,}142\)), and the condition \(\int\mat B_{\mathrm{c}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \mat 0\) is satisfied by QM6 (theorem 15.54), which gives on the cantilever \(0{,}998\) of the true deflection at \(60\) unknowns.
