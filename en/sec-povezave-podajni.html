Chapter 28 Flexible robot links

28.10 Connections

The ideas of this chapter return in the chapters on robots, on modal reduction, on damping, on rotors and on modelling. For each we say where it is needed next and what stays open there. The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

We build the figure as we built the model of the chapter. The hub is the turning part of the joint into which the link is fixed, as the spokes of a wheel are fixed in its hub. Its moment of inertia about the joint axis is \(J_{\mathrm{h}}\). The torque \(\tau\) of the motor turns the hub, and the angle \(\theta\) is the angle between the axis \(\bar x\) of the floating frame from section 28.2 and the horizontal axis of the fixed frame. The link is clamped in the hub, so it bends when the hub turns, and the tip deflection \(v\) is the elastic coordinate that the model of the chapter computes. If the hub turns at a constant angular velocity \(\Omega\), a centrifugal axial force appears, which section 28.7 treats. Dashed is everything the model does not have: the next joint and link at the end and the damper that removes energy. Each of the following points relies on one part of the figure.

  • A robot with several links (next joint and link): chapter 27 (DSK) assembles rigid links into a chain and derives its mass matrix from theorem 8.86 (VD, DSK). Our link is one link of that chain. Corollary 28.14 shows that at \(\boldsymbol q_{\mathrm{d}} = \boldsymbol 0\) and \(\dot{\boldsymbol q}_{\mathrm{d}} = \boldsymbol 0\) the kinetic energy of the link reduces to the kinetic energy of a rigid body from theorem 8.82, so the rigid robot is a special case. Flexibility adds to each link a coupling column \(\boldsymbol b\) and the elastic coordinates (lemma 28.15). Several links joined by joints need constraints with multipliers by theorem 8.95 and a split of the coordinates into independent and dependent ones (SHABANA, §5.4, PDF pp. 230 to 233, §5.8 to §5.10, PDF pp. 251 to 260). We do not prove this in the chapter, because one link does not need it. The equations of a link on a hub from corollary 28.21 are our own derivation from the general equations (SHABANA, §5.5, PDF pp. 234 to 238), because no source in the book treats a flexible robot link with a joint by Lagrange equations, and SPONG has only flexible joints (PDF pp. 314 and 461).
  • A controlled joint (hub and torque): locking the joint (\(\theta = 0\)) is one constraint, so by theorem 28.25 the frequencies of the locked link lie between those of the link with a free joint. At \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0{,}2\) kg m² the order is \(0 \le 30{,}53 \le 59{,}15 \le 191{,}31 \le 198{,}98\) rad/s, so the first elastic frequency with a free joint is \(1{,}94\) times that of the locked link. Which of the two models applies depends on whether the controller holds the joint or leaves it to the torque. That is a question of control, which lies outside the book (SPONG, from chapter 8 on).
  • Spin and blades (angular velocity \(\Omega\)): theorem 28.30 for constant spin is the same derivation as for a turbine or fan blade, where the centrifugal axial force raises the bending stiffness and the frequency rises (FPGL, §10.8, PDF pp. 469 to 472). Here, at \(\Omega = \omega_1 = 30{,}53\) rad/s the first in-plane frequency rises to \(33{,}17\) rad/s, which is \(8{,}7\) %, while the linear model without this axial force falls to zero, a false instability (corollary 28.28). Rotating blades are treated in chapter 24 (DSK). A hub radius larger than zero changes the axial force \(N(x)\) and the in-plane coefficient, and turning combined with axial motion of the link brings a Coriolis coupling that our model leaves out, as FPGL also neglects it (PDF p. 470).
  • Model reduction (bent link): theorem 28.38 replaces \(n\) elastic coordinates by a few modal ones, and the frequencies of the model stay above those of the full model by theorem 10.42. By lemma 28.39 the sum of squared couplings \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) is less than \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = 0{,}624\) kg m², and with five modes it is \(0{,}62380\). The static deflection of the end under a force of \(10\) N, \(23{,}62\) mm, is underestimated by \(2{,}9\) % with a single mode, so we choose modes by the loading and not only by frequency. The static modes that correct this (Craig-Bampton) and the mode acceleration correction are treated in chapter 21 (CK, §17.6, PDF p. 575).
  • Damping (damper): the equations of the chapter have no damping. The steel link has a small internal damping and the joint has friction, which the model does not contain. How damping is added to equations in modes and how it is measured is treated in chapter 20 (DSK).
  • Limits of the model (tip deflection \(v\)): the elastic field is Euler-Bernoulli (definition 5.14) with an inextensible axis. The axial stiffness \(E A / L\) is \(1{,}11 \cdot 10^5\) times the bending stiffness of the tip \(3 E I / L^3\) in the example, so axial stretching is not needed. We neglect shear and rotary inertia: for a thin strip of thickness \(h = 6\) mm the radius of gyration of the section is \(r = h/\sqrt{12} = 1{,}73\) mm and \(r/L = 1{,}73 \cdot 10^{-3}\), so the first rule of corollary 13.64 (\(n r / L \le 0{,}022\), frequency correction below \(1\) %) holds up to \(n = 12\). The corollary is for a simply supported beam, so for the cantilever this is an estimate and not a proof. When the beam model is no longer enough (thicker links, plates and shells, nonlinear models, large deformation) is a question for chapters 17, 18 and 22 (AK, MK). SHABANA treats large deformation in the floating frame in §6.8 (PDF pp. 313 to 315).

What is proved in this chapter and what is only cited: the planar floating frame, the kinetic energy, the elastic energy, the equations of the link, the interlacing of frequencies, the centrifugal stiffening and the reduction are proved from chapters 1, 2, 5, 8, 10, 12 and 13. Cited without proof are the completeness of the cantilever modes (from chapter 13), the spatial floating frame (SHABANA, §5.1, PDF pp. 207 and 208, §6.6, PDF pp. 303 to 308), several links with joints, constraints and multipliers, the slider-crank with a flexible rod (SHABANA, §5.7, PDF pp. 242 to 250, and chapter 23, DSK), numerical integration of the equations (SHABANA, §5.12, PDF pp. 264 to 272) and flexible joints (SPONG, PDF pp. 314 and 461).
