Chapter 12 Beam and frame finite elements

12.11 Connections

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

  • Hermite elements and nodal exactness: theorem 12.14 (exact deflection and rotation at the nodes) holds for a beam with a continuously differentiable displacement field. In the vibration of continuous beams it is compared with the exact frequencies given by the Euler and Timoshenko models, and with the upper bound of the Ritz method for continuous systems, which we used for beam meshes in theorem 10.42 in corollary 12.46 (DSK, chapter 13). For plates a continuous rotation is much harder to get, because it must be continuous in both directions (MK, AK, chapter 18), and for shells the plane of an element is warped (MK, chapter 22).
  • Frames: theorem 12.20 and corollary 12.24 build a frame from elements with a chosen cross section. The symmetry of a structure, constraints between several nodes and the coupling of different kinds of elements continue the multipliers and the penalty method of section 12.6 (MK, AK, chapter 19). Mesh convergence and good modelling practice are in chapter 17 (AK). The torsion constant of a section, which we took as a given number in the space frame, and the warping of a section are explained in chapter 16 (MK). Rotors are written with beam elements (DSK, chapter 24), and flexible links of robots with beam elements and modes (DSK, chapter 28).
  • Locking: shear locking, which we showed in section 12.8 as a penalty system (lemma 12.35), repeats in Mindlin plates with two directions of shear (MK, AK, chapter 18). In nearly incompressible solids the incompressibility condition does the same (AK, chapter 15).
  • Mass matrices: the consistent and lumped mass matrices of section 12.9 are the basis of damping matrices, which in practice are built from mass and stiffness (DSK, chapter 20). Guyan reduction and the assembly of substructures use the split of the unknowns into interior and boundary ones, like the one we made for unknowns without inertia (DSK, chapter 21).
  • Geometric stiffness: theorem 12.53 is proved for a column with constant stiffness and supports that are pinned or fixed, or for a cantilever. For the fixed and pinned column only the weaker bound \(\pi^2 EI/L^2\) is proved, not the true one, because chapter 8 computed its threshold and did not prove it, so the order there is only an observation (section 12.10.5). Linear buckling analysis assumes a perfect column and small displacements. Stability in modelling, imperfections and nonlinear buckling are in chapter 17 (AK).
