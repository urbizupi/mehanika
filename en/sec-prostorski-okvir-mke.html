Chapter 12 Beam and frame finite elements

12.5 The space frame (prostorski okvir)

In section 5.14.5 of chapter 5 we turned the L-frame out of the plane. The force \(F = 500\) N acts in the direction of the axis \(Y\), perpendicular to the plane of the frame, and we obtained the six internal forces from equilibrium (the table in that section). How far does the point \(C\) move? Equilibrium does not tell, because the displacement is determined by stiffness. By that table the column has the torque \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = F\,b = 500\) N m along its whole length, so it twists, and the displacement of the point \(C\) depends on how the cross section resists twisting. Besides \(EA\), \(EI_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(EI_{\mathrm{z}}\) we need one more stiffness, the torsional one.

The plane frame (section 12.4) gave the procedure: the element in local axes, the turn into global axes, assembly and the reduced system. In space the procedure stays the same, but three things change. The element has six unknowns at each node instead of three. Bending happens in two planes. The rotation of a node is no longer a number but a vector, which has to be mapped when the axes are turned. The section therefore first sets up the model and the unknowns, then builds the element matrix, then shows that the rotation is mapped with the same matrix as the displacement, then says that the solution is again exact at the nodes, and finally computes the L-frame.

12.5.1 Model and unknowns of the element

The element is a straight beam with its axis through the centroids of the sections. The local axis \(\bar x\) runs along the element from node 1 towards node 2, the local axes \(\bar y\) and \(\bar z\) are the principal axes of the section (corollary 5.31) and the triple \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\) is right-handed. Bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\) is the Euler-Bernoulli bending of chapter 5 with the moment of inertia \(I_{\mathrm{z}} = \int \bar y^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), and bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\) is the same theory with \(I_{\mathrm{y}} = \int \bar z^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) (section 5.14.5). The axial displacement \(\bar u\) and the transverse displacements \(\bar v\) (in the direction \(\bar y\)) and \(\bar w\) (in the direction \(\bar z\)) are displacements of the element axis.

Torsion needs a model that chapter 5 did not have. We assume uniform torsion (enakomerna torzija): the sections rotate about the axis of the element by the angle \(\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}(\bar x)\) and in doing so may freely bulge out of their plane (warping of the section, zvitje prereza: sections free to warp). A transverse force that passes through the centroid does not rotate the section. For a section with two axes of symmetry, such as our rectangle, the centroid is also the shear centre (strižno središče), the point through which a transverse force must pass so that the section does not rotate about the axis of the element during bending (explained in chapter 16). Torsion therefore causes no bending and bending causes no rotation of the section. The torque is proportional to the rotation of the section per unit length,

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} = G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}' , \tag{12.16}\]

where \(G\) is the shear modulus and \(J\) the torsion constant (torzijska konstanta) of the section, a number with the unit m\(^4\) that depends only on the shape of the section. We take the equation as an assumption of the model, as we took the axial force as \(N = EA\,\bar u'\). Equilibrium of a slice of the element is by equation (5.12) in the component \(x\) equal to \(M_{\mathrm{x}}' + m_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) (\(m_{\mathrm{x}}\) is the distributed torque per unit length), which has the same form as \(N' + q_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) for the axial force. Without a distributed torque \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) is constant along the element and the rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) is linear.

We take the value of \(J\) for our strip \(20 \times 40\) mm as data and do not compute it. The computation needs the shear stresses in a section that warps and is in chapter 16. SADD (9.5.12) (PDF p. 264) gives for a rectangular section with sides \(2a\) and \(2b\), \(a < b\), the torque \(T = G\,\alpha\,J\) with the rotation \(\alpha\) per unit length, in which \(J\) is the series

\[J = \frac{16\,a^3 b}{3} - \frac{1024\,a^4}{\pi^5}\sum_{\mathrm{n} = 1, 3, 5, \dots}\frac{1}{n^5}\tanh\frac{n\pi b}{2a} , \tag{12.17}\]

where SADD writes the shear modulus as \(\mu\) (its \(\mu\) is our \(G\)) and we have taken \(G\,\alpha\) out. Beware that SADD in (9.3.23) calls the product \(G J\) by the symbol \(J\), while we use \(J\) for the geometric part only. For \(a = 10\) mm and \(b = 20\) mm the first term is \(106\,667\) mm\(^4\) and the terms of the series are \(0{,}99627\), \(0{,}00412\), \(0{,}00032\), ..., hence

\[J = 73\,178\ \text{mm}^4 = 7{,}3178\cdot 10^{-8}\ \text{m}^4 , \qquad G J = 75{,}385\ \text{GPa}\cdot J = 5516{,}5\ \text{N m}^2 .\]

No claim of this section depends on how \(J\) is computed: in all of them \(J\) is a constant positive number. Why we do not simply take the polar moment of inertia \(I_{\mathrm{p}} = \int(\bar y^2 + \bar z^2)\,\mathrm{d}A = 133\,333\) mm\(^4\) is shown by the computation at the end of the section. A rectangular section bulges out of its plane in torsion (SADD (9.5.14) and figure 9.13, PDF p. 265), hence \(J = 0{,}549\,I_{\mathrm{p}}\).

The model has one more idealization that we must name. The clamp at \(A\) and the rigid node at \(B\) do not prevent the warping of the section. A real clamp would prevent it and the column would be slightly stiffer than our model shows. For an I-beam five times as long as it is deep, COOK (PDF pp. 42-43) states that complete restraint of warping at the clamp reduces the rotation of the end by about half. How much it would reduce the rotation of our column, this section does not measure.

How do we write the rotation of a section in space? In the plane the rotation was one number, \(\varphi = v'\). In space the section moves as a rigid body (by assumption the section does not deform). A small motion without deformation has by theorem 1.134 the form \(\vek u(P) = \vek a + \mat W\,\vek r_{\mathrm{P}}\) with a constant vector \(\vek a\) and a constant antisymmetric matrix \(\mat W\) (this is the small rotation tensor, definition 1.116). An antisymmetric \(3 \times 3\) matrix has three independent elements. Let us write them as

\[\begin{aligned} \mat W &= \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -\varphi_{\mathrm{z}} & \varphi_{\mathrm{y}} \\ \varphi_{\mathrm{z}} & 0 & -\varphi_{\mathrm{x}} \\ -\varphi_{\mathrm{y}} & \varphi_{\mathrm{x}} & 0 \end{bmatrix} , \\ &\qquad \text{so} \qquad \mat W\,\vek b = \vek\varphi\times\vek b \ \text{ for every vector } \vek b , \end{aligned} \tag{12.18}\]

where \(\vek\varphi = (\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{y}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{z}})\) is the rotation vector (vektor zasuka) and the vector product is the one of definition 2.30 (the left and right sides are equal component by component). The component \(\varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\) is the rotation about the axis \(z\) counterclockwise, seen from the tip of the axis, and in the plane case it is the rotation \(\varphi\) of section 12.4. Thus \(-\varphi_{\mathrm{z}} = W_{\mathrm{xy}} = \omega_{\mathrm{xy}}\), as in step 4 of the proof of lemma 12.18.

How is the rotation \(\vek\varphi\) related to the derivatives \(v'\) and \(w'\)? Before loading the axis of the element has the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), after loading the tangent \((1 + u', v', w')\), which for small slopes is in a first approximation \(\vek e_{\mathrm{x}} + (0, v', w')\). The section is perpendicular to the axis and turns with it, so the difference is \(\vek\varphi\times\vek e_{\mathrm{x}} = (0, \varphi_{\mathrm{z}}, -\varphi_{\mathrm{y}})\). Hence

\[\varphi_{\mathrm{z}} = v' , \qquad \varphi_{\mathrm{y}} = -w' . \tag{12.19}\]

We check the sign of \(\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\) on the strain as well. A point of the section at distances \(y\) and \(z\) from the centroid moves after the rigid rotation in the direction of the axis \(x\) by \((\vek\varphi\times\vek r)_{\mathrm{x}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\,z - \varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\,y = -w'\,z - v'\,y\), so

\[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = u' - y\,v'' - z\,w'' . \tag{12.20}\]

The moment about the axis \(y\) is by definition \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = \int z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A = -E I_{\mathrm{y}}\,w''\) (the vector product \(\vek r\times\vek t\) has the component \(y\) equal to \(z\,t_{\mathrm{x}} - x\,t_{\mathrm{z}}\)), which is the equation of section 5.14.5.

Definition 12.21 Space frame element (element prostorskega okvirja)

A space frame (prostorski okvir) is a model of a structure of straight beams in space that are connected in rigid nodes: at a node the ends of all elements have the same displacement \((u, v, w)\) and the same rotation vector \(\vek\varphi\) (equation (12.18)), as for the plane frame (definition 5.90). Let the element be a straight beam with nodes 1 and 2, length \(h\), axis through the centroids of the sections, right-handed local axes \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\) (the axis \(\bar x\) from 1 to 2, \(\bar y\) and \(\bar z\) the principal axes of the section) and constant \(EA\), \(EI_{\mathrm{y}} = E\int\bar z^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), \(EI_{\mathrm{z}} = E\int\bar y^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) and \(GJ\). The space frame element (element prostorskega okvirja) is this element together with a column of twelve real numbers, the local nodal displacements,

\[\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} = (\bar u_1,\ \bar v_1,\ \bar w_1,\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1},\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}1},\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}1},\ \bar u_2,\ \bar v_2,\ \bar w_2,\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2},\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}2},\ \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}2})^\T ,\]

that is, the displacement and the rotation vector of each node in local axes, and with the fields along the axis: the axial displacement \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}\) and the rotation \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}\) are linear (shape functions of definition 7.2), the transverse displacements are Hermite (definition 12.1), \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\bar v_1, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}1}, \bar v_2, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}2})^\T\) and \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\bar w_1, -\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}1}, \bar w_2, -\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}2})^\T\), because by equation (12.19) \(\bar w' = -\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\). In the global axes \(X\), \(Y\), \(Z\) the element has a column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) of twelve numbers in the same order.

Source: COOK §2.3 (PDF pp. 41-43: element with twelve unknowns, torsional stiffness \(GK/L\), the model holds for two axes of symmetry and free warping), GR §5.3.4 (PDF pp. 404-405: principal bending axes and free warping, rotations \(\psi_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\psi_{\mathrm{z}}\) tied to the displacements by (5.149)). ONATE2 §4.4 (PDF pp. 211-216) has for the space beam an element of Timoshenko type with reduced integration, so we do not cite it here for the matrix. The notation and the sign \(\varphi_{\mathrm{y}} = -w'\) are ours, derived in the text before the definition (COOK PDF p. 42 only mentions sign differences caused by the directions of the rotations).

Figure 12.15 shows the twelve unknowns of the element. Each of the two nodes has three displacements (left) and three components of the rotation vector (right). The rotation vector is drawn as an arrow with two heads along the axis about which it rotates, in the sense of the right-handed screw, like the moments in figure 5.84.

12.5.2 The element matrix

We obtain the element matrix from energy, as for the frame element. The energy of the element is a sum of four parts, each containing its own unknowns: axial, torsional and two bending parts. The matrix is therefore assembled from four blocks and has no other terms.

Lemma 12.22 Stiffness matrix of a space frame element (togostna matrika elementa prostorskega okvirja)

Let a space frame element (definition 12.21) have length \(h\) and no load along it, and let \(\mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the Hermite row of definition 12.3. Then the strain energy of the element is \(U_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\,\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}\) and the forces and moments that the surroundings exert on the nodes of the element, in local components (per node a force \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\) and a moment about \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\)), are \(\bar{\vek F}_{\mathrm{e}} = \bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\,\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}\), where \(\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\) is a symmetric \(12 \times 12\) matrix with four blocks and zeros elsewhere. If we number the unknowns from 1 to 12 in the order of the definition, the blocks are:

  1. axial, on the unknowns \((\bar u_1, \bar u_2)\) (numbers 1 and 7): \(\dfrac{EA}{h}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\),
  2. torsional, on \((\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1}, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2})\) (numbers 4 and 10): \(\dfrac{GJ}{h}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\),
  3. bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\), on \((\bar v_1, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}1}, \bar v_2, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}2})\) (numbers 2, 6, 8, 12): the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) of lemma 12.4 with \(EI_{\mathrm{z}}\),
  4. bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\), on \((\bar w_1, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}1}, \bar w_2, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}2})\) (numbers 3, 5, 9, 11):
    \[\begin{aligned} \mat T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat T &= \frac{EI_{\mathrm{y}}}{h^3}\begin{bmatrix} 12 & -6h & -12 & -6h \\ -6h & 4h^2 & 6h & 2h^2 \\ -12 & 6h & 12 & 6h \\ -6h & 2h^2 & 6h & 4h^2 \end{bmatrix} , \\ &\qquad \mat T = \mathrm{diag}(1, -1, 1, -1) , \end{aligned} \tag{12.21}\]
    where \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the beam element matrix with \(EI_{\mathrm{y}}\).
Proof

We have to show that the energy is a sum of four quadratic forms in separate groups of unknowns (hence the zeros outside the blocks) and that the forces of the surroundings equal \(\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}\) in each block.

  1. The axis passes through the centroid and the axes \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\) are principal, so \(\int\bar y\,\mathrm{d}A = \int\bar z\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) and \(\int\bar y\bar z\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) (corollary 5.31). With \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E(\bar u' - \bar y\,\bar v'' - \bar z\,\bar w'')\) from equation (12.20) we therefore have \(\tfrac12\int E(\bar u' - \bar y\,\bar v'' - \bar z\,\bar w'')^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac12\big(EA\,\bar u'^2 + EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v''^2 + EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w''^2\big)\): the mixed terms are products with three sums that are zero (as in the proof of lemma 5.33 for one plane).
  2. The torsional energy per unit length is the work of the moment \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) on the rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}'\) of the section with the relation (12.16), that is \(\tfrac12\,GJ\,\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}'^2\). By the assumption of the model (the centroid is the shear centre) torsion is not coupled to bending, so the energies add. The energy of the element is \(U_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\int_0^h\big(EA\,\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}'^2 + GJ\,\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}'^2 + EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}''^2 + EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}''^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}\bar x\).
  3. Each of the four terms contains only its own group of unknowns (axial \(\bar u_1\), \(\bar u_2\), torsional \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1}\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2}\), the bending ones four each). A quadratic form in separate groups has all matrix entries between the groups equal to zero, so the matrix is assembled from four blocks and is symmetric.
  4. Axial block: \(\tfrac12\int_0^h EA\,\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}'^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar x = \tfrac12\,\tfrac{EA}{h}(\bar u_2 - \bar u_1)^2\) (lemma 7.5, point 2). Torsional block: since \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}' = (\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2} - \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1})/h\) is constant, \(\tfrac12\int_0^h GJ\,\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}'^2\,\mathrm{d}\bar x = \tfrac12\,\tfrac{GJ}{h}(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2} - \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1})^2\), the same computation under another name.
  5. Bending block in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\): \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{y}}\) with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{y}} = (\bar v_1, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}1}, \bar v_2, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}2})^\T\), so by lemma 12.4 (point 1) the energy is \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{y}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{y}}\) with \(EI_{\mathrm{z}}\).
  6. Bending block in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\): \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat H_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}\) with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{z}} = (\bar w_1, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}1}, \bar w_2, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}2})^\T\), because \(\bar w' = -\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\) (definition 12.21). By lemma 12.4 the energy is \(\tfrac12\,(\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}})^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}) = \tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}^\T(\mat T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat T)\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}\), because \(\mat T\) is symmetric. The product \(\mat T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\mat T\) changes the sign of the entries in which exactly one of the indices is 2 or 4. This gives equation (12.21).
  7. Forces of the surroundings. The work of the forces of the surroundings on a rigid section for a virtual displacement \(\delta\vek a\) and rotation \(\delta\vek\varphi\) is \(\int\vek t\cdot(\delta\vek a + \delta\vek\varphi\times\vek r)\,\mathrm{d}A = \vek F\cdot\delta\vek a + \delta\vek\varphi\cdot\int\vek r\times\vek t\,\mathrm{d}A = \vek F\cdot\delta\vek a + \vek M\cdot\delta\vek\varphi\) (cyclic exchange in the mixed product, lemma 2.51, point 1). The forces belonging to the unknowns \((u, v, w, \varphi_{\mathrm{x}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{y}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{z}})\) are therefore the components of the force and the moment in this order, at the start of the element \(-\vek F(0)\) and \(-\vek M(0)\), at the end \(\vek F(h)\) and \(\vek M(h)\) (definition of the internal force, section 5.2.1).
  8. Axial forces and torques: the axial force is given by lemma 7.5 (point 3), the torque is by equation (5.12) and \(m_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) constant, \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2} - \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1})/h\), so the force of the surroundings on node 1 is \(-M_{\mathrm{x}} = \tfrac{GJ}{h}(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1} - \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2})\) and on node 2 it is \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), which is the torsional block times \((\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}1}, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}2})\).
  9. Bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\): the forces are \((-V_{\mathrm{y}}(0), -M_{\mathrm{z}}(0), V_{\mathrm{y}}(h), M_{\mathrm{z}}(h))\) with \(M_{\mathrm{z}} = EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v''\) and \(V_{\mathrm{y}} = -M_{\mathrm{z}}'\) (equations (5.12), \(m = 0\)), which is the plane \(V\), \(M\) of section 12.1, so \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{y}}\) by lemma 12.4 (point 2). Bending in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\): \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = -EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w''\) and from the component \(y\) of equation (5.12), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}' - V_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\) (because \(\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\times\vek F = (0, -V_{\mathrm{z}}, V_{\mathrm{y}})\)), we get \(V_{\mathrm{z}} = M_{\mathrm{y}}' = -EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w'''\). Comparison with the plane \(V = -EI\,v'''\), \(M = EI\,v''\) shows that \(V_{\mathrm{z}}\) is like \(V\), while \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) is the opposite of the moment \(M\) for \(v = \bar w\). The forces \((-V_{\mathrm{z}}(0), -M_{\mathrm{y}}(0), V_{\mathrm{z}}(h), M_{\mathrm{y}}(h))\) are therefore the plane forces of the field \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}\) with the second and fourth entries of opposite sign, so \(\mat T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}) = (\mat T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\mat T)\,\vek d_{\mathrm{z}}\).

Source: COOK (2.3-8) (PDF p. 41, with a comment on signs on p. 42), GR (5.151) (PDF p. 405). The sources only state the matrix, COOK with different directions of the rotations. The proof, including the block ((12.21)) from the relation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{y}} = -w'\), is not from the sources.

The matrix consists of the bar and beam blocks that we already have and one block that is the same as the axial one. Torsion is thus as simple in the element matrix as the axial force: the torque is \(GJ/h\) times the difference of the rotations, as the axial force is \(EA/h\) times the difference of the displacements. In the L-frame both elements have \(h = 1\) m. The column \(AB\) has the local axes \(\bar y = X\) (width \(40\) mm) and \(\bar z = Y\) (thickness \(20\) mm) and therefore the weak moment of inertia \(I_{\mathrm{y}} = 26\,667\) mm\(^4\) about the axis \(\bar y\), which bends the column in the direction of the force \(Y\). The beam \(BC\) has \(\bar y = Y\) and \(\bar z = Z\), so in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\), in which it bends, also the weak moment of inertia \(I_{\mathrm{z}} = 26\,667\) mm\(^4\). The weak stiffness is \(EI_{\mathrm{w}} = 5226{,}67\) N m\(^2\). The coefficients of the block (12.21) of the column and of the block of the beam in the plane \(\bar x\bar y\) are therefore the same: \(12EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h^3 = 62\,720\) N/m, \(6EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h^2 = 31\,360\) N, \(4EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h = 20\,906{,}7\) N m, \(2EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h = 10\,453{,}3\) N m, and the torsional coefficient is \(GJ/h = 5516{,}5\) N m. The axial block \(EA/h = 1{,}568\cdot 10^8\) N/m does not affect the load in the direction \(Y\).

12.5.3 Turning the axes

We have the matrix \(\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\) in local axes. For assembly we need it in global ones. For the plane frame the axes were turned about \(z\) and the rotation of the node stayed the same number (lemma 12.18, step 4). In space the axes are turned arbitrarily and we do not yet know how the rotation of a node is mapped. We have to map three things: the displacement vector, the rotation vector and the moment, which is a vector product. The displacement and the force are mapped by the rotation matrix (lemma 2.38). The moment is a vector product of two vectors and the rotation comes from an antisymmetric matrix, so we need two more things: how the vector product is mapped and how the rotation is.

The vector product is defined by components in a right-handed system. When we turn the system by the rotation matrix \(\mat Q\), we compute it from the components in the new system. Do we obtain the same vector? We do, if the new system is right-handed. In a left-handed system we would have to multiply each component by \(-1\) (the section on the vector product in chapter 2).

Lemma 12.23 Turning the axes maps the rotation vector and the moment (zasuk osi preslika vektor zasuka in moment)

Let two right-handed orthogonal Cartesian systems with the same origin and the same unit of length be given, the old one with axes \(X\), \(Y\), \(Z\) and the new one with axes \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\), \(\bar z\), and let \(\mat Q\) be the rotation matrix from the old to the new system (definition 2.37). Then:

  1. \(\det\mat Q = 1\) and for all vectors \(\vek a\), \(\vek b\) we have \(\mat Q\,(\vek a\times\vek b) = (\mat Q\,\vek a)\times(\mat Q\,\vek b)\).
  2. A small rigid displacement \(\vek u(P) = \vek a + \mat W\,\vek r_{\mathrm{P}}\) with an antisymmetric matrix \(\mat W\) and rotation vector \(\vek\varphi\) (equation (12.18)) has in the new system the form \(\bar{\vek u}(\bar P) = \bar{\vek a} + \bar{\mat W}\,\bar{\vek r}_{\mathrm{P}}\) with \(\bar{\vek a} = \mat Q\,\vek a\), the antisymmetric matrix \(\bar{\mat W} = \mat Q\,\mat W\,\mat Q^\T\) and the rotation vector \(\bar{\vek\varphi} = \mat Q\,\vek\varphi\).
  3. The moment \(\vek M = \int\vek r\times\vek t\,\mathrm{d}A\) of forces \(\vek t\) on a section has in the new system the components \(\bar{\vek M} = \mat Q\,\vek M\).

Hence for a space frame element with the local axes in the rows of the matrix \(\mat Q\) we have

\[\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} , \qquad \bar{\vek F}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R\,\vek F_{\mathrm{e}} , \qquad \mat R = \mathrm{diag}(\mat Q, \mat Q, \mat Q, \mat Q) , \tag{12.22}\]

where \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\) are the displacements and the forces of the surroundings in global components, and \(\mat R\) is a \(12 \times 12\) matrix with four blocks \(\mat Q\).

Proof

We have to show that the mapping of the vector product, of the displacement and of the rotation is the same matrix \(\mat Q\).

  1. By the section on the determinant (chapter 2), by theorem 2.52 and lemma 2.51 (point 6) we have \((\det\mat Q)^2 = \det(\mat Q\,\mat Q^\T) = \det\mat I = 1\) (lemma 2.40). The rows of the matrix \(\mat Q\) are the components of the vectors \(\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{z}}\) in the old system (definition 2.37), so \(\det\mat Q = \det\mat Q^\T = \bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{x}}\cdot(\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{y}}\times\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{z}})\) by the definition of the determinant. This is the mixed product of a triple that is right-handed by assumption (definition 2.48), hence positive, and \(\det\mat Q = 1\).
  2. Let \(\vek a\), \(\vek b\), \(\vek c\) be arbitrary vectors. The matrix with the columns \(\mat Q\vek a\), \(\mat Q\vek b\), \(\mat Q\vek c\) is the product \(\mat Q\,[\vek a\ \vek b\ \vek c]\), so by theorem 2.52 \(\det(\mat Q\vek a, \mat Q\vek b, \mat Q\vek c) = \det\mat Q\,\det(\vek a, \vek b, \vek c) = \det(\vek a, \vek b, \vek c)\).
  3. By the definition of the determinant and cyclic exchange (lemma 2.51, point 1) we have \(\det(\vek u, \vek v, \vek w) = \vek w\cdot(\vek u\times\vek v)\). So the left side of step 2 equals \(\mat Q\vek c\cdot(\mat Q\vek a\times\mat Q\vek b)\) and the right side \(\vek c\cdot(\vek a\times\vek b)\). The matrix \(\mat Q^\T\) is the inverse of the matrix \(\mat Q\) (lemma 2.40), so also \(\mat Q^\T\mat Q = \mat I\) and \(\mat Q\vek x\cdot\mat Q\vek y = \vek x\cdot\vek y\), hence \(\vek c\cdot(\vek a\times\vek b) = \mat Q\vek c\cdot\mat Q(\vek a\times\vek b)\). The difference \(\vek z = \mat Q(\vek a\times\vek b) - (\mat Q\vek a)\times(\mat Q\vek b)\) therefore has scalar product zero with \(\mat Q\vek c\) for every \(\vek c\). Since \(\mat Q\) is invertible, \(\mat Q\vek c\) is an arbitrary vector, so also \(\vek z\), and \(|\vek z|^2 = 0\) gives \(\vek z = \vek 0\). This is point 1.
  4. Point 2. The point \(P\) has in the new system the position vector \(\bar{\vek r}_{\mathrm{P}} = \mat Q\,\vek r_{\mathrm{P}}\) and the displacement \(\bar{\vek u} = \mat Q\,\vek u\) (lemma 2.38, point 1). By theorem 1.134 we have \(\vek r_{\mathrm{P}} = \mat Q^\T\bar{\vek r}_{\mathrm{P}}\) and \(\bar{\vek u} = \mat Q\vek a + \mat Q\,\mat W\,\mat Q^\T\,\bar{\vek r}_{\mathrm{P}}\). The matrix \(\bar{\mat W} = \mat Q\mat W\mat Q^\T\) is antisymmetric, because \(\bar{\mat W}^\T = \mat Q\,\mat W^\T\mat Q^\T = -\bar{\mat W}\) (lemma 1.101).
  5. For every vector \(\bar{\vek b}\) we have by step 4 and equation (12.18) \(\bar{\mat W}\bar{\vek b} = \mat Q\,\mat W\,(\mat Q^\T\bar{\vek b}) = \mat Q\big(\vek\varphi\times\mat Q^\T\bar{\vek b}\big)\). By step 3 this is \((\mat Q\vek\varphi)\times(\mat Q\mat Q^\T\bar{\vek b}) = (\mat Q\vek\varphi)\times\bar{\vek b}\), because \(\mat Q\mat Q^\T = \mat I\). An antisymmetric matrix that equals \(\bar{\vek b}\mapsto\vek w\times\bar{\vek b}\) for every \(\bar{\vek b}\) has elements determined by \(\vek w\) (we insert \(\bar{\vek b} = \bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{x}}, \bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{y}}, \bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{z}}\)), so the rotation vector of the matrix \(\bar{\mat W}\) equals \(\mat Q\vek\varphi\).
  6. Point 3. The force \(\vek t\) and the position vector \(\vek r\) have in the new system the components \(\mat Q\vek t\) and \(\mat Q\vek r\) (lemma 2.38), so by point 1 we have \(\int(\mat Q\vek r)\times(\mat Q\vek t)\,\mathrm{d}A = \mat Q\int\vek r\times\vek t\,\mathrm{d}A\), because \(\mat Q\) can be taken out of the integral (the integral is linear, lemma 3.39).
  7. The element. The displacement and the rotation vector of a node are mapped by point 2 with \(\mat Q\) each, so \(\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with four blocks \(\mat Q\) (two for each node). The force and the moment of the surroundings are mapped by lemma 2.38 and point 3 with \(\mat Q\), so \(\bar{\vek F}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R\,\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\).

12.5.4 Assembly and accuracy

Is the solution of the assembled system again a solution of the space frame? We showed this for the plane frame (theorem 12.20). The solution of the space frame (rešitev prostorskega okvirja) for a given load is a set of fields \(\bar u\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\bar v\), \(\bar w\) on the elements, with \(N = EA\,\bar u'\), \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ\,\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}'\), \(M_{\mathrm{z}} = EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v''\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = -EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w''\) and \(V_{\mathrm{y}} = -M_{\mathrm{z}}'\), \(V_{\mathrm{z}} = M_{\mathrm{y}}'\), for which the following holds:

  1. On each element \(N' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(V_{\mathrm{y}}' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(V_{\mathrm{z}}' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{z}}\) (equation (5.12), force components) with continuous loads \(\bar q_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\bar q_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\bar q_{\mathrm{z}}\) in local axes, and \(M_{\mathrm{x}}' = 0\) (no distributed torque).
  2. At each node all elements have the same displacement and the same rotation vector.
  3. At each node the joint is in equilibrium with the external force \(\vek F_{\mathrm{J}}\) and the external moment \(\vek M_{\mathrm{J}}\) (vectors).
  4. The supports prevent the corresponding components of displacement or rotation and the reactions act only in these directions.
Corollary 12.24 Finite element method for a space frame (metoda končnih elementov za prostorski okvir)

Let a connected space frame (definition 12.21, from each node we reach every other node along elements) be divided into space frame elements with constant \(EA\), \(EI_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(EI_{\mathrm{z}}\), \(GJ\) on each element, with loads at the nodes (force and moment as vectors), with continuous axial and transverse loads on the elements without a distributed torque and with supports at the nodes. Let the only small rigid displacement of the frame in which the prevented components are zero be the zero one. Let \(\mat K\) and \(\vek f\) be the matrix and the right-hand side of theorem 7.13 with the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) and forces \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) of equation (12.23) (consistent forces \(\bar{\vek f}_{\mathrm{e}}\) by definition 12.6 in both bending planes, in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\) with the sign changed at the rotations because of \(\bar w' = -\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\)) and let \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\). Then:

  1. The rows of the system \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) for the free unknowns of the node \(J\) are the joint equilibrium: \(\sum_{\mathrm{e} \ni \mathrm{J}}\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}^{(J)} = \vek P_{\mathrm{J}}\), where \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}^{(J)}\) are the six components of the forces of the surroundings on the element \(e\) at the node \(J\) (three forces and three moments) and \(\vek P_{\mathrm{J}}\) is the external force and external moment of the node \(J\) (\(\vek F_{\mathrm{J}}\) and \(\vek M_{\mathrm{J}}\) of condition 3).
  2. The reduced system (theorem 7.18) has exactly one solution \(\vek d\).
  3. The displacements and rotation vectors of the nodes of this solution are exactly the displacements and rotation vectors of the solution of the frame, and \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\) are the forces and moments that the surroundings exert on the nodes of the element. If there is no load on the elements, the fields \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}\), \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}\) are exact along the whole length.
Proof

We have to show that the rows of the system give the equilibrium of the joints (point 1), that the matrix of the free unknowns is positive definite (point 2) and that with a correction of the fields we obtain a solution of the frame, which is unique (point 3). The steps follow the proof of theorem 12.20.

  1. By point 1 of theorem 7.13 the row \(I\) equals \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}}(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} - \vek f_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{i}} = P_{\mathrm{I}}\), where \(P_{\mathrm{I}}\) is the component of the external force or moment of the node in the direction of the unknown \(I\). This is \(\sum_{\mathrm{e} \ni \mathrm{J}}\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}^{(J)} = \vek P_{\mathrm{J}}\), component by component.
  2. The force and the moment of the surroundings on the element are by lemma 12.23 in global components \(\mat R^\T\bar{\vek F}_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\), as in equation (12.23). The piece of the frame around the node \(J\) is in equilibrium of forces and moments, and steps 1 to 4 of the proof of lemma 5.91 hold for all three components of the moment, because the moment equilibrium (2.35) is a vector equation. The element acts on the piece with a force and a moment opposite to \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\) (as in step 4 of the proof of lemma 12.19), so equilibrium of the piece gives the same joint equilibrium as in step 1, which is the joint equilibrium that condition 3 of the solution of the frame requires.
  3. Point 2. Let \(\vek d\) have the prescribed unknowns equal to zero and \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = 0\). By point 2 of theorem 7.13 \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\), each term is \(2U_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\) (lemma 12.22, the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R^\T\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\mat R\) is symmetric, so the hypothesis of corollary 7.14 holds for \(\mat K\)), hence \(U_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) for each element. The energy is a sum of four nonnegative parts, so \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}' = 0\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}' = 0\), \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\) and \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\) (as in step 6 of the proof of lemma 12.19): \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}\) and \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}\) are constant, \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}\) and \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}\) linear.
  4. Such a field is a rigid body motion. A point of the axis at \(\bar x\) moves by \(\big(\bar u_0,\ \bar v_0 + \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar x,\ \bar w_0 - \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar x\big) = \bar{\vek a} + \vek\varphi\times\bar x\,\vek e_{\bar{x}}\) with a constant \(\vek\varphi = (\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}, \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{z}})\) (\(\vek\varphi\times\vek e_{\mathrm{x}} = (0, \varphi_{\mathrm{z}}, -\varphi_{\mathrm{y}})\), equation (12.19)), which is a field of the form \(\vek a + \mat W\,\vek r\) of theorem 1.134. Two elements at a common node have the same displacement and the same rotation vector (lemma 12.23, point 2, for rotations in global axes), and such a field is determined by the displacement of one point and the rotation vector, so the connected frame moves as one whole, that is, as a small rigid displacement of the frame, which is zero by assumption because of the supports. Hence \(\vek d = \vek 0\), and by theorem 7.18 the matrix of the free unknowns is positive definite and the system has exactly one solution.
  5. Point 3. Let \(\vek d\) be this solution and \(\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\). For each element let \(\bar u = \bar u_{\mathrm{h}} + \bar u_0\), \(\bar v = \bar v_{\mathrm{h}} + \bar v_0\), \(\bar w = \bar w_{\mathrm{h}} + \bar w_0\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}} = \bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}\), where \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{xh}}\), \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}\), \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}\) are the finite element fields, \(\bar u_0\) is the solution of the equation \(EA\,\bar u_0'' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{x}}\) with zero values at the ends, \(\bar v_0\) the solution of the equation \(EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v_0'''' = \bar q_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(\bar w_0\) the solution of the equation \(EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w_0'''' = \bar q_{\mathrm{z}}\), both with zero values and slopes at the ends. Such functions exist and are unique (theorem 5.45, as in step 3 of the proof of theorem 12.20). Without load \(\bar u_0 = \bar v_0 = \bar w_0 = 0\).
  6. The fields \(\bar u\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\bar v\), \(\bar w\) satisfy conditions 1 and 2 of the solution of the space frame. Condition 1: \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\), \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}'''' = 0\), \(\bar w_{\mathrm{h}}'''' = 0\) (a linear and a cubic function), so for example \(V_{\mathrm{y}}' = -M_{\mathrm{z}}'' = -EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v'''' = -EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v_0'''' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{z}}' = M_{\mathrm{y}}'' = -EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w'''' = -\bar q_{\mathrm{z}}\), and the torque is constant. Condition 2: \(\bar u_0\), \(\bar v_0\), \(\bar w_0\) are zero at the ends of the element with zero slope, so the fields have at the ends the same displacements and rotations as the finite element fields, these are the values of \(\vek d\), which are equal for elements at the same node.
  7. Forces of the surroundings. For the axial, the torsional and both bending planes separately, the force of the surroundings is the sum of the forces of the finite element fields and the forces of the corrections \(\bar u_0\), \(\bar v_0\), \(\bar w_0\). The first are \(\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\,\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}\) (lemma 12.22), because the element has no load, the second are by step 6 of the proof of theorem 12.20 and corollary 12.15 \(-\bar{\vek f}_{\mathrm{e}}\) (in the plane \(\bar x\bar z\) with the same changed sign). The sum is \(\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\,\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}} - \bar{\vek f}_{\mathrm{e}}\), and by equation (12.23) this is in global components \(\vek F_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  8. By point 1 and step 7 equilibrium holds at the nodes, so condition 3 of the solution. Condition 4 is satisfied by \(\vek d\) by construction. The fields \(\bar u\), \(\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\bar v\), \(\bar w\) are therefore a solution of the space frame. It is the only one. The difference of two solutions has no loads. For each element, by theorem 5.62 for \(\bar v\) and for \(\bar w\) (the plane version with \(\bar w\) in place of \(\bar v\), because of \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = -EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w''\) with the boundary term \(V_{\mathrm{z}}\,\delta\bar w + M_{\mathrm{y}}\,\delta\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{y}}\)) and by integration by parts for the axial force and the torque, the virtual work equals the boundary terms \(\bar{\vek F}_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\delta\bar{\vek d}_{\mathrm{e}}\) (step 7 of the proof of lemma 12.22). Over the nodes these terms add up to the work of the external load as in step 4 of the proof of lemma 12.19, with the joint equilibrium of step 2, and the reactions do no work. With the virtual displacement equal to the difference and a zero right-hand side we obtain \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\int_0^{h_{\mathrm{e}}}\big(EA\,\bar u'^2 + GJ\,\bar\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}'^2 + EI_{\mathrm{z}}\,\bar v''^2 + EI_{\mathrm{y}}\,\bar w''^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}\bar x = 0\). As in steps 3 and 4 the difference is a rigid displacement of the frame, which is zero by assumption.

The corollary has the same condition as the theorem for the plane frame: constant \(EI\) on the element and consistent forces for the load on the element. For a load only at the nodes the field is exact along the whole length, for a load on the element the displacements and rotations of the nodes and the forces at the ends are exact. Torsion has no load on the element, so its part is always exact, and as for the plane frame the accuracy depends on the model: the solution of the model with uniform torsion is exact, but not the solution of the spatial body.

12.5.5 The L-frame in space

Let us return to the question of the section. The L-frame of section 5.14.5, the column \(AB\) (\(a = 1\) m) along the axis \(Z\) and the beam \(BC\) (\(b = 1\) m) along the axis \(X\), is clamped at \(A\) and has at \(C\) the force \(F\) in the direction of the axis \(Y\). The points are \(A = (0, 0, 0)\), \(B = (0, 0, 1)\) and \(C = (1, 0, 1)\) m, the strip section is \(20 \times 40\) mm with axial \(EA = 1{,}568\cdot 10^8\) N, \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\) N m\(^2\) (about the strong axis), \(EI_{\mathrm{w}} = 5226{,}67\) N m\(^2\) (about the weak one), \(G = 75{,}385\) GPa and \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) from equation (12.17). The strip stands so that in the plane of the frame \(XZ\) it is strong (\(40\) mm) and out of the plane weak (\(20\) mm).

The local axes of the column are \(\bar x = \vek e_{\mathrm{Z}}\), \(\bar y = \vek e_{\mathrm{X}}\), \(\bar z = \vek e_{\mathrm{Y}}\), so \(\mat Q_{\mathrm{AB}}\) has the rows \((0, 0, 1)\), \((1, 0, 0)\), \((0, 1, 0)\). The triple is right-handed: \(\bar x\times\bar y = \vek e_{\mathrm{Z}}\times\vek e_{\mathrm{X}} = \vek e_{\mathrm{Y}} = \bar z\), and \(\det\mat Q_{\mathrm{AB}} = 1\) (computation in NumPy). The beam has the local axes \(\bar x = \vek e_{\mathrm{X}}\), \(\bar y = \vek e_{\mathrm{Y}}\), \(\bar z = \vek e_{\mathrm{Z}}\), so \(\mat Q_{\mathrm{BC}} = \mat I\). Each node has six unknowns \((u, v, w, \varphi_{\mathrm{X}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z}})\), \(18\) in all, the clamp at \(A\) removes \(6\) of them, so \(12\) are free. Let us see which of these are nonzero. The elements lie along the axes \(Z\) and \(X\), so the block of the element matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat R^\T\bar{\mat k}_{\mathrm{e}}\mat R\) couples the displacement \(v\) only with the rotations \(\varphi_{\mathrm{X}}\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z}}\) (bending out of the plane \(XZ\) and torsion), and the displacements \(u\), \(w\) only with the rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Y}}\) (axial force and bending in the plane \(XZ\)). The group \((v, \varphi_{\mathrm{X}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z}})\) of the nodes \(B\) and \(C\) therefore has in the matrix \(\mat K\) all entries towards the other six free unknowns equal to zero (NumPy confirms that the largest absolute entry between the groups is \(0\)), the load \(F\) is in the first group, so the other six unknowns are zero (the reduced system has one solution by corollary 12.24). What remains is a \(6 \times 6\) system for \(\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = (v_{\mathrm{B}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{B}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}}, v_{\mathrm{C}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{C}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{C}})\):

\[\begin{aligned} &\begin{bmatrix} 125\,440 & 31\,360 & 31\,360 & -62\,720 & 0 & 31\,360 \\ 31\,360 & 26\,423{,}2 & 0 & 0 & -5516{,}5 & 0 \\ 31\,360 & 0 & 26\,423{,}2 & -31\,360 & 0 & 10\,453{,}3 \\ -62\,720 & 0 & -31\,360 & 62\,720 & 0 & -31\,360 \\ 0 & -5516{,}5 & 0 & 0 & 5516{,}5 & 0 \\ 31\,360 & 0 & 10\,453{,}3 & -31\,360 & 0 & 20\,906{,}7 \end{bmatrix}\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} \\ &\qquad = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ F \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} . \end{aligned} \tag{12.24}\]

The units are N/m, N and N m by rows and columns, as fits the unknowns. Let us explain some entries. The displacement \(v_{\mathrm{B}}\) is for the column the displacement of the end (node 2), for the beam the displacement of the start (node 1), each of them contributes \(12EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h^3 = 62\,720\) N/m, so the entry \((1, 1)\) equals \(125\,440\) N/m. The rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{B}}\) is for the column a bending rotation (the entry \(4EI_{\mathrm{w}}/h = 20\,906{,}7\) N m), for the beam a torsional one (the entry \(GJ/h = 5516{,}5\) N m), together \(26\,423{,}2\) N m. The rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}}\) is the other way round: torsional for the column, bending for the beam. The entry \((5, 5) = GJ/h\) is the torsion of the beam, because \(\varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{C}}\) is the rotation of the end of the beam about its axis.

For \(F = 50\) N the solution of the system (NumPy) gives

\[\begin{aligned} v_{\mathrm{B}} &= 3{,}189\ \text{mm} , & v_{\mathrm{C}} &= 15{,}441\ \text{mm} , & \varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{B}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{C}} &= -4{,}783\cdot 10^{-3} , \\ \varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}} &= 9{,}064\cdot 10^{-3} , & \varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{C}} &= 13{,}847\cdot 10^{-3} . \end{aligned}\]

The point \(C\) therefore moves by \(15{,}44\) mm in the direction of the force. Why at \(50\) N and not at \(500\) N, as in section 5.14.5? Statics holds at every size of the force and the internal forces are proportional to \(F\). The displacements, however, are a computation of the linear model, which requires small slopes, \(v'^2 \le 10^{-2}\) (section 5.3.3). At \(500\) N the model would give \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 154{,}4\) mm, \(15\) % of the length of the element, and the slope \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}138\), so \(\varphi^2 = 0{,}019\), beyond the limit. At \(50\) N we have \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{C}}^2 = 1{,}9\cdot 10^{-4}\).

We check the result in three ways. The first is each of the numbers in the system. The rotation of the column about the axis \(X\) is the rotation of a bending cantilever with the force \(F\) at the top, \(\varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{B}} = -F\,a^2/(2EI_{\mathrm{w}}) = -4{,}783\cdot 10^{-3}\), and since the torque of the beam is zero (the force \(F\) passes through its axis), the beam does not twist and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{C}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{X},\mathrm{B}}\). The rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}} = F\,b\,a/(GJ) = 9{,}064\cdot 10^{-3}\) is the twisting of the column with the constant torque \(F\,b\) (by equation (12.16)). The displacement \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\) is the sum of three parts: the bending of the column \(v_{\mathrm{B}} = F\,a^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{w}}) = 3{,}1888\) mm, the rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}}\) of the lever arm \(b\), that is the twisting of the column, \(b\,\varphi_{\mathrm{Z},\mathrm{B}} = F\,b^2\,a/(GJ) = 9{,}0637\) mm, and the bending of the beam \(F\,b^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{w}}) = 3{,}1888\) mm. The sum is \(15{,}4413\) mm, which is the result of the system.

The second way is by energy. The work of the force is by Clapeyron's theorem (theorem 4.86) \(\tfrac12\,F\,v_{\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}38603\) J, and the strain energy of the elements from their fields is \(0{,}22659\) J (torsion of the column) \(+\ 0{,}07972\) J (bending of the column) \(+\ 0{,}07972\) J (bending of the beam) \(= 0{,}38603\) J. Torsion carries \(58{,}7\) % of the energy and \(58{,}7\) % of the displacement. The sum \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = F\big(b^2 a/(GJ) + a^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{w}}) + b^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{w}})\big)\) uses only the internal forces from statics and the formulas for the cantilever and for torsion, so it is independent of the finite element method, and it agrees with it in all the digits.

The third way shows why we need \(J\). If instead of \(J\) we took the polar moment \(I_{\mathrm{p}} = 133\,333\) mm\(^4\), the twisting would be \(F\,b^2\,a/(G\,I_{\mathrm{p}}) = 4{,}97\) mm instead of \(9{,}06\) mm and \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 11{,}35\) mm instead of \(15{,}44\) mm, which is \(26\) % too little (figure 12.16). A frame that twists therefore needs a property of the section that is not a moment of inertia. This is the torsion constant \(J\), which we compute in chapter 16.

Let us summarize. The space frame element is a bar element, a torsion element and two beam elements in separate groups of twelve unknowns (lemma 12.22). Turning the axes maps all the unknowns with the block \(\mat Q\), because the rotation vector behaves as a vector and not as a number (lemma 12.23). The rows of the assembled system are the joint equilibrium, the solution is unique and is a solution of the space frame exactly at the nodes, and for loads only at the nodes along the whole length (corollary 12.24). With the same procedure we computed what statics could not: the displacement of the point \(C\). In doing so we took the torsion constant \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) as data, and this data is the only thing that this section does not prove.
