The lecture of this chapter (figures built as it is explained)

We start this chapter with bodies that spin: a shaft with a disk in a turbine, a pump or a fan. We can already describe their vibration in two separate parts. The shaft is a beam from chapter 5, which we compute with finite elements by chapter 12, and the vibration of masses and springs was treated in chapters 9 and 10. A spinning rigid body was described in chapter 8, and the vibration of a shaft with a disk in chapter 13. Here we join the two kinds of knowledge and ask what changes when the shaft spins while it vibrates.

The chapter name has two words. A rotor (rotor) is, in mechanics, any body that turns about its own axis, here the shaft together with the disks mounted on it. Dynamics (dinamika) is the part of mechanics that computes motion under forces. Rotordynamics is therefore the computation of the motion of spinning shafts with disks, and above all of their vibrations across the axis, which appear because of the spin. The body does not only vibrate, its axis also circles about the bearing axis, and we will call this circling motion whirl (vrtinčenje).

Every fast machine shows why this matters. A turbine wheel, a pump rotor or a fan turns at a speed where even a small unbalance bends the shaft and loads the two bearings. At certain speeds the response grows strongly, so the designer must know where they lie and either take the rotor through these ranges or avoid them. A spinning disk can also carry only a limited speed itself, because the spin stretches it.

The chapter promises the following. First we take the simplest model, one disk at the middle of a shaft, and solve it completely, then compute the response to unbalance and the speed at which the response peaks. Then we look at how a disk that tilts resists the tilting, and how the whirl frequencies therefore change with the spin speed. Next we divide the shaft into finite elements and so treat rotors with several masses. We will show how damping in the spinning material can make a rotor unstable, how a rotor is balanced, and how stresses set the speed limit of a spinning disk. At the end we connect everything with the other chapters.
