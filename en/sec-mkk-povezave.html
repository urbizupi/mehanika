Chapter 15 Continuum finite elements

15.11 Connections

The abbreviations of the courses are the same as in section 1.19.

  • The mesh and its quality, the discretization error and convergence (AK, chapter 17): in this chapter we refined the mesh in two examples, the shell (the number \(n\)) and the point force (the side \(h\) of the brick), and followed the result as the number of unknowns grew. General error estimators are there.
  • Symmetry and antisymmetry: the octant of the shell used three planes of reflection (MK, AK, chapter 19). The octant lies between the axes \(x\), \(y\) and \(z\). Every two of the axes determine one of the three mirror planes \(x = 0\), \(y = 0\) and \(z = 0\).
  • The mass matrix of continuum elements (DSK, chapters 21 and 10): for the bar we obtained it in lemma 10.47, the same idea, the mass with the same shape functions as the stiffness, gives the mass matrix of the brick and the tetrahedron (COOK (11.2-6), PDF p. 390).
  • Contact and the point force (AK, chapter 25).
  • Plates and shells (AK, chapters 18 and 22): one of three approaches to shell elements starts from spatial elements like those of this chapter and specializes them for a thin shell (degenerated solid elements, degenerirani elementi kontinuuma), similarly to the Mindlin elements of thick plates (COOK §16.1, PDF p. 577).
  • Heat conduction in a body (AK, VT, chapter 14): the conduction matrix of the tetrahedron from lemma 15.72 is used also by the spatial heat problem. Thermal stresses in a body follow the same path as for the heated triangle (lemma 15.28): from the strain we subtract the initial strain that the temperature alone makes (COOK §3.12, PDF p. 129).
  • The wave equation in a body (DSK, chapter 13, theorem 13.33): for a body of any shape we solve it numerically with the elements of this chapter and their mass matrix.

Planned in other chapters: error estimation and computing the stresses at the Gauss points, where they are most accurate, in chapter 17 (section 17.4, ONATE1 §6.7, PDF pp. 229-231, has the same rule of optimal points). Only named: an element for the generalized plane strain state (definition 11.34), which the sources of the project do not have (BOWER §4.1.7, PDF pp. 229-230, treats the state as a solution for the cylinder), mixed elements, in which the pressure is an additional unknown, for nearly incompressible materials (COOK §13.6, PDF pp. 517-518), and the nine-node Lagrange element Q9 and the higher elements of this family (ONATE1 §5.3, PDF pp. 184-189). Infinite elements and nonlinear material are not covered.
