Chapter 18 Plates

18.7 Energy methods for plates

So far we have solved the plate with the equation \(D\nabla^4 w = q\) (theorem 18.12) and with series whose terms satisfy the edge conditions by themselves: the double sine series for the simply supported rectangle (theorem 18.30) and a polynomial in \(r\) for the circle (theorem 18.35). Already a square clamped along its whole edge has no such solution. Timoshenko says of the plate with all four edges built in that its treatment meets many difficulties, and that the first solution usable for numerical computation was given by Kojalovich (TIMO, PDF p. 348). What do we do when no series fits the supports? We use the same energies that we used for the beam in chapters 6, 8 and 13. The section has four parts. First the Ritz method for the clamped square. Then Hamilton's principle for the plate, and from it the shapes of vibration of the simply supported rectangle, which we call modes (načini) because each of them is one way in which the plate can vibrate as a whole with a single frequency. Then the Rayleigh quotient, which estimates the lowest frequency without solving the equation. Finally buckling (uklon): the compressive force in the plane of the plate at which the flat state is replaced by a curved one, the same phenomenon as for the column (chapter 8). The model is the same in all four as in section 18.1: a thin plate (definition 18.1) with constant \(D\) and thickness \(h\), small deflections and ideal supports.

18.7.1 Admissible fields and the energy form

For the beam, the Ritz method looked for the smallest total potential energy among the fields that satisfy the geometric conditions (definition 6.32, theorem 6.34). For the plate the potential of the load is \(-\int_\Omega q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\) and the energy is \(U\) of definition 18.14. The total potential energy is

\[\Pi(w) = U(w) - \int_\Omega q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A , \qquad U(w) = \tfrac12\,B(w, w) , \tag{18.48}\]

where \(B\) is the symmetric bilinear form

\[B(v, w) = D\int_\Omega \Big[\nabla^2 v\,\nabla^2 w - (1 - \nu)\big(v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + v_{\mathrm{yy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} - 2\,v_{\mathrm{xy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\big)\Big]\,\mathrm{d}A . \tag{18.49}\]

At \(v = w\) it is twice the energy of definition 18.14. The mass form is \(\mathcal{M}(v, w) = \rho h\int_\Omega v\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\), where \(\rho h\) is the mass of the plate per unit area of the middle plane. The fields that the energy accepts are defined as follows.

Definition 18.40 Admissible field of a plate (dopustno polje plošče)

Let \(\Omega = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) be the middle plane of a plate (definition 18.1). An admissible field (dopustno polje) is a function \(v : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}\) that satisfies two conditions.

  1. The function \(v\) is twice continuously differentiable on \(\Omega\), so that all terms of the integral (18.49) are continuous and the integral exists.
  2. The field satisfies the geometric boundary conditions (geometrijski robni pogoji) of definition 18.19: \(v = 0\) on every clamped or simply supported edge and \(\partial v/\partial n = 0\) on every clamped edge.

The conditions for moment and shear force on the edge (natural conditions) are not required of an admissible field. A trial space (definition 6.32) all of whose fields are admissible is conforming (skladen).

Source: The definition is ours, after definition 13.49. EP §7.2.6 (p. 455) takes the approximating functions as products of one-dimensional functions that satisfy the geometric conditions.

The Gaussian curvature \(v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}} - v_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\) enters the energy because a plate bends in two directions at once, and in (18.49) it is carried by the term with the factor \(1 - \nu\). The next lemma says that on a rectangle where the field is zero on the edge this term vanishes from the integral. It need not be computed, which simplifies all calculations in this section.

Lemma 18.41 The curvature term on a rectangle is zero (Gaussov člen pri pravokotniku je nič)

Let \(v\) be three times continuously differentiable on \(\Omega = [0, a] \times [0, b]\) and \(v = 0\) on all four edges. Then

\[\int_\Omega \big(v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}} - v_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}A = 0 , \tag{18.50}\]

and so \(B(v, v) = D\int_\Omega (\nabla^2 v)^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), and by polarization \(B(v, w) = D\int_\Omega \nabla^2 v\,\nabla^2 w\,\mathrm{d}A\) for two such fields.

Proof

We must show that the integrand is the divergence of a field and that its flux through the edge is zero.

  1. We have \(\partial_{\mathrm{x}}(v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}}) - \partial_{\mathrm{y}}(v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xy}}) = v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}} + v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xyy}} - v_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 - v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xyy}} = v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}} - v_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\). The mixed derivatives agree because the derivatives up to third order are continuous.
  2. The plane divergence theorem (theorem 4.14) for the field \((v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}},\, -v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xy}})\) on the rectangle says that the integral equals \(\oint (v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}}\,n_{\mathrm{x}} - v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xy}}\,n_{\mathrm{y}})\,\mathrm{d}s\).
  3. On the edges \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\) we have \(n_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = \pm 1\), leaving \(\pm v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}}\). On the edge \(v(0, y) = 0\) for every \(y\), so the second derivative along the edge is also zero: \(v_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\). The term is zero.
  4. On the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) we have \(n_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) and \(n_{\mathrm{y}} = \pm 1\), leaving \(\mp v_{\mathrm{x}}\,v_{\mathrm{xy}}\). On the edge \(v(x, 0) = 0\) for every \(x\), so \(v_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) along the edge. The term is zero.
  5. Hence (18.50). The term with \((1 - \nu)\) in (18.49) at \(w = v\) is \(-2D(1 - \nu)\int (v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,v_{\mathrm{yy}} - v_{\mathrm{xy}}^2)\,\mathrm{d}A\), and it vanishes. For two fields \(v\), \(w\) we apply this to \(v + w\), \(v\) and \(w\): the bilinear expression \(v_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} + v_{\mathrm{yy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} - 2\,v_{\mathrm{xy}}\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is the polarization of the Gaussian expression, so its integral is zero.

Source: The proof is not from the sources. EP (pp. 431-434) has the result more generally: for a general edge the term gives only an edge integral, treated by theorem 18.21.

On a rectangle, clamping and simple support therefore give the same energy, because they differ only in the condition on the slope, which did not enter (18.50). The difference shows only in which fields are admissible. The Ritz method for the clamped square is the first test.

18.7.2 The Ritz method for the clamped square

Take a square of side \(a\), clamped along the whole edge and loaded uniformly by \(q\). The steel plate has \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\), \(h = 10\) mm, so \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m, and \(a = 500\) mm, \(q = 10\) kPa, as in the example of the square in section 18.5. The trial fields must be zero and have zero slope on the edge. The polynomial \((1 - \xi^2)^2(1 - \eta^2)^2\) in the dimensionless coordinates \(\xi = 2x/a - 1\), \(\eta = 2y/a - 1\) from \([-1, 1]^2\) does this, since it has a double zero on every edge (\(\xi = \pm 1\) or \(\eta = \pm 1\)). The plate and the trial fields are symmetric about both midlines, so we also take products with \(\xi^{2i}\eta^{2j}\).

Theorem 18.42 Ritz method for a clamped rectangular plate (Ritzova metoda za vpeto pravokotno ploščo)

Let the plate be a rectangle \(\Omega\) clamped along the whole edge, with constant \(D\) and continuous load \(q\). Let \(\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{\mathrm{N}}\) be linearly independent polynomials with \(\varphi_{\mathrm{p}} = 0\) and \(\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}/\partial n = 0\) on the edge, and \(K_{\mathrm{ps}} = B(\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{s}})\), \(f_{\mathrm{p}} = \int_\Omega q\,\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). Then:

  1. The matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric and positive definite, \(K_{\mathrm{ps}} = D\int_\Omega \nabla^2\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\,\nabla^2\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  2. The field \(w_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{p}} c_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\) has the smallest value of \(\Pi\) among all fields of the trial space exactly when \(\mat K\,\vek c = \vek f\).
  3. The work of the load on the Ritz field is \(\int_\Omega q\,w_{\mathrm{N}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \vek c\cdot\vek f\), and it is not larger than the work on the true solution \(w\), if one exists. For nested trial spaces this work does not decrease with \(N\).
Proof

We must show that the clamped field fits lemma 18.41, that \(\Pi\) is a quadratic function with a definite matrix on the trial space, and that theorem 6.34 then applies.

  1. The fields \(\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\) are zero on the edge, so by lemma 18.41 \(K_{\mathrm{ps}} = D\int\nabla^2\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\,\nabla^2\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). Symmetry follows from the symmetry of the product.
  2. Let \(v = \sum c_{\mathrm{p}}\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}\) and \(\vek c^\T\mat K\,\vek c = D\int(\nabla^2 v)^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\). The integrand is continuous and nonnegative, hence zero everywhere (lemma 3.39): \(\nabla^2 v = 0\). Theorem 4.14 for the field \(v\,\nabla v\) gives \(\int|\nabla v|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \oint v\,\partial v/\partial n\,\mathrm{d}s - \int v\,\nabla^2 v\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\), because \(v = 0\) on the edge. Again a continuous nonnegative integrand: \(\nabla v = 0\), so \(v\) is constant and by the edge condition zero. Independence of the fields gives \(\vek c = \vek 0\). The matrix is therefore positive definite.
  3. The potential is \(\Pi(v) = \tfrac12\,\vek c^\T\mat K\,\vek c - \vek c\cdot\vek f\). At \(\mat K\,\vek c^* = \vek f\) we have \(\Pi = \Pi^* + \tfrac12(\vek c - \vek c^*)^\T\mat K(\vek c - \vek c^*)\) with \(\Pi^* = -\tfrac12\,\vek c^*\cdot\vek f\) (expanding the square, symmetry of \(\mat K\)). By step 2 the second term is positive except at \(\vek c = \vek c^*\). This is point 2.
  4. The work is \(\int q\,w_{\mathrm{N}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \sum c_{\mathrm{p}} f_{\mathrm{p}} = \vek c^*\cdot\vek f\) and \(\Pi(w_{\mathrm{N}}) = -\tfrac12\,\vek c^*\cdot\vek f\). The true \(w\) satisfies the weak form \(B(w, \varphi) = \int q\,\varphi\,\mathrm{d}A\) for every clamped field \(\varphi\) (theorem 18.21), and clamped fields have zero essential conditions. By point 2 of theorem 6.34, \(\int q\,w_{\mathrm{N}}\,\mathrm{d}A \le \int q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\). If the first space is contained in the second, the second space also contains the Ritz field of the first, so the smallest value of \(\Pi\) on the second space (point 2) is at most as large as on the first. Since \(\Pi(w_{\mathrm{N}})\) is minus half the work, the work does not decrease.

Source: EP §7.2.6 (pp. 455-461), examples with functions that satisfy the geometric conditions. The proof in steps is not from the sources, it follows theorem 6.34. Existence of the solution \(w\) is assumed and not proved (EP).

The theorem bounds the work, not the central deflection, because the work \(\int q\,w\,\mathrm{d}A\) is a global measure of compliance, while the deflection at one point can be distorted by the error around that point in either direction. What if the field is not admissible? Take the field \(x(a - x)\,y(a - y)\), which is zero on the edge but has a nonzero slope there. It is admissible for the simply supported plate, but not for the clamped one. Lemma 18.41 holds for it, since it is zero on the edge, and \(B(v, v)\) does not contain \(\nu\). Steps 1 to 3 of the proof of theorem 18.42 use only \(v = 0\) on the edge, not the slope, so the same procedure gives \(c = f/B\) with \(f = a^4/36\) and \(B = (22/45)\,D\) (units with \(a = 1\)), hence \(w_{\mathrm{c}} = 0{,}003551\ q a^4/D\) and work \(0{,}001578\ q a^6/D\) (outlines/ch-plosce/num-07.out, part B). This is four times more work than with ten admissible terms and about three times more deflection than the true clamped plate. The reason is in step 4: theorem 6.34 requires the difference \(e = w - w_{\mathrm{N}}\) to be a test function with zero essential conditions, but here \(e\) has a slope on the edge and is not a test function. The trial space is here wider than the clamped fields, hence softer, and it finds the minimum of \(\Pi\) for another problem: a plate whose edge may rotate. The value \(0{,}003551\) is an approximation for the simply supported plate (Navier, section 18.5, \(0{,}004062\)), not for the clamped one.

18.7.3 The plate in motion: Hamilton's principle

For the beam, Hamilton's principle (theorem 13.28) gave the equation of motion and the edge conditions from the stationarity of a single action. The same procedure works for the plate. The kinetic energy of the plate is \(T = \tfrac12\int_\Omega\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), because a piece of the middle plane of area \(A_{\mathrm{k}}\) has mass \(\rho h A_{\mathrm{k}}\) and velocity \(w_{\mathrm{t}}\). This neglects the rotatory inertia of the normal, as for the beam. How good is that? Rotatory inertia adds to the term \(\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\) the term \(\tfrac{\rho h^3}{12}(w_{\mathrm{xt}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{yt}}^2)\). For a shape with wave number \(k\) the ratio is \(k^2h^2/12\) (as for the beam, section 13.5.1). The first mode of the simply supported square has \(k^2 = 2\pi^2/a^2\), so at \(a = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm the ratio is \(6{,}6\cdot 10^{-4}\), and it stays below \(10^{-2}\) as long as \(k\,h < 0{,}34\) (\(0{,}34^2/12 = 0{,}0096\)), that is a wavelength \(2\pi/k\) longer than \(18{,}5\,h\).

Definition 18.43 Action of a plate (akcija plošče)

Let \(\Omega = [0, a]\times[0, b]\) be a plate with constant \(D\) and \(\rho h\), without edge loads, and let \([t_1, t_2]\) be a time interval. The action (definition 8.101) of a field \(w : \Omega\times[t_1, t_2] \to \mathbb{R}\) is

\[\mathcal{S}(w) = \int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}\Big(\tfrac12\rho h\int_\Omega w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A - \Pi(w(\cdot, t))\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t , \tag{18.51}\]

where \(\Pi\) is the potential (18.48). The field \(w\) is admissible if \(w\), \(w_{\mathrm{t}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{tt}}\) and all derivatives of \(w\) in \(x\), \(y\) up to fourth order are continuous on \(\Omega\times[t_1, t_2]\) and \(w(\cdot, t)\) is an admissible field of the plate (definition 18.40) at every \(t\). An admissible variation \(\eta\) is a continuously differentiable function whose derivatives \(\eta_{\mathrm{t}}\), \(\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\eta_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\eta_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\eta_{\mathrm{xy}}\), \(\eta_{\mathrm{yy}}\) are continuous, which is zero at \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) and which satisfies on the edge the same geometric conditions as the field, but with zero (where \(w\) is prescribed, \(\eta = 0\), where the slope is prescribed, \(\partial\eta/\partial n = 0\)).

Source: After theorem 13.28, expressed with the energy of the plate. The definition is ours.

The space \((x, y, t)\) is the box \(\Omega\times[t_1, t_2]\), on which the fundamental lemma with smooth bumps works (lemma 4.122), as for the beam. The difference from the beam is the double integration by parts in space, which for statics theorem 18.21 has already done.

Theorem 18.44 Hamilton's principle for a plate (Hamiltonovo načelo za ploščo)

Let the plate be as in definition 18.43, \(q\) continuous on \(\Omega\times[t_1, t_2]\) and \(w\) an admissible field. Then \(w\) is a stationary point of the action (18.51) exactly when

\[\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + D\,\nabla^4 w = q \quad \text{on } \Omega\times(t_1, t_2) , \tag{18.52}\]

and at every \(t\) on every edge the natural conditions of theorem 18.21 for the given type of edge hold (simply supported: \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), free: \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), with corner forces).

Proof

We must show that the first variation is the sum of a time part, which gives the inertial term, and a static part, which we already have. Then we extract the equation and the edge conditions from it as for the beam.

  1. At \(w + \alpha\eta\) the integrand is a polynomial of degree two in \(\alpha\) with continuous coefficients, so \(\mathcal{S}(w + \alpha\eta) = \mathcal{S}(w) + \alpha\,B_1 + \alpha^2 B_2\) and \(\delta\mathcal{S} = B_1\) with \(B_1 = \int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}\big(\rho h\int_\Omega w_{\mathrm{t}}\eta_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}A - B(w, \eta) + \int_\Omega q\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\), where we used (18.49).
  2. For every \((x, y)\), \(w_{\mathrm{t}}\) and \(\eta\) are continuously differentiable functions of time. Integration by parts (corollary 3.41) gives \(\int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}w_{\mathrm{t}}\eta_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}t = -\int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}w_{\mathrm{tt}}\eta\,\mathrm{d}t\), since \(\eta = 0\) at \(t_1\) and \(t_2\). We swap the order of integration over \(\Omega\) and over \(t\) (theorem 4.9). The first term of \(B_1\) is therefore \(-\int\int\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{tt}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}t\).
  3. At every \(t\), \(-B(w, \eta) + \int q\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\) is minus the first variation of the static potential energy in the direction \(\eta(\cdot, t)\). By theorem 18.21 this is \(\int_\Omega(D\nabla^4 w - q)\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\) and edge terms \(E(w, \eta)\), which contain \(\eta\) or \(\partial\eta/\partial n\) on the edge multiplied by \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\), \(V_{\mathrm{n}}\) or a corner force.
  4. Altogether \(\delta\mathcal{S} = -\int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}\int_\Omega(\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + D\nabla^4 w - q)\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\,\mathrm{d}t - \int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}E(w, \eta)\,\mathrm{d}t\).
  5. If (18.52) and the natural conditions hold, the first integrand is zero and the edge terms are zero, because the coefficient is zero (natural condition) or \(\eta\) (or \(\partial\eta/\partial n\)) is zero there by the definition of an admissible variation. So \(\delta\mathcal{S} = 0\).
  6. Conversely, let \(\delta\mathcal{S}(w, \eta) = 0\) for every admissible \(\eta\). Let \(R = \rho h\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + D\nabla^4 w - q\), a continuous function on the box, and let \(\eta = \kappa_1(x)\,\kappa_2(y)\,\kappa_3(t)\) be a product of smooth bumps (lemma 4.122), zero with its derivatives on the boundary of the box. It is admissible with zero edge terms, so \(\int R\,\eta = 0\) for every box in the interior, and by point 3 of lemma 4.122 \(R = 0\) in the interior, and by continuity also on the boundary. This is (18.52).
  7. At \(R = 0\), \(\int E(w, \eta)\,\mathrm{d}t = 0\) for every admissible \(\eta\). For every edge and every field \(\eta_{\mathrm{e}}(x, y)\) used by the proof of theorem 18.21 to extract the conditions, we take \(\eta = \eta_{\mathrm{e}}(x, y)\,\psi(t)\), where \(\psi\) is any function with a continuous derivative on \([t_1, t_2]\) and \(\psi(t_1) = \psi(t_2) = 0\). The edge terms are linear in \(\eta\), so \(\int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2}E\big(w(\cdot, t), \eta_{\mathrm{e}}\big)\,\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = 0\) for every such \(\psi\). The function \(t \mapsto E\big(w(\cdot, t), \eta_{\mathrm{e}}\big)\) is continuous, so by the fundamental lemma (lemma 4.77) it is zero at every \(t\). From this the proof of theorem 18.21 extracts the natural conditions at every \(t\).

Source: EP §5.3 (pp. 250-251) for Hamilton's principle in general. The proof in steps is not from the sources, it follows theorem 13.28, and the static part theorem 18.21.

Equation (18.52) is the plate equation (18.17) of theorem 18.12, in which we added to the load \(q\) the inertial force per unit area \(-\rho h\,w_{\mathrm{tt}}\). This is also a test of theorem by another route: the balance of a finite piece of the middle plane (lemma 18.10) with this extra force gives the same equation, just as for the beam theorem 13.28 and lemma 13.29 gave the same equation. The edge conditions are the same as at rest, because the edge of the plate has no mass.

18.7.4 Modes of the simply supported rectangle

We look for a motion in which the whole plate moves in the same shape with frequency \(\omega\): \(w(x, y, t) = W(x, y)\cos\omega t\) at \(q = 0\). From (18.52) follows \(D\,\nabla^4 W = \omega^2\rho h\,W\) with the edge conditions of the plate. The zero solution \(W = 0\) always exists, we are interested in solutions \(W \ne 0\). If one exists, we call \(W\) a mode (način) and \(\omega\) a natural frequency (lastna frekvenca). For the simply supported rectangle \(W = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) on the edge. On the edge \(x = 0\) we have \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = -D(W_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu W_{\mathrm{yy}})\), and since \(W = 0\) along the edge, \(W_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) and the condition \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) means \(W_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\), so \(\nabla^2 W = 0\) on every edge.

For the modes we need to know what the scalar product with a double sine function gives. Let \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}(x, y) = \sin(m\pi x/a)\,\sin(n\pi y/b)\) and \(\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 = \pi^2(m^2/a^2 + n^2/b^2)\).

Lemma 18.45 Coefficients of derivatives in the double sine series (koeficienti odvodov v dvojni sinusni vrsti)

Let \(W\) be four times continuously differentiable on \(\Omega\) with \(W = 0\) and \(\nabla^2 W = 0\) on all edges, and \(c_{\mathrm{mn}} = \int_\Omega W\,S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). Then

\[\int_\Omega \nabla^4 W\,S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4\,c_{\mathrm{mn}} , \qquad \int_\Omega W_{\mathrm{xx}}\,S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A = -\Big(\frac{m\pi}{a}\Big)^2 c_{\mathrm{mn}} . \tag{18.53}\]
Proof

We must show that the edge terms in the integration by parts vanish because of the two edge conditions.

  1. Theorem 4.14 for the field \(f\nabla g - g\nabla f\) gives Green's identity \(\int(f\nabla^2 g - g\nabla^2 f)\,\mathrm{d}A = \oint(f\,\partial g/\partial n - g\,\partial f/\partial n)\,\mathrm{d}s\) for twice continuously differentiable \(f\), \(g\).
  2. With \(f = W\), \(g = S_{\mathrm{mn}}\): \(S_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) on the edge and \(W = 0\) on the edge, the edge integral is zero. Since \(\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}} = -\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) (differentiating sines twice), \(-\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 c_{\mathrm{mn}} = \int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\nabla^2 W\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  3. Once more with \(f = \nabla^2 W\), which is zero on the edge: \(\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\nabla^2(\nabla^2 W)\,\mathrm{d}A = -\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\nabla^2 W\,\mathrm{d}A = \kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 c_{\mathrm{mn}}\). This is the first equation.
  4. Theorem 4.14 for the field \((W\,\partial_{\mathrm{x}} S_{\mathrm{mn}} - S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,W_{\mathrm{x}},\ 0)\) gives \(\int(W\,S_{\mathrm{xx}} - S\,W_{\mathrm{xx}})\,\mathrm{d}A = \oint(W\,S_{\mathrm{x}} - S\,W_{\mathrm{x}})\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s = 0\), because \(W\) and \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) are zero on the edge. Since \(S_{\mathrm{xx}} = -(m\pi/a)^2 S\), the second equation follows.

Source: The proof is not from the sources.

Theorem 18.46 Modes of a simply supported rectangular plate (načini prosto podprtega pravokotnika)

Let the plate be a rectangle \([0, a]\times[0, b]\), simply supported along the whole edge, with constant \(D\) and \(\rho h\). The functions \(W_{\mathrm{mn}} = S_{\mathrm{mn}}\), \(m, n \ge 1\), are modes with natural frequencies

\[\omega_{\mathrm{mn}} = \pi^2\Big(\frac{m^2}{a^2} + \frac{n^2}{b^2}\Big)\sqrt{\frac{D}{\rho h}} . \tag{18.54}\]

There are no other modes: every mode \(W\) with frequency \(\omega\) is a linear combination of those \(W_{\mathrm{mn}}\) that have \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}} = \omega\), and \(\omega\) is one of the \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}\).

Proof

We must show that \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) is a mode and that every mode is a combination. For the second we use lemma 18.45 and the completeness of the sines (corollary 13.13) in each variable separately.

  1. The field \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) is \(0\) on the edge, and \(\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}} = -\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) on the edge, so it satisfies both edge conditions.
  2. On substitution into the mode equation, \(D\,\nabla^4 S_{\mathrm{mn}} = D\,\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) (applying \(\nabla^2\) twice). The equation \(D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 = \omega^2\rho h\) gives \(\omega = \kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\sqrt{D/\rho h}\), which is (18.54).
  3. Let \(W\) be a mode with frequency \(\omega\), so \(D\nabla^4 W = \omega^2\rho h\,W\) with edge conditions \(W = 0\), \(\nabla^2 W = 0\). We multiply by \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\), integrate and use (18.53): \(D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 c_{\mathrm{mn}} = \omega^2\rho h\,c_{\mathrm{mn}}\), so \((\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}^2 - \omega^2)\,c_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) for every \((m, n)\).
  4. Let \(P\) be the set of pairs with \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}} = \omega\). The set is finite, because \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}\to\infty\). Let \(\tilde W = W - \tfrac{4}{ab}\sum_{(\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n})\in \mathrm{P}}c_{\mathrm{mn}}S_{\mathrm{mn}}\), since \(\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A = ab/4\) and the \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) are mutually orthogonal (lemma 9.87). All coefficients of \(\tilde W\) are zero: on \(P\) by construction, elsewhere by step 3.
  5. The function \(g_{\mathrm{n}}(x) = \int_0^b\tilde W(x, y)\,\sin(n\pi y/b)\,\mathrm{d}y\) is continuously differentiable (lemma 4.19) and \(g_{\mathrm{n}}(0) = g_{\mathrm{n}}(a) = 0\). Its coefficients \(\int g_{\mathrm{n}}\sin(m\pi x/a)\,\mathrm{d}x = \tilde c_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) (theorem 4.9 for the order), so \(g_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) by corollary 13.13. At every \(x\), \(\tilde W(x, \cdot)\) is therefore a continuously differentiable function with zeros at the ends and zero sine coefficients, and by the same corollary \(\tilde W = 0\).
  6. Hence \(W = \tfrac{4}{ab}\sum_{\mathrm{P}} c_{\mathrm{mn}}S_{\mathrm{mn}}\), and since \(W\ne 0\), \(P \ne \emptyset\) and \(\omega\) is one of the \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}\).

Source: CK §13.6 (13.67) to (13.69) (PDF p. 425), equation (18.54). EP §7.2.4.2 (pp. 443-444). The proof in steps is not from the sources.

What does this say in numbers? The steel plate as above (\(a = b = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm, \(D = 17\,948{,}7\) N m, \(\rho = 7800\) kg/m³) has \(f_{11} = \omega_{11}/(2\pi) = 190{,}62\) Hz. The ratios are \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}/\omega_{11} = (m^2 + n^2)/2\), so \(f_{12} = f_{21} = 476{,}56\) Hz (\(2{,}5\) times), \(f_{22} = 762{,}50\) Hz (\(4\) times), \(f_{13} = f_{31} = 953{,}12\) Hz (\(5\) times). For the beam the ratios grow like \(k^2\), here like \(m^2 + n^2\), and the square has double frequencies \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}} = \omega_{\mathrm{nm}}\). A rectangle with \(b = 1{,}5\,a\) has none: the dimensionless values \(\omega\,a^2\sqrt{\rho h/D}/\pi^2 = m^2 + n^2a^2/b^2\) are \(1{,}444\), \(2{,}778\), \(4{,}444\), \(5{,}000\) and \(5{,}778\) for \((1, 1)\), \((1, 2)\), \((2, 1)\), \((1, 3)\) and \((2, 2)\). The splitting of \((1, 2)\) and \((2, 1)\) is a consequence of the different side lengths, as CK says (PDF p. 426): the plate »divides itself into squares«, and for \(a > b\) the mode \((2, 1)\) is lower than \((1, 2)\).

Let us draw the axes \(x\) and \(y\) and a square. In it is first the basic mode \((1, 1)\): the deflection has the same sign everywhere, and there are no nodal lines except the edges. In \((1, 2)\) the deflection is \(\sin(\pi x/a)\sin(2\pi y/a)\), which is zero at \(y = a/2\): a horizontal nodal line divides the square into two fields of opposite sign. In \((2, 1)\) the same line is vertical, in \((2, 2)\) both are there. The square has \(\omega_{12} = \omega_{21}\), so by theorem 18.46 every combination \(W_{12} + c\,W_{21}\) is also a mode with the same frequency, since it is a sum of two \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) with the same \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}\). Its nodal lines are no longer parallel to the edges. From \(\sin 2\theta = 2\sin\theta\cos\theta\),

\[W_{12} \pm W_{21} = 2\,\sin\frac{\pi x}{a}\,\sin\frac{\pi y}{a}\Big(\cos\frac{\pi y}{a} \pm \cos\frac{\pi x}{a}\Big) , \tag{18.55}\]

and the two factors \(\sin\) are positive in the interior of the square, so the nodal line is the zero of the bracket. The function \(\cos\) is injective on \((0, a)\), so \(\cos(\pi y/a) = -\cos(\pi x/a)\) means \(\pi y/a = \pi - \pi x/a\), that is \(x + y = a\) for the sign \(+\), and \(x = y\) for the sign \(-\). The nodal lines are the diagonals of the square, the sum has one, the difference has the other. The single modes \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) have nodal lines parallel to the edges, the combinations do not, so a nodal line is a property of the chosen shape and not of the frequency. Chladni showed the nodal lines of plates with sand that collects on them (TIMO, PDF p. 127). For a square, therefore, the shape we see depends on how we excite the plate.

18.7.5 The Rayleigh quotient of a plate

The Rayleigh quotient of chapter 13 (definition 13.52) is for the plate \(R(v) = B(v, v)/\mathcal{M}(v, v)\) for an admissible field \(v \ne 0\), and at \(v = W_{\mathrm{mn}}\) it equals \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\). We want to know whether for an arbitrary admissible field of the simply supported rectangle it is always above \(\omega_{11}^2\), as for the beam (theorem 13.54).

Theorem 18.47 Rayleigh quotient of a simply supported plate (Rayleighov količnik prosto podprte plošče)

Let the plate be a simply supported rectangle and \(v \ne 0\) an admissible field (definition 18.40) that is three times continuously differentiable. Let Parseval's equality \(\int v^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac{ab}{4}\sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}}b_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\) hold for the coefficients \(b_{\mathrm{mn}} = \tfrac{4}{ab}\int v\,S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) (completeness assumption). Then \(R(v)\ge\omega_{11}^2\), with \(\omega_{11}\) from (18.54).

Proof

We must show that the \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) are an orthogonal family with respect to both forms, and then repeat the steps of the proof of theorem 13.54.

  1. For admissible \(v\), \(B(S_{\mathrm{mn}}, v) = D\int\nabla^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,\nabla^2 v\,\mathrm{d}A\) (lemma 18.41 after polarization) \(= -D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\nabla^2 v\,\mathrm{d}A\). Green's identity (step 1 of lemma 18.45) with \(S_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) and \(v = 0\) on the edge gives \(\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\nabla^2 v\,\mathrm{d}A = -\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}\,v\,\mathrm{d}A\). Hence \(B(S_{\mathrm{mn}}, v) = D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}v\,\mathrm{d}A = \omega_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,\mathcal{M}(S_{\mathrm{mn}}, v)\).
  2. Let \(S_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}\le \mathrm{N}}b_{\mathrm{mn}}S_{\mathrm{mn}}\). This is an admissible field. For \(v - S_{\mathrm{N}}\) we have \(B(v - S_{\mathrm{N}}, v - S_{\mathrm{N}}) \ge 0\) by lemma 18.41. Expanding gives \(B(v, v) - 2B(v, S_{\mathrm{N}}) + B(S_{\mathrm{N}}, S_{\mathrm{N}}) \ge 0\).
  3. By step 1 and \(\int S_{\mathrm{mn}}S_{\mathrm{kl}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac{ab}{4}\delta_{\mathrm{mk}}\delta_{\mathrm{nl}}\), \(B(v, S_{\mathrm{N}}) = B(S_{\mathrm{N}}, S_{\mathrm{N}}) = \sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}\le \mathrm{N}}\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\,\rho h\,\tfrac{ab}{4}\,b_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\). So \(B(v, v)\ge\sum_{\mathrm{m}, \mathrm{n}\le \mathrm{N}}\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\rho h\tfrac{ab}{4}b_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\) for every \(N\), and with \(N\to\infty\) for the whole sum.
  4. By the assumption, \(\mathcal{M}(v, v) = \rho h\tfrac{ab}{4}\sum b_{\mathrm{mn}}^2\). Since \(\omega_{\mathrm{mn}}\ge\omega_{11}\), \(B(v, v)\ge\omega_{11}^2\,\mathcal{M}(v, v)\), and \(R(v)\ge\omega_{11}^2\).

Source: CK (PDF p. 418): the Rayleigh method is an upper bound of the lowest frequency, which CK only announces. The proof in steps is not from the sources, it follows theorem 13.54. The completeness assumption is derived in each direction separately by lemma 13.53, in both together it is not proved.

Let us try \(v = x(a - x)\,y(a - y)\), which is zero on the edge. For \(a = 1\), \(B(v, v) = D\int(\nabla^2 v)^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 22\,D/45\) (independent of \(\nu\) by lemma 18.41) and \(\mathcal{M}(v, v) = \rho h/900\), so \(\omega^2a^4\rho h/D = 440\) and \(\omega a^2\sqrt{\rho h/D} = 20{,}976\), while the true value is \(2\pi^2 = 19{,}739\). The quotient is \(12{,}9\) % above \(\omega_{11}^2\), the frequency \(6{,}3\) % above the true one. The theorem is thereby tested and holds. The Ritz method for the clamped square (section 18.7.2) gives with ten terms \(\omega a^2\sqrt{\rho h/D} = 35{,}9863\), with six \(35{,}9888\), with three and one term \(36{,}000\), falling toward the true value. EP in table 7.2.11 (p. 475) gives \(36{,}000\) at \(a/b = 1\) for \(\nu = 0{,}25\), computed with the Ritz method with four terms (EP: \(N = 2\) functions in each direction), which agrees with our value for one and three terms. This is not the true value, which after ten terms is at most \(35{,}9863\) if the upper bound holds. For clamping the frequency does not depend on \(\nu\), because the expression for \(B\) by lemma 18.41 does not contain \(\nu\), which agrees. For \(a = 500\) mm, \(h = 10\) mm this is \(f_1 = 347{,}53\) Hz, which is \(1{,}82\) times the \(f_{11} = 190{,}62\) Hz of the simply supported square. We do not prove theorem 13.55 for the clamped plate, because we would need completeness of the modes of the clamped plate, so the falling of the values here is an observation and not a proof.

What if the field is not admissible? Take \(v = 1\), which is not zero on the edge. Then \(B(v, v) = 0\) and \(\mathcal{M}(v, v) = \rho h\,a^2 > 0\), so \(R(1) = 0 < \omega_{11}^2\). The quotient is below the lowest simply supported eigenvalue. In step 1 of the proof Green's identity fails: the edge term \(\oint v\,\partial S_{\mathrm{mn}}/\partial n\,\mathrm{d}s\) is not zero, because \(v = 1\) on the edge. Physically the field \(v = 1\) is not a deflection of a supported plate but a rigid shift of the whole plate in the direction of the deflection, which has no energy.

18.7.6 Buckling of the simply supported rectangle

In chapter 8 (theorem 8.134) we loaded columns with a compressive force and looked for the smallest at which a curved position exists besides the straight one. Plates compressed by a force in their plane have the same question, only the compressive force is distributed along the edge and we measure it per unit length of edge.

From history

Navier derived the differential equation of the buckled surface of a plate under compressive forces uniformly distributed along the edge (TIMO, PDF p. 130). On the harder problem, a plate supported at its four corners, he did not reach an acceptable solution. For the simply supported rectangle the formula for the critical compressive stress was given by Bryan, and it was the first theoretical investigation of the stability of compressed plates. As an example of its use he mentions the choice of thickness of compressed steel sheets in the hull of a ship (TIMO, PDF p. 238). Bryan solved precisely this simply supported rectangle, which we shall solve too.

The story showed that the question about a compressed plate arises for the simply supported rectangle. At what force on the edge does the plate stop being flat? For the computation we need the work of the in-plane forces on the deflection.

Lemma 18.48 Work of in-plane forces on the deflection of a plate (delo ravninskih sil na povesu plošče)

Let \(\Omega = [0, a]\times[0, b]\) and \(w\) continuously differentiable with \(w(0, y) = w(a, y) = 0\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\le 10^{-2}\). A compressive force \(N_0\) per unit length of edge, acting on the edges \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\) in the direction of the \(x\) axis, has at deflection \(w\) the potential energy \(-\tfrac{N_0}{2}\int_\Omega w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) with relative error at most \(w_{\mathrm{x},\max}^2/4\le 0{,}25\) %.

Proof

We must show that every strip \(y = \text{const}\) is a column of theorem 8.134, and add over the strips.

  1. For every \(y\), \(v_{\mathrm{y}}(x) = w(x, y)\) is continuously differentiable with \(v_{\mathrm{y}}(0) = v_{\mathrm{y}}(a) = 0\) and \(v_{\mathrm{y}}'^2\le 10^{-2}\). By lemma 8.133 the shortening \(\Delta(y)\) of this strip lies between \((1 - v'^2_{\max}/4)\tfrac12\int v_{\mathrm{y}}'^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) and \(\tfrac12\int v_{\mathrm{y}}'^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  2. The compressive force \(N_0\) on a piece of edge of length \(\ell\) is \(N_0\ell\), its potential is \(-N_0\ell\,\Delta\) (lemma 8.133). The load on the edge is distributed over \(y\in[0, b]\), so the total potential is \(-N_0\int_0^b\Delta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\) (lemma 3.39).
  3. We insert the bounds of step 1 and swap the order of integration (theorem 4.9): \(\int_0^b\tfrac12\int_0^a w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y = \tfrac12\int_\Omega w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\).

Source: As lemma 8.133. EP §7.2.4.3 (p. 445) has the same term with \(\hat N_{\mathrm{xx}}\) in the plate equation. The proof is not from the sources.

The idealization: before buckling the plate is in a uniform plane stress state with force \(N_0\) per unit length (definition 11.32), and the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) are free in the plane. The buckled shape \(w\) thus adds to the energy essentially only the term of the lemma and the bending energy. The term with the in-plane strain of \(w\) itself, which is of fourth order in \(w\), we neglect.

Theorem 18.49 Buckling of a simply supported rectangular plate (uklon prosto podprtega pravokotnika)

Let the plate be a simply supported rectangle \([0, a]\times[0, b]\), compressed by a force \(N_0\) per unit length on the edges \(x = 0\) and \(x = a\). The equilibrium equation for a curved position that is zero on the edge, with \(w = 0\) and \(\nabla^2 w = 0\) on all edges, is

\[D\,\nabla^4 w + N_0\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0 . \tag{18.56}\]

A nonzero solution exists exactly when \(N_0 = N_{\mathrm{mn}}\) for some pair \((m, n)\), where

\[N_{\mathrm{mn}} = \pi^2 D\,\frac{(m^2/a^2 + n^2/b^2)^2}{m^2/a^2} = \frac{\pi^2 D}{b^2}\Big(\frac{m b}{a} + \frac{n^2 a}{m b}\Big)^2 , \tag{18.57}\]

and the solution is then a combination of the \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) with \(N_{\mathrm{mn}} = N_0\). The smallest such force is

\[N_{\mathrm{cr}} = k\,\frac{\pi^2 D}{b^2} , \qquad k = \min_{\mathrm{m}\ge 1}\Big(\frac{m b}{a} + \frac{a}{m b}\Big)^2 , \tag{18.58}\]

reached at \(n = 1\). We have \(k\ge 4\), and \(k = 4\) at every integer \(a/b\).

Proof

We must show that (18.56) is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the second variation, that its solutions are exactly the combinations of double sines, and that the smallest force is at \(n = 1\) and at the minimum over \(m\).

  1. The second variation of the total potential energy about the flat position is the quadratic form \(\Pi_2(w) = \tfrac12 B(w, w) - \tfrac{N_0}{2}\int w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) (lemmas 18.48, 18.41). Its first variation in the direction \(\eta\) is \(B(w, \eta) - N_0\int w_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  2. Theorem 4.14 for the field \((w_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta, 0)\) gives \(\int w_{\mathrm{x}}\eta_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}A = -\int w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A + \oint w_{\mathrm{x}}\,\eta\,n_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}s\). On the edge \(\eta = 0\), so the edge term is zero. Together with theorem 18.21 for \(B\), the first variation is \(\int(D\nabla^4 w + N_0 w_{\mathrm{xx}})\,\eta\,\mathrm{d}A\) and edge terms that vanish for a simply supported edge at \(w = 0\), \(\nabla^2 w = 0\). Stationarity requires (18.56).
  3. Let \(w = S_{\mathrm{mn}}\). Then \(D\nabla^4 S_{\mathrm{mn}} = D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and \(N_0 S_{\mathrm{xx}} = -N_0(m\pi/a)^2 S_{\mathrm{mn}}\), so the equation holds exactly at \(N_0 = D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4/(m\pi/a)^2 = N_{\mathrm{mn}}\).
  4. Let \(w\) be a solution of (18.56) with the edge conditions. We multiply by \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\) and use (18.53): \(\big(D\kappa_{\mathrm{mn}}^4 - N_0(m\pi/a)^2\big)c_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\), so \(c_{\mathrm{mn}} = 0\) except at \(N_{\mathrm{mn}} = N_0\). As in steps 4 to 6 of the proof of theorem 18.46, \(w\) is a combination of the corresponding \(S_{\mathrm{mn}}\).
  5. For fixed \(m\), \(N_{\mathrm{mn}}\) grows with \(n\) (the term \(n^2/b^2\) in the bracket), so the smallest value is at \(n = 1\): \(N_{\mathrm{m}1} = \tfrac{\pi^2 D}{b^2}\big(\tfrac{mb}{a} + \tfrac{a}{mb}\big)^2\) after expanding the square and dividing by \(m^2/a^2\).
  6. Let \(t = mb/a > 0\). From \((t - 1)^2\ge 0\) it follows that \(t + 1/t\ge 2\) and \((t + 1/t)^2\ge 4\), with equality at \(t = 1\), that is \(a/b = m\). Hence \(k\ge 4\) and \(k = 4\) at integer \(a/b\).

Source: EP §7.2.4.3 (pp. 445-447), (7.2.144), (7.2.151) to (7.2.152), table 7.2.6 (p. 447). The proof in steps is not from the sources.

Figure 18.22 shows \(k\) against \(a/b\). We draw the axes \(a/b\) and \(k\). For every \(m\), \(k_{\mathrm{m}} = (m b/a + a/(mb))^2\) is a curve with lowest point \(k = 4\) at \(a/b = m\): \(m = 1\), \(m = 2\), \(m = 3\), \(m = 4\). The plate buckles into the shape with the smallest \(k\), so the critical value is the lower envelope of the curves. The switch from \(m\) to \(m + 1\) is where \(m b/a + a/(mb) = (m+1)b/a + a/((m+1)b)\), that is at \((a/b)^2 = m(m+1)\), and EP (p. 447) says the same as »\(\sqrt{m_1m_2} = a/b\)«. The switching points are at \(a/b = \sqrt{2}\), \(\sqrt{6}\), \(\sqrt{12}\).

The numbers (SymPy and NumPy, outlines/ch-plosce/num-07.out part D and outlines/ch-plosce/c18.out part F) are \(k = 6{,}25\), \(4{,}00\), \(4{,}50\), \(4{,}34\), \(4{,}00\), \(4{,}13\), \(4{,}00\) at \(a/b = 0{,}5\), \(1\), \(\sqrt{2}\), \(1{,}5\), \(2\), \(2{,}5\), \(3\). At \(a/b = \sqrt 2\), \(m = 1\) and \(m = 2\) have the same \(k = 4{,}5\) (a switch). EP table 7.2.6 (p. 447) has for \(\nu = 0{,}3\) and \(\gamma = 0\) the values \(6{,}250\), \(4{,}000\), \(4{,}340\), \(4{,}000\), \(4{,}134\), \(4{,}000\), which agrees, because \(k\) does not contain \(\nu\). A square with \(b = 500\) mm and \(h = 10\) mm has \(N_{\mathrm{cr}} = 4\pi^2D/b^2 = 2834{,}3\) kN/m, so a stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}} = N_{\mathrm{cr}}/h = 283{,}43\) MPa. At \(h = 5\) mm \(D\) is eight times smaller and \(h\) twice, so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}\) is four times smaller, \(70{,}86\) MPa, because \(\sigma_{\mathrm{cr}}\propto h^2\). A thin plate thus fails by buckling at a stress that steel still carries, a thick one fails first by yielding, which depends on the yield stress of the steel, not yet introduced in this book.

The Rayleigh quotient for buckling, \(N(v) = B(v, v)/\int v_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), gives at \(v = S_{11}\) exactly \(4\pi^2D/b^2\) for the square. For the field \(x(a - x)\,y(a - y)\) at \(a = 1\) we have \(B = 22D/45\) and \(\int v_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 1/90\), so \(N = 44\,D/a^2\) and \(k = 44/\pi^2 = 4{,}458\), which is \(11{,}5\) % above the true value \(4\), so above it as for vibration. That this is really an upper bound would be shown by the proof of theorem 18.47 with \(\int v_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) in place of \(\mathcal{M}\), which we do not repeat here. EP §7.2.8 (pp. 476-485) uses the Ritz method for buckling with other edge conditions.

A test with a case that fails. For the column (theorem 8.134) the critical force falls as \(1/L^2\), a longer column is weaker. Is a longer plate weaker? Expanding (18.57) at \(n = 1\) gives \(N_{\mathrm{m}1} = \pi^2 D\big[m^2/a^2 + 2/b^2 + a^2/(m^2 b^4)\big]\). The first term is the force of a column of length \(a/m\) with \(D\) in place of \(EI\) per unit width, and it falls as \(a\) grows at fixed \(m\). The second and third terms are the price of transverse curvature: the edges \(y = 0\) and \(y = b\) are supported, so the mode has at least one half wave across the width, which adds bending energy in the \(y\) direction. At \(m = 1\), \(N_{11}\) has its lowest value at \(a = b\) and then grows like \(a^2\), since the third term overtakes the first. So at larger \(a\) the plate buckles into more half waves, each making a field close to a square. The curves for \(m = 1, 2, 3\) in figure 18.22 have their lowest point \(4\) at \(a/b = m\), and the envelope returns to \(4\) at every integer \(a/b\). A longer plate is therefore not weaker than a square, its \(k\) never falls below \(4\) (step 6 of the proof), while the column term \(b^2/a^2\) falls toward zero: at \(a/b = 3\) it is \(0{,}111\) against \(k = 4{,}000\). Similarly in vibration the plate divides itself into squares (CK, PDF p. 426). At \(a/b = 3\) and \(m = 3\), \(N_{\mathrm{cr}} = 4\pi^2D/b^2\), which is \(36\) times more than the force \(\pi^2D/a^2 = \pi^2D/(9b^2)\) that a plate of the same length would have as a column without transverse support.

Validity of the model: \(k\) does not contain \(\nu\), so \(\nu\) enters only through \(D\). Lemma 18.48 requires \(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\le 10^{-2}\), that is a slope up to \(0{,}1\). For buckling this only bounds the amplitude, which the linear calculation does not determine (every multiple of a solution of (18.56) is a solution), so we choose it small enough. Buckling of a circular plate we cite and do not prove (EP §7.2.2.2, pp. 411-414). Larger deflections and nonlinear buckling (von Kármán) we name and do not treat.

The section has shown that three problems of the plate (statics of the clamped square, vibration and buckling) follow from the same energy. The Ritz theorem 18.42 remains the tool for edge conditions that sines do not satisfy. In the next sections we let the normal cease to be perpendicular to the middle plane, and so arrive at the Mindlin plate, which does not neglect shear.
