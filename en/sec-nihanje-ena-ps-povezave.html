Chapter 9 Vibration of a system with one degree of freedom (nihanje sistema z eno prostostno stopnjo)

9.14 Connections

The abbreviations of the subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

  • Several degrees of freedom and modal superposition (VD, chapter 10): a system with several degrees of freedom splits into motions, each described by one equation of the form from theorem 9.3 (this holds for classical, proportional damping, where the damping matrix is a linear combination of the mass and stiffness matrices, for other damping the equations couple), so the results of sections 9.5 to 9.13 are applied once for each shape. There chapter 10 also treats time stepping, which we did not need here, because we computed with exact solutions (section 9.4).
  • Continuous systems (VD, chapter 13): strings, shafts and beams have infinitely many degrees of freedom. The natural frequency of cantilever A with the mass of the beam we estimated in section 9.1 with one shape and the term \(33/140\) of the mass of the beam: \(34{,}9078\ \text{1/s}\). Chapter 13 computes the first natural frequency of the continuous beam with a weight, \(34{,}9074\ \text{1/s}\), so our one-shape model agrees to \(0{,}0012\) %, while the model without the mass of the beam (\(35{,}4175\ \text{1/s}\), from the static sag in section 9.13) differs by \(1{,}46\) %.
  • Damping models (DSK, chapters 20 and 21): equivalent viscous, hysteretic and viscoelastic damping, which we only named in sections 9.1 and 9.13. The damping ratio that we measure as in section 9.13 is a datum that such methods need.
  • Unbalance and rotors (DSK, chapters 23 and 24): the excitation \(m_{\mathrm{u}} a_{\mathrm{u}}\Omega^2\cos\Omega t\) from section 9.8 returns in balancing and piston machines, the curve of the dynamic factor from section 9.6 in the critical speeds of shafts. The torsional vibration of a disc on a shaft has the same equation \(J\ddot\varphi + c_{\mathrm{t}}\dot\varphi + k_{\mathrm{t}}\varphi = M_{\mathrm{t}}(t)\), and the stiffness \(k_{\mathrm{t}}\) is given by chapter 16.
  • Equations of order four with constant coefficients (DSK, chapter 25): the beam on an elastic foundation with the equation \(EI\,v'''' + k\,v = q\) we already solved for \(q = 0\) in section 9.4, with the polynomial \(s^4 + 4\beta^4\) (\(4\beta^4 = k/EI\)) and its roots \(\beta(\pm 1 \pm \mathrm{i})\), so with the same decaying and growing oscillations as for one pair of complex roots in this chapter. Chapter 25 adds the load and the boundary conditions.
