Chapter 21 Modal reduction and eigenvalue solvers

21.9 Damping of reduced models

So far we have let the reduced models of sections 21.4 and 21.5 vibrate without damping, but a real structure does not vibrate for ever. When we added dampers to the full model in chapter 10, we obtained a damping matrix (matrika dušenja) \(\mat C\) of order \(n\), and classical, Rayleigh and modal damping (definitions 10.44, 10.45 and 10.47). Now we have a model with \(m\) coordinates in place of \(n\). The question is what happens to the damping when we reduce. Which matrix of order \(m\) is the damping of the reduced model? Does Rayleigh damping stay Rayleigh? And does the modal damping of the full model stay modal, so that the reduced model again decouples into \(m\) oscillators? The answers to the last two questions differ, and the second is the more instructive.

We read the word "reduced" (skrajšan) by the list of coordinates: the list of \(n\) coordinates of the full model is shortened to a list of \(m\) coordinates. It is the same thing that chapter 10 called the reduced pair, now carrying damping as well.

The model is the cantilever of chapter 12, the same as in section 21.5 (a steel beam \(20 \times 40\) mm from chapter 13, length \(L = 1\) m, \(\omega_0 = 57{,}883\) 1/s, frequencies measured in units of \(\omega_0\)), with sixteen elements with consistent mass and \(n = 32\) free coordinates (the deflection and the rotation of each of the sixteen free nodes). We divide it into two halves of eight elements, the shared node is the middle one. The reduced model is the Craig-Bampton model (definition 21.23) with the two constraint modes of the middle node (deflection and rotation) and one fixed-interface mode of each half, so \(m = 4\) coordinates. By theorem 10.42 the Ritz frequencies are upper bounds, and indeed \(\tilde\omega_{1\dots 4} = 3{,}51625\), \(22{,}1747\), \(68{,}4215\) and \(183{,}637\), while the full model has \(\omega_{1\dots 4} = 3{,}51602\), \(22{,}0346\), \(61{,}6997\) and \(120{,}920\) (NumPy). The errors are \(+0{,}007\), \(+0{,}64\), \(+10{,}9\) and \(+51{,}9\) %: the first two frequencies are good, the third moderate, the fourth poor. We shall need this when we see what happens to the damping of the higher modes.

21.9.1 The damping matrix of the reduced model

The reduced model is a Ritz model (definition 10.41): the displacement of the full model is restricted to \(\vek q = \mat\Psi\,\vek a\), where \(\mat\Psi\) is a matrix with \(n\) rows and \(m\) independent columns and \(\vek a(t)\) a column of \(m\) numbers. Figure 21.32 shows how one large matrix gives rise to a small one. The full model has a damping matrix \(\mathbf C\) with \(32 \times 32\) elements. The displacement that the reduced model still allows is a combination of the columns of the matrix \(\mathbf\Psi\), which has \(32\) rows and \(4\) columns. If we put the matrix \(\mathbf C\) between \(\mathbf\Psi\) on the right and its transpose \(\mathbf\Psi^{\mathsf T}\) on the left and multiply all three, we obtain the matrix \(\tilde{\mathbf C}\) of order \(4\). The numbers at the edges are sizes: the row \(4 \times 32\) is multiplied by the square \(32 \times 32\) and the column \(32 \times 4\) (definition 1.98).

Why is this the right matrix? In the Ritz method for eigenvalues we multiplied the matrices \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\) by \(\mat\Psi\) (lemma 10.40). In the motion there are, besides them, the damping forces \(-\mat C\dot{\vek q}\). A model that the force \(-\mat C\dot{\vek q}\) brakes loses energy with the power \(\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat C\,\dot{\vek q}\). The power of a force is force times velocity, \((-\mat C\dot{\vek q})^\T\dot{\vek q} = -\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat C\,\dot{\vek q}\), and the power taken away, \(\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat C\,\dot{\vek q}\), is nonnegative because \(\mat C\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15). The reduced model must keep this power for the motions it can still make. But the equations of the full model at \(\vek q = \mat\Psi\,\vek a\) cannot in general be satisfied in all \(n\) rows with \(m\) free functions, so we demand that their virtual work vanishes on every virtual displacement the model allows (as in the principle of virtual displacements, theorem 8.3).

Lemma 21.42 Damping matrix of the reduced model (matrika dušenja skrajšanega modela)

Let \(\mat M\), \(\mat K\) be symmetric positive definite and \(\mat C\) a symmetric positive semidefinite (definition 7.15) matrix of order \(n\), \(\vek Q(t)\) a column of \(n\) functions and \(\mat\Psi\) a matrix with \(n\) rows and \(m\) linearly independent columns. Let \(\tilde{\mat M} = \mat\Psi^\T\mat M\,\mat\Psi\), \(\tilde{\mat K} = \mat\Psi^\T\mat K\,\mat\Psi\) and \(\tilde{\mat C} = \mat\Psi^\T\mat C\,\mat\Psi\). Then:

  1. The column \(\vek a(t)\) satisfies \(\delta\vek a^\T\mat\Psi^\T\big(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat C\dot{\vek q} + \mat K\vek q - \vek Q\big) = 0\) for every column \(\delta\vek a\) at \(\vek q = \mat\Psi\,\vek a\) if and only if
    \[\tilde{\mat M}\,\ddot{\vek a} + \tilde{\mat C}\,\dot{\vek a} + \tilde{\mat K}\,\vek a = \mat\Psi^\T\vek Q(t) . \tag{21.49}\]
  2. The matrix \(\tilde{\mat C}\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite.
  3. At \(\dot{\vek q} = \mat\Psi\,\dot{\vek a}\) the power that the damping forces take away is the same in both models, \(\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat C\,\dot{\vek q} = \dot{\vek a}^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\dot{\vek a} \ge 0\).
Proof

We must show the equivalence of the virtual work and equation (21.49), and the properties of the matrix \(\tilde{\mat C}\).

  1. The matrix \(\mat\Psi\) does not depend on time, so from \(\vek q = \mat\Psi\,\vek a\) also \(\dot{\vek q} = \mat\Psi\,\dot{\vek a}\) and \(\ddot{\vek q} = \mat\Psi\,\ddot{\vek a}\).
  2. We insert this in the bracket and use associativity of the product (lemma 1.99): \(\mat\Psi^\T\big(\mat M\mat\Psi\,\ddot{\vek a} + \mat C\mat\Psi\,\dot{\vek a} + \mat K\mat\Psi\,\vek a - \vek Q\big) = \tilde{\mat M}\,\ddot{\vek a} + \tilde{\mat C}\,\dot{\vek a} + \tilde{\mat K}\,\vek a - \mat\Psi^\T\vek Q =: \vek r\) with a column \(\vek r\) of \(m\) numbers.
  3. The condition of point 1 is \(\delta\vek a^\T\vek r = 0\) for every \(\delta\vek a\). If we choose a column \(\delta\vek a\) with one element \(1\) and the others \(0\), we get that every element of \(\vek r\) is zero, and conversely \(\vek r = \vek 0\) gives the condition. This is equation (21.49).
  4. By lemma 1.101, \(\tilde{\mat C}^\T = \mat\Psi^\T\mat C^\T\mat\Psi = \tilde{\mat C}\), because \(\mat C\) is symmetric.
  5. For a column \(\vek b\), \(\vek b^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\vek b = (\mat\Psi\,\vek b)^\T\mat C\,(\mat\Psi\,\vek b) \ge 0\), because \(\mat C\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15). This is point 2, and at \(\vek b = \dot{\vek a}\) also point 3.

Source: CK (17.2) (PDF p. 551) writes the Ritz coordinate transformation as \(\vek u^{(s)} = \mat\Psi^{(s)}\vek p^{(s)}\) with the component-mode matrix of component \(s\), for an undamped component (CK (17.1)). The proof for the damped system is not from the sources.

The lemma says that the reduced model inherits damping through the matrices \(\mat\Psi^\T\) and \(\mat\Psi\), just as it does stiffness and mass, and that it does not lose positive semidefiniteness, so it cannot add energy. For \(\vek Q = \vek 0\) and \(\mat C = \mat 0\), equation (21.49) is the equation of undamped vibration \(\tilde{\mat M}\,\ddot{\vek a} + \tilde{\mat K}\,\vek a = \vek 0\). Its harmonic solutions \(\vek a = \hat{\vek a}\cos\omega t\) give \(\tilde{\mat K}\,\hat{\vek a} = \omega^2\tilde{\mat M}\,\hat{\vek a}\), which is the reduced eigenproblem of definition 10.41. For none of the Ritz bases of this chapter (Guyan, Craig-Bampton, subspace and Krylov bases) does the lemma require more than independent columns.

21.9.2 Rayleigh damping stays Rayleigh

We start with the simplest damping. Rayleigh damping \(\mat C = a_0\mat M + a_1\mat K\) (definition 10.45) is a sum of matrices that \(\mat\Psi\) already knows how to reduce.

Corollary 21.43 Rayleigh damping of the reduced model (Rayleighovo dušenje skrajšanega modela)

Let \(\mat C = a_0\mat M + a_1\mat K\) with \(a_0, a_1 \ge 0\) and \(\mat\Psi\) as in lemma 21.42. Then \(\tilde{\mat C} = a_0\tilde{\mat M} + a_1\tilde{\mat K}\) and \(\tilde{\mat C}\) is classical damping of the reduced pair \((\tilde{\mat K}, \tilde{\mat M})\) with damping ratios

\[\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{a_0}{2\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}} + \frac{a_1\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}}{2} , \qquad \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}} = \sqrt{\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{s}}} , \quad s = 1, \dots, m , \tag{21.50}\]

where \(\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{s}}\) are the Ritz values (definition 10.41).

Proof

We must show the form of the matrix \(\tilde{\mat C}\) and apply the lemma on Rayleigh damping to the reduced pair.

  1. By lemma 21.42 and the rules for the matrix product (lemma 1.99), \(\tilde{\mat C} = \mat\Psi^\T(a_0\mat M + a_1\mat K)\mat\Psi = a_0\,\mat\Psi^\T\mat M\,\mat\Psi + a_1\,\mat\Psi^\T\mat K\,\mat\Psi = a_0\tilde{\mat M} + a_1\tilde{\mat K}\).
  2. By lemma 10.40, point 1, \(\tilde{\mat M}\) is symmetric positive definite and \(\tilde{\mat K}\) symmetric. Since \(\mat K\) is positive definite, \(\tilde{\mat K}\) is positive definite too (\(\vek b^\T\tilde{\mat K}\,\vek b = (\mat\Psi\,\vek b)^\T\mat K\,(\mat\Psi\,\vek b) > 0\) for \(\vek b \ne \vek 0\) by definition 6.22), so \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}} > 0\).
  3. By lemma 10.46, applied to the pair \((\tilde{\mat K}, \tilde{\mat M})\) of order \(m\) with \(\tilde{\mat C}\) from step 1, \(\tilde{\mat C}\) is classical damping with the ratios (21.50).

Source: The proof is not from the sources.

The corollary says two things. The constants \(a_0\) and \(a_1\) stay the same, so the reduced model need not be refitted, and the reduced model decouples into \(m\) oscillators like the full one (theorem 10.49). The ratios \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\), however, are by equation (21.50) the value of the same curve \(\zeta(\omega) = a_0/(2\omega) + a_1\omega/2\) at the Ritz frequencies \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\), not at the exact ones.

We test it on the cantilever. Let \(\zeta = 0{,}02\) at \(\omega_1\) and \(\omega_2\) of the full model. Equation (10.57) gives \(a_0 = 0{,}12129\,\omega_0 = 7{,}0205\) 1/s and \(a_1 = 0{,}001566/\omega_0 = 2{,}7046\cdot 10^{-5}\) s. The computation with the reduced matrix \(\mat\Psi^\T(a_0\mat M + a_1\mat K)\mat\Psi\) agrees with \(a_0\tilde{\mat M} + a_1\tilde{\mat K}\) to \(2\cdot 10^{-14}\) of the largest element, and the off-diagonal elements in the eigencoordinates of the reduced model are below \(10^{-12}\). Figure 21.33 shows the curve \(\zeta(\omega)\) on a logarithmic \(\omega/\omega_0\) axis, because the frequencies are spread from \(3{,}5\) to \(184\). We read the ratio in percent on the vertical axis. We fitted the Rayleigh damping to two modes of the full model, so there it is exactly \(2\) %. The open blue circles are the exact frequencies \(\omega_1, \dots, \omega_4\) with the ratios \(2\), \(2\), \(4{,}93\) and \(9{,}52\) %. The filled orange dots are the Ritz frequencies of the reduced model on the same curve, with the ratios \(1{,}9999\), \(2{,}0092\), \(5{,}444\) and \(14{,}41\) %.

At the first and second mode the ratio hardly changes (\(-0{,}005\) % and \(+0{,}46\) % of the prescribed \(2\) %), but at the third and fourth it moves along the steep part of the curve. The fourth Ritz frequency is \(51{,}9\) % too high, and the curve gives \(14{,}41\) % there instead of \(9{,}52\) %, which is \(51\) % more. So corollary 21.43 does not claim that the reduced model gets the same ratio as the full one. It claims that the damping stays classical and that the rule for ratios and constants keeps its form. At a frequency that the reduced model computes wrongly, the ratio is wrong together with it, and this is a consequence, not a fault of the procedure.

21.9.3 Modal damping gets mixed in reduction

With two constants of Rayleigh damping we prescribe only two ratios (section 10.9.1), but the ratios of all modes are measured, and for structures they lie between \(0{,}01\) and \(0{,}1\) (CK PDF p. 322). So models of the full structure use modal damping (definition 10.47) \(\mat C = \mat M\mat\Phi\,\mathrm{diag}(2\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}})\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M\) with ratios that we choose or measure. The question is whether the reduced matrix \(\tilde{\mat C}\) of lemma 21.42 is again diagonal in the eigencoordinates of the reduced pair with the ratios \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\).

We test it on the cantilever with modal damping \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0{,}02\) in all \(32\) modes and the reduced matrix \(\tilde{\mat C} = \mat\Psi^\T\mat C\,\mat\Psi\). In the eigencoordinates of the reduced pair (the columns \(\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}\) of lemma 10.40) we get the diagonal ratios

\[\tilde\zeta_1,\ \tilde\zeta_2,\ \tilde\zeta_3,\ \tilde\zeta_4 = 1{,}9999,\ 1{,}9908,\ 1{,}9107,\ 1{,}6308\ \%\]

instead of \(2\) %, and off-diagonal elements: the normalized element between modes \(3\) and \(4\) is \(7{,}92\) %, between \(2\) and \(4\) it is \(1{,}27\) %, and between \(2\) and \(3\) it is \(1{,}18\) % (NumPy). So the reduced model does not decouple, and all four ratios are below the prescribed one, the two highest noticeably so. Why exactly so? The answer is in the expansion of the Ritz shape in the exact modes.

Let \(\vek v_{\mathrm{s}} = \mat\Psi\,\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}\) be the Ritz shape (definition 10.41), a column of \(n\) numbers. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat\Phi^{-1} = \mat\Phi^\T\mat M\), so \(\vek v_{\mathrm{s}} = \mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek v_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) with the coefficients

\[c_{\mathrm{sr}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} . \tag{21.51}\]

The number \(c_{\mathrm{sr}}\) says how much of the exact mode \(r\) there is in the Ritz shape \(s\). In the reduced Craig-Bampton model the first Ritz shape is almost entirely the first mode: \(c_{11}^2 = 1{,}0000\). In the fourth Ritz shape the fourth mode has only \(c_{44}^2 = 0{,}706\), the fifth \(0{,}229\) and the third \(0{,}032\), and the modes with \(r > 4\) together \(0{,}262\). The poorer the Ritz shape, the wider the mixture of modes, and in it the dampings of different modes get mixed.

Theorem 21.44 Modal damping of the full model in the reduced model (modalno dušenje polnega modela v skrajšanem modelu)

Let \(\mat C\) be modal damping with ratios \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}} \ge 0\) (definition 10.47) and \(\mat K\), \(\mat M\) positive definite. Let \(\mat\Psi\) be a matrix with \(m\) independent columns, \((\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{s}}, \vek a_{\mathrm{s}})\) a Ritz pair (lemma 10.40), \(\vek v_{\mathrm{s}} = \mat\Psi\,\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}\), \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}} = \sqrt{\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{s}}}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{sr}}\) from equation (21.51). Then:

  1. \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}} = \delta_{\mathrm{st}}\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\,c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}} = \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2\,\delta_{\mathrm{st}}\).
  2. \(\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{t}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} 2\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\,c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}}\).
  3. If all \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}} = \zeta\), the diagonal element \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}/(2\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}})\) equals \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\,\mu_{\mathrm{s}}/\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\) with \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}^2\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\), and \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) with
    \[V_{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}^2(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} - \mu_{\mathrm{s}})^2}{\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2} \in [0,\,1] . \tag{21.52}\]
    So \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} \le \zeta\), and for \(\zeta > 0\), \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\) if and only if all modes \(r\) with \(c_{\mathrm{sr}} \ne 0\) have the same frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\).
  4. If every Ritz shape \(\vek v_{\mathrm{s}}\) is an exact mode, then \(\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{t}} = 2\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{st}}\) with the corresponding \(r\), and the reduced damping is classical.
Proof

We must show four points, each from the expansion (21.51).

  1. By lemma 10.16, \(\mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M = \mat I\), so \(\mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T = \mat M^{-1}\) (definition 1.107). Hence \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\,\mat\Phi\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek v_{\mathrm{t}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\,\vek v_{\mathrm{t}} = \vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat M}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{t}} = \delta_{\mathrm{st}}\) (lemma 10.40, point 2).
  2. From \(\mat\Phi^\T\mat K\,\mat\Phi = \mathrm{diag}(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2)\) and \(\mat\Phi^{-\T} = \mat M\mat\Phi\) it follows that \(\mat K = \mat M\mat\Phi\,\mathrm{diag}(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2)\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M\) (as in point 2 of lemma 10.48). Hence \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat K\,\vek v_{\mathrm{t}} = \tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{t}}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat M}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{t}} = \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2\delta_{\mathrm{st}}\), again by lemma 10.40, point 2.
  3. By lemma 21.42, \(\vek a_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\tilde{\mat C}\,\vek a_{\mathrm{t}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat C\,\vek v_{\mathrm{t}} = \vek v_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\,\mat\Phi\,\mathrm{diag}(2\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}})\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek v_{\mathrm{t}}\), which is the sum of point 2 of the theorem.
  4. With all \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}} = \zeta\), point 2 of the theorem gives \(2\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = 2\zeta\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}^2\omega_{\mathrm{r}} = 2\zeta\mu_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\mu_{\mathrm{s}}/\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\). The numbers \(w_{\mathrm{r}} = c_{\mathrm{sr}}^2\) are nonnegative and by point 1 sum to \(1\), so \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} w_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) is their mean. We expand \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} w_{\mathrm{r}}(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} - \mu_{\mathrm{s}})^2 = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} w_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - 2\mu_{\mathrm{s}}\sum_{\mathrm{r}} w_{\mathrm{r}}\omega_{\mathrm{r}} + \mu_{\mathrm{s}}^2 = \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2 - \mu_{\mathrm{s}}^2\) by point 1 of the theorem (second equality) and the definition of \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}}\). The left side is a sum of nonnegative numbers, so \(0 \le \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2 - \mu_{\mathrm{s}}^2 \le \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2\) (the second because \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}} \ge 0\)), hence \(V_{\mathrm{s}} \in [0, 1]\) and \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}}/\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}} = \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}} \le 1\). The sum is zero if and only if every term is zero, that is \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} = \mu_{\mathrm{s}}\) wherever \(c_{\mathrm{sr}} \ne 0\). For \(\zeta > 0\), \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) equals \(\zeta\) if and only if \(V_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\), so exactly in this case.
  5. If \(\vek v_{\mathrm{s}} = \pm\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{s})}\) for every \(s\), then \(c_{\mathrm{sr}} = \pm\delta_{\mathrm{r}\,\mathrm{r}(\mathrm{s})}\) by \(\mat M\)-orthonormality (lemma 10.16), and the sum in point 2 of the theorem has one term. For \(s \ne t\) we have \(r(s) \ne r(t)\), because by point 1 of the theorem \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{sr}}c_{\mathrm{tr}} = 0\), while for \(r(s) = r(t)\) this sum would be \(\pm 1\). So for \(s \ne t\) the term is zero.

Source: The proof is not from the sources. CK PDF p. 550 says that the damping of reduced models is "most often" treated as modal damping imposed on the modes of the reduced model, not as the reduced matrix of the full model (see below).

Point 3 says when and by how much the ratio is too small. The ratio \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}/\zeta = \mu_{\mathrm{s}}/\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the weighted mean \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}}\) of the frequencies of the modes that make up the Ritz shape \(s\), divided by the Ritz frequency, which by theorem 10.42 lies above the exact one. The mean is never above \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\), because \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}^2\) is the mean of the squared frequencies (point 1) and the mean of squares is never below the square of the mean. This is not a property of a particular model. The more the Ritz shape is a mixture of modes with different frequencies, the larger \(V_{\mathrm{s}}\) and the larger the shortfall. For the Craig-Bampton model with four coordinates \(\mu_{\mathrm{s}} = 3{,}51603\), \(22{,}0722\), \(65{,}3653\) and \(149{,}733\), and \(V_{\mathrm{s}} = 0{,}00013\), \(0{,}0092\), \(0{,}087\) and \(0{,}335\). The shortfall \(1 - \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) is then \(0{,}006\), \(0{,}46\), \(4{,}5\) and \(18{,}5\) % of \(\zeta\), and it agrees with the numbers above (\(1{,}9999\), \(1{,}9908\), \(1{,}9107\), \(1{,}6308\) %). The right part of the figure shows the ratio \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}/\zeta\): the blue bars fall from \(1{,}000\) at \(s = 1\) to \(0{,}815\) at \(s = 4\).

How large is the shortfall for small \(V_{\mathrm{s}}\), without computing the root? We multiply \(1 - \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) by \(1 + \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) and obtain \(1 - (1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}) = V_{\mathrm{s}}\), so

\[1 - \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}} = \frac{V_{\mathrm{s}}}{1 + \sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}} .\]

For \(V_{\mathrm{s}} \in [0, 1]\) the denominator lies between \(1\) and \(2\), so the shortfall lies between \(V_{\mathrm{s}}/2\) and \(V_{\mathrm{s}}\). For small \(V_{\mathrm{s}}\), \(\sqrt{1 - V_{\mathrm{s}}}\) is close to \(1\) and the shortfall close to \(V_{\mathrm{s}}/2\). At \(V_2 = 0{,}0092\), \(V_2/2 = 0{,}46\) %, which is the computed shortfall. At \(V_4 = 0{,}335\), \(\sqrt{1 - V_4} = 0{,}815\) is already far from \(1\), and the estimate \(V_4/2 = 16{,}8\) % is below the true \(18{,}5\) %.

Without equal \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\) the bound \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} \le \zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\) does not hold. Rayleigh damping is classical (lemma 10.46), hence modal with the ratios (10.56) (lemma 10.48), which grow with the frequency above \(\sqrt{\omega_1\omega_2} = 8{,}80\). For the cantilever \(\tilde\zeta_4 = 14{,}41\) % lies above \(\zeta_4 = 9{,}52\) % (figure 21.33), because the Ritz shape \(4\) has a share \(0{,}229\) of the fifth mode, whose ratio is \(\zeta_5 = 15{,}7\) %.

Let us return to modal damping with equal \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}} = 2\) %. With more coordinates the shortfall and the off-diagonal elements fall quickly. For the Craig-Bampton model with eight coordinates (three fixed-interface modes per half), \(\tilde\zeta_{1\dots 4} = 2{,}0000\), \(1{,}9999\), \(1{,}9999\) and \(1{,}9974\) %, and the off-diagonal elements among the first four are below \(0{,}02\) %: the orange bars in the right part of the figure all reach almost to the dashed line \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\). Over all eight modes the largest off-diagonal element is \(5{,}69\) %, between the seventh and the eighth, so higher than among the first four. The reason is the same as before: poorer Ritz shapes are mixtures, and the mixing happens mostly in the upper part of the reduced spectrum.

21.9.4 Damping imposed on the reduced modes

Theorem 21.44 says that the reduced matrix of full modal damping is not modal. So in practice \(\mat\Psi^\T\mat C\,\mat\Psi\) is not computed for reduced models. Damping is most often treated as modal damping imposed on the modes of the reduced model (CK, PDF p. 550: "Damping is most often treated as modal damping imposed on the modes of the reduced-order system model"), and special procedures for general viscous damping are not discussed in CK because they are little used. This is a choice, not a derivation. In the reduced pair \((\tilde{\mat K}, \tilde{\mat M})\) we choose, by lemma 10.40, ratios \(\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\) for the Ritz modes and assemble

\[\tilde{\mat C} = \tilde{\mat M}\mat A\,\mathrm{diag}(2\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}})\,\mat A^\T\tilde{\mat M} , \qquad \mat A = (\vek a_1\ \cdots\ \vek a_{\mathrm{m}}) , \tag{21.53}\]

which is modal damping (definition 10.47) of the pair \((\tilde{\mat K}, \tilde{\mat M})\). By lemma 10.48 it is classical, and by theorem 10.49 the reduced model decouples into \(m\) oscillators with the ratios \(\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\), without computing \(\mat\Psi^\T\mat C\,\mat\Psi\). For the Craig-Bampton model with four coordinates this means four oscillator equations in place of \(32\) coupled ones.

The procedure has its test. The ratio \(\zeta_{\mathrm{s}}\) that we impose belongs to the mode of the full model that we measure. The Ritz shape \(s\) is its approximation, and a good one only if the error of \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{s}}\) is small. At \(s = 1, 2\) (errors \(0{,}007\) and \(0{,}64\) %) the imposed ratio is \(2\) % and the derived ones are \(1{,}9999\) and \(1{,}9908\) %, so they agree to \(0{,}5\) %. At \(s = 4\) (\(+51{,}9\) %) the Ritz shape is \(26\) % made of modes above the fourth, so the ratio \(2\) % imposed on this shape no longer belongs to the fourth mode of the full model, and we do not take the results of the fourth reduced mode seriously. The general rule is the same as for frequencies: for modes that the Ritz approximation shows poorly we trust neither the frequency nor the ratio. Damping with equivalent viscous, hysteretic and viscoelastic models, which a reduced model requires as well, is treated in chapter 20.

Let us sum up what the section showed. Lemma 21.42 showed that the damping matrix of the reduced model is \(\mat\Psi^\T\mat C\,\mat\Psi\), and corollary 21.43 that Rayleigh damping stays Rayleigh with the same two constants. Theorem 21.44 showed that modal damping of the full model is not diagonal in the reduced model, and that for equal \(\zeta_{\mathrm{r}}\) the ratio is never above \(\zeta\), with a shortfall measured by the spread \(V_{\mathrm{s}}\) of the frequencies in the Ritz shape. So the damping of a reduced model is most often imposed on its own modes with chosen ratios, and not derived from the full model.
