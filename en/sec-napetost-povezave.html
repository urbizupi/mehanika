Chapter 2 Stress and equilibrium

2.14 Connections

The subject abbreviations are the same as in section 1.19.

  • The traction vector and Cauchy's formula: boundary conditions with prescribed forces on the surface (VT, MK, chapter 11), the internal forces of a beam as sums of the stresses over the cross section (MK, VT, chapter 5), stresses in plates and shells (MK, chapters 18 and 22).
  • The equilibrium equations: the Navier-Lamé equations (VT, MK, chapter 11), energy methods and the weak form (all subjects, chapters 4 and 6), the equations of motion of a continuum, with the density times the acceleration on the right side (DSK, chapter 13).
  • The moment of a force and the second condition of equilibrium: the dynamics of a rigid body (VD, chapter 8), the internal moments of beams (MK, chapter 5).
  • Turned axes and tensors: the transformation of the element stiffness matrix from local to global coordinates (AK, chapter 7), stresses in cylindrical and spherical coordinates (MK, chapter 11), the inertia tensor (VD, chapter 8).
  • The determinant: the Jacobian determinant of the isoparametric mapping of a finite element (AK, chapter 15).
  • Principal stresses and principal directions: a similar proof, in which we look for the largest value among the vectors \(\vek\phi\) with \(\vek\phi^\T\mat M\vek\phi = 1\), where \(\mat M\) is the mass matrix, not on the unit sphere, gives the natural frequencies and the orthogonality of the mode shapes with respect to the mass matrix (VD, DSK, chapter 10, CK (10.20), PDF p. 307).
  • The largest shear stress, the deviator and the von Mises stress: judging the danger of yielding in FEM computations (AK, chapter 17).
  • The Piola-Kirchhoff tensors and the polar decomposition: large rotations of flexible links (DSK, chapter 28).
