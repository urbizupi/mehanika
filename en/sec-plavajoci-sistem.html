Chapter 28 Flexible robot links

28.2 The floating frame of reference

Section 28.1 showed that the displacement of a turned link must not be measured in fixed axes and used to compute small strains. At a turn of \(30°\) the false stretch is \(\cos\theta - 1 = -0{,}134\), while the true strain of the link is about \(10^{-4}\). We must therefore describe the turn and the bending each with its own numbers: the turn with a position and an angle, the bending with another set of numbers. Where does a point of the link stand when the hub turns and the link bends at the same time? We build the answer on figure 28.3, piece by piece, as on a blackboard.

First we draw the fixed axes \(x\) and \(y\) with origin \(O\). In them we measured the position of a rigid body in the plane in chapter 8, and by lemma 8.40 it is determined by three numbers: the position of one point of the body and an angle. For the point we choose the place where the link is fixed to the hub and give it the position \(\vek o\), shown by the arrow from \(O\). At this point we draw two new axes \(\bar x\) and \(\bar y\). The axis \(\bar x\) is turned against the axis \(x\) by the angle \(\theta\), measured from the direction of the axis \(x\), which is dashed in the figure, and the axis \(\bar y\) is a quarter turn further on. Because the hub turns, \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\) change with time. The link is clamped to the hub, so in these axes it is unloaded always in the same place: straight and along the axis \(\bar x\). The reason is the rigid motion of lemma 8.40: a point of a rigid body has the position \(\vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,\bar{\vek r}_0\), and the two numbers \(\bar{\vek r}_0\) do not change during the motion. The point labelled \(x\) on the axis of the unloaded link therefore always has, in the turned axes, the position \(\bar{\vek r}_0 = (x, 0)\), measured in the figure from \(\vek o\) along \(\bar x\). We call these numbers the components in the frame, because they are measured in the axes \(\bar x\) and \(\bar y\).

Under load the link bends and its axis is the blue curve. The point \(x\) of the axis moves to the point \(P\), and its displacement \(\bar{\vek u}\) is again measured in the turned axes. For our link this displacement is perpendicular to \(\bar x\) and equal to the deflection \(v(x)\). The point is therefore at \(\bar{\vek r}_0 + \bar{\vek u}\) in the turned axes, and in the fixed axes we get its position \(\vek r\) if we turn this sum by \(\theta\) and add \(\vek o\),

\[\vek r = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,\big(\bar{\vek r}_0 + \bar{\vek u}\big) . \tag{28.8}\]

This is the rule of lemma 8.40 with the displacement \(\bar{\vek u}\) in the brackets, and for \(\bar{\vek u} = \vek 0\) it is that lemma.

Definition 28.3 Floating frame of reference of a link (plavajoči koordinatni sistem člena)

Let a link move in the plane \(xy\) (lemma 8.40). The floating frame of reference, the frame for short (plavajoči koordinatni sistem, okvir), is a rectangular coordinate system with origin at the point with position \(\vek o(t) \in \mathbb R^2\) and with axes in the directions \(\mat R(\theta)\,(1, 0)^\T\) and \(\mat R(\theta)\,(0, 1)^\T\), where \(\theta(t)\) is an angle in radians and \(\mat R(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}\). The functions \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\) are twice continuously differentiable, \(\vek o(0) = \vek 0\) and \(\theta(0) = 0\). The components of a point in the frame (komponente točke v okviru) are the numbers \(\bar{\vek w} \in \mathbb R^2\) for which the position of the point is \(\vek r = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,\bar{\vek w}\). The components in the frame of a vector \(\vek w\) (a difference of positions) are \(\bar{\vek w} = \mat R(\theta)^\T\,\vek w\).

Source: SHABANA, PDF p. 202 (body reference in floating frame of reference), PDF p. 203 (the planar case \(\vek r = \vek R + \mat A\,\bar{\vek u}\)). Notation after chapter 8: \(\vek o\) as in lemma 8.40, angle \(\theta\) instead of \(\varphi\).

The frame is called floating because its origin is not necessarily tied to the same material point of the body: it "floats" with the motion of the body and follows only the requirements that we fix below (SHABANA, PDF p. 202). In our link \(\vek o\) will be the hub, which is a material point, but in other frames (\(\bar x\) along the chord of the link) it would not be. The motion of the frame is therefore not the rigid motion of the link: for the same link we can choose different frames (figure 28.4), and the formulation does not separate rigid motion from deformation (SHABANA, PDF p. 202).

Definition 28.4 Reference and elastic coordinates of a link (referenčne in elastične koordinate člena)

Let the link in the unloaded state have its axis as the segment \([0, L]\) on the axis \(\bar x\) of the frame of definition 28.3, and let the point of the axis labelled \(x\) have the components \(\bar{\vek r}_0 = (x, 0)^\T\). Let \(\psi_1, \dots, \psi_{\mathrm{n}} \colon [0, L] \to \mathbb R\) be twice continuously differentiable and linearly independent functions (definition 6.32, trial functions) and \(\mat\Psi(x) = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \psi_1(x) & \cdots & \psi_{\mathrm{n}}(x) \end{bmatrix}\) a matrix of size \(2 \times n\). The elastic coordinates (elastične koordinate) \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}(t) \in \mathbb R^n\) determine the displacement of the point \(x\) in the frame,

\[\bar{\vek u}(x, t) = \mat\Psi(x)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}(t) , \qquad v(x, t) = \sum_{\mathrm{k} = 1}^{n} \psi_{\mathrm{k}}(x)\,q_{\mathrm{k}}(t) , \tag{28.9}\]

and the reference coordinates (referenčne koordinate) are the three numbers \(\vek q_{\mathrm{r}} = (o_{\mathrm{x}}, o_{\mathrm{y}}, \theta)^\T\) of definition 28.3. The position of the point \(x\) of the axis is

\[\vek r(x, t) = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,\Big(\bar{\vek r}_0 + \mat\Psi(x)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\Big) = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\begin{bmatrix} x \\ v(x, t) \end{bmatrix} , \tag{28.10}\]

and the coordinates of the link are \((\vek q_{\mathrm{r}}, \vek q_{\mathrm{d}})\), together \(3 + n\) numbers. A point at a distance \(y\) from the axis (in the plane of motion) has, in the Euler-Bernoulli model (theorem 5.15), the displacement \(\bar{\vek u} = (-y\,v'(x),\ v(x))^\T\).

Source: SHABANA, PDF p. 202 (equation 5.4, reference coordinates), PDF p. 203 (equations 5.6 and 5.7), PDF p. 204 (equations 5.8 and 5.9). SHABANA has a shape matrix \(\mat S\) with an axial and a transverse part, we take only the transverse part (idealization, section 28.1).

Why these names? The reference coordinates tell where the frame stands, the frame to which (refer) everything else is related. The elastic coordinates measure how the link bends relative to the frame, that is the elastic part of the motion. The subscript \(_{\mathrm{d}}\) comes from the word deformation, because in chapter 7 the subscript \(\mathrm f\) already has the meaning "free". The angle \(\theta\) is the angle \(\varphi\) of lemma 8.40, because \(\varphi\) in chapter 12 means the rotation of a node. This chapter does not use the shear rotation \(\theta\) of chapter 5. The matrix \(\mat\Psi\) is the shape matrix of SHABANA (there \(\mat S\)), the \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) are the trial functions of the Ritz method from chapter 6, and the matrix \(\mat S\) in this book is the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor and the geometric stiffness matrix.

There are three idealizations and we name them. First: the motion is planar. Second: the displacement of the axis is only transverse, the first component of the vector \(\bar{\vek u}\) of the axis is zero. Under deflection the axis of a real beam keeps its length \(L\), but its projection on the axis \(\bar x\) shortens to the length \(\ell < L\). We estimate the shortening \(\Delta = L - \ell\) as in step 2 of the proof of lemma 8.133, which does not use the values of \(v\) at the ends: from \(L = \int_0^\ell\sqrt{1 + v'^2}\,\mathrm dx\) and \(\sqrt{1 + p^2} \le 1 + p^2/2\) it follows that \(\Delta \le \tfrac12\int_0^\ell v'^2\,\mathrm dx \le \tfrac12\int_0^L v'^2\,\mathrm dx\), since \(\ell < L\) and \(v'^2 \ge 0\). For the first mode with a tip deflection of \(10\) mm this bound is \(0{,}058\) mm, that is \(5{,}8 \cdot 10^{-5}\) of the length. This shortening will not disappear, it will return in section 28.7, where it becomes essential because of the rotation. Third: the frame and the shapes \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) are chosen so that the position is unique. This requires the next subsection.

28.2.1 Does the position have one decomposition

The triple \((\vek o, \theta, \vek q_{\mathrm{d}})\) is a triple of numbers, while the position of the link is a curve \(\vek r(x)\). The equations of motion have as many unknowns as there are numbers in the triple. If the position did not change when the triple changes in some direction, the equations would have more unknowns than motions: to this direction would belong a zero velocity of all points, and the mass matrix would be singular. First we ask: does the position \(\vek r(x)\) determine the triple through equation (28.10) at all? The mass matrix is checked in section 28.4.

The answer depends on how we measure \(v\). Let us look at two ways and a bad example, shown in figure 28.4.

In the first way the axis \(\bar x\) lies on the tangent of the link where the link is clamped in the hub. The bent link moves away from the axis \(\bar x\), and the displacement \(v\) is its distance from the axis. The conditions are therefore \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v'(0) = 0\), and exactly these two conditions are satisfied by all the cantilever modes of theorem 13.37. In the second way the axis \(\bar x\) is the chord that joins both ends, so \(v(0) = v(L) = 0\). The link has the distance \(v\) from the chord. We leave the length of the axis equal to \(L\), as in the first way, because the shortening \(\Delta\) of the projection, which we estimated above, is of second order.

The conditions \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v'(0) = 0\) in the first way and \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v(L) = 0\) in the second are similar: each pair removes the displacement and the rotation of the hub from the field \(v\). When the position in general determines the triple is stated by the next definition.

Definition 28.5 Reference conditions of a link (referenčna pogoja člena)

Let \(v = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) be the transverse elastic field of the link (definition 28.4). The reference conditions (referenčna pogoja) are two equations \(\ell_1(v) = 0\) and \(\ell_2(v) = 0\), where each \(\ell_{\mathrm{j}}(v)\) is the value \(v(s_{\mathrm{j}})\) or the slope \(v'(s_{\mathrm{j}})\) of the field at a point \(s_{\mathrm{j}} \in [0, L]\), and the matrix

\[\mat C = \begin{bmatrix} \ell_1(1) & \ell_1(x) \\ \ell_2(1) & \ell_2(x) \end{bmatrix} \tag{28.11}\]

is invertible. Here \(1\) and \(x\) are the fields \(v(x) = 1\) and \(v(x) = x\), that is a displacement of the hub and a rotation, which we call the rigid fields of first order (toga polja).

Source: SHABANA §6.2, PDF pp. 286 to 287: the conditions determine the nature of the frame, their number must be at least equal to the number of rigid modes in the field. The matrix \(\mat C\) is not from the source, the condition on its invertibility writes this requirement down.

The matrix \(\mat C\) tells whether one of the fields \(a + b\,x\) satisfies both conditions: if \(\mat C\) is invertible, only \(a = b = 0\) does. For the clamped frame the conditions are \(v(0)\) and \(v'(0)\) and the matrix is the identity. For the chord frame the conditions are \(v(0)\) and \(v(L)\), and the matrix is \(\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 1 & L \end{bmatrix}\) with determinant \(L \ne 0\).

Lemma 28.6 Unique decomposition of the position of a link (enolična razstavitev lege člena)

Let \(\psi_1, \dots, \psi_{\mathrm{n}}\) be as in definition 28.4.

  1. Let \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}(0) = \psi_{\mathrm{k}}'(0) = 0\) for every \(k\) (clamped frame). If the position of the axis is \(\vek r(x) = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,(x,\ v(x))^\T\) with \(v = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\), then \(\vek o = \vek r(0)\) and \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)^\T = \vek r'(0)\). The angle \(\theta\) is determined by the position up to a multiple of \(2\pi\), and exactly under continuity in time and \(\theta(0) = 0\) (definition 28.3), and \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) is uniquely determined by the position. The conditions \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v'(0) = 0\) are reference conditions and every choice of \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) satisfies them.
  2. Let \(\ell_1\), \(\ell_2\) be reference conditions and \(v\) an arbitrary field. Then there is exactly one pair of numbers \((a, b)\) for which the field \(v - a - b\,x\) satisfies both conditions.
Proof

We must show that the position determines \(\vek o\), \(\theta\) and \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) (point 1) and that the solution of the system of two equations is unique (point 2).

  1. At \(x = 0\) we have \(v(0) = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}\psi_{\mathrm{k}}(0) = 0\), so by equation (28.10) \(\vek r(0) = \vek o + \mat R(\theta)\,(0, 0)^\T = \vek o\).
  2. The vector \(\vek o\) and the matrix \(\mat R(\theta)\) do not depend on \(x\), so \(\vek r'(x) = \mat R(\theta)\,(1,\ v'(x))^\T\). At \(x = 0\) we have \(v'(0) = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}\psi_{\mathrm{k}}'(0) = 0\), so \(\vek r'(0) = \mat R(\theta)\,(1, 0)^\T = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)^\T\). Two numbers with equal cosine and sine differ by a multiple of \(2\pi\): by point 3 of definition 8.9 we shift them by multiples of \(2\pi\) into \((-\pi, \pi]\), there the sign of the sine separates \([0, \pi]\) from \((-\pi, 0)\), and the cosine is strictly decreasing on \([0, \pi]\) and even (lemma 8.10, points 3 and 4), so the shifted numbers are equal. Now let \(\theta\) and \(\tilde\theta\) be continuous functions of time with the same vector \((\cos, \sin)^\T = \vek r'(0)\) at every time and with \(\theta(0) = \tilde\theta(0) = 0\). The function \(g = (\theta - \tilde\theta)/(2\pi)\) is continuous and has only integer values. If it had different values at times \(t_1 < t_2\), then by theorem 2.57 it would also take a non-integer value between them on \([t_1, t_2]\). So \(g\) is constant, and \(g(0) = 0\) gives \(\tilde\theta = \theta\).
  3. From equation (28.10) it follows that \((x,\ v(x))^\T = \mat R(\theta)^\T\,(\vek r(x) - \vek o)\) (\(\mat R^\T\mat R = \mat I\) because \(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1\)), so \(v(x)\) follows from the position and the already known \(\vek o\), \(\theta\). If two choices of coordinates give the same \(v\), then \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} (q_{\mathrm{k}} - q_{\mathrm{k}}')\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}} = 0\), and by linear independence \(q_{\mathrm{k}} = q_{\mathrm{k}}'\) for every \(k\).
  4. Point 2: the sought numbers \((a, b)\) must satisfy \(\ell_{\mathrm{i}}(v) - a\,\ell_{\mathrm{i}}(1) - b\,\ell_{\mathrm{i}}(x) = 0\) for \(i = 1, 2\) (the conditions are linear). This is the system \(\mat C\,(a, b)^\T = (\ell_1(v), \ell_2(v))^\T\), which for an invertible matrix \(\mat C\) has exactly one solution.

Source: The proof is not from the source. SHABANA, PDF pp. 286 to 287 says that reference conditions remove the rigid modes that the field contains.

Point 1 matters for us: the cantilever modes of theorem 13.37 satisfy \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}(0) = \psi_{\mathrm{k}}'(0) = 0\), so for them the frame is determined exactly by the hub, \(\vek o\) is the hub and \(\theta\) the direction of the link axis at the hub. Point 2 holds for any other pair of conditions. For the chord frame \(a = v(0)\) and \(b = (v(L) - v(0))/L\), and the field \(v - a - b\,x\) is indeed zero at both ends. Point 2 is a statement about fields. It has a geometric meaning only to first order: a frame turned by a small angle \(b\) changes the transverse displacement by \(b\,x\) and the first component of the point by \(x\,(1 - \cos b) \approx x\,b^2/2\), which for \(b = 0{,}01\) is \(5 \cdot 10^{-5}\,x\).

Let us also test the bad example: a single shape \(\psi_1 = x/L\), which satisfies only \(v(0) = 0\) and not \(v'(0) = 0\) (figure 28.4, third part). With the axis \(\bar x\) of frame A the straight link at \(q_1 = q\) is turned by \(\beta\) with \(\tan\beta = q/L\). Its offset \(q\) at the end is measured from \(\bar x\). The same link can also be described with frame B with the axis \(\bar x'\), turned by \(2\beta\) against the first, and with \(q' = -q\): in this frame the link has the offset \(-q\). The computation for an arbitrary point \(x\):

\[\mat R(\theta)\begin{bmatrix} x \\ q\,x/L \end{bmatrix} = x\sqrt{1 + (q/L)^2}\ \mat R(\theta + \beta)\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} , \qquad \mat R(\theta + 2\beta)\begin{bmatrix} x \\ -q\,x/L \end{bmatrix} = x\sqrt{1 + (q/L)^2}\ \mat R(\theta + \beta)\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} , \tag{28.12}\]

because \((1, q/L) = \sqrt{1 + (q/L)^2}\,(\cos\beta, \sin\beta)\) and \(\mat R(2\beta)\,(\cos\beta, -\sin\beta)^\T = (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)^\T\). The same position therefore has two triples, \((\theta, q)\) and \((\theta + 2\beta, -q)\). For \(q = 10\) mm and \(L = 1\) m we have \(2\beta = 2\arctan 0{,}01 = 0{,}0199993\) rad \(= 1{,}146°\), and the computer finds in the two descriptions points that differ by less than \(2 \cdot 10^{-16}\) m. The reason is that \(\psi_1 = x/L\) contains a rigid rotation. Point 1 of the lemma does not hold, because \(\psi_1'(0) = 1/L \ne 0\). The condition \(v(0) = 0\) alone is not a pair of reference conditions, because the matrix \(\mat C\) has one row \((1, 0)\), and the field \(x\) satisfies this condition. In section 28.4 the same example will give a singular mass matrix at \(q = 0\), where the two triples merge into one.

This field has one more fault. In the frame where \(v = q\,x/L\) and \(\bar{\vek u} = (0, q\,x/L)\), the displacement gradient has a single non-zero entry \(\partial\bar u_{\mathrm{y}}/\partial x = q/L\), so by lemma 1.113 \(E_{\mathrm{xx}} = \tfrac12(q/L)^2\). The axis really lengthens: a piece of the axis of length \(h\) has the length \(h\sqrt{1 + (q/L)^2}\) in the position, and the stretch \(\sqrt{1 + (q/L)^2} - 1\) differs from \(\tfrac12(q/L)^2\) by less than \(2 \cdot 10^{-9}\). For \(q/L = 0{,}01\) this is \(5 \cdot 10^{-5}\) or \(0{,}05\) mm at the end. In size this is the false stretch \(\cos\theta - 1 = -5 \cdot 10^{-5}\) at \(\theta = 0{,}01\) rad from lemma 28.1, only the sign is opposite: a rotation written as an elastic displacement gives a false deformation.

28.2.2 Strain in the floating frame

Section 28.1 promised that the rotation would no longer fake the deformation. The theorem that follows fulfils this: the Green-Lagrange strain tensor (definition 1.112), computed from the displacement in the frame, does not depend on \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\).

Theorem 28.7 Strain in the floating frame of reference (deformacija v plavajočem koordinatnem sistemu)

Let the body be planar (the displacement in the direction \(z\) is zero, all quantities are independent of \(z\)), let a point of the body have in the unloaded state the position \(\vek X = (X_1, X_2, 0)^\T\) and at time \(t\) the position \(\vek r = \vek o + \mat Q(\theta)\,(\vek X + \bar{\vek u}(\vek X, t))\) with \(\mat Q(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \mat R(\theta) & \vek 0 \\ \vek 0^\T & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) and a continuously differentiable field \(\bar{\vek u}\) (components in the frame, third component zero). Let \(\bar{\mat H} = \partial\bar{\vek u}/\partial\vek X\) be its gradient and \(\det(\mat I + \bar{\mat H}) > 0\). Let \(\ten\sigma\) be the matrix of stresses after loading at the point and \(\bar{\ten\sigma} = \mat Q^\T\ten\sigma\,\mat Q\). Then:

  1. the deformation gradient is \(\mat F = \mat Q(\theta)\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})\),
  2. the Green-Lagrange strain tensor is \(\mat E = \tfrac12\big(\bar{\mat H} + \bar{\mat H}^\T + \bar{\mat H}^\T\bar{\mat H}\big)\) and does not depend on \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\),
  3. if \(\mat I + \bar{\mat H} = \bar{\mat R}\,\bar{\mat U}\) is the polar decomposition, then the polar decomposition of the gradient is \(\mat F = (\mat Q\,\bar{\mat R})\,\bar{\mat U}\), so the stretch \(\bar{\mat U}\) does not depend on \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\),
  4. the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor is \(\mat S = \bar J\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-1}\,\bar{\ten\sigma}\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-\T}\) with \(\bar J = \det(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})\), so we compute it from the components of the stress in the frame,
  5. if \(|\bar H_{\mathrm{ij}}| \le m \le \tfrac16\), then \(|E_{\mathrm{ij}} - \bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}}| \le \tfrac32\,m^2\) and \(|\bar U_{\mathrm{ij}} - I_{\mathrm{ij}} - \bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ij}}| \le 18\,m^2\) for \(\bar{\ten\varepsilon} = \tfrac12(\bar{\mat H} + \bar{\mat H}^\T)\),
  6. the small strain tensor in the fixed axes for the same motion is \(\ten\varepsilon = \tfrac12(\mat H + \mat H^\T)\) with \(\mat H = \mat F - \mat I\), and for \(\bar{\mat H} = \mat 0\) it is \(\ten\varepsilon = (\cos\theta - 1)\,\mathrm{diag}(1, 1, 0)\).
Proof

We must show six statements. The gradient is obtained by differentiating the position with respect to \(\vek X\), the other statements follow from definitions and theorems of chapters 1 and 2.

  1. The vector \(\vek o\) and the matrix \(\mat Q\) are functions of time, not of the point, so \(\partial\vek r/\partial\vek X = \mat Q\,(\mat I + \partial\bar{\vek u}/\partial\vek X) = \mat Q\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})\). This is the deformation gradient, since \(\mat F = \mat I + \mat H\) with \(\mat H\) the gradient of the displacement \(\vek r - \vek X\) (definition 1.110).
  2. The matrix \(\mat Q\) is orthogonal, \(\mat Q^\T\mat Q = \mat I\) (definition 8.38, equation (2.40)). By definition 1.112, \(\mat E = \tfrac12(\mat F^\T\mat F - \mat I) = \tfrac12\big((\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^\T\mat Q^\T\mat Q\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H}) - \mat I\big) = \tfrac12\big(\bar{\mat H} + \bar{\mat H}^\T + \bar{\mat H}^\T\bar{\mat H}\big)\). The result contains neither \(\vek o\) nor \(\theta\).
  3. The matrix \(\mat I + \bar{\mat H}\) has a positive determinant, so by theorem 2.85 it splits into \(\bar{\mat R}\,\bar{\mat U}\). The product \(\mat Q\,\bar{\mat R}\) is an orthogonal matrix with determinant \(1 \cdot 1 = 1\) (a product of orthogonal matrices is orthogonal), and \(\bar{\mat U}\) is symmetric with positive eigenvalues. By the uniqueness in theorem 2.85 this is the polar decomposition of the matrix \(\mat F\).
  4. Because \(\det\mat Q = 1\) we have \(\det\mat F = \bar J\). The inverse is \(\mat F^{-1} = (\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-1}\mat Q^\T\), so \(\mat F^{-\T} = \mat Q\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-\T}\). Substituting in equation (2.83) gives \(\mat S = \bar J\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-1}\,(\mat Q^\T\ten\sigma\,\mat Q)\,(\mat I + \bar{\mat H})^{-\T}\).
  5. For \(\mat Q = \mat I\) and \(\vek o = \vek 0\) the gradient \(\bar{\mat H}\) is the gradient of the displacements of the body, so corollary 1.122 (first estimate) and lemma 2.86 hold with \(\bar{\mat H}\) in place of \(\mat H\).
  6. For \(\bar{\mat H} = \mat 0\) we have \(\mat H = \mat Q - \mat I\) and \(\ten\varepsilon = \tfrac12(\mat Q + \mat Q^\T) - \mat I\). Along the diagonal this is \(\cos\theta - 1\) in the first two places and \(1 - 1 = 0\) in the third, off the diagonal it is \(-\sin\theta + \sin\theta = 0\).

Source: SHABANA §5.1 and §5.2, PDF pp. 203 to 204 (position as \(\vek R + \mat A\,\bar{\vek u}\)). The relation to the strain in the frame by the theorems of chapters 1 and 2, the proof is not from the source.

The theorem says what the frame gives us: \(\mat E\) and \(\bar{\mat U}\) do not know the rotation. If the link is only turned (\(\bar{\mat H} = \mat 0\)), then \(\mat E = \mat 0\) and \(\bar{\mat U} = \mat I\), while in the fixed axes the small strain tensor is, by point 6, \(\mathrm{diag}(-0{,}134, -0{,}134, 0)\) at \(30°\), which lemma 28.1 shows. The reason is that the rotation in the frame hides in \(\mat Q\), which drops out of \(\mat Q^\T\mat Q = \mat I\).

Let us test the theorem on our link, first cantilever mode, \(L = 1\) m, \(h = 6\) mm, \(y = \pm 3\) mm. With the Euler-Bernoulli field (definition 28.4) we have \(\bar{\mat H} = \begin{bmatrix} -y\,v'' & -v' \\ v' & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), \(E_{\mathrm{xx}} = -y\,v'' + \tfrac12\big(y^2v''^2 + v'^2\big)\), and the first term is \(\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\). For a tip deflection of \(10\) mm the maximal values are \(\max|v'| = 1{,}38 \cdot 10^{-2}\) and \(y\max|v''| = 1{,}05 \cdot 10^{-4}\), so \(m = 1{,}38 \cdot 10^{-2}\) and \(\tfrac32 m^2 = 2{,}8 \cdot 10^{-4}\). The estimate of point 5 is therefore larger than the bending strain itself, \(1{,}05 \cdot 10^{-4}\), and nothing follows from it. The expansion \(\mat E - \bar{\ten\varepsilon} = \tfrac12\bar{\mat H}^\T\bar{\mat H}\) is enough, however: on the axis \(E_{\mathrm{xx}} = \tfrac12 v'^2 \le 9{,}5 \cdot 10^{-5}\), which is \(90\) % of the bending strain at \(10\) mm and \(9\) % at \(1\) mm (\(9{,}5 \cdot 10^{-7}\) against \(1{,}05 \cdot 10^{-5}\)). This is not a true strain: under deflection the axis of a real beam keeps its length and only its projection on \(\bar x\) shortens (the second idealization above), whereas the field \((0, v)\) lengthens the axis. In section 28.5 we shall therefore take \(\bar\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -y\,v''\) for the energy, which is linear in \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) (lemma 5.76), and drop \(\tfrac12 v'^2\). The same term will return as \(\tfrac12\int N v'^2\,\mathrm dx\) in section 28.7, where it is essential at steady rotation.

A bad example for the theorem is a rotation written as an elastic field: in the frame let \(v = b\,x\) and take the Euler-Bernoulli field \(\bar{\vek u} = (-b\,y,\ b\,x)\) (definition 28.4). The gradient \(\bar{\mat H} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -b \\ b & 0 \end{bmatrix}\) is antisymmetric, so \(\bar{\ten\varepsilon} = \mat 0\) and the motion is to first order a rotation. Nevertheless \(\mat E = \tfrac12\bar{\mat H}^\T\bar{\mat H} = \tfrac12 b^2\,\mathrm{diag}(1, 1)\), so \(\mat E \ne \mat 0\). On the axis \(E_{\mathrm{xx}} = \tfrac12 b^2\), the same as in the bad example above with \(b = q/L\). The theorem does not prevent this, because it claims only that \(\vek o\) and \(\theta\) do not influence \(\mat E\). It says nothing about a rotation written into \(\bar{\vek u}\). The frame must therefore prevent a rotation from entering the elastic coordinates, and the reference conditions of lemma 28.6 achieve this.
