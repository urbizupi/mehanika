Chapter 5 Beam theory (teorija nosilcev)

5.9 Deflection curves (upogibnice)

How far do our three beams deflect, and is the deflection small enough for the model from which we computed it? Equilibrium gave us the shear force (prečna sila) \(V\) and the bending moment (upogibni moment) \(M\), and the moment gave the stress. The deflection (poves) \(v\) itself does not follow from equilibrium. The moment fixes only the curvature, \(v'' = M/(EI)\), and the deflection is its double integral over the whole length of the beam. Equation (5.33), \(EI\,v'''' = q\) (theorem 5.42), does this integral by itself. Because it contains a fourth derivative, its solution has four constants (theorem 5.45), which four boundary conditions fix (section 5.7.2). The graph of the solution \(v(x)\), the shape the axis of the beam takes, is the deflection curve, which we named in section 5.7.1.

In all three examples the beam is the same steel flat strip from section 5.1.3: width \(b = 20\) mm, height \(h = 40\) mm, modulus of elasticity \(E = 196\) GPa, area \(A = 800\ \text{mm}^2\), second moment of area (vztrajnostni moment) \(I = b\,h^3/12 = 1{,}0667\cdot 10^{-7}\ \text{m}^4\) (section 5.4.3), hence the bending stiffness (upogibna togost) \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\). The self weight of the strip per unit length is \(\rho g A = 7800 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 8\cdot 10^{-4} = 61{,}21\) N/m. The stiffness is also constant along the beam, so we can take it out of the derivatives. The \(x\) axis runs along the axis of the beam, the \(y\) axis points up, and the deflection \(v\) and the load \(q\) are positive upward.

We fixed the signs in sections 5.2.1 and 5.2.3, here we only collect them. With this sign, the shear force and the moment are the components of the force and the moment on the cross section with outward normal \(+\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), that is, of what the part at larger \(x\) exerts on the part at smaller \(x\). By theorem 5.35 and corollary 5.11 (on a piece without point forces and couples, \(m = 0\)),

\[M = EI\,v'' , \qquad V = -M' = -EI\,v''' . \tag{5.51}\]

The minus in \(V\) has the reason we gave in section 5.2.3: \(V\) is the force of the right part on the left cross section, and \(M\) is the moment of this force and the other forces of the right part about the left cross section. At the free end \(x = L\) we have, by section 5.7.2, \(V(L) = F_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(M(L) = M_0\), where \(F_{\mathrm{y}}\) is the force and \(M_0\) the couple acting on the beam there. At the end \(x = 0\) the beam is acted on by \(-V(0)\) and \(-M(0)\).

From history

Euler was interested mainly in the geometric shapes of elastic curves (TIMO, PDF p. 39). In 1744 his book on finding curves with a largest or smallest property appeared, the first book on the calculus of variations and the first systematic treatment of elastic curves (TIMO, PDF p. 37). For the elastic bar he accepted Bernoulli's statement that the curvature is proportional to the moment without discussion and started from it (TIMO, PDF p. 38-39). Because he did not restrict himself to small deflections, he could not drop the term \(y'^2\) in the denominator and the equation was complicated (TIMO, PDF p. 40). He integrated it by series and showed that for a small deflection \(f\) of the end of a cantilever with a load \(P\) at the free end and length \(l\) it is approximately \(f = P l^3/(3 C)\), where \(C\) is Bernoulli's constant (TIMO, PDF p. 40). This is the usual formula for the deflection of a cantilever. The dropped term in the numerator allows for the fact that, because of the deflection, the length \(l\) is somewhat smaller than the original length of the bar (TIMO, PDF p. 40).

Euler considered several ways of loading and classified them by the angle between the direction of the force \(P\) and the tangent at the point where it acts. When this angle is very small, we have a column under an axial compressive force. He showed that the equation of the elastic curve can then be solved easily and that the column buckles at the force \(P = C\pi^2/(4 l^2)\) (TIMO, PDF p. 41-42). For the same elasticity and thickness the force that the column can still carry is therefore inversely proportional to the square of the height, and a column twice as high carries only a quarter of the force (TIMO, PDF p. 42). He also treated a cantilever whose stiffness is proportional to the distance \(x\) (TIMO, PDF p. 42).

Let us return to the question of the section: what is the deflection of our three beams, and is it small enough for our model to hold. The term \(y'^2\) in the denominator, which Euler could not drop, we do drop, so at the end of the section we measure the error that this makes for our beams.

5.9.1 Four integrations (štiri integracije)

We solve all three beams in the same way, so we first write down what four integrations give for a constant load. The length of the beam is \(L\), the stiffness \(EI\) is constant, and the load \(q\) is a number that does not depend on \(x\).

Lemma 5.55 Deflection curve under a constant load (upogibnica pri stalni obtežbi)

Let \(EI > 0\), \(q\) and \(L > 0\) be numbers. Let \(v\) be a function with input \(x \in [0, L]\) and output a real number, with continuous derivatives up to and including the third order on \([0, L]\) and with a fourth derivative that satisfies \(EI\,v'''' = q\) on \((0, L)\). Then there exist numbers \(C_0, C_1, C_2, C_3\) such that for every \(x \in [0, L]\)

\[\begin{aligned} EI\,v'''(x) &= q\,x + C_3 , \\ EI\,v''(x) &= \tfrac12\,q\,x^2 + C_3\,x + C_2 , \\ EI\,v'(x) &= \tfrac16\,q\,x^3 + \tfrac12\,C_3\,x^2 + C_2\,x + C_1 , \\ EI\,v(x) &= \tfrac1{24}\,q\,x^4 + \tfrac16\,C_3\,x^3 + \tfrac12\,C_2\,x^2 + C_1\,x + C_0 , \end{aligned} \tag{5.52}\]

and the numbers are \(C_0 = EI\,v(0)\), \(C_1 = EI\,v'(0)\), \(C_2 = M(0)\) and \(C_3 = -V(0)\). Conversely, every function (5.52) with given numbers \(C_{\mathrm{k}}\) is a solution of the equation \(EI\,v'''' = q\). The moment and the shear force are

\[M(x) = \tfrac12\,q\,x^2 + C_3\,x + C_2 , \qquad V(x) = -q\,x - C_3 . \tag{5.53}\]
Proof

We must show that the lemma is theorem 5.45 for a constant right-hand side, written with constants that are the values at \(x = 0\).

  1. The function \(v\) has the properties of theorem 5.45 with \(n = 4\), \(a = 0\), \(b = L\) and the constant, hence continuous, right-hand side \(f = q/(EI)\). The repeated integrals (equation (5.43)) are \(I_{\mathrm{k}}(x) = q\,x^k/(EI\,k!)\). For \(k = 0\) this holds, and if it holds for \(k - 1\), then \(I_{\mathrm{k}}(x) = \int_0^x q\,t^{k-1}/(EI\,(k-1)!)\,\mathrm{d}t = q\,x^k/(EI\,k!)\), because \(t^k/k!\) has derivative \(t^{k-1}/(k-1)!\) (lemma 1.81 and part 1 of theorem 3.40).
  2. By theorem 5.45, therefore, \(v = q\,x^4/(24\,EI) + c_0 + c_1 x + c_2 x^2 + c_3 x^3\) for some numbers \(c_0, \dots, c_3\). Multiply by \(EI\) and write \(C_0 = EI\,c_0\), \(C_1 = EI\,c_1\), \(C_2 = 2EI\,c_2\), \(C_3 = 6EI\,c_3\). This is the fourth row of (5.52).
  3. We differentiate the polynomial by the rules of differentiation (lemma 1.81): the first derivative of the fourth row is the third row, the second derivative the second row and the third derivative the first row. Derivatives of a polynomial are polynomials, so they hold on all of \([0, L]\), at the ends as one-sided derivatives.
  4. At \(x = 0\) all terms with \(x\) vanish, so the rows give \(EI\,v'''(0) = C_3\), \(EI\,v''(0) = C_2\), \(EI\,v'(0) = C_1\) and \(EI\,v(0) = C_0\). By equation (5.51), \(M(0) = EI\,v''(0) = C_2\) and \(V(0) = -EI\,v'''(0) = -C_3\).
  5. The first and the second row, inserted in (5.51), give (5.53).
  6. Conversely: theorem 5.45 says "if and only if", so every function (5.52) with given numbers \(C_{\mathrm{k}}\) is a solution of the equation \(EI\,v'''' = q\).

Source: The proof is not from a source, it follows from theorem 5.45. The same steps are in SADD, PDF p. 578-579, for a cantilever with a load at the end, and in EP, p. 175-176.

The lemma tells what the constants mean. By step 4, \(C_0, \dots, C_3\) are the deflection, the slope, the moment and the shear force at the left end, each with its stiffness or sign. The four boundary conditions of each beam are therefore four equations for these numbers, and they are linear, because all terms in the lemma are linear in \(C_{\mathrm{k}}\). For each beam we will write them down with this lemma. Whether each of the three beams has only one deflection curve we have already shown: the determinants of the boundary conditions of the cantilever, the simply supported beam and the propped cantilever are nonzero (section 5.8), so by theorem 5.49 each of them has exactly one solution for every load, and the one we find is the only one.

5.9.2 Cantilever (konzola)

The cantilever is a beam of length \(L = 1\) m, clamped (vpet) at \(x = 0\), pulled down at the free end \(x = L\) by a force \(F = 500\) N (figure 5.51). We neglect the self weight for now, \(q = 0\). The clamp is rigid (section 5.1.2): the end of the strip neither moves nor rotates, \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v'(0) = 0\). At the free end a force downward means \(F_{\mathrm{y}} = -F\), with no couple, \(M_0 = 0\). So \(V(L) = -F\) and \(M(L) = 0\). The question is how far the end of the strip goes down and by how much it rotates. We expect it to go down, because the load is downward, and the descent to be proportional to \(F\), because the equations are linear.

Theorem 5.56 Deflection curve of a cantilever (upogibnica konzole)

A beam of length \(L\) with constant bending stiffness \(EI\) is clamped at \(x = 0\) and free at \(x = L\). The assumptions of beam theory from section 5.3.2 hold and the displacements are small.

  1. If a force \(F\) acts downward at the free end and there are no other loads, then
    \[\begin{aligned} v(x) &= -\frac{F\,x^2\,(3L - x)}{6EI} , \\ v(L) &= -\frac{F\,L^3}{3EI} , \\ v'(L) &= -\frac{F\,L^2}{2EI} . \end{aligned} \tag{5.54}\]
  2. If only the self weight acts, \(q = -q_0\) with \(q_0 = \rho g A\), then
    \[v(x) = -\frac{q_0\,x^2\,(6L^2 - 4L\,x + x^2)}{24EI} , \qquad v(L) = -\frac{q_0\,L^4}{8EI} . \tag{5.55}\]
Proof

We must show that the boundary conditions of lemma 5.55 determine the constants \(C_0, \dots, C_3\) uniquely. For part 1, \(q = 0\).

  1. Fixed end: \(C_0 = EI\,v(0) = 0\) and \(C_1 = EI\,v'(0) = 0\) (lemma 5.55, step 4).
  2. Shear force at the free end: \(V(L) = -F\). By (5.53), \(V(L) = -C_3\) for \(q = 0\), so \(C_3 = F\).
  3. Moment at the free end: \(M(L) = 0\). By (5.53), \(M(L) = C_3 L + C_2 = F L + C_2\), so \(C_2 = -F L\).
  4. Insert in the fourth row of (5.52): \(EI\,v = \tfrac16 F\,x^3 - \tfrac12 F L\,x^2 = -\tfrac16 F\,x^2\,(3L - x)\). This is the first equation in (5.54).
  5. At \(x = L\), \(EI\,v(L) = -\tfrac16 F L^2 \cdot 2L = -\tfrac13 F L^3\). The third row with \(C_1 = 0\), \(C_2 = -F L\), \(C_3 = F\) gives \(EI\,v'(L) = \tfrac12 F L^2 - F L^2 = -\tfrac12 F L^2\).

For part 2, \(q = -q_0\), and the free end has no force and no couple, \(V(L) = 0\), \(M(L) = 0\).

  1. Fixed end as in step 1: \(C_0 = C_1 = 0\).
  2. Shear force: \(V(L) = q_0 L - C_3 = 0\) by (5.53), so \(C_3 = q_0 L\).
  3. Moment: \(M(L) = -\tfrac12 q_0 L^2 + C_3 L + C_2 = \tfrac12 q_0 L^2 + C_2 = 0\), so \(C_2 = -\tfrac12 q_0 L^2\).
  4. Fourth row: \(EI\,v = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 x^4 + \tfrac16 q_0 L\,x^3 - \tfrac14 q_0 L^2 x^2 = -\tfrac1{24} q_0\,x^2\,(x^2 - 4L\,x + 6L^2)\), and at \(x = L\) it is \(-\tfrac1{24} q_0 L^4 (1 - 4 + 6) = -\tfrac18 q_0 L^4\).

Source: SADD, p. 578-579 (PDF), where the cantilever is fixed at \(x = L\) and loaded at \(x = 0\): with the substitution \(x \to L - x\), \(v = P(-x^3 + 3L^2 x - 2L^3)/(6EI)\) is the same as (5.54), and \(v_{\max} = -PL^3/(3EI)\) (p579-b3). EP, p. 80, equation p80-b10, and p. 176, equation p176-b10, without the couple. Part 2 is not from the sources, checked with SymPy.

Let us check on our numbers. For \(F = 500\) N, \(L = 1\) m and \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\),

\[v(L) = -\frac{500 \cdot 1^3}{3 \cdot 20\,906{,}7}\ \text{m} = -7{,}972\ \text{mm} , \qquad v'(L) = -\frac{500 \cdot 1^2}{2 \cdot 20\,906{,}7} = -0{,}01196 .\]

The slope is a ratio of two lengths and has no unit. The end of the strip goes down by 7,972 mm, which is 0,8 % of the length, and the axis tilts there by 0,01196, a small number compared with 1. The descent is thus small compared with the length and the slope is small compared with 1, which is what the model needs (section 5.2, the assumption of small displacements). We check all this for all the beams at the end of the section.

The shape of the curve has its reason in the moment. By (5.51) the curvature is \(v'' = M/(EI) = -F\,(L - x)/(EI)\). At the clamp this is \(-500/20\,906{,}7 = -0{,}02392\) 1/m, so the radius of curvature is \(1/0{,}02392 = 41{,}8\) m. The stress at the upper edge \(y = 20\) mm is, by theorem 5.35, \(-M\,y/I = 500 \cdot 0{,}02/(1{,}0667\cdot 10^{-7}) = 93{,}75\) MPa, tension. Toward the free end the moment falls linearly to zero, so the axis hardly bends there and is almost straight. The descent of the end therefore does not arise right at the end, but is brought by the slope the axis has gathered on the way: the slope is the integral of the curvature, and the descent is the integral of the slope.

The self weight of the strip is the same cantilever with another load (part 2 of the theorem). For \(q_0 = 61{,}21\) N/m,

\[v(L) = -\frac{61{,}21 \cdot 1^4}{8 \cdot 20\,906{,}7}\ \text{m} = -0{,}366\ \text{mm} ,\]

which is 4,6 % of the descent due to the force 500 N (figure 5.52). This is a small share, and so the beam hardly changes its shape because of its self weight. We treat both loads together at the end of this section, in the addition of solutions.

5.9.3 Simply supported beam (prostoležeči nosilec)

The simply supported beam has length \(L = 2\) m (ratio \(L/h = 50\)), at \(x = 0\) it is held by a pin support (nepomična členkasta podpora), at \(x = L\) by a roller support (pomična členkasta podpora). The supports prevent the vertical displacement, \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v(L) = 0\), but take no moment, so \(M(0) = 0\) and \(M(L) = 0\), which by (5.51) is the same as \(v''(0) = v''(L) = 0\). These are four boundary conditions. The two loads we treat have the same largest moment \(M_{\max} = 500\) N m (section 5.2.2), so that we can compare how the beam bends: a uniform load \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m, and a force \(F = 1\) kN at the middle. This is a choice, not a law.

Theorem 5.57 Deflection curve of a simply supported beam under uniform load (upogibnica prostoležečega nosilca pod enakomerno obtežbo)

A beam of length \(L\) with constant bending stiffness \(EI\) is held by a pin support at \(x = 0\) and by a roller support at \(x = L\) and carries a uniform load \(q = -q_0\) (downward), \(q_0 > 0\). The assumptions of beam theory hold and the displacements are small. Then

\[\begin{aligned} v(x) &= -\frac{q_0\,x\,(L - x)\,(L^2 + L\,x - x^2)}{24EI} , \\ v\!\left(\tfrac{L}{2}\right) &= -\frac{5\,q_0\,L^4}{384\,EI} , \\ v'(0) &= -v'(L) = -\frac{q_0\,L^3}{24EI} . \end{aligned} \tag{5.56}\]

The support forces, \(-V(0)\) and \(V(L)\), are both \(q_0 L/2\) upward.

Proof

We must show that the boundary conditions determine the constants of lemma 5.55 with \(q = -q_0\) uniquely.

  1. The condition \(v(0) = 0\) gives \(C_0 = 0\). The condition \(M(0) = 0\) gives \(C_2 = M(0) = 0\) (lemma 5.55, step 4).
  2. The condition \(M(L) = 0\): by (5.53), \(M(L) = -\tfrac12 q_0 L^2 + C_3 L = 0\), so \(C_3 = \tfrac12 q_0 L\).
  3. The condition \(v(L) = 0\): the fourth row of (5.52) gives \(0 = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 L^4 + \tfrac16 C_3 L^3 + C_1 L = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 L^4 + \tfrac1{12} q_0 L^4 + C_1 L = \tfrac1{24} q_0 L^4 + C_1 L\). So \(C_1 = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 L^3\).
  4. Insert in the fourth row: \(EI\,v = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 x^4 + \tfrac1{12} q_0 L\,x^3 - \tfrac1{24} q_0 L^3 x = -\tfrac1{24} q_0\,x\,(x^3 - 2L\,x^2 + L^3)\). The polynomial \(x^3 - 2L\,x^2 + L^3\) is divisible by \(L - x\), because at \(x = L\) it equals \(L^3 - 2L^3 + L^3 = 0\), and \(x^3 - 2L\,x^2 + L^3 = (L - x)(L^2 + L\,x - x^2)\), which we see by multiplying: \((L - x)(L^2 + L\,x - x^2) = L^3 + L^2 x - L x^2 - L^2 x - L x^2 + x^3\).
  5. At \(x = L/2\), \(x\,(L - x) = L^2/4\) and \(L^2 + L^2/2 - L^2/4 = 5L^2/4\), so \(EI\,v(L/2) = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 \cdot \tfrac{5}{16} L^4\).
  6. By the lemma, the slope at \(x = 0\) is \(C_1/EI = -q_0 L^3/(24EI)\). From the third row, \(EI\,v'(L) = \tfrac16(-q_0)L^3 + \tfrac12 C_3 L^2 + C_1 = (-\tfrac16 + \tfrac14 - \tfrac1{24})\,q_0 L^3 = \tfrac1{24} q_0 L^3\).
  7. By (5.53) the shear force is \(V(0) = -C_3 = -\tfrac12 q_0 L\) and \(V(L) = q_0 L - C_3 = \tfrac12 q_0 L\). On the left end the support acts with the force \(-V(0) = \tfrac12 q_0 L\) upward, on the right end with the force \(V(L) = \tfrac12 q_0 L\) upward.

Source: SADD, figure D.12 (PDF p. 579): \(v = -w x (L^3 - 2L x^2 + x^3)/(24EI)\) and \(v_{\max} = v(L/2) = -5wL^4/(384EI)\), which is the same by the factorization above. SADD (8.1.38) (PDF p. 177): \(v_{\max} = 5wl^4/(24EI)\) for a beam of length \(2l\), so with \(l = L/2\) the same as (5.56). Equation (8.1.37) of the same page is the deflection from plane elasticity, which differs from it by a term with \(c^2/l^2\) that the elementary theory does not have.

For \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m, \(L = 2\) m and \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\),

\[v\!\left(\tfrac{L}{2}\right) = -\frac{5 \cdot 1000 \cdot 2^4}{384 \cdot 20\,906{,}7}\ \text{m} = -9{,}965\ \text{mm} , \qquad v'(0) = -\frac{1000 \cdot 2^3}{24 \cdot 20\,906{,}7} = -0{,}01594 ,\]

and at the right end the slope is \(+0{,}01594\) (figure 5.53). By step 7 the support forces are 1000 N each, which is the same as from equilibrium in section 5.2.2: equilibrium and deflection agree. The product \(x\,(L - x)\) and the sum \(L^2 + L\,x - x^2\) do not change under the substitution \(x \to L - x\), so the deflection curve is symmetric about the middle, the slope at the middle is zero and the largest deflection is at the middle, 0,5 % of the length. We obtained the same two numbers as a test of the conditions already in section 5.7.2 (equations (5.37) and (5.38)), and here the result is a theorem for arbitrary \(L\), \(q_0\) and \(EI\).

The second example is the same beam (\(L = 2\) m, pin support and roller support, \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\)) with a force \(F = 1\) kN downward at the middle and no distributed load. A point force interrupts the load, so we do not integrate over the whole beam but by pieces. We have already done this in derivation 5.54 of section 5.8.4: on each half \(q = 0\) and \(v\) is a polynomial of third degree, and the eight constants are determined by four boundary conditions and four matching conditions at the force (\(v\), \(v'\) and \(M\) continuous, \(V\) jumps by \(-F_{\mathrm{y}}\) by lemma 5.12). We do not repeat the derivation, we only write the result for a beam of length \(L\) with a force \(F\) downward at the middle (figure 5.54). For \(0 \le x \le L/2\),

\[\begin{aligned} v(x) &= -\frac{F\,x\,(3L^2 - 4x^2)}{48EI} , \\ v\!\left(\tfrac{L}{2}\right) &= -\frac{F\,L^3}{48EI} , \\ v'(0) &= -\frac{F\,L^2}{16EI} , \end{aligned} \tag{5.57}\]

and for \(L/2 \le x \le L\), \(v(x) = v(L - x)\). For \(F = 1\) kN, \(L = 2\) m, \(v(L/2) = -1000 \cdot 2^3/(48 \cdot 20\,906{,}7)\ \text{m} = -7{,}972\) mm and \(v'(0) = -1000 \cdot 2^2/(16 \cdot 20\,906{,}7) = -0{,}01196\).

The deflection and the slope are exactly the numbers of the cantilever from the previous section, \(-7{,}972\) mm and \(-0{,}01196\). The reason is the slope at the middle, which by step 7 of the derivation is zero. The beam and the load are mirror images about the middle, so the cross section at the middle would turn in the opposite sense from the one the mirror half would turn it, and it turns nowhere. The left half is therefore a beam of length \(L/2 = 1\) m, at its right end without rotation (as if clamped, although the clamp only moves down) and at its left end loaded by the support force \(F/2 = 500\) N upward (figure 5.55). These are the length and the force of the cantilever from the previous section, only the force points up. Let us check: with \(\xi = L/2 - x\), \(x\,(3L^2 - 4x^2) - L^3 = -(2x - L)^2 (x + L) = -4\xi^2\,(3L/2 - \xi)\), so

\[v(x) - v\!\left(\tfrac{L}{2}\right) = \frac{(F/2)\,\xi^2\,(3L/2 - \xi)}{6EI} ,\]

which is the deflection curve (5.54) with length \(L/2\) and force \(F/2\), turned upward. The free end therefore rises relative to the clamp by \((F/2)(L/2)^3/(3EI) = F L^3/(48EI) = 7{,}972\) mm. The support does not move, so the middle is lower than it by this much.

A third example: the self weight of the simply supported beam. This is the same beam and load as for the uniform load above, with \(q_0 = 61{,}21\) N/m: the deflection at the middle is \(-5 \cdot 61{,}21 \cdot 2^4/(384 \cdot 20\,906{,}7)\ \text{m} = -0{,}610\) mm.

Let us also compare the descents at the middle. The uniform load gives 9,965 mm, the force at the middle 7,972 mm, and the ratio is exactly \(5/4\). The reason is the choice of the loads: the same largest moment, 500 N m, means \(q_0 L^2/8 = F L/4\), so \(F = q_0 L/2\), and the ratio of the descents is \((5 q_0 L^4/384)/(F L^3/48) = 5/4\). The moment under the uniform load is larger over a larger part of the beam, for example at a quarter of the length \(M(L/4) = \tfrac{q_0}{2}\,\tfrac{L}{4}\,\tfrac{3L}{4} = 375\) N m, while under the force at the middle it is only \(F\,L/8 = 250\) N m. A larger curvature \(M/(EI)\) at more places means a larger deflection.

5.9.4 Propped cantilever (podprta konzola)

The propped cantilever has length \(L = 2\) m, at \(x = 0\) it is clamped, at \(x = L\) it is held by a roller support, and it carries a uniform load \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m downward. The unknowns are the reactions: at the left end a force and a couple, at the right end a force. These are three unknowns, while the equilibrium of the beam gives only two equations (the sum of vertical forces and the sum of moments about a chosen point, chapter 2). The beam is statically indeterminate (statično nedoločen), like the truss in section 4.7.5 of chapter 4, where we had to add energy to get the forces in the bars. Here the deflection curve equation suffices: there are four boundary conditions, as many as constants, and we get the reactions as a by-product from \(V\) and \(M\) at the ends.

Theorem 5.58 Deflection curve of a propped cantilever (upogibnica podprte konzole)

A beam of length \(L\) with constant bending stiffness \(EI\) is clamped at \(x = 0\), held by a roller support at \(x = L\) and carries a uniform load \(q = -q_0\), \(q_0 > 0\). The assumptions of beam theory hold and the displacements are small. Then

\[v(x) = -\frac{q_0\,x^2\,(3L - 2x)\,(L - x)}{48EI} . \tag{5.58}\]

The moment is \(M(x) = -\tfrac18 q_0\,(L - x)(L - 4x)\) and the shear force \(V(x) = q_0 x - \tfrac58 q_0 L\). The reactions are: at the clamp a force \(\tfrac58 q_0 L\) upward and a couple \(\tfrac18 q_0 L^2\) in the positive sense (counterclockwise), at the roller support a force \(\tfrac38 q_0 L\) upward. The largest deflection is at \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = \tfrac{15 - \sqrt{33}}{16}\,L\) and the slope at the right end is \(v'(L) = q_0 L^3/(48EI)\).

Proof

We must show that the four boundary conditions determine the constants of lemma 5.55 with \(q = -q_0\) uniquely, and that the reactions and the largest deflection then follow from them.

  1. Fixed end: \(C_0 = EI\,v(0) = 0\) and \(C_1 = EI\,v'(0) = 0\).
  2. Roller without moment: \(M(L) = 0\). By (5.53), \(-\tfrac12 q_0 L^2 + C_3 L + C_2 = 0\), so \(C_3 L + C_2 = \tfrac12 q_0 L^2\).
  3. Roller: \(v(L) = 0\). The fourth row of (5.52) with \(C_0 = C_1 = 0\) gives \(-\tfrac1{24} q_0 L^4 + \tfrac16 C_3 L^3 + \tfrac12 C_2 L^2 = 0\). Divide by \(L^2\) and multiply by 24: \(4 C_3 L + 12 C_2 = q_0 L^2\).
  4. From step 2, \(C_2 = \tfrac12 q_0 L^2 - C_3 L\). Insert in step 3: \(4 C_3 L + 6 q_0 L^2 - 12 C_3 L = q_0 L^2\), so \(-8 C_3 L = -5 q_0 L^2\) and \(C_3 = \tfrac58 q_0 L\).
  5. Then \(C_2 = \tfrac12 q_0 L^2 - \tfrac58 q_0 L^2 = -\tfrac18 q_0 L^2\).
  6. Fourth row: \(EI\,v = -\tfrac1{24} q_0 x^4 + \tfrac5{48} q_0 L\,x^3 - \tfrac1{16} q_0 L^2 x^2 = -\tfrac1{48} q_0\,x^2\,(2x^2 - 5L\,x + 3L^2)\). Because \(2x^2 - 5Lx + 3L^2 = (2x - 3L)(x - L) = (3L - 2x)(L - x)\), this is (5.58).
  7. By (5.53), \(M(x) = -\tfrac12 q_0 x^2 + \tfrac58 q_0 L\,x - \tfrac18 q_0 L^2 = -\tfrac18 q_0 (4x^2 - 5L\,x + L^2)\), so \(M(x) = -\tfrac18 q_0 (L - x)(L - 4x)\) and \(V(x) = q_0 x - \tfrac58 q_0 L\).
  8. At the clamp, \(V(0) = -\tfrac58 q_0 L\) and \(M(0) = -\tfrac18 q_0 L^2\). The beam is acted on there by the force \(-V(0) = \tfrac58 q_0 L\) upward and the couple \(-M(0) = \tfrac18 q_0 L^2\) in the positive sense (section 5.7.2). At the roller support, \(V(L) = q_0 L - \tfrac58 q_0 L = \tfrac38 q_0 L\), which is the force of the roller on the beam, upward.
  9. By the third row of (5.52) with the constants inserted, \(EI\,v'(x) = -\tfrac16 q_0 x^3 + \tfrac5{16} q_0 L\,x^2 - \tfrac18 q_0 L^2 x = -\tfrac1{48} q_0\,x\,(8x^2 - 15L\,x + 6L^2)\). The zeros of the quadratic part are \(x = L\,(15 \pm \sqrt{33})/16\). The larger lies beyond \(L\), because \((15 + \sqrt{33})/16 = 1{,}296 > 1\). The smaller, \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = L\,(15 - \sqrt{33})/16\), is the only stationary point on \((0, L)\). By (5.58), \(v < 0\) everywhere on \((0, L)\), because \(x^2\), \(3L - 2x\) and \(L - x\) are positive there, and \(v(0) = v(L) = 0\). A continuous function on \([0, L]\) has a smallest value (theorem 1.73), which is negative and therefore lies inside, where \(v' = 0\), that is at \(x_{\mathrm{m}}\). This is the largest deflection downward. Slope at \(x = L\): \(EI\,v'(L) = -\tfrac1{48} q_0 L^3 (8 - 15 + 6) = \tfrac1{48} q_0 L^3\).

Source: EP, example 4.4.3, p. 201-202 (equation p202-b8: \(F_{\mathrm{s}} = -3q_0 L/8\) with the assumed direction downward, so in fact \(3q_0 L/8\) upward, and \(\theta(0) = -q_0 L^3/(48EI)\) clockwise, so the slope is \(+q_0 L^3/(48EI)\)) and example 4.3.5, p. 192-194 (there by the principle of minimum total potential energy, not virtual forces). Example 4.4.3 uses virtual forces and states that its result agrees with 4.3.5. Checked with SymPy.

For \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m and \(L = 2\) m the reactions are \(\tfrac58 \cdot 1000 \cdot 2 = 1250\) N and \(\tfrac38 \cdot 1000 \cdot 2 = 750\) N upward and the couple at the clamp is \(\tfrac18 \cdot 1000 \cdot 4 = 500\) N m. We check them with the equilibrium of the whole beam, which we did not use: the sum of vertical forces is \(1250 + 750 - 1000 \cdot 2 = 0\), and the sum of moments about \(x = 0\) is \(500 + 750 \cdot 2 - 2000 \cdot 1 = 0\) N m, where the force of the roller support acts upward at the arm 2 m and the weight 2000 N downward at the arm 1 m. Both equations indeed hold, which is a test that the boundary conditions were written correctly.

The largest deflection is at \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = 0{,}5785\,L = 1{,}157\) m and is \(v(x_{\mathrm{m}}) = -4{,}145\) mm, which is 0,21 % of the length. The slope at the roller support is \(+q_0 L^3/(48EI) = 8000/(48 \cdot 20\,906{,}7) = +0{,}007972\), and the axis turns there in the positive sense, that is upward. The shape of the deflection curve has its reason in the moment (figure 5.56). The moment \(M(x) = -\tfrac18 q_0 (L - x)(L - 4x)\) is \(-500\) N m at the clamp, which means the upper edge is in tension, it has a zero at \(x = L/4 = 0{,}5\) m and is positive after it, with a peak \(M(5L/8) = \tfrac{9}{128} q_0 L^2 = 281{,}25\) N m at \(x = 1{,}25\) m, where \(V = 0\), and zero at the roller support. The curvature \(v'' = M/(EI)\) therefore changes sign at \(x = 0{,}5\) m (this is an inflection point (prevoj), a point where the curve passes from curving downward to curving upward): until there the axis bends downward as in the cantilever, then upward as in the simply supported beam. Thanks to the clamp the descent is only 42 % of the descent of the simply supported beam with the same load (4,145 against 9,965 mm), although the largest moment, 500 N m, is the same.

The conclusion matters. The deflection curve equation and four boundary conditions solved a beam that equilibrium alone does not determine, by the same means as the two statically determinate examples: four integrations and four linear equations. No additional principle was needed. This is possible because the deflection curve equation contains how the beam deforms (the relation \(M = EI\,v''\)), and not only equilibrium. Equilibrium alone does not suffice when there are more supports than a rigid body needs: then several sets of reactions are in equilibrium, and the condition \(v(L) = 0\) picks the one for which the beam does not move above the roller.

5.9.5 Addition of solutions (seštevanje rešitev)

Take the cantilever from the first section, loaded at once by its self weight and a force of 500 N at the end. Must we solve the equation again? No, because the equation is linear, as it was for the body (lemma 4.134 in chapter 4). Here we show this directly for the deflection curve equation.

Lemma 5.59 Addition of deflection curves (seštevanje upogibnic)

Let \(EI > 0\) be a number and \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) numbers. A boundary condition is an equation \(B(v) = g\), where \(B(v)\) is one of the quantities \(v(0)\), \(v'(0)\), \(M(0) = EI\,v''(0)\), \(V(0) = -EI\,v'''(0)\) or the same quantity at \(x = L\), and \(g\) is a given number. Let \(v_1\) be a four times continuously differentiable solution of the equation \(EI\,v_1'''' = q_1\) and \(v_2\) a solution of the equation \(EI\,v_2'''' = q_2\) on \((0, L)\), where \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) are continuous functions, and let \(v_1\) satisfy four boundary conditions \(B_{\mathrm{k}}(v_1) = g^{(1)}_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(v_2\) the same four boundary conditions with right-hand sides \(g^{(2)}_{\mathrm{k}}\), \(k = 1, \dots, 4\). Then \(\alpha\,v_1 + \beta\,v_2\) is a solution of the equation \(EI\,v'''' = \alpha q_1 + \beta q_2\) with the boundary conditions \(B_{\mathrm{k}}(v) = \alpha\,g^{(1)}_{\mathrm{k}} + \beta\,g^{(2)}_{\mathrm{k}}\).

Proof

We must show that the equation and the boundary conditions are linear in \(v\).

  1. The derivative of a linear combination is the linear combination of derivatives (lemma 1.81), so \(EI\,(\alpha v_1 + \beta v_2)'''' = \alpha\,EI\,v_1'''' + \beta\,EI\,v_2'''' = \alpha q_1 + \beta q_2\).
  2. By the same rule each of the quantities \(v(0)\), \(v'(0)\), \(EI\,v''(0)\), \(-EI\,v'''(0)\) and their values at \(L\) is linear in \(v\), so \(B(\alpha v_1 + \beta v_2) = \alpha B(v_1) + \beta B(v_2) = \alpha g^{(1)} + \beta g^{(2)}\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. For the body: lemma 4.134, chapter 4.

For the cantilever, by the lemma: the boundary conditions are \(v(0) = v'(0) = 0\) for both solutions, \(V(L) = F_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M(L) = M_0\), where the right-hand sides of the sum are the sum of the right-hand sides. The solution for the force 500 N (part 1 of theorem 5.56) has \(V(L) = -500\) N, the solution for the self weight (part 2) has \(V(L) = 0\). Their sum has \(V(L) = -500\) N, and this is the cantilever with both loads. The descent of the end is (figure 5.57)

\[v(L) = -7{,}972\ \text{mm} - 0{,}366\ \text{mm} = -8{,}338\ \text{mm} .\]

The same holds for \(M = EI\,v''\): at the clamp \(M(0) = -500\ \text{N m} - \tfrac12 \cdot 61{,}21 \cdot 1^2\ \text{N m} = -530{,}6\) N m and the stress at the top is \(530{,}6 \cdot 0{,}02/(1{,}0667\cdot 10^{-7}) = 99{,}5\) MPa. The self weight adds 6 % of stress and 4,6 % of descent, and is therefore small for this strip, although not zero.

Addition holds also for the end of a cantilever loaded by a force and a couple. Let the cantilever be as before, and let a force \(F_{\mathrm{y}}\) (positive upward) and a couple \(M_0\) (positive in the sense of the positive moment) act at the free end. The solution for the force alone is, by part 1 of theorem 5.56 with \(F = -F_{\mathrm{y}}\), and for the couple alone it follows from lemma 5.55 at \(q = 0\) with the conditions \(C_0 = C_1 = 0\), \(C_3 = -V(L) = 0\), \(C_2 = M(L) = M_0\) at once \(EI\,v = \tfrac12 M_0 x^2\). The sum is

\[v(L) = \frac{F_{\mathrm{y}}\,L^3}{3EI} + \frac{M_0\,L^2}{2EI} , \qquad v'(L) = \frac{F_{\mathrm{y}}\,L^2}{2EI} + \frac{M_0\,L}{EI} . \tag{5.59}\]

In matrix form this says that the deflection and the slope of the end are a linear function of the force and the couple, with the flexibility matrix (podajnostna matrika) of the end (definition 4.138, here for the pair force and couple):

\[\begin{bmatrix} v(L) \\ v'(L) \end{bmatrix} = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} L^3/(3EI) & L^2/(2EI) \\ L^2/(2EI) & L/(EI) \end{bmatrix}}_{\mat{D}} \begin{bmatrix} F_{\mathrm{y}} \\ M_0 \end{bmatrix} . \tag{5.60}\]

For \(L = 1\) m and \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\), \(\mat D = \begin{bmatrix} 1{,}594 & 2{,}392 \\ 2{,}392 & 4{,}783 \end{bmatrix}\cdot 10^{-5}\), in units m/N, 1/N and 1/(N m) in turn. The matrix is symmetric: \(D_{12} = D_{21} = L^2/(2EI)\). The deflection of the end due to a couple of 1 N m equals the slope of the end due to a force of 1 N, \(2{,}392\cdot 10^{-5}\) in both. This is the same symmetry as in Maxwell's theorem (theorem 4.140), which is proved for forces on patches. For the pair force and couple it is checked here by calculation, and later in this chapter we will prove it in general from the energy.

An example of use: the end of the cantilever is rigidly tied to the rest of the structure, which does not let it rotate, \(v'(L) = 0\), and the force \(F = 500\) N acts downward on it. Which couple \(M_0\) holds it? From the second row of (5.59) with \(F_{\mathrm{y}} = -500\) N, \(-\tfrac12 \cdot 500 \cdot 1^2 + M_0 \cdot 1 = 0\), so \(M_0 = 250\) N m. The descent of the end is then \(v(L) = -500/(3EI) + 250/(2EI)\) in meters, which is \(-\tfrac{1}{12} \cdot 500/EI = -1{,}993\) mm, four times less than for the free end. We will need the same calculation for structures of several joined beams, later in this chapter, where a connection of the same kind holds the end of a beam.

Finally let us answer the question of the section: are the descents small enough for the model? The model assumes small slopes. The curvature we took as \(v''\) is, by SADD, p. 578, exactly \(v''/(1 + v'^2)^{3/2}\), we replace it by \(v''\), and the relative error is therefore \((1 + v'^2)^{3/2} - 1 \approx \tfrac32\,v'^2\) at the largest slope (section 5.3).

beam \(\lvert v \rvert_{\max}\) \(\lvert v \rvert_{\max}/L\) \(\lvert v' \rvert_{\max}\) error
cantilever, \(F\), \(L = 1\) m 7,972 mm 0,80 % 0,01196 \(2{,}1\cdot 10^{-4}\)
simply supported, \(q_0\), \(L = 2\) m 9,965 mm 0,50 % 0,01594 \(3{,}8\cdot 10^{-4}\)
simply supported, \(F\) at the middle 7,972 mm 0,40 % 0,01196 \(2{,}1\cdot 10^{-4}\)
propped cantilever, \(q_0\), \(L = 2\) m 4,145 mm 0,21 % 0,00797 \(9{,}5\cdot 10^{-5}\)

In all of them the error is below 0,04 %, much less than the uncertainty of the modulus of elasticity of steel. The deflection is, even at the largest descent, less than 1 % of the length and the slope less than 0,016. The assumption of small displacements therefore holds for all three beams with a large margin. For a cantilever ten times longer with the same force the descent would grow a thousand times (\(L^3\)), to 7,97 m, and the slope a hundred times, to 1,2. Then the error of the curvature would be about 280 % and the model would no longer hold. Where the limits of beam theory lie, we show later in this chapter.
