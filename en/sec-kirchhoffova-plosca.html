Chapter 18 Plates

18.1 The Kirchhoff plate

The beam of chapter 5 is a slender body: one measure, the length, is much larger than the other two, and so we describe it by a function of one variable, the deflection of its axis. A surface structure has one measure, the thickness, much smaller than the other two (section 5.1.1). If it is flat, we call it a plate (plošča). The floor of a storey, the cover of a ditch, the sheet metal of a car body and the wing of an aircraft are plates. We ask the same question as for the beam, now for two dimensions. How do we describe a plate by a function of two variables, how do its points move, which forces does a cross-section of the plate carry, and what is the stress that belongs to these forces? This section answers these questions. From them we shall derive, in the rest of the chapter, the equation that determines the deflection.

One example accompanies all the calculations: a steel plate with a square plan of side \(a = 500\) mm and thickness \(h = 10\) mm. The material is the steel of the book, \(E = 196\) GPa and \(\nu = 0{,}3\) (section 3.1.2). The plate is thus fifty times wider than it is thick, \(a/h = 50\). The load \(q = 10\) kPa, a force of \(10\,000\) N on every square metre, will be introduced in the next section. All results of the chapter are linear in the load.

18.1.1 The plate

Let us draw what we are talking about (figure 18.1). For visibility we drew the thickness far too large, in reality \(h/a = 0{,}02\).

We start with two parallel planes, the upper and the lower, set apart by the thickness \(h\). The body between them is the plate, bounded in plan by its sides. The plane halfway between them is the middle plane, drawn dashed. In it we place the axes: \(x\) and \(y\) lie in the middle plane, \(z\) is perpendicular to it and points up. The triple stays right-handed, as in the earlier chapters, and \(z\) is the same direction as \(y\) for the beam, where we measured the height upwards. The plan has measures \(a\) and \(b\), the thickness is \(h\). The name comes from everyday speech: a plate is something flat, that is thin and wide, like a stone slab or a plate on the floor. We choose the middle plane because the stresses, as the theorem below will show, are odd functions of the height \(z\) above it.

Definition 18.1 Plate (plošča)

Let \(\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^2\) be a bounded open set of the plane \(xy\) whose boundary consists of finitely many smooth curves, and let \(h > 0\) be a number. A plate is the body

\[P = \{ (x, y, z) : (x, y) \in \Omega,\ -h/2 \le z \le h/2 \} . \tag{18.1}\]

The set \(\Omega\) is the middle plane (srednja ravnina) of the plate, taken as the set of points with \(z = 0\), the number \(h\) is the thickness (debelina), the planes \(z = \pm h/2\) are the faces (ploskvi), and \(\partial\Omega \times [-h/2, h/2]\) is the edge of the plate. By \(a\) we denote a characteristic measure of the plan: for a rectangle the shorter side, for a circle the radius (the sources measure the diameter, so for a circle our limit is twice as strict as theirs). The plate is thin if \(h/a \le 0{,}05\). It is loaded by a load perpendicular to the middle plane and by forces and moments on the edge.

Source: VK (PDF p. 6) describes a plate as a flat structure between two parallel planes with a small thickness against the other measures, and the load mostly perpendicular to it. The limit of a thin plate \(h/a \le 0{,}05\) is in ONATE2 (PDF p. 268), \(t/L \le 0{,}05\). EP (p. 392) writes that a plate is thin if the ratio of thickness to the smallest in-plane measure is \(20\) or more, which is a misprint: at a ratio of thickness to measure of \(20\) the plate would be thicker than it is wide. The meaning EP intends is \(a/h \ge 20\) and it coincides with the limit \(h/a \le 0{,}05\) when \(a\) is the smallest in-plane measure. ONATE2 measures \(L\) as the average side, which for a square is the same measure. For a circle the smallest measure is the diameter, while our \(a\) is the radius, so for a circle our limit is stricter than both sources.

We took the limit \(h/a \le 0{,}05\) from two sources and explain it in the part on idealizations below. Our plate satisfies it with room to spare: \(h/a = 0{,}02\), that is \(a/h = 50\). The beam of chapter 5 has thus become flat in this chapter: there \(h\) was the height of the cross-section, small against the length, here it is small against both measures of the plan.

18.1.2 Kirchhoff idealizations and the displacement field

For the beam we used the model of Euler and Bernoulli: cross-sections stay plane and perpendicular to the axis (definition 5.14). We use the same idea for the plate, where what stays straight is a straight line perpendicular to the middle plane. We call it the normal (normala) of the middle plane: the segment \(\{(x, y, z) : |z| \le h/2\}\) at constant \(x\), \(y\) (normal in figure 18.2).

Look at figure 18.2. Grey is the plate before loading: the middle plane is dashed, the axis \(x\) horizontal, \(z\) up. The point \(A\) on the middle plane and the point \(B\) above it on the upper face lie on the same normal. After bending the middle plane curves into the curve \(z = w(x)\), the faces curve with it, and the normal stays a straight line perpendicular to the curved middle plane. It therefore rotates through an angle equal to the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) of the middle plane (for a small slope, as we had for the beam). The point \(B\) therefore moves horizontally by the distance \(z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\) towards smaller \(x\), where \(z\) is the height of the point above the middle plane, and vertically by \(w\). These are the displacements that we write down in the theorem below.

Before we write them down, we must say what we demand of the model. For the beam we listed the conditions in a definition, and here we do the same.

Definition 18.2 Kirchhoff plate (Kirchhoffova plošča)

Let the plate \(P\) of definition 18.1 be made of a homogeneous, isotropic, linearly elastic material with modulus of elasticity \(E\) and Poisson's ratio \(\nu\), \(-1 < \nu < \tfrac12\) (definitions 3.1 and 3.2). A Kirchhoff plate has five idealizations.

  1. It is thin: \(h/a \le 0{,}05\).
  2. Deflections are small: the displacement of a point of the middle plane in the direction \(z\), the deflection \(w(x, y)\), is at most \(h/5\) in absolute value, and the square of the slope is small, \(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{y}}^2 \le 10^{-2}\).
  3. Normals stay straight and perpendicular to the middle plane and keep their length: at interior points of the plate \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xz}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yz}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\).
  4. The middle plane does not move in its own plane: \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = u_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) at \(z = 0\).
  5. The stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\) is zero, every layer \(z = \text{constant}\) is in a plane stress state (definition 11.32), and its in-plane strains follow from Hooke's law (theorem 3.11) with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\).

Source: VK (PDF pp. 12-13) lists six Kirchhoff assumptions, of which our 1, 3, 4 and 5 are the same (VK 4: straight normals of preserved length, VK 5: the stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}}\) is neglected, VK 6: the middle plane stays unstretched). Assumption 2 is only qualitative in VK (small deflections), and the limits \(h/5\) and \(10^{-2}\) we derive below. ONATE2 (PDF pp. 267-268) says that the condition of straight and perpendicular normals holds only for thin plates. EP (pp. 427-428) combines the assumptions into the displacement field (7.2.78) for pure bending.

Let us name what these idealizations mean. The first says that the plate is much wider than it is thick, so that a straight line across the thickness can be treated as a normal of the middle plane at all: the thicker the plate, the more the normal distorts (ONATE2 PDF p. 268, \(0{,}05 \le t/L < 0{,}10\) is a moderately thick plate and \(t/L \ge 0{,}10\) a very thick one). The third is the same conclusion as for the beam: the model describes the deformation across the thickness by three numbers, \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(w\), instead of an unknown function. The fifth says what still carries stress: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\), which are linear in \(z\), as we shall see. The idealizations are only an approximation and we shall test them with numbers at the end of the section.

By the third idealization we neglect the stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yz}}\) in the deformation, but they are not zero: the transverse shear forces need them (section 18.2). The Euler-Bernoulli beam had the same contradiction (section 5.3.4). The way out is the same: we get the stress from equilibrium, not from Hooke's law.

Theorem 18.3 Displacement field of a plate (polje pomikov plošče)

Let the plate \(P\) be a Kirchhoff plate (definition 18.2) and let its displacement field \((u_{\mathrm{x}}, u_{\mathrm{y}}, u_{\mathrm{z}})\) have continuous partial derivatives of first and second order at interior points of the plate. Then there is a function \(w(x, y)\) on \(\Omega\) with continuous partial derivatives up to second order such that at every interior point of the plate

\[u_{\mathrm{x}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{x}} , \qquad u_{\mathrm{y}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{y}} , \qquad u_{\mathrm{z}} = w(x, y) , \tag{18.2}\]

where the index denotes a partial derivative (\(w_{\mathrm{x}} = \partial w/\partial x\)). The nonzero strain components are

\[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} , \qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{yy}} , \qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xy}} . \tag{18.3}\]
Proof

We must show that \(u_{\mathrm{z}}\) does not depend on \(z\), that \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(u_{\mathrm{y}}\) depend linearly on \(z\) with coefficients \(-w_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(-w_{\mathrm{y}}\), and that the strains follow from this. We use corollary 1.85 on a function with zero derivative, on segments in the interior of the plate along lines parallel to the axis \(z\), as in the proof of theorem 5.15.

  1. From \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = \partial u_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial z = 0\) (idealization 3), by corollary 1.85 on the segment from \((x, y, 0)\) to \((x, y, z)\), it follows that \(u_{\mathrm{z}}(x, y, z) = u_{\mathrm{z}}(x, y, 0) =: w(x, y)\). The function \(w\) is the restriction of \(u_{\mathrm{z}}\) to the plane \(z = 0\) and so has continuous derivatives up to second order.
  2. Since \(u_{\mathrm{z}} = w(x, y)\) for every \(z\), we have \(\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial x = w_{\mathrm{x}}\) at every point. From \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xz}} = \tfrac12(\partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial z + \partial u_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial x) = 0\) (equation (1.63), idealization 3) it follows that \(\partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial z = -w_{\mathrm{x}}(x, y)\).
  3. The function \(u_{\mathrm{x}} + z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\) has the derivative \(\partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial z + w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) with respect to \(z\). By corollary 1.85 it is therefore equal to its value at \(z = 0\), that is \(u_{\mathrm{x}}(x, y, 0) = 0\) (idealization 4). Hence \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\). In the same way \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yz}} = 0\) gives \(u_{\mathrm{y}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{y}}\).
  4. We get the strains from equation (1.63): \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial x = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = \tfrac12(\partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial y + \partial u_{\mathrm{y}}/\partial x) = -\tfrac12 z\,(w_{\mathrm{xy}} + w_{\mathrm{yx}}) = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xy}}\), because the mixed derivatives are equal (theorem 8.30). The components \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xz}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yz}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}}\) are zero by idealization 3.
  5. Rotation of the normal: by definition 1.116, \(\omega_{\mathrm{xz}} = \tfrac12(\partial u_{\mathrm{x}}/\partial z - \partial u_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial x) = -w_{\mathrm{x}}\) and similarly \(\omega_{\mathrm{yz}} = -w_{\mathrm{y}}\). The size of the rotation of the normal is therefore the slope of the middle plane, as figure 18.2 shows.

Source: EP (7.2.78), (7.2.79) (p. 428), VK (2.1)-(2.6) (PDF pp. 22-24), ONATE2 (5.5), (5.7) (PDF pp. 267-268). VK writes \(w\) positive downwards, so the displacements in VK (2.2) have the opposite sign, but are the same in substance.

Idealization 2 now has its meaning. We need the deflection \(w \le h/5\) and the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{y}}^2 \le 10^{-2}\) so that the middle plane is really unstretched (idealization 4). How well this holds, we test on the example. Let the plate be simply supported along its edges (the points of the edges cannot move sideways) and bend into \(w = w_0 \sin(\pi x/a)\). Then the middle plane stretches, because the curve is longer than its chord: the stretch of a longitudinal line is about \(\tfrac12 w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\), which averaged over \(x\) becomes \(\tfrac14 (\pi w_0/a)^2\). The bending strain on the face is by equation (18.3) \(\tfrac h2\,w_0\,(\pi/a)^2\). The ratio of the two is \(w_0/(2h)\). For our plate with \(w_0 = 0{,}014\,h\) (\(w_0 = 0{,}14\) mm, the deflection of the centre of the square in section 18.5) the ratio is \(0{,}007\) or \(0{,}7\) %. For larger deflections we compute the ratio in the table (NumPy, the ratio is exactly \(w_0/(2h)\)). The columns are: the deflection of the centre \(w_0\) in units of \(h\), the largest slope, the mean stretch of the middle plane, the bending strain of the face and the ratio of the two.

\(w_0/h\) largest slope \(\pi w_0/a\) mean stretch of the middle plane bending strain of the face ratio
\(0{,}014\) \(8{,}8\cdot 10^{-4}\) \(1{,}93\cdot 10^{-7}\) \(2{,}76\cdot 10^{-5}\) \(0{,}007\)
\(0{,}1\) \(6{,}3\cdot 10^{-3}\) \(9{,}87\cdot 10^{-6}\) \(1{,}97\cdot 10^{-4}\) \(0{,}05\)

The table says that at \(w_0 = h/5\) the stretch of the middle plane (\(3{,}95\cdot 10^{-5}\)) counts for \(10\) % of the bending strain (\(3{,}95\cdot 10^{-4}\)). That is the limit we took in idealization 2: at it we neglect ten percent of the strain. At \(w_0 = h\) the stretch would already be half of the bending strain and the model would be wrong. How wrong, we do not compute here. Large deflections are described by the theory of von Kármán, which the book does not treat: VK (PDF p. 11) says that the final form of the equation of large deflections was developed by von Kármán. The slope at \(w_0 = h/5\) is still well below the limit: \(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2 = 1{,}6\cdot 10^{-4} \le 10^{-2}\).

Theorem 18.3 gives us all the strains from one function. The function that records how the plate curves, we name.

Definition 18.4 Curvatures of a plate (ukrivljenosti plošče)

Let \(w\) be the deflection of a Kirchhoff plate, a twice continuously differentiable function on \(\Omega\) (theorem 18.3). The curvatures of a plate are the functions of the point \((x, y)\)

\[\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} = w_{\mathrm{xx}} , \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm{y}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} , \qquad \kappa_{\mathrm{xy}} = w_{\mathrm{xy}} , \tag{18.4}\]

each with the unit \(\mathrm{m}^{-1}\), each giving one number at every point. \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\) is the curvature of the curve \(w(\cdot, y)\) in the section plane \(y = \text{constant}\), \(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}\) of the curve \(w(x, \cdot)\), and \(\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is the twist (zvitje): the rate of change of the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) in the direction \(y\).

Source: EP (7.2.79) (p. 428) has the derivatives \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{yy}}\), \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) in the strains, VK (2.7) (PDF p. 24) has curvatures with the opposite sign, because VK counts \(w\) downwards. Our \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\) is as for the beam in definition 5.16, \(\kappa = v''\).

We see the curvature in the direction \(x\) in figure 18.2: the curve is a section of the middle plane, its second derivative is \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\). The curvature in the direction \(y\) is the same picture in the section \(x = \text{constant}\). The twist \(\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is new, the beam has none, and so it has its own moment, as we shall see. With \(\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}} \ne 0\) the plate twists like a piece of cardboard pulled up at two diagonal corners and down at the other two (section 18.2). For a small slope (\(w_{\mathrm{x}}^2 + w_{\mathrm{y}}^2 \le 10^{-2}\)) \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\) is an approximation of the exact curvature \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}/(1 + w_{\mathrm{x}}^2)^{3/2}\), which differs from it by less than \(1{,}5\) % (relatively \(\tfrac32 w_{\mathrm{x}}^2 \le 1{,}5\cdot 10^{-2}\)), as for the beam (definition 5.16).

18.1.3 Moments and stresses

Now we compute the stresses from the strains and from them the forces in the cross-section. For the beam the cross-section is a segment or a rectangle and the forces in it are three numbers: the axial force, the shear force and the moment (lemma 5.9). For the plate the section with the plane \(x = \text{constant}\) is a rectangle of height \(h\) and arbitrary width, and we measure the forces along it per unit length. In figure 18.3 let us see which parts of the stress give the moments.

Each panel shows a piece of plate (panel (c) its face), with the thickness enlarged for the drawing, and its middle plane, drawn dashed. Panel (a) shows the section of the plate with the plane \(y = \text{constant}\), with the axis \(x\) to the right and \(z\) up, and a piece cut at \(x\) and \(x + 3\) (drawing units). On each of the two faces of the piece acts the longitudinal stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\): above it presses on the face, below it pulls, and the arrows are longer the farther from the middle plane they lie. The sum of these forces over the height is zero, because the stress is an odd function of \(z\), and what remains is a pair of forces, that is a moment. We denote it \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\). In panel (b) is the same conclusion in the section \(x = \text{constant}\), with the axis \(y\) to the right, with the stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) and the moment \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\). In panel (c) we look at the face \(x = \text{constant}\) from the front, with the axis \(y\) to the right and \(z\) up: the shear stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is directed one way above and the opposite way below, and their pair is the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\). The shear stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yz}}\) give the transverse forces, which we introduce in the next section.

Definition 18.5 Moments of a plate (momenti plošče)

Let \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) be continuous functions of the variables \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) on a plate of thickness \(h\) (definition 18.1). The bending moments (upogibna momenta) \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) and the twisting moment (zvojni moment) \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) of the plate are functions of the point \((x, y)\) of the middle plane

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} = -\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}z , \quad M_{\mathrm{y}} = -\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\mathrm{d}z , \quad M_{\mathrm{xy}} = -\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\mathrm{d}z . \tag{18.5}\]

They have the unit N m/m: a moment per unit length of the edge, since the integral is over the height and not over the width. A positive \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) means compression above the middle plane and tension below it, like the positive moment of the beam \(M = -\int y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) (lemma 5.9). The longitudinal forces per unit length of the edge are \(N_{\mathrm{x}} = \int \sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}z\) and \(N_{\mathrm{y}} = \int \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\,\mathrm{d}z\), the in-plane shear force \(N_{\mathrm{xy}} = \int \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\,\mathrm{d}z\).

Source: EP (7.2.83) (p. 429) has \(M_{\mathrm{xx}} = \int \sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,z\,\mathrm{d}z\) without the minus, so its sign is opposite to ours. VK (2.12) (PDF p. 26) has \(z\) downwards and a positive moment for tensile stresses in the lower half of the plate, which is the same physical sign as ours (compression above, tension below). The moment of the beam \(M = -\int y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) (lemma 5.9) has the same sign.

Let us say specifically why we took the minus. For the beam we took \(M = -\int y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) so that \(M = EI\,v''\) has no minus (theorem 5.35). We shall get the same for the plate: \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) when \(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\). The naming: bending, because \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) bend the plate, twisting, because \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) twists the plate, as we shall see in the example. The index of \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) is the index of the stress from which the moment comes, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), not the axis about which the moment turns. The moment \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) turns about the axis \(y\).

Before we compute the moments, we need a quantity that says how much moment belongs to a curvature, like \(EI\) for the beam.

Definition 18.6 Flexural rigidity of a plate (upogibna togost plošče)

Let a plate of thickness \(h\) be made of a homogeneous isotropic material with modulus of elasticity \(E\) and Poisson's ratio \(\nu\), \(-1 < \nu < \tfrac12\). The flexural rigidity of a plate is the number

\[D = \frac{E\,h^3}{12\,(1 - \nu^2)} , \tag{18.6}\]

with the unit N m.

Source: VK (2.14) (PDF p. 27), EP (7.2.89) (p. 430), ONATE2 (5.15) (PDF p. 270). For the beam the corresponding quantity is \(EI\) of definition 5.36, and for a beam of unit width \(I = h^3/12\).

Let us explain each term. The factor \(h^3/12\) is the integral \(\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} z^2\,\mathrm{d}z\) (theorem 3.40, \(\big[z^3/3\big]_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} = h^3/12\)), the second moment of area of a cross-section \(1\) wide and \(h\) high about the middle plane. \(E/(1 - \nu^2)\) is the modulus of a layer in plane stress when the layer cannot contract sideways (theorem 18.7 below). For our example \(D = 196\cdot 10^9\cdot 10^{-6}/(12\cdot 0{,}91) = 17\,948{,}7\) N m, while the rigidity of a beam of unit width is \(EI = E h^3/12 = 16\,333{,}3\) N m. The ratio is \(D/EI = 1/(1 - \nu^2) = 1{,}0989\).

Theorem 18.7 Moments of a plate from curvatures (momenti plošče iz ukrivljenosti)

Let the plate be a Kirchhoff plate (definition 18.2), with strains by theorem 18.3. Then the stresses in the layer \(z\) are

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -\frac{E\,z}{1 - \nu^2}\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}) , \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\frac{E\,z}{1 - \nu^2}\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}) , \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = -\frac{E\,z}{1 + \nu}\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}} , \tag{18.7}\]

the longitudinal forces \(N_{\mathrm{x}} = N_{\mathrm{y}} = N_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\) and the moments

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{y}} = D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}) , \qquad M_{\mathrm{xy}} = D\,(1 - \nu)\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}} . \tag{18.8}\]
Proof

We must show the stresses of a layer from Hooke's law, then the integrals over the thickness. We use idealization 5, that is a layer in plane stress with \(E' = E\) and \(\nu' = \nu\).

  1. By equation (11.43) with \(E' = E\) and \(\nu' = \nu\) we have \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = (\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} - \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}})/E\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = (\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} - \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}})/E\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = (1 + \nu)\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}/E\).
  2. We invert. To the first equation we add \(\nu\) times the second: \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \big( \sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} - \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} + \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} - \nu^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} \big)/E = (1 - \nu^2)\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}/E\). Hence \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \nu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}})/(1 - \nu^2)\) and, swapping \(x \leftrightarrow y\), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = E\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} + \nu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}})/(1 - \nu^2)\). The third equation gives \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = E\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}}/(1 + \nu)\).
  3. We insert the strains of equation (18.3), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = -z\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\). These are the stresses (18.7).
  4. All three stresses are linear in \(z\), so odd functions of \(z\) at constant \(x\), \(y\). The integral of an odd function over \([-h/2, h/2]\) is zero, hence \(N_{\mathrm{x}} = N_{\mathrm{y}} = N_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\).
  5. For the moments we take out the coefficients that do not depend on \(z\) (lemma 3.39) and use \(\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2} z^2\,\mathrm{d}z = h^3/12\): \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = -\int z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}z = \dfrac{E\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}})}{1 - \nu^2}\cdot\dfrac{h^3}{12} = D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}})\). Likewise \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\).
  6. For the twisting moment, \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = \dfrac{E\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}}{1 + \nu}\cdot\dfrac{h^3}{12}\). Since \(\dfrac{E}{1 + \nu} = \dfrac{E\,(1 - \nu)}{1 - \nu^2}\), this is \(D\,(1 - \nu)\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\).

Source: VK (2.10), (2.13) (PDF pp. 26-27), EP (7.2.87)-(7.2.89) (p. 430), ONATE2 (5.13)-(5.15) (PDF p. 270). We compared the signs at definition 18.5 and checked the relations with SymPy.

The theorem says two things. First, the moments \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\) are coupled: \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) depends not only on \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\) but also on \(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}\) through \(\nu\). The transverse curvature therefore changes the moment in the direction \(x\). Second, the twisting moment \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is proportional to the twist. For the beam \(M = EI\,v''\), this is theorem 5.35. For the plate the same theorem splits into three.

Corollary 18.8 Stresses of a plate from moments (napetosti plošče iz momentov)

Let theorem 18.7 hold. Then

\[\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -\frac{12\,M_{\mathrm{x}}}{h^3}\,z , \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\frac{12\,M_{\mathrm{y}}}{h^3}\,z , \qquad \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = -\frac{12\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}}{h^3}\,z , \tag{18.9}\]

and the largest absolute value of each of them at a point of the middle plane, along its normal, is on the faces \(z = \pm h/2\):

\[|\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}|_{\max} = \frac{6\,|M_{\mathrm{x}}|}{h^2} , \qquad |\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}|_{\max} = \frac{6\,|M_{\mathrm{y}}|}{h^2} , \qquad |\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}|_{\max} = \frac{6\,|M_{\mathrm{xy}}|}{h^2} . \tag{18.10}\]
Proof

We must show that \(E/(1 - \nu^2)\) and \(E/(1 + \nu)\) are expressed by \(D\) and that \(|z| \le h/2\).

  1. From \(D = E h^3/(12\,(1 - \nu^2))\) we have \(E/(1 - \nu^2) = 12\,D/h^3\). The stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\) of theorem 18.7 is therefore \(-12\,D\,z\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}})/h^3 = -12\,M_{\mathrm{x}}\,z/h^3\), because \(D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}) = M_{\mathrm{x}}\). Likewise \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\).
  2. For \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\), \(E/(1 + \nu) = 12\,D\,(1 - \nu)/h^3\), and \(-12\,D\,(1 - \nu)\,\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\,z/h^3 = -12\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}\,z/h^3\).
  3. The absolute value \(|z| \le h/2\) is largest at \(|z| = h/2\), where \(12\,|M|\,(h/2)/h^3 = 6\,|M|/h^2\).

Source: Stresses from moments: VK (2.15) (PDF p. 27). The largest stress \(6\,M/h^2\) is the same as for the beam at \(\sigma = M\,h/(2I) = 6\,M/h^2\) for \(I = h^3/12\).

Let us test the corollary on the example. Take our plate, curved only in the direction \(x\) with \(w = -x^2/(2R)\), \(R = 50\) m, so \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -0{,}02\) 1/m, \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) (in the direction \(y\) it is held by the neighbouring parts, which section 18.4 will show in more detail). At \(x = 0{,}25\) m the deflection is \(0{,}625\) mm, that is \(0{,}06\,h\). The strain on the upper face (\(z = h/2 = 5\) mm) is by equation (18.3) \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 1{,}00\cdot 10^{-4}\), tension. The stress by theorem 18.7 is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}/(1 - \nu^2) = 21{,}538\) MPa and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = 6{,}462\) MPa, because \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\). The moment is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -358{,}97\) N m/m and \(M_{\mathrm{y}} = \nu\,M_{\mathrm{x}} = -107{,}69\) N m/m. By the corollary 18.8, \(6\,|M_{\mathrm{x}}|/h^2 = 6\cdot 358{,}97/10^{-4} = 21{,}538\) MPa and \(6\,|M_{\mathrm{y}}|/h^2 = 6{,}462\) MPa, all three calculations agree. The moment is negative because the upper face is in tension: by definition 18.5 the moment is positive for compression above, and here it is just the opposite.

Now the twist: the plate \(w = c\,x\,y\) with \(c = 0{,}01\) 1/m has \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}} = c\). By theorem 18.7 \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = M_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(M_{\mathrm{xy}} = (1 - \nu)\,D\,c = 125{,}64\) N m/m, so by the corollary \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}|_{\max} = 6\,M_{\mathrm{xy}}/h^2 = 7{,}538\) MPa and the shear strain is \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = 2\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}} = 1{,}00\cdot 10^{-4}\) on the face. A plate that has no bending curvature in the direction of the axes thus has a twisting moment. We check correctness in rotated axes: the surface \(x\,y = (s^2 - t^2)/2\) in axes \(s\), \(t\) rotated by \(45^\circ\) has curvatures \(c\) and \(-c\) (SymPy), and the bending moment from the stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{ss}}\) is, by theorem 18.7 written in these axes, \(D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{s}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{t}}) = D\,(1 - \nu)\,c = M_{\mathrm{xy}}\). The twist is thus only bending in oblique directions.

18.1.4 Test: a narrow beam is not a plate

Theorem 18.7 holds for every Kirchhoff plate, but \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}} + \nu\,\kappa_{\mathrm{y}})\) differs from \(EI\,\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\), which the beam has. Let us see when each relation gives the right moment. Take the same steel part, bent with the same curvature \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -0{,}02\) 1/m, but in two cases (figure 18.4).

In panel (a) the body is a narrow beam of width \(b = 10\) mm, of a similar size as \(h\). Its sides are free. Nothing prevents the sideways deformation, so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) in every layer and the upper, stretched layers contract sideways by Poisson's effect by \(-\nu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\), while the lower, compressed layers expand. The section of the beam therefore curves the other way, a phenomenon called anticlastic curvature (antiklastična ukrivljenost, from Greek anti, opposite, and klasis, break: curvature of the opposite sign). In panel (b) is a piece of a wide plate, where the neighbouring parts hold the sides and a layer cannot contract sideways: \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = \nu\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), as in the example above. The same body behaves differently depending on whether it is free or held at the side.

The calculation for the narrow beam. Theorem 18.7 does not hold here: a narrow beam is not a thin plate, since idealization 1 requires \(h/a \le 0{,}05\), while here the width \(b\) equals the thickness \(h\). The narrow beam is the beam of chapter 5 with fibres in uniaxial stress, so \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}} = 0\) in every layer. The fibre at height \(z\) has \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and contracts sideways by \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\nu\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \nu\,z\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) (definition 3.2). A sideways strain linear in \(z\) means a transverse curvature: as for the plate in equation (18.3), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -z\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\), so \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\nu\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = +0{,}006\) 1/m, a radius of curvature of \(166{,}67\) m (SymPy). Across the width \(b = 10\) mm the edge rises relative to the centre by \(\tfrac12\,w_{\mathrm{yy}}\,(b/2)^2 = 0{,}075\ \mu\mathrm{m}\), which is very little and is therefore enlarged in the figure. The moment is given by theorem 5.35, \(M = EI\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) with \(I = b\,h^3/12\). For the whole beam of width \(b = 10\) mm, \(M = -3{,}267\) N m, and per unit width \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = \dfrac{E h^3}{12}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -326{,}67\) N m/m, which is \(D\,(1 - \nu^2)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\). The stress on the upper face is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = E\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = -E\,\tfrac h2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 19{,}600\) MPa, while \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yy}}\) is zero, as the free sides require.

In a plate curved only in the direction \(x\) (\(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), panel (b)) we have \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -358{,}97\) N m/m and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = 21{,}538\) MPa. The ratio of the stresses of the beam and the plate is \(19{,}600/21{,}538 = 0{,}9100 = 1 - \nu^2\). If we computed the narrow beam with the plate formula, we would overestimate the moment and stress by \(1/0{,}91 - 1 = 9{,}9\) %, and if we computed the plate as a beam, we would underestimate them by \(9\) %. This is the test: a plate is not a beam, \(D\) is not \(EI\), and which relation holds is decided by whether the neighbouring parts hold the lateral sides. Where the limit between the two cases lies, we do not compute. For a plate curved only in the direction \(x\), section 18.4 computes the deflection and the moment and compares them with the beam.

18.1.5 What the Kirchhoff model neglects

For the beam, section 5.3.4 listed what the model neglects. Here we do the same for the plate and estimate with numbers how much.

Shear stress. The strains \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yz}}\) are zero, but the stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{yz}}\) are not, since they carry the transverse forces. In the next section we compute them from equilibrium and it turns out that they run across the thickness as a parabola, largest at \(z = 0\) and zero on the faces, as for the beam (theorem 5.39). The mean of the function \(1 - 4z^2/h^2\) across the thickness is \(\tfrac1h\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2}(1 - 4z^2/h^2)\,\mathrm{d}z = \tfrac23\) (SymPy), its largest value \(1\) is therefore one and a half times the mean, and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) at \(z = 0\) is one and a half times the mean stress \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}/h\).

Transverse normal stress. Idealization 5 sets \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\), but by idealization 3 \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\), whereas Hooke's law at \(\sigma_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\) gives \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = -\nu\,(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{yy}})/E\). For the plate of the example above, on the upper face \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = -\nu\,(1 + \nu)\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}/E = -4{,}29\cdot 10^{-5}\). That is \(43\) % of the bending strain \(1{,}00\cdot 10^{-4}\), but it acts only across the thickness: the change of the half-thickness \(h/2 = 5\) mm is on average \(-2{,}14\cdot 10^{-5}\) times \(5\) mm, that is \(-0{,}107\ \mu\mathrm{m}\). That is \(2{,}1\cdot 10^{-5}\) of the half-thickness and \(1{,}7\cdot 10^{-4}\) of the deflection \(0{,}625\) mm. We therefore neglect a strain that is large compared with the bending strain, but it affects the shape of the plate only by a tenth of a micrometre.

Limit of thickness. Idealization 1 is \(h/a \le 0{,}05\). Our plate has \(0{,}02\), so \(a/h = 50\). By ONATE2 (PDF p. 268) the condition of a perpendicular normal holds only up to \(h/a = 0{,}05\). In moderately thick (\(0{,}05 \le h/a < 0{,}10\)) and very thick plates (\(h/a \ge 0{,}10\)) the normal distorts more and more during deformation, and a better approximation is a theory with an independent rotation of the normal (section 18.8). For \(a/h = 5\), \(10\), \(20\) we shall compute later by how much the deflection differs from the Kirchhoff one.

18.1.6 Notation in this chapter

The plate uses letters that the book already has for other quantities. Let us list them so that we do not make a mistake.

  • \(w\) is the deflection of the plate, as for the vibration of the beam in chapter 13. For the static beam (chapter 5) the deflection was \(v\), the same quantity.
  • \(h\) is the thickness of the plate. In chapter 12 \(h\) is the length of an element, so in plate elements we write \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\). In chapter 14 \(h\) is the heat transfer coefficient, and we never use the two together.
  • \(D\) is the flexural rigidity of the plate in N m. In chapter 4 \(D_{\mathrm{ij}}\) is a compliance, always with two indices.
  • \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{xy}}\) are moments per unit length in N m/m, for the beam \(M\) is a moment in N m. The sign is as for the beam: at \(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = D\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and for the beam \(M = EI\,v''\) (equation (5.24)), both positive for compression above.
  • \(Q_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(Q_{\mathrm{y}}\) are transverse forces per unit length in N/m (section 18.2). For the beam the shear force is \(V\) in N, in chapter 14 \(Q\) is a heat source.
  • \(q\) is the load per unit area in N/m\(^2\), for the beam per unit length in N/m.
  • \(\kappa_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\kappa_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(\kappa_{\mathrm{xy}}\) are the curvatures of the plate, for the beam \(\kappa = v''\).
  • \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}}\) will be the rotations of the normals in the Mindlin plate (section 18.8), \(\theta\) is the rotation of the cross-section in the Timoshenko beam. \(k\) is the shear correction factor as for the beam (definition 5.86), in chapter 14 it is conductivity. \(N_{\mathrm{x}}\) is a longitudinal force per unit length in N/m, for the beam the axial force \(N\) in N.

The sign: the axis \(z\) points up, and \(w\) and the load \(q\) are positive in the same direction. This choice keeps the right-handed triple of axes and the sign of the moment as for the beam (\(M > 0\) for compression above). If the load presses downwards, \(q < 0\). Since the results of the chapter are linear in \(q\), every result then has the sign opposite to that for a load of the same size upwards.

In the next section we introduce the transverse forces and the load and from the equilibrium of a piece of plate derive the equation that determines the deflection (section 18.2).
