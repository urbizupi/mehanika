Chapter 1 Strain

1.19 Connections

The concepts of this chapter return in all five subjects. For each concept we say where we will need it again. Subject abbreviations: VT Advanced Strength of Materials (Višja trdnost), MK Mechanics of Structural Elements (Mehanika konstrukcijskih elementov), AK FEM Structural Analysis (Analiza konstrukcij z MKE), VD Advanced Dynamics (Višja dinamika), DSK Dynamics of Machines and Structures (Dinamika strojev in konstrukcij).

  • The displacement field and the small strain tensor: the relation between stress and strain (VT, chapter 3), the elasticity problem with displacements and forces prescribed on the boundary of the body, where the displacements are the unknowns (VT, MK, chapter 11), the strains of bent bars and plates from an assumed displacement field (MK, chapters 5 and 18), the strains in a finite element from the displacements of its nodes (AK, chapters 7 and 15).
  • The deformation gradient and the Green-Lagrange tensor (definitions 1.110 and 1.112), an exact measure of deformation that does not drop the products of partial derivatives: stresses referred to the original shape (VT, chapter 2), large rotations with small strains in flexible robot links, where the small strain tensor would wrongly predict stretching, as for the rotation in this chapter (DSK, chapter 28).
  • The Eulerian description and the Euler-Almansi tensor (theorem 1.120): stress is a force per unit area of a cut in the body after loading, where the forces act. So like the Euler-Almansi tensor it is given with respect to the shape after loading. We carry it over to the shape before loading with the matrices \(\mat F^{-1}\) and \(\mat F^{-\T}\), where \(\mat F\) is the deformation gradient (definition 1.110) (VT, chapter 2).
  • The split into strain and rotation: small rotations in the theory of bent bars and plates (MK, chapters 5 and 18).
  • A displacement determined up to a rigid-body displacement (theorem 1.134 and corollary 1.135): the strains determine the displacement only up to three shifts and three rotations of the body as a whole. The supports of a structure must therefore prevent all six, like the fixing of the hanging bar at the point \(O\), which prevents the displacement and the rotation. In the finite element method the stiffness matrix of a structure (corollary 7.14) without supports is singular, and the system of equations does not have a single solution (COOK §2.6, PDF p. 52) (AK, MK, chapter 7).
  • Bodies with holes: to the compatibility equations we add six conditions for each hole that makes a body multiply connected (BARBER, PDF p. 48), and displacement fields with a jump across the cut describe cracks and dislocations (SADD, PDF p. 54). In the boundary value problem of elasticity (definition 11.1) we find the displacement from the stresses only in a simply connected body (VT, chapter 11).
  • Compatibility conditions: when we solve a problem with stresses or strains instead of displacements, we must require the compatibility equations separately, so that we get a displacement from the solution at all (SADD, PDF p. 54). This is so for the Beltrami-Michell equations (theorem 11.9) and the Airy stress function (VT, chapter 11) and for Prandtl's function in torsion (MK, VD, chapter 16).
  • The change of a field over a short step and the mean value theorem: linearizing the equations of motion about an equilibrium position (VD, chapter 8, DSK, chapter 9), the error estimates of finite elements (AK, chapter 17).
  • The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem and the extreme value theorem: the existence of the plane through a point of a body on which the forces pull the material apart most strongly (chapter 2), and of natural frequencies as the largest and smallest values of the Rayleigh quotient (VD, DSK, chapter 10).
  • Matrices: the stiffness matrix and the assembly of the system of equations (AK, MK, chapter 7), the mass matrix (VD, DSK, chapter 10), the matrices that describe the turning of robot links (DSK, chapter 27).
