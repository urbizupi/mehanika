Chapter 1 Strain

1.19 Connections

The concepts of this chapter return in all five subjects. For each concept we say where we will need it again. Subject abbreviations: VT Advanced Strength of Materials (Višja trdnost), MK Mechanics of Structural Elements (Mehanika konstrukcijskih elementov), AK FEM Structural Analysis (Analiza konstrukcij z MKE), VD Advanced Dynamics (Višja dinamika), DSK Dynamics of Machines and Structures (Dinamika strojev in konstrukcij).

  • The displacement field and the small strain tensor: the relation between stress and strain (VT, chapter 3), the boundary value problem of elasticity, where the displacements are the unknowns (VT, MK, chapter 11), the strains of beams and plates from an assumed displacement field (MK, chapters 5 and 18), the strains in a finite element from the displacements of its nodes (AK, chapters 7 and 15).
  • The deformation gradient and the Green-Lagrange tensor: stresses referred to the original shape (VT, chapter 2), large rotations with small strains in flexible robot links, where the small strain tensor would wrongly predict stretching, as for the rotation in this chapter (DSK, chapter 28).
  • The Eulerian description and the Euler-Almansi tensor: stress is a force per unit area of a cut in the body after loading, so like the Euler-Almansi tensor it refers to the shape after loading, and the matrices \(\mat F^{-1}\) and \(\mat F^{-\T}\) carry it over to the shape before loading (VT, chapter 2).
  • The split into strain and rotation: small rotations in the theory of beams and plates (MK, chapters 5 and 18).
  • Compatibility conditions: solving with stresses and the Airy stress function (VT, chapter 11), Prandtl's function in torsion (MK, VD, chapter 16).
  • The change of a field over a short step and the mean value theorem: linearizing the equations of motion about an equilibrium position (VD, chapter 8, DSK, chapter 9), the error estimates of finite elements (AK, chapter 17).
  • The Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem and the extreme value theorem: the existence of the plane through a point of a body on which the forces pull the material apart most strongly (chapter 2), and of natural frequencies as the largest and smallest values of the Rayleigh quotient (VD, DSK, chapter 10).
  • Matrices: the stiffness matrix and the assembly of the system of equations (AK, MK, chapter 7), the mass matrix (VD, DSK, chapter 10), the matrices that describe the turning of robot links (DSK, chapter 27).
