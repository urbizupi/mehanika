Chapter 19 Symmetry, periodicity and constraints

19.10 Vibration of a cyclically symmetric structure

The wheel of section 19.9 has its hub at the point \(O\), rigidly clamped, six spokes of length \(1\) m and a rim of six straight beams with chord \(1\) m. All members are steel beams with a \(20 \times 40\) mm cross-section, as in chapter 12 (\(E = 196\) GPa, \(\rho = 7800\) kg/m³), and each member is one finite element. Sector \(j\) (\(j = 0, \dots, 5\)) is rim node \(j\) with the spoke and the rim beam attached to it. Its three unknowns are the radial displacement, the tangential displacement and the rotation, written in the axes of the sector, and we collect them in a column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}}\) (\(n = 3\)). For a static load, cyclic symmetry with the split into harmonics replaced one problem with \(18\) unknowns by six problems with three unknowns. Now we ask whether the same holds for vibration: which frequencies and shapes does the wheel have if we displace it from equilibrium and let go?

The model follows what holds for all vibration in chapter 10. The stiffness matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric and positive definite because the hub is clamped (theorem 7.18), the mass matrix \(\mat M\) is symmetric and positive definite, and we look for eigenpairs \(\mat K\vek\varphi = \lambda\mat M\vek\varphi\) with \(\omega = \sqrt\lambda\) (definition 10.7). This is the undamped vibration of a model with one element per member. The frequencies we quote are those of this model and not directly those of a real wheel, since we did not estimate the error of the coarse mesh. The spokes are \(25\) times longer than the section height of \(40\) mm, so the beam model is appropriate.

In the axes of the sectors both matrices are block circulant by lemma 19.43. For \(\mat K\) this is the lemma, for \(\mat M\) the same proof holds, since the mass matrix is also assembled from identical sectors. Row \(j\) then has off the diagonal only the block \(\mat K_1^\T\) at sector \(j - 1\) and \(\mat K_1\) at sector \(j + 1\), and \(\mat K_0\) on the diagonal (all blocks are \(3 \times 3\) matrices, sector indices are taken modulo \(N = 6\), and \(\mat K_0\) is symmetric). We have \(\mat M_0\) and \(\mat M_1\) in the same way. For every \(k = 0, \dots, N - 1\) and \(w = \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k/N}\) let

\[\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} = \mat K_0 + w\,\mat K_1 + \bar w\,\mat K_1^\T , \qquad \mat M_{\mathrm{k}} = \mat M_0 + w\,\mat M_1 + \bar w\,\mat M_1^\T , \tag{19.19}\]

the sector matrices of section 19.9 (in this section \(k\) is not a stiffness but the number of the harmonic, and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}\) is not the entry \(k\)). We call \(k\) the harmonic because the pattern around the wheel is a discrete sine and cosine that repeats \(k\) times in one turn (definition 9.102).

For vibration the question is whether the wheel can carry waves that run around it, so that every sector equals its neighbour, only shifted in phase. The reason to try is the turn of the wheel: if we turn the wheel by one sector, we get a structure with the same stiffness and mass, hence by Newton's law the same equation of motion, and the turned mode is again a mode with the same frequency. Waves with a constant phase step are the columns that the turn merely multiplies by a number.

Theorem 19.49 Vibration of a cyclic structure by harmonics (nihanje ciklične konstrukcije po harmonikih)

Let \(N \ge 3\), and let the real block circulant matrices \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\) (\(N \times N\) blocks of order \(n\)) have blocks \(\mat K_0, \mat K_1\) and \(\mat M_0, \mat M_1\) as above, with \(\mat K_0\) and \(\mat M_0\) symmetric and \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\) symmetric positive definite. Let \(w_{\mathrm{k}} = \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k/N}\) and let \(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}\), \(\mat M_{\mathrm{k}}\) be the matrices of equation (19.19).

  1. For a number \(\lambda\) and a nonzero complex column \(\vek U\) with \(n\) entries, the column \(\vek d\) with blocks \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}} = w_{\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}\,\vek U\) (\(j = 0, \dots, N - 1\)) solves \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) if and only if \((\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\,\vek U = \vek 0\).
  2. For every number \(\lambda\) the dimension of the space of solutions of \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) (the multiplicity of the eigenvalue \(\lambda\)) equals the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}=0}^{N-1}\dim\ker(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\). The eigenvalues of the wheel are therefore exactly the eigenvalues of the \(N\) sector problems together, with multiplicities added.
  3. All these values are real and positive. For \(k = 0\) and (for even \(N\)) \(k = N/2\) we have \(w_{\mathrm{k}} = \pm 1\) and \(\vek U\) can be taken real. For other \(k\) the real and imaginary parts \(\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d\) are real modes.
Proof

We have to show three points. In the first, the matrix \(\mat K\) acts on a wave as a multiplication. In the second, we split a wave into harmonics with the DFT (definition 9.102). The third follows from the chapter on eigenpairs.

  1. By the structure of a block circulant matrix, block \(j\) of the column \(\mat K\vek d\) is \((\mat K\vek d)_{\mathrm{j}} = \mat K_1^\T\vek d_{\mathrm{j}-1} + \mat K_0\vek d_{\mathrm{j}} + \mat K_1\vek d_{\mathrm{j}+1}\), indices modulo \(N\). For \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}} = w^j\vek U\) we have \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}\pm 1} = w^{\pm 1}w^j\vek U\), because \(w^N = \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k} = 1\) (theorem 9.29), so going around past \(N\) changes nothing, and \(w^{-1} = \bar w\) (\(|w| = 1\), lemma 9.20).
  2. Hence \((\mat K\vek d)_{\mathrm{j}} = w^j(\mat K_1^\T\bar w + \mat K_0 + \mat K_1 w)\vek U = w^j\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}\vek U\) and likewise \((\mat M\vek d)_{\mathrm{j}} = w^j\mat M_{\mathrm{k}}\vek U\). The equation \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) is then \(w^j(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\vek U = \vek 0\) for every \(j\), and \(w^j \ne 0\) (theorem 9.29, item 2), which is point 1.
  3. Let \(\vek d\) be an arbitrary complex column with blocks \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(\vek U_{\mathrm{k}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathrm{j}}w_{\mathrm{k}}^{-j}\vek d_{\mathrm{j}}\) (the DFT of each of the \(n\) entries, definition 9.102). Insert into \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} w_{\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}\vek U_{\mathrm{k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{l}}\vek d_{\mathrm{l}}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathrm{k}} \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}k(j-l)/N}\). By lemma 9.103 the inner sum is \(N\) at \(l = j\) and zero for the other \(l \in \{0, \dots, N-1\}\), so \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} w_{\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}\vek U_{\mathrm{k}}\). The map \(\vek d \mapsto (\vek U_0, \dots, \vek U_{\mathrm{N}-1})\) is therefore linear and invertible.
  4. Let \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\). By step 2 and linearity, \((\mat K\vek d - \lambda\mat M\vek d)_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} w_{\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\vek U_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek 0\) for every \(j\). Multiply by \(w_{\mathrm{l}}^{-j}/N\) and sum over \(j\): by lemma 9.103 only the term \(k = l\) remains, so \((\mat K_{\mathrm{l}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{l}})\vek U_{\mathrm{l}} = \vek 0\) for every \(l\). The solution \(\vek d\) is thus a sum of waves from point 1. Conversely, by point 1 and linearity every sum of waves with \(\vek U_{\mathrm{k}} \in \ker(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\) is a solution. The map of step 3 is therefore an invertible map of the space of solutions onto the sum of the spaces \(\ker(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\), and the two spaces have the same (complex) dimension. For real \(\lambda\) the matrix \(\mat K - \lambda\mat M\) is real, so the complex dimension of its kernel equals the real one, which is point 2.
  5. Let \(\lambda\) be an eigenvalue of sector problem \(k\) with vector \(\vek U \ne \vek 0\). By point 1 the wave \(\vek d\) is a nonzero solution of \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) and by lemma 10.18 \(\lambda\) is a real number. Let \(\vek d = \vek a + \mathrm{i}\,\vek b\) with real \(\vek a\), \(\vek b\). Since \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\) are real and symmetric, \(\overline{\vek d}^{\T}\mat K\vek d = \vek a^\T\mat K\vek a + \vek b^\T\mat K\vek b > 0\) and likewise for \(\mat M\), so \(\overline{\vek d}^{\T}\mat K\vek d = \lambda\,\overline{\vek d}^{\T}\mat M\vek d\) gives \(\lambda > 0\).
  6. For \(k = 0\) and \(k = N/2\) we have \(w = \pm 1\) and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}\), \(\mat M_{\mathrm{k}}\) are real, so the real matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}}\) with a nontrivial kernel has a real vector in its kernel. For other \(k\) the matrices \(\mat K\), \(\mat M\) and the number \(\lambda\) are real, so splitting \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) into real and imaginary parts gives \(\mat K\,\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d = \lambda\mat M\,\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d\) and the same for \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d\). Both are nonzero: with \(\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) the blocks \(\vek d_0 = \vek U\) and \(\vek d_1 = w\vek U\) would be purely imaginary, with \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) real, and in both cases \(w\) would be real (the quotient of corresponding nonzero entries), which is impossible for \(|w| = 1\) and \(w \ne \pm 1\).

Source: The proof is not from the sources. The split of the vibration of a cyclic structure into harmonics is our derivation from the DFT of chapter 9 (a gap in the sources, section 19.9). COOK, PDF p. 448, only warns that most vibration modes of a shell of revolution have waves around the circumference.

The theorem says that the wheel's frequencies come from six smaller problems and that every frequency is real. How many frequencies there are and what the shapes look like, we show on the wheel.

The whole wheel has \(18\) unknowns, hence \(18\) frequencies. Solving the full model gives, in order of size, \(17{,}044\), \(125{,}734\), \(152{,}907\), \(152{,}907\), \(223{,}512\), \(223{,}512\), \(347{,}674\), \(536{,}826\), \(536{,}826\), \(773{,}881\), \(773{,}881\), \(821{,}531\), \(1044{,}582\), \(1221{,}490\), \(1221{,}490\), \(1722{,}704\), \(1722{,}704\), \(2081{,}798\) Hz. The same holds for shapes: the column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{j}} = w^j\vek U\) from the sector problem solves the full problem with a relative residual of at most \(3 \cdot 10^{-13}\) for all six \(k\).

We build figure 19.33 row by row. Each row is one sector problem: row \(k = 0\) has dots at \(17{,}044\) and \(347{,}674\) Hz (the third, \(1044{,}582\) Hz, is off the figure). Rows \(k = 1\) and \(k = 5\) have dots at \(223{,}512\) and \(536{,}826\) Hz, that is at the same places. Rows \(k = 2\) and \(k = 4\) likewise have dots at \(152{,}907\) and \(773{,}881\) Hz. Only row \(k = 3\) has no partner, its dots are at \(125{,}734\) and \(821{,}531\) Hz. The axis under the rows is shared, so the dots can be compared by position. The agreement of rows \(k\) and \(6 - k\) is no accident, and corollary 19.51 explains it.

What do the shapes look like? Figure 19.34 shows, for each \(k\), the lowest shape of the sector problem, drawn as the deformed wheel.

We draw the wheel from above: the grey hexagon with its spokes is the wheel at rest, the blackwhite dot is the clamped hub. In panel \(k = 0\) we have \(w = 1\), every sector equals its neighbour, so the rim turns as a whole about the hub and the spokes bend. In panel \(k = 3\) we have \(w = \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi} = -1\), so neighbouring sectors move in opposite directions and the pattern repeats three times around the wheel. The \(k = 1\) panels show the same wave twice, the first as the real part, the second as the imaginary part of the column \(\vek d\), and the \(k = 2\) panels likewise, with the first and the second mode. Why there are two pictures for one frequency at \(k = 1\) and \(k = 2\) is explained by the next corollary.

For our wheel, the agreement of frequencies \(k\) and \(6 - k\) is explained by mirror symmetry. Besides turns, the wheel also has the reflection across spoke \(0\) (section 19.1), which takes sector \(j\) to \(-j\). If a wave runs around the wheel in one direction, its mirror image runs in the other, so it has the phase step \(-2\pi k/N\), which is harmonic \(N - k\). The structure is the same after reflection, so the mirror wave is a mode with the same frequency. Since for \(k \ne 0, N/2\) the wave and its mirror image belong to the different harmonics \(k\) and \(N - k\), neither is a multiple of the other (step 3 of the proof of theorem 19.49), and they are two independent modes with the same frequency. The corollary shows more. Its proof by conjugation needs only that \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\) are real, not mirror symmetry: the conjugate wave \(\overline{\vek d}\) runs around the wheel in the direction opposite to \(\vek d\), and since the matrices are real it is again a solution. So the frequencies \(k\) and \(N - k\) agree also for a cyclic structure without a mirror plane. We checked this on a wheel whose spokes are attached to a rigidly clamped ring of radius \(0{,}2\) m, each at a point turned by \(20^\circ\) in the same direction, so that it has no mirror plane: of the \(18\) frequencies again six are single and six double (outlines/finish-2026-11-16/ch-simetrija-vezi/check-10.py).

Corollary 19.51 Double frequencies of a cyclic structure (dvojne frekvence ciklične konstrukcije)

In the setting of theorem 19.49 let \(0 < k < N\) and \(2k \ne N\). Then \(\mat K_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} = \overline{\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}}\) and \(\mat M_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} = \overline{\mat M_{\mathrm{k}}}\), and \(\ker(\mat K_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}}) = \overline{\ker(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})}\) for every number \(\lambda\) (columns with conjugated entries). Every eigenvalue of sector problem \(k\) therefore has multiplicity at least \(2\) for the whole structure: if \(\vek d\) is a wave from point 1 of the theorem, then \(\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d\) are linearly independent real modes of the same frequency, and every linear combination of them is a mode.

Proof

We have to show the equality of matrices, the equality of kernels and the independence of two modes.

  1. From \(w_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} = \mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}(N-k)/N} = \mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}k/N} = \bar w_{\mathrm{k}}\) (theorem 9.29) and the reality of \(\mat K_0, \mat K_1, \mat M_0, \mat M_1\) it follows that \(\mat K_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} = \mat K_0 + \bar w_{\mathrm{k}}\mat K_1 + w_{\mathrm{k}}\mat K_1^\T = \overline{\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}}\), and likewise for \(\mat M\).
  2. If \((\mat K_{\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{k}})\vek U = \vek 0\) and \(\lambda\) is real (point 3 of theorem 19.49), conjugating the equation gives \((\mat K_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} - \lambda\mat M_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}})\overline{\vek U} = \vek 0\), and conversely. The kernels have the same dimension.
  3. Since \(k\) and \(N - k\) are different classes modulo \(N\) (from \(2k \ne N\) and \(0 < k < N\)), by point 2 of theorem 19.49 both contribute to the multiplicity: \(\dim\ker_{\mathrm{k}} + \dim\ker_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}} = 2\dim\ker_{\mathrm{k}} \ge 2\).
  4. The wave \(\vek d\) belongs to the sum at \(k\), the wave \(\overline{\vek d}\) at \(N - k\) (its blocks are \(\bar w_{\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}\overline{\vek U} = w_{\mathrm{N}-\mathrm{k}}^{\,j}\overline{\vek U}\)), so by step 3 of the theorem's proof they are linearly independent over \(\mathbb{C}\). Since \(\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d = (\vek d + \overline{\vek d})/2\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d = (\vek d - \overline{\vek d})/(2\mathrm{i})\) (real and imaginary part: lemma 9.20) span the same space, \(\mathrm{Re}\,\vek d\) and \(\mathrm{Im}\,\vek d\) are also independent over \(\mathbb{C}\), hence over \(\mathbb{R}\). A linear combination is a mode because the equation \(\mat K\vek d = \lambda\mat M\vek d\) is linear.

Source: The proof is not from the sources. The mirror explanation follows theorem 19.18 on the modes of a mirror symmetric structure.

The points \(k = 0\) and \(k = N/2\) do not fall under the corollary, since there \(w = \pm 1\) and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{k}}\) is real, so the conjugate wave \(\overline{\vek d}\) is a wave of the same harmonic and gives no second independent mode. For the wheel these are two sector problems with three single frequencies each (rows \(k = 0\) and \(k = 3\) of figure 19.33). For all other \(k\) the frequencies are double: of the \(18\) frequencies, six are single and six are double (altogether \(12\) different values), and none is triple. That the double ones are exactly double is not stated by the corollary (the multiplicity is at least \(2\)), the list counts it: at \(152{,}907\) Hz the full model has two values that differ by \(5 \cdot 10^{-12}\) Hz, and no third in the list.

Example 19.52 The double frequency \(152{,}907\) Hz and the turned mode (dvojna frekvenca in zasukani način)

Take \(k = 2\) and the lowest vector \(\vek U\) of the sector problem, with its phase chosen so that its largest entry is real. Let \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}} = \mathrm{Re}\,\vek d\) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}} = \mathrm{Im}\,\vek d\). Both solve the full problem with a relative residual of \(3 \cdot 10^{-14}\), their \(\mat M\)-norms are equal (\(1{,}732\)) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}^\T\mat M\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}} = 6 \cdot 10^{-16}\), so they are \(\mat M\)-orthogonal (definition 10.10). Turn the pattern \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}\) by one sector, that is \(\vek d'_{\mathrm{j}} = (\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}})_{\mathrm{j}-1}\), where \((\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}})_{\mathrm{j}} = \mathrm{Re}(w^j\vek U)\) is block \(j\) of the column \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}\). The wheel has the same stiffness and mass at every sector, so \(\vek d'\) is also a mode with the same frequency, and we express it with \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}}\). From \((\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}})_{\mathrm{j}-1} = \mathrm{Re}(w^{-1}w^j\vek U) = \cos\theta\,\mathrm{Re}(w^j\vek U) + \sin\theta\,\mathrm{Im}(w^j\vek U)\) with \(\theta = 2\pi k/N = 120^\circ\) (\(w^{-1} = \cos\theta - \mathrm{i}\sin\theta\)) it follows that

\[\vek d' = \cos 120^\circ\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}} + \sin 120^\circ\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}} = -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}} + \tfrac{\sqrt 3}{2}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}} , \tag{19.20}\]

which holds numerically with a largest difference of \(2 \cdot 10^{-15}\) for entries of size \(2{,}09\). By point 2 of theorem 19.49 the space of modes of this frequency is two-dimensional, since \(152{,}907\) Hz appears in the table only in rows \(k = 2\) and \(k = 4\), once each, and so it equals \(\mathrm{span}(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}})\). For a double eigenvalue, the computer returns an arbitrary column from this space: in our run, with radial components at the six rim nodes divided by the largest one, \(-0{,}044\), \(1\), \(-0{,}956\), \(-0{,}044\), \(1\), \(-0{,}956\), which is the combination with coefficients \(-0{,}577\) at \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(-0{,}022\) at \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{b}}\) (residual \(6 \cdot 10^{-14}\)). Such a shape is not mirror symmetric across spoke \(0\) although the wheel is, and it is no error: it is one mode of a two-dimensional space, and its shape depends on how the computation was carried out.

We build figure 19.35 from the left. The first panel is the mode \(\varphi_{\mathrm{a}}\), the deformed wheel in orange. The second panel is the mode \(\varphi_{\mathrm{b}}\), of the same frequency but with a different pattern. The third panel is the pattern of the first panel turned by \(60^\circ\) about the hub, and at the same time the sum \(-\tfrac12\varphi_{\mathrm{a}} + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\varphi_{\mathrm{b}}\) of equation (19.20). The turn gives no new independent mode, since the turned mode is only another combination of the same two. So at \(k = 2\) the wheel has two independent pictures and no third: there are many turns, but all lie in the same two-dimensional space.

One question remains that the result demands: what do we get if from section 19.9 we take only the sector with equal displacements at both edges, as COOK proposed (PDF pp. 368-370), that is \(k = 0\)?

Example 19.53 The sector with \(k = 0\) alone misses most modes (odsek le s \(k = 0\) zgreši večino načinov)

The sector with \(d_{\mathrm{B}} = d_{\mathrm{A}}\) is the sector problem for \(k = 0\) (matrix \(\mat K_0 + \mat K_1 + \mat K_1^\T\)). It gives three frequencies, row \(k = 0\) of figure 19.33: \(17{,}044\), \(347{,}674\) and \(1044{,}582\) Hz. Of the \(18\) frequencies of the wheel it finds \(3\). The lowest eight frequencies of the full model are \(17{,}044\), \(125{,}734\), \(152{,}907\), \(152{,}907\), \(223{,}512\), \(223{,}512\), \(347{,}674\) and \(536{,}826\) Hz, of which it finds \(2\) (\(17{,}044\) and \(347{,}674\)). The lowest frequency we do get, so the error might go unnoticed. The second frequency of the wheel is \(125{,}734\) Hz (\(k = 3\)), but from this sector we would read \(347{,}674\) Hz for the second one, which is \(2{,}765\) times too high. That the lowest frequency lies at \(k = 0\) is a property of this wheel. We did not compute another wheel, so we do not conclude that the lowest frequency is always at \(k = 0\). The sector with equal edges therefore suffices for a static load that is the same in all sectors, and for the lowest mode of this wheel, but not for vibration.

COOK (PDF p. 448) warns that most vibration modes of a shell of revolution have waves around the circumference, and this is the same difficulty. For vibration the sector with equal displacements at both edges is not enough, and we have to solve the sector problems for all \(k = 0, \dots, \lfloor N/2\rfloor\), since the remaining \(k\) are repeats by corollary 19.51. For \(N = 6\) these are four problems with \(3 \times 3\) complex matrices instead of one with \(18 \times 18\).
