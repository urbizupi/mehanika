Chapter 19 Symmetry, periodicity and constraints

19.6 Rigid links and constraints between several nodes

Take two steel bars with a 20 mm × 40 mm section and lay one on the other, like two planks. Fix one end into a wall and hang a force \(F = 100\) N on the free end. If the bars slide over each other, each bends on its own, and together they are only as stiff as two sections. If we glue them, they can no longer slide and behave as one bar with a 20 mm × 80 mm section. Chapter 5 already says how much the two cases differ: by Steiner's theorem (5.28) the second moment of the glued section is \(I_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(I + A\,e^2)\) with \(I = 106\,667\) mm⁴, \(A = 800\) mm² and the distance \(e = 20\) mm between the axis of each bar and the contact surface, so \(853\,333\) mm⁴, eight times the \(I\) of one bar. With free sliding it is only \(2I = 213\,333\) mm⁴.

In the finite element model of chapter 12 each bar is a line with nodes on its own axis, the axes are \(2e = 40\) mm apart and the nodes do not touch. How do we say that the bars are glued? We need a constraint that connects nodes of different bars. Such a constraint is called a constraint between several nodes, and the simplest one is the rigid link: a stick that neither bends nor lengthens.

Let us first look at it for one pair of nodes. Chapter 12 described a frame node by three numbers: the displacement \(u\) in the \(x\) direction, the displacement \(v\) in the \(y\) direction and the rotation of the section \(\varphi\), counterclockwise (definition 12.17). Every node thus has three unknowns, and a link will tie exactly these.

We draw two horizontal lines, the lower on the contact surface of the bars and the upper on the axis of the upper bar. On the lower one lies the master node \(M\), on the upper one the dependent node \(Z\) right above it. Between them is the rigid link, a stick of length \(e\) that neither bends nor lengthens. Why rigid: that is the name of a body that does not deform, and the link is a piece of such a body fixed to both sections. Why master and dependent: the unknowns of the master node stay in the system, those of the dependent node are already determined by them, they depend on them.

Now we load the structure. The master node moves by \(u_{\mathrm{M}}\) to \(M'\) and the section turns by \(\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\). The link is fixed to the section, so it turns with it, and the point \(Z\) goes to \(Z'\), which is displaced horizontally by \(e\sin\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\) to the left of \(M'\). For a small rotation this is \(e\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\) and the displacement of the point \(Z\) along \(x\) is \(u_{\mathrm{M}} - e\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\), while the vertical one does not change. That is the whole content of the link: the three numbers of the dependent node are expressed by the three of the master. We state it in general.

Definition 19.28 Rigid link (toga povezava)

Let \(M\) and \(Z\) be nodes of a plane frame (definition 5.93) with unknowns \((u_{\mathrm{M}}, v_{\mathrm{M}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{M}})\) and \((u_{\mathrm{Z}}, v_{\mathrm{Z}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z}})\) in the sense of definition 12.17, lying at distinct points, \(\overrightarrow{MZ} = (a, b) \ne (0, 0)\) before loading. A rigid link (toga povezava) between them is the model in which the two points and the sections at them belong to the same rigid body, which moves by lemma 4.72 on a small rigid displacement with the unknowns of node \(M\) as \(\delta\vek a = (u_{\mathrm{M}}, v_{\mathrm{M}})\) and \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi = \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\). Node \(M\) is the master (glavno vozlišče), node \(Z\) the dependent node (odvisno vozlišče).

Source: COOK §8.5 (PDF p. 294-295): "imaginary rigid links", the unknowns of one element become "slave" to those of another, "rotations are small". The notation with the small rigid displacement of chapter 4 is ours.

Does the link really determine the dependent unknowns by the master ones, and how? We show it with three equations and with the matrix that we shall need for elimination.

Lemma 19.29 Unknowns of the dependent node (neznanke odvisnega vozlišča)

For a rigid link with master node \(M\), dependent node \(Z\) (definition 19.28) and offset \(\overrightarrow{MZ} = (a, b)\):

  1. \(u_{\mathrm{Z}} = u_{\mathrm{M}} - b\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\), \(\quad v_{\mathrm{Z}} = v_{\mathrm{M}} + a\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\), \(\quad \varphi_{\mathrm{Z}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\).
  2. In the columns \(\vek d_{\mathrm{M}} = (u_{\mathrm{M}}, v_{\mathrm{M}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{M}})^\T\) and \(\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}} = (u_{\mathrm{Z}}, v_{\mathrm{Z}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{Z}})^\T\) this reads
    \[\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}} = \mat T_0\,\vek d_{\mathrm{M}} , \qquad \mat T_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & -b \\ 0 & 1 & a \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} . \tag{19.9}\]
  3. The three equations of item 1 are linear constraints (definition 12.25), \(\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}} - \mat T_0\,\vek d_{\mathrm{M}} = \vek 0\), with the constraint matrix \([\mat I\ \ -\mat T_0]\) on the columns \((\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}}, \vek d_{\mathrm{M}})\) and three pivots in the columns of the dependent node.
  4. The distance between the points \(M\) and \(Z\) grows after the displacement of item 1 by at most the factor \(1 + \tfrac12\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}^2\) (lemma 4.72).
Proof

We must show that the displacement of the point \(Z\) is the displacement of a rigid body with origin at \(M\) and that the rotation of the section is the same everywhere. We write the vector product in components.

  1. By definition 19.28 the displacement of the point \(Z\) is \(\delta\vek u(Z) = \delta\vek a + \delta\boldsymbol\varphi\times\overrightarrow{MZ}\) with \(\delta\vek a = (u_{\mathrm{M}}, v_{\mathrm{M}}, 0)\), \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi = (0, 0, \varphi_{\mathrm{M}})\) and \(\overrightarrow{MZ} = (a, b, 0)\).
  2. By the definition of the vector product (definition 2.33), \((0, 0, \varphi_{\mathrm{M}})\times(a, b, 0) = (0\cdot 0 - \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,b,\ \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,a - 0\cdot 0,\ 0) = (-b\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}},\ a\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}},\ 0)\). Hence \(u_{\mathrm{Z}} = u_{\mathrm{M}} - b\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\) and \(v_{\mathrm{Z}} = v_{\mathrm{M}} + a\,\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\).
  3. The field \(\delta\vek u(P) = \delta\vek a + \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times\overrightarrow{MP}\) has in the plane the form \((u_{\mathrm{M}} - \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,y, v_{\mathrm{M}} + \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\,x)\) in the coordinates \(x\), \(y\) of the point \(P\) relative to \(M\). Its small rotation tensor has the only nonzero component \(\omega_{\mathrm{xy}} = -\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\), whatever the point (definition 1.122, as in step 4 of the proof of lemma 12.18). The section at \(Z\) therefore turns by \(\varphi_{\mathrm{Z}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{M}}\).
  4. Item 2 is steps 2 and 3 in matrix form, item 3 is a rewriting of the equations in the form \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\) with the row block on the columns \(\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}}\) equal to \(\mat I\), which has three pivots.
  5. Item 4 is the statement of lemma 4.72 for the points \(M\) and \(Z\) and the angle \(|\delta\boldsymbol\varphi| = |\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}|\).

Source: COOK §8.5 (PDF p. 294, "rotations are small", equation 8.5-4, and PDF p. 295-296, equation 8.5-6) for the form \(\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}} = \mat T_0\,\vek d_{\mathrm{M}}\). The proof is not from the source, it follows from lemma 4.72.

The link is linear and therefore approximate. In figure 19.27 we get the true new position \(Z'\) (the orange dot) by turning the link about \(M'\). It differs from the place where the lemma puts it (the open circle) vertically by \(e\,(1 - \cos\varphi_{\mathrm{M}})\), which is of order \(\tfrac12 e\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}^2\), and the lemma leaves this term out. Why this does no harm: with the constraint we only have to put the node \(Z\) in the right place to first order, since the equations of the elements themselves (lemma 12.18) hold only for small displacements. The bound is item 4 of the lemma: the link lengthens by at most the factor \(1 + \tfrac12\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}^2\). In the cantilever that we compute below the rotation at the free end is \(|\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}| = 2{,}99\cdot 10^{-4}\) (glued pair, force 100 N), so the lengthening of a link of length \(e = 20\) mm is at most \(20\cdot 0{,}5\cdot(2{,}99\cdot 10^{-4})^2 \approx 0{,}9\) nm. For \(|\varphi_{\mathrm{M}}| \le 10^{-2}\) it is at most \(5\cdot 10^{-5}\,e\). For a rotation of \(0{,}5\) rad as in the figure the lemma would allow up to \(12{,}5\,\%\), which is why the figure is enlarged and not a true picture.

The constraints \(\vek d_{\mathrm{Z}} - \mat T_0\,\vek d_{\mathrm{M}} = \vek 0\) of the lemma are a special kind of constraint, to which chapter 12 gave no name.

Definition 19.30 Constraint between several nodes (multipoint constraint, vez med več vozlišči)

Let \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) be a system of linear constraints (definition 12.25) in a finite element model with the unknowns of several nodes. A constraint whose row of \(\mat C\) has nonzero numbers at the unknowns of one node only is a single-point constraint (enotočkovna vez), for instance a prescribed displacement of a node or a support from section 19.5. A constraint with nonzero numbers at the unknowns of at least two nodes, such as a rigid link, is a multipoint constraint (vez med več vozlišči).

Source: COOK §13.1 (PDF p. 504): for single-point constraints "[C_r] null and [C_c] a unit matrix", for several points the choice of the dependent unknowns is not unique, and §8.5 (PDF p. 295): "a multipoint constraint". FELIPPA §9.1.2 (PDF p. 127): \(u_{\mathrm{x}2} - 2u_{\mathrm{x}4} + u_{\mathrm{x}6} = 0{,}25\) is a constraint among three nodes, linear and nonhomogeneous.

Each of the three equations of lemma 19.29 contains the unknowns of two nodes, so it is a multipoint constraint. The constraint that equates only the deflection, \(v_{\text{up}} - v_{\text{low}} = 0\) between the nodes of the upper and the lower bar, is also a multipoint constraint and will be enough for free sliding. With a rigid link the dependent unknowns offer themselves, the unknowns of the dependent node: the constraint rows have the unit matrix on its columns, square and invertible, as COOK says they must be (PDF p. 504, "square and nonsingular"). This choice is not the only one. COOK (PDF p. 504) says that for multipoint constraints the choice of dependent unknowns is not unique and that different choices give the same result. The matrix \(\mat T_0\) has determinant \(1\) and is invertible, so we could also take the unknowns of node \(M\) as dependent. In elimination by theorem 19.26 we choose the unknowns of node \(M\) as master unknowns and get \(\vek d = \mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}\) with \(\mat T\) built from blocks \(\mat I\) and \(\mat T_0\). COOK (PDF p. 296) warns that for a rigid triangle a badly chosen triple of unknowns would lead to a division by zero. Here there is no such danger, since the block at the dependent node is the unit matrix.

19.6.1 The glued and the free pair of bars

Now we test the link on the pair of bars from the beginning of the section. The model is a cantilever of length \(L = 1\) m made of two equal steel bars with a 20 mm × 40 mm section (width 20 mm, height 40 mm), \(E = 196\) GPa, \(EA = 1{,}568\cdot 10^{8}\) N, \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\) N m². The bars lie one on the other, the axes are at \(y = \pm e\) with \(e = 20\) mm, the contact surface is at \(y = 0\). Both are clamped at \(x = 0\), at the free end a force \(F = 100\) N acts downwards. Each bar is divided into \(n\) equal frame elements (definition 12.17, Euler-Bernoulli beam with axial deformation) of length \(h = L/n\). Idealization: the glue is an infinitely stiff link without slip, a real glue has a finite shear stiffness and would give something in between. The linear model is fair, the stress at the clamp of the glued pair is \(F L\cdot 0{,}04\ \text{m}/I_{\mathrm{c}} = 4{,}7\) MPa, since 0,04 m is half the height of the pair.

We draw the wall with the two clamped bars and the pair of bars that slide, because nothing holds them together. At the free end acts the force \(F\). Both bars bend equally, since only the equal deflection joins them, and at the free end a slip therefore appears: the contact surface of the upper bar moves by \(e\varphi\) in one direction, that of the lower by \(e\varphi\) in the other, so together \(2e\varphi = 0{,}048\) mm. Below we draw the glued pair with the same wall and the same force, only that the sections at the nodes carry rigid links that hold the bars together. There is no slip, and the end deflects four times less. The deflections \(\delta_1\) and \(\delta_2\) are in the ratio \(4 : 1\), as Steiner predicts: \(213\,333 \to 853\,333\) mm⁴.

In the free pair the multipoint constraint acts on the deflection only: \(v_{\text{up}} = v_{\text{low}}\) at every node, while the axial unknowns and the rotations of the two bars are free. The solution is unique (the matrix after clamping is positive definite) and therefore symmetric between the bars, which have the same \(EI\): they have the same rotation, and the system is a single beam with \(2EI\) under the force \(F\), which by theorem 12.14 is exact at the nodes. So the tip deflection is \(F L^3/(3\cdot 2EI) = 0{,}79719\) mm for every \(n\), as num-06.py computes.

The glued pair has three master unknowns at each node, six on both bars together, so the number of unknowns halves: at \(n = 4\) there are \(2\cdot 5\cdot 3 = 30\) unknowns of the bars and \(15\) master ones, \(12\) after clamping. The reduced matrix is positive definite (smallest eigenvalue \(6{,}57\cdot 10^{4}\), theorem 19.26, since after clamping the structure has no rigid motions left). The results:

What do we notice? The tip deflection approaches the exact Steiner value from above, and with every halving of \(h\) the error shrinks by a factor of four (error column: \(0{,}14947 \to 0{,}03737 \to 0{,}00934\)). This is unusual: for the beam of chapter 12 the deflection at the nodes was exact for every \(n\) (theorem 12.14), here it is not, and the free pair kept that exactness. Why the glued pair lost it, what the error is and why a factor four, the next theorem shows.

Theorem 19.31 Glued cantilever with rigid links (zlepljena konzola iz togih povezav)

Let a cantilever of length \(L\) consist of two equal bars with \(EA\) and \(EI\), axes at \(y = \pm e\), each made of \(n\) equal frame elements of length \(h = L/n\) with nodes at \(x_{\mathrm{i}} = i\,h\). At node \(i\) there is the master node \(M_{\mathrm{i}}\) on \(y = 0\) with unknowns \((u_{\mathrm{i}}, v_{\mathrm{i}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})\), and the bar nodes at \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) are tied to it by rigid links with offsets \((0, +e)\) and \((0, -e)\) (definition 19.28). Clamping: \(u_0 = v_0 = \varphi_0 = 0\). At \(M_{\mathrm{n}}\) a force \(F\) acts downwards, there are no other loads. Let \(r = EAe^2/EI\), \(EI_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(EI + EAe^2) = 2EI\,(1 + r)\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{i}} = -\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{i}} = -v_{\mathrm{i}}\). Then:

  1. \(u_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\) for all \(i\).
  2. \(\theta_{\mathrm{i}} = \dfrac{F\,x_{\mathrm{i}}\,(2L - x_{\mathrm{i}})}{2EI_{\mathrm{c}}}\), which is the rotation of a cantilever with bending stiffness \(EI_{\mathrm{c}}\) by theorem 5.57, exact at every node.
  3. \(w_{\mathrm{i}} = \dfrac{F\,x_{\mathrm{i}}^2\,(3L - x_{\mathrm{i}})}{6EI_{\mathrm{c}}} + i\,\dfrac{F\,h^3}{24\,EI}\,\dfrac{r}{1 + r}\), so the tip deflection exceeds the exact one by \(\dfrac{F L^3}{24\,EI\,n^2}\,\dfrac{r}{1+r}\), relatively by \(\dfrac{r}{4n^2}\).
Proof

We must show three items. From the energy of the reduced system we get the axial unknowns, then the equilibrium at the nodes, then the rotations and at the end the deflection from the Hermite form of the element.

  1. By lemma 19.29 (\(a = 0\), \(b = \pm e\)) the unknowns of the upper bar at node \(i\) are \((u_{\mathrm{i}} - e\varphi_{\mathrm{i}},\ v_{\mathrm{i}},\ \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})\) and of the lower bar \((u_{\mathrm{i}} + e\varphi_{\mathrm{i}},\ v_{\mathrm{i}},\ \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})\). By theorem 19.26 the solution for the master unknowns is the solution of the symmetric positive definite system \(\mat T^\T\mat K\,\mat T\,\vek d_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat T^\T\vek f\), each of whose rows is the derivative with respect to an unknown of the function \(\Pi(\vek d_{\mathrm{m}}) = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} U_{\mathrm{e}} + F\,v_{\mathrm{n}}\), where \(U_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the energy of the element (lemma 12.18, item 2) with the unknowns of the element expressed by the master ones, and \(F\,v_{\mathrm{n}}\) is the potential of the force \(F\) downwards (\(-F\) along \(y\), work \(-F\,v_{\mathrm{n}}\)).
  2. The energy of element \(j\) (between nodes \(j - 1\) and \(j\)) with both bars is by step 1 and lemma 12.18 (proof, step 1) the axial part \(\tfrac12\,\tfrac{EA}{h}\big[(\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}} - e\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}})^2 + (\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}} + e\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}})^2\big] = \tfrac{EA}{h}\,(\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}}^2 + e^2\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}}^2)\) and the bending part \(2\cdot\tfrac12\,EI\int_0^h v_{\mathrm{h}}''^2\,\mathrm{d}x = EI\int_0^h v_{\mathrm{h}}''^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), where \(\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}} = u_{\mathrm{j}} - u_{\mathrm{j}-1}\), \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{j}} - \varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1}\) and \(x\) is the distance from node \(j - 1\). The cross term between \(\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}}\) cancels.
  3. The \(u_{\mathrm{i}}\) occur only in \(\tfrac{EA}{h}\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}}^2\), which no force loads. \(\Pi\) splits into a function of the \(u_{\mathrm{i}}\) alone and one of the \((v_{\mathrm{i}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})\) alone. The first is least (zero) at \(\Delta u_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\), so with \(u_0 = 0\) we get \(u_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\). This is item 1.
  4. Let \(M_{\mathrm{j}}(x) = EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) and \(V_{\mathrm{j}} = EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}'''\) on element \(j\). The function \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is cubic (definition 12.17), so \(M_{\mathrm{j}}\) is linear, \(V_{\mathrm{j}}\) constant and \(M_{\mathrm{j}}' = V_{\mathrm{j}}\). By integration by parts \(\int_0^h EI\,v_{\mathrm{h}}''\,\delta v_{\mathrm{h}}''\,\mathrm{d}x = M_{\mathrm{j}}(h)\,\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}} - M_{\mathrm{j}}(0)\,\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1} - V_{\mathrm{j}}\,(\delta v_{\mathrm{j}} - \delta v_{\mathrm{j}-1})\), since \(M_{\mathrm{j}}'' = 0\).
  5. We write the vanishing of the variation \(\delta\Pi\) with respect to \(\delta v_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) (\(1 \le i \le n\)) by steps 2 and 4, with \(s = EAe^2/h\). At \(\delta v_{\mathrm{i}}\) (\(i < n\)) the coefficient is \(-2V_{\mathrm{i}} + 2V_{\mathrm{i}+1} = 0\), at \(\delta v_{\mathrm{n}}\) it is \(-2V_{\mathrm{n}} + F = 0\). So \(V_{\mathrm{j}} = F/2\) for every \(j\): each bar carries half the force. At \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) (\(i < n\)) the coefficient is \(2\,[M_{\mathrm{i}}(h) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}] - 2\,[M_{\mathrm{i}+1}(0) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1}] = 0\), at \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{n}}\) it is \(2\,[M_{\mathrm{n}}(h) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{n}}] = 0\), since there is no couple at the end. Let \(m_{\mathrm{i}}\) be the common value of both expressions: \(m_{\mathrm{i}} = M_{\mathrm{i}}(h) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}} = M_{\mathrm{i}+1}(0) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1}\) for \(1 \le i < n\). At the ends we take the expression that exists: \(m_0 = M_1(0) + s\,\Delta\varphi_1\) and \(m_{\mathrm{n}} = M_{\mathrm{n}}(h) + s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\).
  6. On element \(j\), \(M_{\mathrm{j}}(h) - M_{\mathrm{j}}(0) = V_{\mathrm{j}}\,h = Fh/2\) (steps 4, 5), so \(m_{\mathrm{j}} - m_{\mathrm{j}-1} = Fh/2\) and with \(m_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\), \(m_{\mathrm{j}} = -F\,(L - x_{\mathrm{j}})/2\). Because \(v_{\mathrm{h}}''\) is a linear function, \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}} = \int_0^h v_{\mathrm{h}}''\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{h}{2EI}\,(M_{\mathrm{j}}(0) + M_{\mathrm{j}}(h)) = \tfrac{h}{2EI}\,(m_{\mathrm{j}-1} + m_{\mathrm{j}} - 2s\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}})\). With \(hs/EI = r\) we get \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}}\,(1 + r) = \tfrac{h}{2EI}\,(m_{\mathrm{j}-1} + m_{\mathrm{j}}) = -\tfrac{h}{2EI}\,F\,(L - \bar x_{\mathrm{j}})\) with \(\bar x_{\mathrm{j}} = \tfrac12(x_{\mathrm{j}-1} + x_{\mathrm{j}})\), so \(\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}} = -hF\,(L - \bar x_{\mathrm{j}})/EI_{\mathrm{c}}\).
  7. The sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{j} \le \mathrm{i}} h\,(L - \bar x_{\mathrm{j}})\) is the integral of \(L - x\) over \([0, x_{\mathrm{i}}]\), as the midpoint rule is exact for a linear function, and equals \(L\,x_{\mathrm{i}} - x_{\mathrm{i}}^2/2\). With \(\varphi_0 = 0\), \(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}} = -F\,x_{\mathrm{i}}\,(2L - x_{\mathrm{i}})/(2EI_{\mathrm{c}})\), item 2 (the derivative of the deflection curve of theorem 5.57).
  8. On a cubic piece \(v(x) = v_{\mathrm{j}-1} + \varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1}\,x + c_0\,x^2/2 + c_1\,x^3/6\), \(v'' = c_0 + c_1\,x\), we have \(v_{\mathrm{j}} - v_{\mathrm{j}-1} = h\varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1} + c_0\,h^2/2 + c_1\,h^3/6\) and \(\tfrac{h}{2}(\varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1} + \varphi_{\mathrm{j}}) = h\varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1} + c_0\,h^2/2 + c_1\,h^3/4\), so \(v_{\mathrm{j}} - v_{\mathrm{j}-1} = \tfrac{h}{2}(\varphi_{\mathrm{j}-1} + \varphi_{\mathrm{j}}) - c_1\,h^3/12\). By steps 4 and 5, \(c_1\,h = (M_{\mathrm{j}}(h) - M_{\mathrm{j}}(0))/EI = Fh/(2EI)\), exactly \(c_1 = F/EI_{\mathrm{c}}\). Rotations agree (step 7), so the increment is larger by \(\tfrac{Fh^3}{12}\,\big(\tfrac{1}{2EI} - \tfrac{1}{EI_{\mathrm{c}}}\big) = \tfrac{Fh^3}{24\,EI}\,\big(1 - \tfrac{1}{1+r}\big) = \tfrac{Fh^3}{24\,EI}\,\tfrac{r}{1+r}\), equal for every element.
  9. With \(w_0 = 0\) and \(i\) increments, item 3 follows. For \(i = n\) we have \(n\,h^3 = L^3/n^2\) and after division by the exact value \(F L^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{c}}) = F L^3/(6EI\,(1 + r))\) the relative error is \(\tfrac{(1+r)}{4n^2}\,\tfrac{r}{1+r} = \tfrac{r}{4n^2}\).

Source: The theorem and the proof are not from the sources. COOK §8.5 (PDF p. 295) describes only qualitatively that a beam-plate assembly is "too flexible" when the curvature varies and that it approaches the exact value with finer subdivision. The numbers are checked in num-06.py.

In our example \(r = EAe^2/EI = 800\cdot 400/106\,667 = 3{,}000\) and \(EI_{\mathrm{c}} = 8\,EI\). Item 3 gives the error \(F L^3/(32\,EI\,n^2)\), which is \(0{,}14947\), \(0{,}03737\), \(0{,}00934\), \(0{,}00234\), \(0{,}00058\), \(0{,}00015\) mm, equal to the column of the table to five places. The relative error is \(r/(4n^2) = 0{,}75/n^2\), so below \(1\,\%\) only from \(n \ge 9\). The rotations at the nodes are exact to \(10^{-16}\) rad, as the computation confirms (num-06.out).

The error grows with \(r\), that is with the Steiner term. At \(e = 0\) we have \(r = 0\), the links have no length and there is no error. Figure 19.29 shows the error on doubly logarithmic axes.

We draw the axes: on the horizontal one the number of elements \(n\) in doublings, on the vertical one the error \(|\delta_{\mathrm{n}} - \delta|\) in mm, where \(\delta_{\mathrm{n}}\) is the tip deflection with \(n\) elements per bar and \(\delta\) the exact one, both axes logarithmic. Each computation is placed as a point. The points lie on a line of slope \(-2\), which is the \(n^{-2}\) of the theorem. On the step from \(n = 8\) to \(n = 16\) the error falls to a quarter.

Why does theorem 12.14 not hold here? Its step 1 claims that every field of elements is an admissible virtual deflection, that is, a field belonging to the same model as the exact solution. For the glued pair the exact model is the common section: the axial displacement of each bar is \(\mp e\,v'(x)\) along its whole length, which is a quadratic function, because \(v\) is cubic. The field of elements has on an element a linear axial displacement of the bar (definition 12.17) and the rigid links demand \(\mp e\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) only at the nodes. Inside an element the bars can therefore still slip against each other. The difference between a quadratic and a linear function on an element is of order \(h^2\), and that is the reason for the factor four. The field of elements is not in the space of the common section, so Galerkin orthogonality (lemma 6.36) fails. The rotations are nevertheless exact, because they are determined by the moment equilibrium (steps 5 and 6), in which the link enters only as the stiffness \(EAe^2\) that the exact \(EI_{\mathrm{c}}\) already contains. The error is in the slope of the curvature \(c_1\): the shear force in an element is carried by the bars themselves by bending (\(V = F/2\) each), while in a common section it is carried mostly by the change of the axial forces (for \(r = 3\) three quarters, the bending of the bars only \(2EI/EI_{\mathrm{c}} = 1/(1 + r) = 1/4\)), and the increment of the deflection changes accordingly (step 8). COOK (PDF p. 295) says the same qualitatively: in pure bending the beam-plate assembly has the correct stiffness, when the curvature varies it is "too flexible", and finer subdivision brings the curvature on each element closer to constant and removes the error. Step 8 shows it in a formula: the increment of the deflection differs by \(h^3/12\) times the difference of the curvature slopes \(c_1\), and at constant moment both slopes are zero. Item 3 of the theorem says in addition how fast the error goes to zero and what it depends on.

Now Steiner. Theorem 19.31 obtained from the rigid link the bending stiffness \(EI_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(EI + EAe^2)\), and chapter 5 already had it from theorem 5.28. The agreement is a theorem, not a coincidence.

Corollary 19.32 Steiner's theorem from rigid links (Steinerjev izrek iz togih povezav)

For the glued cantilever of theorem 19.31, \(EI_{\mathrm{c}} = E\,I_{\mathrm{c}}\), where \(I_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(I + A\,e^2)\) is the second moment of the section of both bars together about the contact line \(y = 0\). The tip deflection tends with \(n \to \infty\) to \(F L^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{c}})\), by chapter 5 the deflection of a cantilever with a 20 mm × 80 mm section and \(I_{\mathrm{c}} = 853\,333\) mm⁴.

Proof

We must show the equality of the number \(EI_{\mathrm{c}}\) with the Steiner sum and the limit of item 3.

  1. Each bar has its centroid on its own axis, at distance \(e\) from the line \(y = 0\), area \(A\) and own second moment \(I\). By theorem 5.28 its second moment about \(y = 0\) is \(I + A\,e^2\). The moment of both together is their sum, \(I_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(I + A\,e^2)\).
  2. By theorem 19.31, \(EI_{\mathrm{c}} = 2\,(EI + EAe^2) = E\cdot 2\,(I + A\,e^2) = E\,I_{\mathrm{c}}\).
  3. The error of item 3 of the theorem is zero as \(n \to \infty\), because \(n^{-2}\) has limit zero, so the limit of the tip deflection is \(F L^3/(3EI_{\mathrm{c}})\).

Source: The proof is not from the sources. Steiner's theorem and the deflection of a cantilever are from chapter 5.

So the link is Steiner's theorem at work: the term \(2EAe^2\) in \(EI_{\mathrm{c}}\) comes from the axial forces \(N = \mp EA\,e\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}}/h\) of the two bars (step 2 of the proof of theorem 19.31), which form a couple with arm \(2e\) and moment \(2EAe^2\,\Delta\varphi_{\mathrm{j}}/h\). In the free pair there are no such forces.

Let us sum up what we saw and with what. The rigid link expresses the three unknowns of the dependent node by the three of the master (lemma 19.29), so it is a multipoint constraint (definition 19.30) and we eliminate it as in section 19.5. The glued pair showed that the link carries Steiner's term (corollary 19.32), and that the exactness of the deflection at the nodes from chapter 12 is lost with links while the rotation is kept (theorem 19.31, error \(r/(4n^2)\)). Limitations: as COOK says (PDF p. 296), a rigid link does not expand with temperature, and in dynamics a diagonal mass matrix is no longer diagonal after elimination. A similar link between a beam and a plate (an eccentric stiffener, COOK PDF p. 294) needs the plate elements of chapter 18, so we only mention it here.

Notation: the letter \(e\) here is the offset of the link, Euler's number is written \(\mathrm{e}\) (chapter 9). The letter \(F\) is a force, \(N\) the axial force (not the number of sectors in cyclic symmetry), \(\mat T\) the matrix of the transformation of unknowns, not the sign matrix of the space element and not the kinetic energy.
