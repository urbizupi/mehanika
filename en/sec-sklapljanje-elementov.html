Chapter 19 Symmetry, periodicity and constraints

19.7 Coupling different elements

So far all elements that meet at a node have had the same unknowns. The bars of a truss have two at a node (the displacements \(u\) and \(v\)), the frame elements three (\(u\), \(v\) and the rotation \(\varphi\)). What happens when we have to attach a beam to a wall built of bars? Two elements meet that do not speak about the same quantities, so we call them dissimilar (različni elementi, COOK §8.5, PDF p. 292). Coupling (sklapljanje) means joining such elements into one system of equations. The word is from everyday language: as we couple two wagons so that they are pulled together, we couple two elements so that they move together.

The model is the following (figure 19.21). The wall is a block of five bars. Nodes \(1\) and \(4\) are clamped, both of their displacements are zero, nodes \(2\) and \(3\) are free and are joined by bars along the edges and along both diagonals. The bars have the cross-section \(20\ \text{mm}^2\) and the steel of chapter 12, \(E = 196\) GPa. This is a deliberately weak wall, so that the turning of its edge shows in the numbers. The distance between the free nodes is \(d = 0{,}2\) m. To the right edge we attach a beam of length \(1\) m with the cross-section \(20 \times 40\) mm as in chapter 12, \(EI = 20\,907\) N m\(^2\), \(EA = 1{,}568\cdot 10^{8}\) N, with one frame element (definition 12.17). The hollow node \(5\) is the start of the beam at the middle of the edge \(2\)-\(3\), it is not a node of the wall, node \(6\) is the end of the beam. The force \(F = 100\) N acts on it downward. The force has the letter \(F\) because \(P\) and \(S\) are reserved in this chapter for the mirror matrix (section 19.1).

We accept three idealizations. The bars are pinned and carry only axial force (chapter 7), the displacements are small (we get the bound for the rotation below) and the material is linearly elastic. The question is how to connect the unknowns \(u_5\), \(v_5\), \(\varphi_5\) of the beam with the unknowns \(u_2\), \(v_2\), \(u_3\), \(v_3\) of the wall, so that the system has a unique solution and the right stiffness. We go in three steps. First we check what a bar does to a frame node, since that is the simplest joint of different elements. Then we simply lay the beam on a node of the wall and see that it does not hold. At the end we write a constraint that does.

19.7.1 A bar at a frame node

Take a node of a frame (definition 5.90) where a frame element and a bar meet. The frame element comes from the left and has three unknowns at the node \(A\). The bar comes from the upper right and is attached at the point \(A\), so that its axis runs through this point (figure 19.22). Around the node we draw a small circle, as for a free body, and write into it the forces that the piece of beam and the bar exert on it. On the circle acts the force of the bar \(F_{\mathrm{p}}\) along its axis, and on the circle act the shear force \(Q\) and the moment \(M\) of the frame. The frame also acts on it with an axial force, which we do not draw, since like \(Q\) it acts at \(A\) and has no lever arm in the moments about \(A\). By the equilibrium of the circle, which the reader knows from school physics (the sum of forces and the sum of moments are zero, chapter 5), the moment of the bar does not enter: its force passes through the point \(A\), so it has zero lever arm. What this says in the language of matrices is told by the next lemma.

Lemma 19.33 A bar at a frame node is a hinge (palica v vozlišču okvirja je členek)

Let \(A\) be a node of a plane frame with the unknowns \(u_{\mathrm{A}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{A}}\), \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) (definitions 5.90 and 12.17) and let a bar be attached at it whose axis passes through \(A\) (lemma 7.28). Let the connection table and the selection matrices of the elements be as in definition 7.11. Then:

  1. The matrix of the bar \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) in the space of all unknowns has the row and the column of the unknown \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) equal to zero.
  2. The equation of the row \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) of the assembled matrix contains only frame elements: the sum of the moments at the ends of the frame elements at \(A\) equals the given external couple at \(A\). The bar does not transmit a moment.
  3. The force of the bar on the node is \(\pm N\,\vek n\) along its axis and has zero moment about \(A\).
Proof

We must show that \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) does not appear in the connection table of the bar, and from this obtain items 1 and 2 by the assembly theorem.

  1. By lemma 7.28 the bar carries only the displacements of its end points, that is four unknowns \(u\), \(v\) of each end. The connection table of the bar therefore does not contain \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\).
  2. By item 2 of lemma 7.12, \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) has nonzero entries only at the places \((I, J)\) where \(I\) and \(J\) are in the connection table of the element. The places \((\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}, J)\) and \((I, \varphi_{\mathrm{A}})\) are not in it, so the row and the column are zero. This is item 1.
  3. By item 1 of theorem 7.13, \((\mat K\,\vek d)_{\varphi_\mathrm{A}}\) is the sum over the elements whose table contains \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\). These are only frame elements, and the row of the equation \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) says that this sum equals \(f_{\varphi_\mathrm{A}}\). The moment of a frame element at its end is the third component of the forces of the surroundings on the end (lemma 12.18, item 1). This is item 2.
  4. By lemma 7.28 the force of the surroundings on the end of the bar is \(\pm N\,\vek n\) (the sign depends on the end) with the direction \(\vek n\) of the axis. The axis passes through \(A\), the lever arm is zero and the moment of the force about \(A\) is \(0\). This is item 3.

Source: COOK §8.5, figure 8.5-2a (PDF p. 293) says that the joint of a beam and plane elements without a rotation is a hinge and that no moment is transferred at the point \(A\). The proof is ours.

Let us test the lemma on a bar of the wall. The bar \(2\)-\(3\) is vertical, \(\vek n = (0, 1)\), \(L = d = 0{,}2\) m, \(EA = 196\ \text{GPa}\cdot 20\ \text{mm}^2 = 3{,}92\cdot 10^{6}\) N, so \(EA/L = 1{,}96\cdot 10^{7}\) N/m. By lemma 7.28 the nonzero block is \(\vek n\,\vek n^\T = \left(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\), so the matrix of the bar, if its ends were frame nodes with three unknowns \((u, v, \varphi)\), has only four nonzero entries \(\pm 1{,}96\cdot 10^{7}\) N/m in the rows and columns of the vertical displacements \(v_2\), \(v_3\). The rows and columns of the rotations \(\varphi_2\), \(\varphi_3\) are zero, as item 1 says.

Where does the lemma fail? Take a bar that runs parallel to the axis of a beam and is attached to it off the axis, at the edge of the height \(40\) mm, so with the offset \(e = 20\) mm from the axis. With the bar force \(500\) N (the same number as we shall get below in the wall) its force acts along a line at the distance \(e\) from the point \(A\) and has the moment \(500 \cdot 0{,}020 = 10\) N m. The assumption of the lemma, that the axis of the bar runs through the node, does not hold, item 3 fails and the row \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) gets a term that the matrix of the bar does not contain. Such joints are written with a rigid link (section 19.6).

19.7.2 A beam on a node without rotations

The simplest way to attach the beam would be to put its start into a node of the wall. So we replace node \(5\) by node \(2\): at node \(2\) the beam is attached so that it shares the displacements \(u_2\), \(v_2\) with the wall, and the rotation \(\varphi_5\) is its own new unknown, since the wall has no rotations (figure 19.23). There are then eight unknowns: four in the wall, \(\varphi_5\) and three at node \(6\).

Let us draw what this means. The wall and the clamp are as before, the beam starts at node \(2\). Let us turn it by a small angle \(\theta\) about this point, as the orange position shows: the end \(6\) moves to \(6'\). The bars of the wall do not change in this, since the nodes of the wall did not move. Whether such a motion really costs no energy and what follows from this is told by the next lemma.

Lemma 19.34 A beam on a node without rotations can turn (nosilec na vozlišču brez zasukov je vrtljiv)

Let \(A\) be a node that only bars have (two displacements) and let a frame element of length \(h\) and stiffnesses \(EA\), \(EI\) (definition 12.17) be attached at \(A\) so that it shares the displacements \(u\), \(v\) of the node, and its rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) is a new unknown that appears in the connection table only at this element. There are no other elements with the unknowns of the end of the beam, and no external couple acts at \(A\). Then:

  1. The moment at the start of the beam is zero, so the joint is a hinge.
  2. For every small angle \(\theta\) the displacement in which the nodes of the wall do not move and \((\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}, u_6, v_6, \varphi_6) = \theta\,(1,\ 0,\ h,\ 1)\) (the beam along the axis \(x\), end \(6\)) costs no energy.
  3. The matrix \(\mat K\) of the system is therefore singular, it necessarily has a zero eigenvalue.
Proof

We must show that the moment in the row \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) is zero and that the turning of the rigid beam about \(A\) gives no energy, and then use the lemma on zero energy.

  1. The unknown \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) is in the connection table only at this element, so by item 1 of theorem 7.13 the row \(\varphi_{\mathrm{A}}\) of the system \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) says \((\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}})_3 = f_{\varphi_\mathrm{A}}\). There is no external couple at \(A\), so the moment at the end of the element is zero. This is item 1.
  2. Let the beam lie along the local axis \(\bar x \in [0, h]\) from \(A\) towards \(6\). By lemma 4.72, in a small rigid turn about \(A\) by \(\theta\) the point at the distance \(\bar x\) moves by \(\theta\,\bar x\) transversally and the cross-section turns by \(\theta\). So \(\bar u = 0\), \(\bar v(\bar x) = \theta\,\bar x\) and \(\varphi = \bar v' = \theta\) and the nodal displacements are \((0, 0, \theta, 0, \theta h, \theta)\).
  3. By definition 12.17 the field of the element is linear in \(\bar u\) and a Hermite polynomial of degree three in \(\bar v\), determined by the values and slopes at both ends. The line \(\bar v = \theta\,\bar x\) is a polynomial of degree at most three with the same four data \((0, \theta, \theta h, \theta)\), and the polynomial of degree at most three with these data is unique, so the field of the element is this line, \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) and \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\). The strain energy of the element \(\tfrac12\int_0^h (EA\,\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}'^2 + EI\,\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}''^2)\,\mathrm d\bar x\) (lemma 12.18, item 1, step 1) is therefore \(0\).
  4. The bars of the wall have zero elongations, since the displacements of the nodes of the wall are zero. By item 2 of theorem 7.13 the total energy \(\tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d\) is the sum of the energies of the elements, so \(0\).
  5. The matrix \(\mat K\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15), since the energy of every element is nonnegative. By lemma 7.16, \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = 0\) gives \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek 0\) for a nonzero \(\vek d\). This is item 3.

Source: COOK §8.5, figure 8.5-2a (PDF p. 293): a joint without a rotation is a "hinge mechanism". The proof is ours.

Let us look at the numbers. The matrix of the eight unknowns has by computation (script num-07.py) the three smallest eigenvalues \(0\), \(4{,}18\cdot 10^{4}\) and \(3{,}66\cdot 10^{5}\), and the vector of the zero eigenvalue is at \(h = 1\) m \((\varphi_5, u_6, v_6, \varphi_6) = (1, 0, 1, 1)\,\theta\), as item 2 of the lemma predicts. The zero value does not change if we strengthen the wall: it is the same for bars with the cross-sections \(800\), \(100\) and \(20\ \text{mm}^2\). The equation \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\) with the force \(F = 100\) N at node \(6\) has no solution, since the load is not orthogonal to the zero vector: \(\vek f\cdot\vek d = -F\,h\,\theta \ne 0\). Orthogonality is needed, since a solution \(\vek d^*\) would give \(\vek d^\T\vek f = \vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d^* = (\mat K\,\vek d)^\T\vek d^* = 0\) by the symmetry of \(\mat K\) and lemma 1.101. This is the unbalanced moment \(F\,h = 100\) N m about node \(2\), which a hinge cannot take.

Let us also consider a nearby remedy: we set the rotation \(\varphi_5\) at node \(2\) to zero, because "the wall does not allow turning". The beam then gets a clamp and the system has a solution. For a rigid wall the tip deflection would be \(F\,h^3/(3EI) = 100 \cdot 1^3/(3 \cdot 20\,907) = 1{,}5944\) mm, for our weak wall with such a constraint \(1{,}6053\) mm, and the true value (below) is \(1{,}9103\) mm, so the deflection is \(16\,\%\) too small. A clamp that the wall cannot give makes the beam too stiff, since the wall has no rotations and the edge turns by \(2{,}8\cdot 10^{-4}\).

19.7.3 The rotation of an edge from two normal displacements

The beam must feel the rotation of the wall. The wall has on the edge \(2\)-\(3\) only the displacements of the nodes, but these two displacements can differ, and the difference tells how the edge turned. Let us draw it (figure 19.24). The edge \(2\)-\(3\) is a vertical segment of length \(d\) with the point \(5\) in the middle, and the beam is perpendicular to it. Let us turn the edge by a small angle \(\varphi\) counterclockwise about the point \(5\) (orange position). The upper node moves to the left, the lower to the right. The normal displacements \(u_2\) and \(u_3\) are horizontal and have opposite signs. By the rule of the small rigid displacement (lemma 4.72) the horizontal displacement of a point at the height \(y\) above the point \(5\) is \(-\varphi\,y\), so the rotation is \(\varphi = (u_3 - u_2)/d\).

The point \(5\) is an arbitrary point of the edge, not necessarily the middle. Let us write the general case and prove it.

Lemma 19.35 The rotation constraint from two normal displacements (vez zasuka in pomika iz dveh vozlišč zidu)

Let \(2\) and \(3\) be nodes of the wall lying on a vertical line at the distance \(d > 0\), node \(3\) below \(2\), and let \(5\) be a point between them at the height \(s\,d\) above \(3\), \(0 \le s \le 1\). Let the displacements \((u_2, v_2, u_3, v_3)\) of the nodes be small, which means that the rotation of the edge is \(|\varphi| \le 10^{-3}\). Then:

  1. In a small rigid displacement of the edge \(2\)-\(3\) with the displacement \((u_5, v_5)\) of the point \(5\) and the rotation \(\varphi\) counterclockwise we have, to first order in \(\varphi\),
    \[\begin{pmatrix} u_5 \\ v_5 \\ \varphi_5 \end{pmatrix} = \mat T_{\mathrm{w}} \begin{pmatrix} u_2 \\ v_2 \\ u_3 \\ v_3 \end{pmatrix} , \qquad \mat T_{\mathrm{w}} = \begin{pmatrix} s & 0 & 1 - s & 0 \\ 0 & s & 0 & 1 - s \\ -1/d & 0 & 1/d & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \tag{19.10}\]
    where \(\varphi_5 = \varphi\).
  2. A force \(X\) in the horizontal direction, a force \(Y\) in the vertical direction and a couple \(M\) counterclockwise, all at the point \(5\) and collected in the column \(\mat F_5 = (X, Y, M)^\T\), are statically equivalent to forces at the nodes \(2\) and \(3\) with the horizontal components \(X_2 = s\,X - M/d\) and \(X_3 = (1 - s)\,X + M/d\) and the vertical ones \(Y_2 = s\,Y\), \(Y_3 = (1 - s)\,Y\). These four expressions are the entries of the column \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}^\T\,\mat F_5\).
Proof

We must show that equation (19.10) follows from the rigid displacement of the edge and that the statics of the edge gives the same column as \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}^\T\,\mat F_5\).

  1. By lemma 4.72 the displacement of the point with the vector \(\overrightarrow{OP}\) from the point \(5\) is \(\delta\vek a + \delta\boldsymbol\varphi\times\overrightarrow{OP}\). For the rotation \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi = \varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) and the point \((0, y)\) we have \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}} \times (0, y) = (-y, 0)\), so \(u = u_5 - \varphi\,y\) and \(v = v_5\).
  2. Node \(2\) is at the height \(y_2 = (1 - s)\,d\) above \(5\) and node \(3\) at the height \(y_3 = -s\,d\). By step 1, \(u_2 = u_5 - \varphi\,(1 - s)\,d\) and \(u_3 = u_5 + \varphi\,s\,d\), \(v_2 = v_3 = v_5\).
  3. Subtract: \(u_3 - u_2 = \varphi\,d\), so \(\varphi = (u_3 - u_2)/d\). Add \(s\) times the first and \((1-s)\) times the second equation of step 2: \(s\,u_2 + (1-s)\,u_3 = u_5 - \varphi\,s(1-s)d + \varphi\,(1-s)\,s\,d = u_5\). The same computation gives \(v_5 = s\,v_2 + (1-s)\,v_3\). This is equation (19.10).
  4. Two systems of forces are statically equivalent on a rigid body if they have the same resultant and the same moment about the same point (equilibrium of a rigid body, chapter 5). The sum of horizontal forces: \(X_2 + X_3 = X\). The moment about \(5\): the force \(X_2\) at the height \((1 - s)\,d\) has the moment \(-(1 - s)\,d\,X_2\), the force \(X_3\) at the height \(-s\,d\) has \(+s\,d\,X_3\), and the sum must be \(M\).
  5. From step 4: \(s\,d\,X_3 - (1-s)\,d\,X_2 = M\) and \(X_3 = X - X_2\) give \(X_2 = s\,X - M/d\) and \(X_3 = (1-s)\,X + M/d\). Statics determines the vertical forces only by their sum, \(Y_2 + Y_3 = Y\), and the division in the ratio \(s\) is the one that the interpolation in equation (19.10) gives (the second row of the matrix \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}\)). The column \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}^\T\,\mat F_5\) has, by the first and third rows of the matrix \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}\), exactly the entries \(s\,X - M/d\) and \((1-s)\,X + M/d\), and by the second row \(s\,Y\) and \((1 - s)\,Y\).

Source: COOK (8.5-3) (PDF p. 293): the rotation of node \(5\) is the difference of the normal displacements of nodes \(2\) and \(3\) divided by the distance, the translations are interpolated linearly. ONATE1 §9.3 (PDF pp. 334-335) likewise expresses the displacements of one element by the nodal displacements of the other. The proof from the rigid displacement is ours.

The proof showed that the relations (19.10) are exact for a rigid displacement of the edge (to first order) and that the same matrix \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}\), which turns the displacements of nodes \(2\) and \(3\) into the displacements of the end of the beam, carries the forces back when transposed. We now write the coupling of the wall and the beam as an elimination: the dependent unknowns \((u_5, v_5, \varphi_5)\) of the beam are expressed by the master unknowns of the wall by equation (19.10) (definition 19.25), and from the matrix of the beam \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) we get the matrix \(\mat T^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat T\) and add it to the wall (theorem 19.26). The theorem requires that every nonzero \(\vek d\) that satisfies the constraint has positive energy. This holds here. At zero energy no bar is stretched. Since nodes \(1\) and \(4\) are clamped, the elongations of the bars \(1\)-\(2\) and \(4\)-\(2\) are the components of the displacement of node \(2\) in two different directions, so node \(2\) stays at rest, and likewise node \(3\) with the bars \(4\)-\(3\) and \(1\)-\(3\). By the constraint (19.10) then \(u_5 = v_5 = \varphi_5 = 0\). The beam has zero energy only at \(\bar u_{\mathrm{h}}' = 0\) and \(\bar v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\), that is in a rigid displacement, which is zero when the start neither moves nor turns, so the end \(6\) stays at rest too. The theorem therefore guarantees that the reduced matrix is positive definite and that the solution agrees with the multipliers.

The smallness of the rotation is an idealization that we must estimate. In a rigid turn by \(\varphi\) about the point \(5\) the exact horizontal displacement of the point at the height \(y\) is \(-y\,\sin\varphi\) and the vertical one \(y\,(\cos\varphi - 1)\), and the first order replaces them by \(-y\,\varphi\) and \(0\). The vertical error \(y\,(1 - \cos\varphi) \le y\,\varphi^2/2\) is, compared with the horizontal displacement \(y\,\varphi\), smaller by the factor \(\varphi/2\), so at \(|\varphi| \le 10^{-3}\) at most \(5\cdot 10^{-4}\). In the example below \(|\varphi| = 2{,}806\cdot 10^{-4}\) and for node \(2\) (\(y = d/2 = 0{,}1\) m) the vertical error is \(3{,}9\cdot 10^{-9}\) m, which is \(7\cdot 10^{-5}\) of the difference \(|u_3 - u_2| = 5{,}6\cdot 10^{-5}\) m.

Example 19.36 A beam on a wall of bars (nosilec na zidu iz palic)

For the model of figure 19.21 the matrix \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}\) at \(s = \tfrac12\) and \(d = 0{,}2\) m is

\[\mat T_{\mathrm{w}} = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac12 & 0 & \tfrac12 & 0 \\ 0 & \tfrac12 & 0 & \tfrac12 \\ -5 & 0 & 5 & 0 \end{pmatrix} ,\]

where the numbers in the third row are \(\mp 1/d\) in the unit \(\text{m}^{-1}\) and \(\varphi_5\) has no unit. There are \(4\) unknowns (the wall) and \(3\) (node \(6\)), \(7\) in all, as in COOK (PDF p. 293). The computation (num-07.py) gives: the smallest eigenvalue of the reduced matrix is \(1{,}52\cdot 10^{4}\), so it is positive definite as theorem 19.26 guarantees, the tip deflection is \(-1{,}9103\) mm, the rotation \(\varphi_5 = -2{,}806\cdot 10^{-4}\) and the displacement \(v_5 = -0{,}0353\) mm. The deflection is the sum of three parts: \(1{,}5944\) mm is the beam on a rigid wall, \(0{,}2806\) mm is the rotation of the edge times the length \(h = 1\) m, \(0{,}0353\) mm is the displacement of node \(5\) (the sum is \(1{,}9103\) mm). In the wall node \(2\) moves by \(u_2 = +0{,}02806\) mm and \(3\) by \(-0{,}02806\) mm: the upper fibre of the beam is stretched at the clamp, so node \(2\) is pulled out of the wall.

Statics checks the forces. The beam presses on the edge with the force \(100\) N downward and with the couple \(F\,h = 100\) N m clockwise, and the edge acts on it oppositely. The force of the surroundings on the end of the beam is therefore \(\mat F_5 = (X, Y, M) = (0, 100\ \text{N}, 100\ \text{N m})\), upward and counterclockwise. By lemma 19.35 (item 2, \(s = \tfrac12\)) the statically equivalent forces at nodes \(2\) and \(3\) are horizontal \(X_2 = -M/d = -500\) N and \(X_3 = +500\) N and vertical \(50\) N each, and the computation (num-07.py) gives the same column \(\mat T_{\mathrm{w}}^\T\,\mat F_5 = (-500, 50, 500, 50)\). The moment of these forces about the point \(5\) is \(-0{,}1\cdot(-500) - (-0{,}1)\cdot 500 = 100\) N m, so \(M\). These are the forces with which nodes \(2\) and \(3\) hold the beam. The beam acts on the nodes with the opposite forces: it pulls node \(2\) out of the wall with \(500\) N, pushes node \(3\) into it with \(500\) N and pulls both down with \(50\) N each, which agrees with the displacements \(u_2 > 0\) and \(u_3 < 0\). The wall takes the couple \(M\) as a pair of forces \(\pm 500\) N at the distance \(d\) and not as forces at one point.

What happens if we choose displacements that do not carry the rotation? For a beam we are used to the rotation being the difference of vertical displacements divided by the horizontal distance, \(\varphi \approx v'\). If we copy this habit to a vertical edge, we take \(\varphi_5 = (v_2 - v_3)/d\). In a rigid displacement of the edge \(v_2 = v_3\) at every rotation (step 2 of the proof of lemma 19.35), so this expression does not see the rotation, it records only the stretching of the edge. With it the computation gives the deflection \(-1{,}7170\) mm instead of \(-1{,}9103\) mm, so \(10\,\%\) too small, and the rotation \(\varphi_5 = -1{,}13\cdot 10^{-4}\) instead of \(-2{,}81\cdot 10^{-4}\). The error is smaller for a stronger wall and larger for a weaker one, since the wrong constraint underestimates the rotation of the edge, and for a weaker wall that rotation is a larger part of the deflection. With the bar cross-section \(800\ \text{mm}^2\) the error is by the same computation \(0{,}3\,\%\), with \(100\ \text{mm}^2\) \(2{,}3\,\%\) and with \(5\ \text{mm}^2\) \(27\,\%\). At a vertical edge the rotation is carried by the normal displacements \(u_2\) and \(u_3\), as figure 19.24 shows.

A last remark concerns the choice of the beam element. In this section we took the Euler-Bernoulli beam (definition 12.17), in which the rotation of the cross-section equals the slope of the axis, \(\varphi = \bar v'\). In the Timoshenko element (definition 12.31) the deflection and the tilt of the section \(\theta\) are independent unknowns, and the shear strain \(\gamma = v' - \theta\) can appear (definition 5.84). The constraint (19.10) ties the rotation of the cross-section to the rotation of the wall edge, since the section at the wall follows the edge, and that is the same unknown \(\varphi_5\) or \(\theta_5\) in both elements. The matrix \(\mat T\) is therefore the same, only the element matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) changes. In the Timoshenko element the section at the wall can yield in shear, in the Euler-Bernoulli one it cannot.

If the wall is made of plane or solid elements instead of bars, the idea stays the same: the displacements of the edge are interpolated from the nodes of the edge, the rotation is the difference of the normal displacements divided by the distance. How plane elements interpolate the displacements along an edge is treated in chapter 15. COOK (PDF p. 293) also knows another, ad hoc way (priložnostni način): the beam is extended into the wall by one or two elements that share the nodal displacements with the wall, while only the beam has rotations. The extension cannot turn rigidly without its nodes in the wall moving transversely to it, so the wall takes the moment by transverse forces at these nodes, much as by the pair of forces \(\pm 500\) N above. Since these forces are concentrated at single nodes, the stresses near the joint are not accurate, as COOK warns.

Let us sum up. A bar at a frame node gives a hinge (lemma 19.33), a beam on a single node without rotations can turn and the system is singular (lemma 19.34), a constraint that takes the rotation from two normal displacements (lemma 19.35) gives a system with a unique solution. Each of the three tells which unknowns can carry a moment. The elimination by theorem 19.26 is the same tool as for rigid links (section 19.6).
