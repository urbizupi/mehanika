We begin with the taut string met in chapter 13. There the string was at rest, and it had a wave equation, modes and frequencies. Now the string does not only move sideways, it also runs along its own length. The chapter shows what changes because of this, and it uses what we know from chapters 8 and 10, where we treated systems with finitely many degrees of freedom.

The words of the title mean something simple. A belt (jermen) is a band that runs over wheels and carries motion from one to another. Axially (aksialno) means along the axis, that is along the band through which the material runs. A moving continuum (gibajoči se zvezni sistem) is a body that we describe not by a few points but by all its points at once, and that moves as a whole. The title therefore says: we treat a belt as a continuous body through which material flows without end.

Why does this matter? A belt in a machine, a band saw between two wheels and a cable car between two towers are all such systems. For a steel strip of length 1 m, running at 40 m/s lowers the first frequency of vibration from 56.61 Hz to 49.55 Hz. At a high enough speed the frequencies fall to zero and the string loses stability, which a string at rest does not predict.

The chapter promises three things. First, from the balance of a piece of string we derive the equation for the running string and show that the waves in the two directions travel at different speeds. Next we find the modes of vibration, the speed at which the frequencies fall to zero, and a quantity that is conserved while the string runs. Last, we extend the equation to a belt with bending stiffness, turn it into finite elements and apply it to a drive with two pulleys, saying what our model leaves out.
