Chapter 16 Torsion

16.9 Bounds of the torsion constant and finite elements

We took the torsion constant \(J\) of the \(20 \times 40\) mm strip in section 16.5 from a series, because Prandtl's equation on a rectangle is solved by separation of variables. For an L-shaped section, a section with rounded corners or any other outline there is no such series. The engineer still has to know how far the shaft twists, and besides the number needs to know how much it may be off. In this section we show that the torsion constant allows such knowledge. Both boundary problems of sections 16.2 and 16.3 are the search for the smallest value of something. So every trial function, even a poor one, gives a bound for \(J\): the warping function from one side, Prandtl's function from the other. When we compute these two bounds on a mesh of triangles we obtain finite elements for torsion whose error we know.

16.9.1 Upper bound from the warping function

Take a prism in Saint-Venant torsion (definition 16.1) and try another function \(\tilde\psi\) in place of the true warping function \(\psi\), that is a trial displacement field \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = \alpha\tilde\psi\). By lemma 16.8 the two shear strains of this field are \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \alpha(\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{y}} - z)\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xz}} = \alpha(\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{z}} + y)\). Write the pair \((\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{y}} - z,\ \tilde\psi_{\mathrm{z}} + y)\) as the vector \(\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w\), where \(\vek w = (z, -y)\) is the field that every point of the section gets from the rotation of the section as a rigid body. The strain energy density is \(\tfrac12(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} + \sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\gamma_{\mathrm{xz}}) = \tfrac12 G\alpha^2\,|\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w|^2\), and the energy per unit length of the prism is \(\tfrac12 G\alpha^2 F(\tilde\psi)\) with the integral

\[F(\tilde\psi) = \int_{\mathrm{S}} |\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w|^2\,\mathrm{d}A , \qquad \vek w = (z, -y) . \tag{16.50}\]

The true warping function has energy \(\tfrac12 G J\alpha^2\) (by theorem 16.10 \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ\alpha\), and the work of the moment over the twist \(\alpha\) is half the product). So we expect \(F(\psi) = J\) and \(F(\tilde\psi) \ge J\) for every other function. This is the principle of minimum total potential energy (theorem 4.91) for the twisting of a prism. We prove it directly, because a section is not a body of that theorem.

Lemma 16.51 Warping functional and an upper bound of \(J\) (funkcional zvitja in zgornja meja \(J\))

Let \(S\) be the section of a prism in Saint-Venant torsion with the warping function \(\psi\) of theorem 16.10, whose second derivatives are continuous in the interior and first derivatives continuous up to the boundary. Let \(S\) be cut into finitely many pieces \(S_1, \dots, S_{\mathrm{m}}\) for which lemma 16.9 holds also for fields continuously differentiable up to the boundary of the piece (for a triangle chapter 15 proves it), meeting along whole edges. Let \(\tilde\psi\) be continuous on \(S\) and continuously differentiable up to the boundary on every piece. Then:

  1. \(F(\psi) = J\).
  2. \(J \le F(\tilde\psi)\).
  3. Equality \(J = F(\tilde\psi)\) holds exactly when \(\tilde\psi - \psi\) is a constant.
Idea

The difference \(F(\tilde\psi) - F(\psi)\) is the energy of the difference of the fields, because the mixed term vanishes through equilibrium and the free lateral surface.

Proof
  1. Let \(\vek q = \nabla\psi - \vek w\) and \(g = \tilde\psi - \psi\). The field \(\vek q\) is continuous up to the boundary. Its divergence is \(\nabla^2\psi - (\partial z/\partial y + \partial(-y)/\partial z) = 0 - 0\), because \(\psi\) is harmonic and \(\partial z/\partial y = \partial y/\partial z = 0\). On the boundary \(\vek q\cdot\vek n = \partial\psi/\partial n - (z\,n_{\mathrm{y}} - y\,n_{\mathrm{z}}) = 0\) by the boundary condition of theorem 16.10.
  2. From \(\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w = \vek q + \nabla g\) it follows that \(F(\tilde\psi) = \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\vek q|^2\,\mathrm{d}A + 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\nabla g\cdot\vek q\,\mathrm{d}A + \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla g|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), where the first term is \(F(\psi)\).
  3. On a piece \(S_{\mathrm{k}}\) the field \(g\,\vek q\) has divergence \(\nabla g\cdot\vek q + g\,\mathrm{div}\,\vek q = \nabla g\cdot\vek q\). By lemma 16.9, \(\int_{\mathrm{S}_\mathrm{k}}\nabla g\cdot\vek q\,\mathrm{d}A = \oint_{\partial \mathrm{S}_\mathrm{k}}g\,\vek q\cdot\vek n_{\mathrm{k}}\,\mathrm{d}s\).
  4. Add over the pieces. An edge that two pieces share has opposite normals for the two, while \(g\) and \(\vek q\) are continuous there, so the contributions cancel. On the boundary \(\partial S\) we have \(\vek q\cdot\vek n = 0\). So \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}\nabla g\cdot\vek q\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\).
  5. From steps 2 and 4, \(F(\tilde\psi) = F(\psi) + \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla g|^2\,\mathrm{d}A \ge F(\psi)\). Equality requires \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla g|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\), so \(\nabla g = 0\) on every piece (a continuous nonnegative function with zero integral is zero everywhere, as in the proof of lemma 16.17). A continuous function with zero gradient on every piece is constant on a connected section.
  6. The function \(\tilde\psi = 0\) is one of the admissible ones (one piece). From step 5, \(F(0) = F(\psi) + \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla\psi|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), and \(F(0) = \int_{\mathrm{S}}(z^2 + y^2)\,\mathrm{d}A = I_{\mathrm{p}}\). By lemma 16.12, \(J = I_{\mathrm{p}} - \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla\psi|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), so \(F(\psi) = J\). This proves points 1 and 2, point 3 is in step 5.

Source: The idea is the principle of minimum energy from chapter 4, recast for twisting. The form of \(F\) and step 6 are ours, with the equations of section 16.2. SADD section 9.3.2 (PDF pp. 251-252) writes the same problem for the warping function with other letters (his \(z\) is the bar axis, the section has \(x\) and \(y\)).

The function \(\tilde\psi\) does not have to be chosen so that it satisfies the boundary condition \(\partial\tilde\psi/\partial n = z\,n_{\mathrm{y}} - y\,n_{\mathrm{z}}\). That condition is natural, as zero force at a free end is for the bar (chapter 6), and the functional finds it itself. This is the whole strength of the lemma: it asks only for a continuous function.

We test the lemma on a circle of radius \(R = 20\) mm, where we know the answer. For the circle \(\psi = 0\) (theorem 16.3), \(J = I_{\mathrm{p}} = \pi R^4/2 = 251\,327\) mm\(^4\). Take \(\tilde\psi = c\,y z\) with an unknown number \(c\). Then \(\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w = ((c - 1)z,\ (c + 1)y)\) and

\[F(c\,yz) = (c - 1)^2\int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2\,\mathrm{d}A + (c + 1)^2\int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2\,\mathrm{d}A = (c^2 + 1)\,I_{\mathrm{p}} , \tag{16.51}\]

because \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2\,\mathrm{d}A = I_{\mathrm{p}}/2\) (the circle is symmetric under swapping \(y\) and \(z\)) and the terms \(\mp 2c\) cancel. The smallest value is at \(c = 0\) and equals \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\), so it is exact. At \(c = 0{,}3\) we get \(F = 1{,}09\,I_{\mathrm{p}} = 273\,947\) mm\(^4\), which is \(9{,}00\) % above \(J\). So the bound holds, and it also says that the warping function of the circle really is zero: every \(c \ne 0\) adds energy.

16.9.2 Lower bound from Prandtl's function

With the warping we caught \(J\) from above. Now we need it from below. Divide Prandtl's function by \(G\alpha\) to get \(\hat\Phi = \Phi/(G\alpha)\). By theorem 16.16 we then have \(\nabla^2\hat\Phi = -2\) in \(S\), \(\hat\Phi = 0\) on \(\partial S\) and \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A = GJ\alpha\), so

\[J = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\hat\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A . \tag{16.52}\]

The function \(\hat\Phi\) has units mm\(^2\), and we see it as Prandtl's function for \(G\alpha = 1\).

Lemma 16.52 Prandtl functional and a lower bound of \(J\) (funkcional Prandtlove funkcije in spodnja meja \(J\))

Let \(S\) be a section as in theorem 16.16 with \(\hat\Phi\) twice continuously differentiable up to the boundary, cut into pieces as in lemma 16.51. Let \(f\) be continuous on \(S\), continuously differentiable up to the boundary on every piece, \(f = 0\) on \(\partial S\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A > 0\). Then

\[J \ge \frac{4\Big(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\Big)^2}{\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A} , \tag{16.53}\]

and equality holds exactly when \(f = c\,\hat\Phi\) for some constant \(c\).

Idea

The square \(|\nabla f - t\nabla\hat\Phi|^2\) is nonnegative for every \(t\). We choose the best \(t\).

Proof
  1. The field \(f\,\nabla\hat\Phi\) has divergence \(\nabla f\cdot\nabla\hat\Phi + f\,\nabla^2\hat\Phi = \nabla f\cdot\nabla\hat\Phi - 2f\) on a piece. By lemma 16.9 on every piece the integral equals the flux through the boundary of the piece. When we add, the contributions of a shared edge cancel (the fields \(f\) and \(\nabla\hat\Phi\) are continuous, the normals are opposite), and on the boundary of the section \(f = 0\). So \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}\nabla f\cdot\nabla\hat\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  2. For \(f = \hat\Phi\), step 1 and equation (16.52) give \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla\hat\Phi|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\hat\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A = J\).
  3. For every real number \(t\), \(0 \le \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f - t\nabla\hat\Phi|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A - 4t\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A + t^2J\) by steps 1 and 2.
  4. At \(t = \frac{2}{J}\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\) this is \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A - \frac{4}{J}\big(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\big)^2 \ge 0\). Multiply by \(J > 0\) and divide by \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\).
  5. Equality requires \(\nabla f = t\nabla\hat\Phi\) everywhere, so \(f - t\hat\Phi\) is a constant that is zero on the boundary.

Source: The computation is ours, with the equations of section 16.3. The assumption of two derivatives up to the boundary carries the same reservation as lemma 16.15: for a rectangle the second derivatives jump at the corners and the cut near a corner is not proved, SADD cites it (PDF pp. 246-251). The lower bound for the rectangle in this section therefore rests on that cited step.

In the language of energy the lemma is the principle of minimum complementary energy (theorem 4.100). By section 16.3 the stresses from any \(f\) are in equilibrium and the free lateral surface is unloaded, and the complementary energy per unit length is \(\tfrac{1}{2G}\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) at the moment \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\). The true stress picture has the smallest complementary energy \(M_{\mathrm{x}}^2/(2GJ)\). The same inequality follows. The lemma also says what matters about \(f\): the function must be zero on the whole boundary, and its height plays no role, since it cancels in the quotient. We test this.

On a circle of radius \(R\) take \(f = R^2 - r^2\), which is the exact shape (section 16.1). The integrals over the circle by the polar change of variables of theorem 4.59 are \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\pi\int_0^R(R^2 - r^2)\,r\,\mathrm{d}r = \pi R^4/2\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 2\pi\int_0^R 4r^2\cdot r\,\mathrm{d}r = 2\pi R^4\), and the quotient \(4(\pi R^4/2)^2/(2\pi R^4) = \pi R^4/2 = I_{\mathrm{p}}\) equals the exact value. By contrast \(f = (R^2 - r^2)^2\) has a zero on the boundary but the wrong shape: \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A = \pi R^6/3\), \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 4\pi R^8/3\), and the lower bound is \(\pi R^4/3 = \tfrac23I_{\mathrm{p}}\), which is \(167\,552\) mm\(^4\) at \(R = 20\) mm, \(33{,}33\) % below \(J\). The bound holds but is poor.

16.9.3 Bounds on the \(20 \times 40\) mm strip

Now take the strip of section 16.5 with half sides \(a = 10\) mm along \(y\) and \(b = 20\) mm along \(z\), where we know the exact value \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) from the series and use it only for comparison. For the lower bound we need a function that is zero on all four sides. Such a function is \(f = (a^2 - y^2)(b^2 - z^2)\), the function \(\Phi_{\mathrm{t}}\) of example 16.21, which was not a solution of the equation there. Here its accuracy does not matter, because we are only looking for the best function of this form.

We draw it together with the true function [figure 16.33]. Everywhere it is the same \(20 \times 40\) mm section, at the scale \(0{,}8\) cm per \(10\) mm, with the \(y\) axis across the width and the \(z\) axis along the length. In the left section are the level lines of the function \(f\) at \(0{,}2\), \(0{,}4\), \(0{,}6\) and \(0{,}8\) of the peak, with the peak \(100\) mm\(^2\) at the centre (\(f\) has been multiplied by the constant \(c = 0{,}0025\), which the quotient (16.53) does not feel). In the middle section are the same lines of the true function \(\hat\Phi\), computed with the series of section 16.5, with peak \(91{,}1\) mm\(^2\). Both families are rounded closed curves, but the true lines are flatter near the middle of the longer side, because there \(\hat\Phi\) changes more slowly with \(z\). On the axis \(y = 0\), \(\hat\Phi\) falls from \(z = 0\) to \(z = 5\) mm by \(3{,}2\) % (from \(91{,}10\) to \(88{,}21\) mm\(^2\)), the trial function by \(6{,}25\) %. In a very long strip \(\hat\Phi\) would be nearly independent of \(z\) in the middle (section 16.6), at the ratio \(2\) this is only beginning.

We compute the quotient (16.53) for \(f\). The integrals are \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{-\mathrm{a}}^{a}(a^2 - y^2)\,\mathrm{d}y\int_{-\mathrm{b}}^{b}(b^2 - z^2)\,\mathrm{d}z = \tfrac{4a^3}{3}\cdot\tfrac{4b^3}{3} = 14\,222\,222\) mm\(^4\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac{128}{45}a^3b^3(a^2 + b^2) = 1{,}1378\cdot10^{10}\) mm\(^6\) (the term \(4y^2(b^2 - z^2)^2\) and the term \(4z^2(a^2 - y^2)^2\) give \(\tfrac{128}{45}a^3b^5\) and \(\tfrac{128}{45}a^5b^3\)). The quotient is \(\tfrac{40}{9}\,a^3b^3/(a^2 + b^2) = 71\,111\) mm\(^4\), which is \(-2{,}82\) % relative to \(J\). Since the trial function has only the form \(c\,(a^2 - y^2)(b^2 - z^2)\), we add a term in \(y^2\) with a second free constant: \(f = (a^2 - y^2)(b^2 - z^2)(c_1 + c_2y^2/a^2)\). The best choice (\(c_1 = 0{,}002476\), \(c_2 = 0{,}0001268\), from \(\partial/\partial c_1 = \partial/\partial c_2 = 0\) of the functional \(\tfrac12\int|\nabla f|^2 - 2\int f\)) gives \(71\,152\) mm\(^4\), \(-2{,}77\) %. The extra parameter improved the bound by barely \(0{,}05\) percentage points, because the error is not in the shape across the width (there the true function is nearly a parabola) but along the length, where the trial function has no flat part. For a thinner strip this gets worse: when \(b/a\) goes beyond all limits, \(\tfrac{40}{9}a^3b^3/(a^2 + b^2) \to \tfrac{40}{9}a^3b\), while the thin strip has by section 16.6 \(J \to \tfrac{16}{3}a^3b\), so the ratio is \(5/6\) and the error tends to \(-16{,}7\) %. The error \(-2{,}82\) % holds for the ratio \(2\), not for every strip.

Example 16.53 A function that is not zero on the whole boundary is no lower bound (funkcija, ki ni nič na vsem robu, ni spodnja meja)

Try \(f_2 = b^2 - z^2\) with peak \(b^2 = 400\) mm\(^2\). This function is zero on the shorter sides \(z = \pm b\), but on the long sides \(y = \pm a\) (the thick lines in the figure) it is not. The computation by equation (16.53) nevertheless gives \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f_2\,\mathrm{d}A = 2a\cdot\tfrac{4b^3}{3} = 213\,333\) mm\(^4\), \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f_2|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 2a\cdot\tfrac{8b^3}{3} = 426\,667\) mm\(^6\) and the quotient \(426\,667\) mm\(^4\), which is \(5{,}83\,J\). That is more than \(J\), so it is not a bound. Step 1 of the proof of lemma 16.52 has failed: the boundary term \(\oint f_2\,\partial\hat\Phi/\partial n\,\mathrm{d}s\) on the long sides \(y = \pm a\) is not zero, because \(f_2\) is not zero there and \(\partial\hat\Phi/\partial n\) is largest in absolute value there (the shear stress is largest at the middle of the long side, section 16.5). The same failure happens for \(f = 1\): the gradient is zero and the quotient has no value, because the denominator vanishes, and at the same time \(f\) is not zero on the boundary. The lemma asks for both, zero on the whole boundary and nonzero energy.

For the upper bound we need trial warping functions. The rectangle is a single piece and lemma 16.9 holds for it. Try \(\tilde\psi = k\,yz\), where \(k\) is a number. As for the circle, \(\nabla\tilde\psi - \vek w = ((k - 1)z,\ (k + 1)y)\) and

\[F(k\,yz) = (k - 1)^2\int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2\,\mathrm{d}A + (k + 1)^2\int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2\,\mathrm{d}A , \qquad \int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac{4a^3b}{3} = 26\,667 , \quad \int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac{4ab^3}{3} = 106\,667 \ \text{mm}^4 . \tag{16.54}\]

At \(k = 0\) (no warping, the assumption of section 16.1) \(F = I_{\mathrm{p}} = 133\,333\) mm\(^4\), \(82{,}2\) % above \(J\). At \(k = 0{,}3\), \(F = 97\,333\) (\(+33{,}0\) %). The derivative with respect to \(k\) is zero at \(k^* = (\int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2 - \int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2)/I_{\mathrm{p}} = (b^2 - a^2)/(a^2 + b^2) = 0{,}600\), and then \(F = 4\int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2\int_{\mathrm{S}} z^2/I_{\mathrm{p}} = 85\,333\) mm\(^4\) (\(+16{,}6\) %). The same \(k^*\) is also the warping function of the ellipse with the same semi-axes (theorem 16.18). At \(k = 1\), \(F = 4\int_{\mathrm{S}} y^2 = \tfrac{16}{3}a^3b = 106\,667\) mm\(^4\). This is exactly the thin-strip value \(h\,t^3/3\) with \(h = 2b = 40\) mm and \(t = 2a = 20\) mm. So the function \(yz\) is the warping of the thin strip, and the upper estimate \(J < ht^3/3\) of theorem 16.33 is, for the rectangle, a consequence of lemma 16.51.

We put all the bounds on a number line [figure 16.34]. We draw the axis for \(J\) in units of \(10^3\) mm\(^4\) from \(60\) to \(140\), with marks every \(20\). On it sits the exact value \(J = 73{,}178\) from the series. To the left of it is the lower bound \(71{,}111\) from the function \(f\). To the right is the upper bound \(85{,}333\) from \(k = 0{,}6\), and also \(106{,}667\) (\(k = 1\)) and \(133{,}333\) (\(k = 0\)), which are worse.

The lower bound is \(2{,}82\) % from the exact value, the best upper bound \(16{,}6\) %. The difference is in the trial functions. The true warping function of the strip is by equation (16.33) \(yz\) minus a series that corrects \(yz\) near the shorter sides \(z = \pm b\). With one number \(k\) we cannot capture that correction. The trial function \(f\), on the other hand, has almost exactly the right shape across the width and lacks only the flat part.

Theorem 16.54 Bounds of the torsion constant (meji torzijske konstante)

Let \(S\) be a section of lemma 16.52 and lemma 16.51. Then for every trial function \(f\) of lemma 16.52 and every trial function \(\tilde\psi\) of lemma 16.51

\[\frac{4\Big(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A\Big)^2}{\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A} \ \le\ J\ \le\ F(\tilde\psi) . \tag{16.55}\]

For the \(20 \times 40\) mm strip (\(a = 10\) mm, \(b = 20\) mm) it therefore holds that

\[71\,111\ \text{mm}^4 \ \le\ J\ \le\ 85\,333\ \text{mm}^4 , \tag{16.56}\]

with \(f = (a^2 - y^2)(b^2 - z^2)\) and \(\tilde\psi = 0{,}6\,yz\). At \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 50\) N m and \(G = 75{,}385\) GPa the twist per unit length \(\alpha = M_{\mathrm{x}}/(GJ)\) is therefore between \(0{,}00777\) and \(0{,}00933\) rad/m.

Idea

The left inequality is lemma 16.52, the right one lemma 16.51. The numbers only have to be computed.

Proof
  1. The left inequality (16.55) is lemma 16.52, the right one lemma 16.51.
  2. For \(f\) on the strip: \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f\,\mathrm{d}A = 14\,222\,222\) mm\(^4\) and \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 1{,}1378\cdot10^{10}\) mm\(^6\) (above), the quotient is \(71\,111\) mm\(^4\).
  3. For \(\tilde\psi = 0{,}6\,yz\), by equation (16.54), \(F = 0{,}16\cdot106\,667 + 2{,}56\cdot26\,667 = 17\,067 + 68\,267 = 85\,333\) mm\(^4\).
  4. Twist: \(\alpha = M_{\mathrm{x}}/(GJ)\) with \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 50\,000\) N mm and \(G = 75\,385\) N/mm\(^2\) equals \(7{,}77\cdot10^{-6}\) rad/mm \(= 0{,}00777\) rad/m at \(J = 85\,333\) mm\(^4\), and \(0{,}00933\) rad/m at \(J = 71\,111\) mm\(^4\).

Source: Inequality (16.55) and the numbers are ours (num-09.py). SADD in Example 16.4 (PDF pp. 535-536) solves Prandtl's equation with linear triangular elements, but gives no bounds for the torsion constant.

The exact value \(0{,}00906\) rad/m lies in this range, which is \(19{,}4\) % of \(J\) wide. For an engineer this is already useful: the shaft has a stiffness of at least \(G\cdot71\,111\ \text{mm}^4 = 5361\) N m\(^2\) and at most \(6433\) N m\(^2\), and for the torsional twist we get a bound that depends on no series.

16.9.4 Mesh of triangles

Two bounds on a section raise a question: how to find better trial functions when we do not know by shape where the true one is flat? The answer is the same as for the bar in chapter 7. We cut the section into pieces and write the function on every piece as a straight line, in the plane as a linear function. The pieces are triangles, because three values at the corners determine a linear function \(c_0 + c_1y + c_2z\) exactly.

For the \(20 \times 40\) mm strip we take a mesh. We cut the section into \(n \times 2n\) squares and each of them with one diagonal into two right isosceles triangles. The figure shows \(n = 2\): squares of side \(10\) mm, \(2\) across the width and \(4\) along the length, \(16\) triangles in all. The corners of the triangles are nodes (vozlišča). In the figure there are \(15\) of them, of which 12 on the boundary (open circles) and 3 free (filled circles), inside the section on the line \(y = 0\) at \(z = -10\), \(0\), \(10\) mm.

Definition 16.55 Triangle mesh and piecewise linear function (mreža trikotnikov in odsekoma linearna funkcija)

A triangle mesh (mreža trikotnikov) of the section \(S\) is a cut of \(S\) into finitely many triangles that meet along whole edges or at corners, and whose edges cover \(\partial S\). A piecewise linear function (odsekoma linearna funkcija) \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a continuous function on \(S\) that is linear on every triangle. It is determined by the values at the nodes, \(f_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}N_{\mathrm{i}}\), where \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the piecewise linear function with value \(1\) at node \(i\) and \(0\) at the other nodes (shape function, oblikovna funkcija).

Explanation

It is continuous because the edge shared by two triangles carries on both a linear function of one variable with equal values at the end points, hence the same function. The shape functions are the same as for the bar in chapter 7 (definition 7.2), only we have pieces of a plane instead of pieces of a line.

Source: The definition is ours. The linear triangle in its general form will come in chapter 15.

First look at what one of the triangles does [figure 16.36]. The shaded triangle in the upper left corner has nodes 1, 2 and 3, node 1 at the right angle. Since the integral \(\int|\nabla f_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\) over it depends only on three values, we write it with a matrix that is a special case of the element matrix for heat conduction (chapter 14), because the operator is the same.

Lemma 16.56 Matrix of a right isosceles triangle (matrika enakokrakega pravokotnega trikotnika)

Let a triangle with legs of length \(s\) have node 1 at the right angle and nodes 2 and 3 on the legs. Let \(f_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^3\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}N_{\mathrm{i}}\) be linear on it. Then

\[\int|\nabla f_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \vek\varphi^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek\varphi , \qquad \mat K_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac12\begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} , \tag{16.57}\]

independent of \(s\), and \(\int N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{d}A = s^2/6\) for all three nodes.

Idea

Gradients of linear functions are constant, so the integral is the area times the scalar product of the gradients.

Proof
  1. The scalar product \(\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\nabla N_{\mathrm{j}}\) does not change under a rotation of the coordinates (a rotation preserves scalar products). So we may take coordinates \(\xi\), \(\eta\) along the legs with node 1 at the origin, node 2 at \((s, 0)\) and node 3 at \((0, s)\).
  2. The linear functions with value \(1\) at their own node and \(0\) at the others are \(N_1 = 1 - \xi/s - \eta/s\), \(N_2 = \xi/s\), \(N_3 = \eta/s\). Their gradients \((-1, -1)/s\), \((1, 0)/s\) and \((0, 1)/s\) are constant.
  3. The area is \(A = s^2/2\). The entry \((\mat K_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = A\,\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\nabla N_{\mathrm{j}}\) is for \(i = j = 1\) equal to \(\tfrac{s^2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{2}{s^2} = 1\), for \((1, 2)\) and \((1, 3)\) it is \(\tfrac{s^2}{2}\cdot\tfrac{-1}{s^2} = -\tfrac12\), for \((2, 2)\) and \((3, 3)\) it is \(\tfrac12\), for \((2, 3)\) it is \(0\). The length \(s\) cancels.
  4. The integral \(\int N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{d}A = A/3\) for a linear shape function of a triangle is proved in chapter 15 (the formula for the integral of area coordinates). With \(A = s^2/2\) this is \(s^2/6\).

Source: The computation is ours. We checked the matrix with \(s = 20\), \(10\), \(5\) mm in NumPy (num-09.py): the difference from the tabulated matrix is zero. COOK (PDF p. 483) states that torsion, with the warping function or with Prandtl's stress function, is described by the same equation as plane steady heat conduction, for the warping function with \(k = 1\).

The row sum \(1 - \tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0\) says that a constant function costs nothing, as for a bar without supports (lemma 7.16): this property will matter at once. All triangles of the mesh have legs along \(y\) or \(z\) and differ only in which corner has the right angle. Each contributes its matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\) at the places of its three nodes in the global matrix \(\mat K\), as when assembling bars (theorem 7.13). The sum over all triangles gives \(\int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = \vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi\).

Prandtl's function on the mesh

Take a piecewise linear \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) with value zero at all boundary nodes. Since the boundary of the section consists of triangle edges, \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) is zero on the whole boundary too (a linear function with zero values at the ends of an edge is zero there). So \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) is admissible in lemma 16.52. With the vector \(\vek b\) whose entry \(b_{\mathrm{i}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{d}A\) is twice the integral of the shape function (from lemma 16.56), \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} f_{\mathrm{h}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \tfrac12\vek b^\T\vek\varphi\) and the quotient of the lemma is \((\vek b^\T\vek\varphi)^2/(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi)\). Its largest value among functions with a zero on the boundary is the solution of the system

\[\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\,\vek\varphi = \vek b_{\mathrm{p}} , \qquad J_{\mathrm{h}}^{\Phi} = \vek b_{\mathrm{p}}^\T\vek\varphi = \vek\varphi^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi , \tag{16.58}\]

where the index \(p\) marks only the free nodes. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\) is positive definite: if \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi = \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla f_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\), then \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) is constant and hence zero, since it is zero on the boundary. The largest value of the quotient is indeed \(\vek b_{\mathrm{p}}^\T\vek\varphi\), because for every other \(\vek\varphi'\) we have \((\vek b_{\mathrm{p}}^\T\vek\varphi')^2 = (\vek\varphi'^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi)^2 \le (\vek\varphi'^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi')(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi)\) (the same computation with a square as in the proof of lemma 16.52, with \(\int|\nabla\cdot|^2\) replaced by \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{pp}}\vek\varphi\)). For a node in the interior, the six triangles around it (at the right angle in two of them, at an acute angle in four) add up to \(K_{\mathrm{ii}} = 2\cdot1 + 4\cdot\tfrac12 = 4\), each of the four neighbours on legs gives \(-1\), and the neighbours on the diagonal give \(0\). Let us look at \(n = 2\).

For \(n = 2\) every free node has six triangles of area \(50\) mm\(^2\), so \(b_{\mathrm{i}} = 2\cdot6\cdot50/3 = 200\) mm\(^2\). The neighbours to the left and right lie on the boundary, where the value is zero, so the equations at the three free nodes have the form

\[4\varphi_1 - \varphi_2 = 200 , \qquad -\varphi_1 + 4\varphi_2 - \varphi_3 = 200 , \qquad -\varphi_2 + 4\varphi_3 = 200 . \tag{16.59}\]

From the symmetry \(\varphi_1 = \varphi_3\) and from the first equation \(\varphi_2 = 4\varphi_1 - 200\), into the second: \(-2\varphi_1 + 16\varphi_1 - 800 = 200\), so \(\varphi_1 = 71{,}43\) and \(\varphi_2 = 85{,}71\) mm\(^2\) (these are the numbers beside the free nodes in the figure). The true value at the centre is \(91{,}1\) mm\(^2\). The lower bound is \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi = 200\,(71{,}43 + 85{,}71 + 71{,}43) = 45\,714\) mm\(^4\), \(37{,}5\) % below \(J\).

Warping function on the mesh

For the upper bound take a piecewise linear \(\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{h}}\) with values \(\vek\psi\) at all nodes, since there is no condition on the boundary. By equation (16.50), \(F(\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{h}}) = \vek\psi^\T\mat K\vek\psi - 2\vek\psi^\T\vek b_{\mathrm{w}} + I_{\mathrm{p}}\), where \((b_{\mathrm{w}})_{\mathrm{i}} = \int_{\mathrm{S}}\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\vek w\,\mathrm{d}A\). The field \(\vek w = (z, -y)\) is linear and \(\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\) is constant on a triangle, so the contribution of a triangle is the area times the value at the centroid (the integral of a linear function over a triangle, chapter 15). At \(\mat K\vek\psi = \vek b_{\mathrm{w}}\) the smallest value is \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi = I_{\mathrm{p}} - \vek\psi^\T\vek b_{\mathrm{w}} = I_{\mathrm{p}} - \vek\psi^\T\mat K\vek\psi\), because for any other vector \(\vek\psi + \vek d\) the value of \(F\) is larger by \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \int_{\mathrm{S}}|\nabla d_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\,\mathrm{d}A \ge 0\), where \(d_{\mathrm{h}}\) is the piecewise linear function with the values \(\vek d\).

Example 16.57 The warping matrix without a fixed node is singular (matrika zvitja brez pritrjenega vozlišča je singularna)

For \(n = 2\) take all \(15\) nodes as free. The row sums of \(\mat K\) are zero (step 3 of lemma 16.56), so \(\mat K\) has the eigenvector \((1, \dots, 1)\) with eigenvalue zero: NumPy gives the smallest eigenvalue \(2{,}2\cdot10^{-17}\) and the second \(0{,}250\). The matrix is singular, so the system \(\mat K\vek\psi = \vek b_{\mathrm{w}}\) has no unique solution. This is not an error of the mesh: the warping function is determined up to a constant, as in lemma 16.11, and \(\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{h}} + c\) has the same energy. At the same time \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}}(b_{\mathrm{w}})_{\mathrm{i}} = \int_{\mathrm{S}}\nabla(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}})\cdot\vek w\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\), because \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\) and the gradient of a constant is zero, so the system is solvable. We fix one node, the lower left one (the square in the right figure), \(\psi_1 = 0\), and the matrix becomes positive definite: from \(\vek\psi^\T\mat K\vek\psi = 0\) it follows that \(\nabla\tilde\psi_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), so a constant function, zero at the fixed node. The result is \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi = 87\,619\) mm\(^4\). If we fix the node at the centre or at the opposite corner \((a, b)\) instead, \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi\) is the same (\(87\,619\)), and the two fields differ only by a constant (the largest difference after subtracting the mean is \(1{,}6\cdot10^{-13}\) mm\(^2\)). Without fixing, a program reports a singular matrix, with fixing the answer does not depend on the choice of node.

The example answers a question every program must have: which node do we fix? The answer is any, because \(F\) does not feel a constant. For Prandtl's function this question does not arise, because the boundary is already fixed by the zero.

Corollary 16.58 Bounds from a triangle mesh (meje iz mreže trikotnikov)

Let the section \(S\) be a polygon that meets the assumptions of lemmas 16.51 and 16.52, and \(\mathcal{T}\) a triangle mesh (definition 16.55). Then

\[J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi\ \le\ J\ \le\ J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi , \tag{16.60}\]

where \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi\) is the solution of equation (16.58) and \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi = I_{\mathrm{p}} - \vek\psi^\T\mat K\vek\psi\) for \(\mat K\vek\psi = \vek b_{\mathrm{w}}\). If \(\mathcal{T}'\) is obtained from \(\mathcal{T}\) so that every triangle is a union of triangles of \(\mathcal{T}'\), then \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi(\mathcal{T}) \le J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi(\mathcal{T}')\) and \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi(\mathcal{T}') \le J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi(\mathcal{T})\).

Idea

The bounds are lemma 16.52 and lemma 16.51 with piecewise linear functions. A finer mesh has more functions, so the maximum is larger and the minimum smaller.

Proof
  1. A piecewise linear function with zero values at the boundary nodes is zero on the boundary, continuous and continuously differentiable on every triangle, so admissible in lemma 16.52. By equation (16.58), \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi\) is the largest value of the quotient over all such functions, so by the lemma \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi \le J\).
  2. Every piecewise linear function is admissible in lemma 16.51, and \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi\) is the smallest \(F\) over all these functions, so \(J \le J_{\mathrm{h}}^\psi\).
  3. If every triangle of \(\mathcal{T}\) is a union of triangles of \(\mathcal{T}'\), every function linear on the triangles of \(\mathcal{T}\) is also linear on the smaller ones, and continuity is kept. The space of functions for \(\mathcal{T}\) is thus a part of the space for \(\mathcal{T}'\). A maximum over a larger set is not smaller, a minimum over a larger set is not larger.

Source: The inequality and the numbers are ours (num-09.py). The lower bound for a rectangle requires the step that lemma 16.52 cites at a corner (see there).

On the strip, for the mesh in which every square is divided into four (from \(n\) to \(2n\)) and every triangle becomes a union of four smaller ones, point 3 holds. We checked it: the function of the mesh \(n = 2\), drawn on the mesh \(n = 4\), has the same value of the quotient (\(45\,714\) mm\(^4\)), and likewise \(n = 4\) and \(n = 8\) (\(65\,284\) mm\(^4\)).

Example 16.59 A mesh with no free node (mreža brez prostega vozlišča)

For \(n = 1\) the mesh has \(6\) nodes, all on the boundary, and \(4\) triangles. No node is free, so \(\vek\varphi = 0\) and \(J_{\mathrm{h}}^\Phi = 0\). Lemma 16.52 is not applicable for \(f_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) (\(\int|\nabla f_{\mathrm{h}}|^2 = 0\)), and the bound \(0 \le J\) holds but says nothing, so the row \(-100\) % in the table is not a program error. The upper bound at \(n = 1\) is \(106\,667\) mm\(^4\), \(45{,}8\) % above \(J\). A mesh becomes useful only when it has free nodes, as at \(n = 2\) (three free nodes, gap \(57{,}3\) %).

The mesh \(n = 2\) is one picture of everything: the values of \(\hat\Phi_{\mathrm{h}}\) at the free nodes on the left and the mesh for the warping \(\psi_{\mathrm{h}}\) on the right, with the fixed node. Which of the two meshes gives the lower and which the upper bound is told by inequality (16.60): on the left \(\hat\Phi_{\mathrm{h}}\) (value zero on the whole boundary) gives \(45\,714\), on the right \(\psi_{\mathrm{h}}\) (no condition on the boundary) gives \(87\,619\) mm\(^4\).

From history

Chapter 6 said in section 6.7 that at a lecture in 1941 Courant divided the problem of torsion of a hollow shaft into triangles and interpolated the stress function on each by straight lines from the values at the nodes (COOK, PDF p. 24). The stress function of torsion is in section 16.3 Prandtl's function, so Courant's interpolation by straight lines on triangles is exactly the piecewise linear function \(f_{\mathrm{h}}\) of this section, and its value at the nodes is the unknown of system (16.58). Courant treated a hollow shaft, where on a hole there is one more condition (section 16.8), which this section does not include.

Let us sum up what we have seen, because this section leans on several chapters. The warping function gives an upper bound of \(J\) (lemma 16.51, the principle of minimum potential energy), Prandtl's function a lower one (lemma 16.52, the principle of minimum complementary energy), both from arbitrary continuous functions, with no boundary conditions for \(\tilde\psi\) and with a zero on the boundary for \(f\). On a mesh of triangles both bounds become linear systems with the same matrix \(\mat K\), as for heat conduction in chapter 14 with source \(2\) and zero temperature on the boundary: the operator is the same, the phenomenon is not. The upper bound needs one node fixed, the lower does not. The one step that remained unproved is for the rectangle at the corners (lemma 16.52), and the numbers of the series and of the meshes agree with the two bounds.
