Chapter 10 Vibration of systems with several degrees of freedom and modal analysis (nihanje sistemov z več prostostnimi stopnjami in modalna analiza)

10.5 Zero frequencies and rigid-body modes (ničelne frekvence in togi načini)

So far every system has had supports that prevent every displacement without deformation. What if the supports are missing? Take bracket B (konzola B) from section 10.2: bar AC is horizontal and 4 m long, bar BC is 5 m long and points from the wall to C in the direction \((4, -3)/5\), both have axial stiffness \(EA = 19{,}6\) MN, and the pin C carries a weight of mass \(m = 1000\) kg (the truss of section 4.8.1, the stiffness matrix from section 4.6.7). We remove bar AC (figure 10.15). The pin C can now leave its former position without bar BC lengthening, at least to first order. What does the theory say about a motion that costs no energy?

First we compute the stiffness matrix. The pin C is displaced by \(\vek d = (u, v)\). By lemma 7.28 bar BC lengthens by the scalar product \(\vek n\cdot\vek d\) with the unit vector \(\vek n = (0{,}8,\ -0{,}6)\) along the bar, and its energy is \(\tfrac12 k\,(\vek n\cdot\vek d)^2\) with bar stiffness \(k = EA/L = 19{,}6\cdot 10^6\ \text{N}/5\ \text{m} = 3{,}92\) MN/m. Hence \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = k\,(\vek n\cdot\vek d)^2\), so

\[\mat K = k\,\vek n\,\vek n^\T = 10^6\begin{pmatrix} 2{,}5088 & -1{,}8816 \\ -1{,}8816 & 1{,}4112 \end{pmatrix}\ \text{N/m} . \tag{10.27}\]

This is the matrix of definition 7.4 without the contribution of bar AC, since the matrix of the whole truss has the entry \((1, 1)\) equal to \(7{,}4088\cdot 10^6\) N/m, bar AC contributes \(EA/4\ \text{m} = 4{,}9\cdot 10^6\) N/m to this entry, and \(2{,}5088\cdot 10^6\) N/m remains. The matrix is symmetric and \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = k\,(\vek n\cdot\vek d)^2 \ge 0\), so it is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15). The mass matrix is still \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\), positive definite. For the column \(\vek t = (0{,}6,\ 0{,}8)\) we have \(\vek n\cdot\vek t = 0{,}48 - 0{,}48 = 0\), hence \(\mat K\,\vek t = k\,\vek n\,(\vek n\cdot\vek t) = \vek 0\). For \(\vek n\) itself \(\mat K\,\vek n = k\,\vek n\), since \(\vek n\cdot\vek n = 1\). So we have two eigenpairs of the pair \((\mat K, \mat M)\) (definition 10.7):

\[\lambda_1 = 0 ,\ \vek\varphi_1 \parallel \vek t \qquad\text{and}\qquad \lambda_2 = \frac{k}{m} = 3920\ \text{s}^{-2} ,\ \vek\varphi_2 \parallel \vek n ,\]

the second corresponds to the circular frequency \(\omega_2 = 62{,}610\) s\(^{-1}\) and the frequency \(9{,}9647\) Hz. Since \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\), \(\mat M\)-orthogonal is the same as perpendicular, and indeed \(\vek t\cdot\vek n = 0\). In the mode \(\vek\varphi_2\) the pin C moves along the bar and stretches and shortens it, in the mode \(\vek\varphi_1\) it moves perpendicular to it and the bar does no work.

The spectral theorem (theorem 10.13) does not exclude this example. It assumed only that \(\mat K\) is symmetric and \(\mat M\) positive definite, and the eigenvalue zero is allowed. A second question opens. The expansion in theorem 10.21 divides by \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\), because for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) the solution of the modal equation is \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\cos\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t + \eta_{\mathrm{r}}'(0)\,\sin(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t)/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\). As \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) tends to zero, \(\cos\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t\) tends to \(1\) and \(\sin(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} t)/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) tends to \(t\). So we expect a solution \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = a + bt\): a pin that we give a velocity in the mode \(\vek\varphi_1\) keeps moving on with that velocity, since no force holds it back. This is not the instability of chapter 8: the shape stays the same, the strain energy is zero at all times, and no modal coordinate grows like \(\cosh\) or \(\sinh\) (for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} < 0\) it would grow). The following definition names such modes.

Definition 10.22 Zero-frequency mode, rigid-body mode and mechanism (način z ničelno frekvenco, togi način in mehanizem)

Let \((\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}})\) be an eigenpair of the pair \((\mat K, \mat M)\) from definition 10.7, where \(\mat K\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite and \(\mat M\) is positive definite. If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), then \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is a zero-frequency mode (način z ničelno frekvenco). If in addition \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is a rigid-body displacement of the system (definition 1.11), it is a rigid-body mode (togi način), otherwise a mechanism (mehanizem) as for the truss in definition 7.32. A mode with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) is an elastic mode (prožni način).

Source: CK §9.4 (PDF p. 285): "Rigid-body modes have a corresponding natural frequency of zero", "An eigenvalue problem that results in one or more zero eigenvalues ... is called a semidefinite eigenvalue problem" (PDF p. 286). The distinction between a rigid-body mode and a mechanism is from definition 7.32 (COOK, PDF p. 53), CK does not separate mechanisms.

The bracket without AC has one zero-frequency mode, and since B is at rest, the displacement of the pin C along the tangent is not a rigid-body displacement of the truss, so it is a mechanism. What is certain about such modes for every system is stated by a theorem. Its first part says when a frequency is zero, the second how many such modes there are, the third how their modal coordinate changes, the fourth how they lie relative to the elastic modes.

We must say what the first-order model claims. The model with the matrix \(\mat K\) says that the pin C travels along the tangent. The real motion is a rotation of bar BC about B, that is, motion along a circle of radius \(L = 5\) m. Take a displacement \(s = 10\) mm along \(\vek t\). The bar lengthens by \(\sqrt{L^2 + s^2} - L = 9{,}9999\cdot 10^{-6}\) m, which is \(s^2/(2L)\), and its energy is \(\tfrac12 k\,(10^{-5}\ \text{m})^2 = 1{,}96\cdot 10^{-4}\) J. The same displacement along the bar would cost \(\tfrac12 k\,s^2 = 196\) J, a million times more: for a displacement along the tangent the lengthening is in the ratio \(s/(2L) = 0{,}001\) to the lengthening for a displacement along the bar, and the energy is proportional to the square of the lengthening. As long as the pin moves by a small part of the bar length, the first-order mechanism is a good model. For a displacement comparable to \(L\) it is replaced by a rotation about B, which chapter 8 treats with the angle as a free coordinate.

Theorem 10.23 Zero frequencies of a positive semidefinite stiffness matrix (ničelne frekvence pri pozitivno semidefinitni togostni matriki)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric positive semidefinite and \(\mat M\) a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\). Let \(\lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_{\mathrm{n}}\) and the \(\mat M\)-orthonormal (definition 10.10) \(\vek\varphi_1, \dots, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{n}}\) be the eigenvalues and modes of theorem 10.13, \(\mathcal N\) the set of columns \(\vek w\) with \(\mat K\,\vek w = \vek 0\), and \(\vek Q(t)\) a continuous column. Then:

  1. All eigenvalues satisfy \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} \ge 0\), and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) exactly when \(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek 0\).
  2. A column \(\vek w\) lies in \(\mathcal N\) exactly when it has zero energy, \(\vek w^\T\mat K\,\vek w = 0\). The number \(z\) of zero eigenvalues equals the largest number of linearly independent columns in \(\mathcal N\), and every column of \(\mathcal N\) is a linear combination of the modes \(\vek\varphi_1, \dots, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\).
  3. Every column of \(\mathcal N\) is \(\mat M\)-orthogonal to every elastic mode, \(\vek w^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) for \(\lambda_{\mathrm{s}} > 0\).
  4. For \(r \le z\) the modal coordinate (definition 10.19) of the solution of \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek Q(t)\) equals
    \[\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) + \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\,t + \int_0^t\!\!\int_0^s \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}s , \tag{10.28}\]
    where \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q(0)\) and \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}(0)\).
Proof

We must show four points. The first follows from the energy of a mode, the second from lemma 7.16 and the expansion in modes, the third from the symmetry of the matrix \(\mat K\), the fourth from the modal equation. We always use \(\mat M\)-orthonormality, \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}} = 1\) for \(r = s\) and \(0\) otherwise.

  1. From \(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) we get, multiplying by \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\), \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\). The matrix \(\mat K\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15), hence \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} \ge 0\).
  2. If \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), then by step 1 \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), so by lemma 7.16 \(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek 0\). If \(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek 0\), then \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) by step 1. This is the first point. The first sentence of the second point is lemma 7.16.
  3. Let \(\vek w \in \mathcal N\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{s}} > 0\). From \(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}} = \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}\mat M\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\) and the symmetry of \(\mat K\), \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M = \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(\mat K\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}})^\T = \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat K\). Hence \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\,\vek w = \lambda_{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat K\,\vek w = 0\). This is the third point.
  4. By corollary 10.14, \(\vek w = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek w)\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\). By step 3 the terms with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) vanish, so \(\vek w\) is a combination of modes with \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). Since \(\lambda_1 \le \dots \le \lambda_{\mathrm{n}}\), these are the first \(z\) modes \(\vek\varphi_1, \dots, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\).
  5. The modes \(\vek\varphi_1, \dots, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{z}}\) lie in \(\mathcal N\) (step 2) and are linearly independent: from \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} c_{\mathrm{r}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek 0\) we get, multiplying by \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat M\), \(c_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) (definition 6.22).
  6. Let \(\vek w_1, \dots, \vek w_{\mathrm{p}} \in \mathcal N\) be linearly independent. By step 4, \(\vek w_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{z} c_{\mathrm{rj}}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\). If \(p > z\), the homogeneous system \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}} c_{\mathrm{rj}}x_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\) for \(r = 1, \dots, z\) would by lemma 10.9 have a nonzero solution \((x_1, \dots, x_{\mathrm{p}})\), and \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}} x_{\mathrm{j}}\vek w_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}\big(\sum_{\mathrm{j}} c_{\mathrm{rj}}x_{\mathrm{j}}\big)\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek 0\) would contradict independence. So \(p \le z\), and step 5 shows that \(p = z\) is attained. This completes the second point.
  7. By theorem 10.20, \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q(t)\), so for \(r \le z\) we get \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = f(t)\) with the continuous \(f = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q\). By theorem 9.36 with characteristic polynomial \(s^2\) (the root \(0\) twice) this equation has exactly one solution of order 2 for given \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\) and \(\dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\). The initial values are \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\vek q(0)\) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}(0)\) from the relation \(\eta = \mat\Phi^\T\mat M\,\vek q\) (definition 10.19).
  8. The function \(f\) is continuous, so by theorem 3.40 (point 2) the function \(g(s) = \int_0^s f(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\) is differentiable with \(g' = f\). By the same point of the theorem, applied to the continuous function \(g\), the function \(\eta(t) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) + \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\,t + \int_0^t g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\) is differentiable with \(\eta'(t) = \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0) + g(t)\), and this is differentiable with \(\eta'' = g' = f\). The function satisfies the equation and the initial values \(\eta(0) = \eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\), \(\eta'(0) = \dot\eta_{\mathrm{r}}(0)\), so by step 7 it is that solution. This is equation (10.28).

Source: CK (11.54) and (11.55) (PDF p. 371): the coordinates of rigid-body modes are obtained by double integration. CK §9.4 (PDF pp. 285-286), CK Example 10.2 (PDF pp. 305-306, "Since det(K) = 0, K is a singular matrix"). The proof is not from the source.

The theorem showed that the whole theory of the previous sections accepts positive semidefiniteness, except that at zero eigenvalues straight-line motion replaces oscillation. The last point also says how many times the force must be integrated: twice, as for a freely moving particle. For \(z \ge 2\) the zero-frequency modes are not determined uniquely: any \(\mat M\)-orthonormal basis of the space \(\mathcal N\) is allowed (CK, PDF p. 311, "these two rigid-body modes are not unique"). The elastic modes, however, are by the third point all \(\mat M\)-orthogonal to the whole of \(\mathcal N\).

Let us test it on an example that has a rigid-body mode. Two railway wagons of masses \(m_1 = 20\) t and \(m_2 = 30\) t, joined by a buffer of stiffness \(k = 2\cdot 10^6\) N/m, move on a straight track without friction. The displacements of the wagons along the track from the position with the unstretched buffer are \(q_1\) and \(q_2\). The buffer is an ideal spring, the wagons are point masses. The kinetic energy is \(\tfrac12(m_1\dot q_1^2 + m_2\dot q_2^2)\), the potential energy \(\tfrac12 k\,(q_2 - q_1)^2\), so by theorem 10.2

\[\mat M = \begin{pmatrix} m_1 & 0 \\ 0 & m_2 \end{pmatrix} ,\qquad \mat K = k\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} .\]

The energy \(k\,(q_2 - q_1)^2\) is zero exactly when \(q_1 = q_2\), so \(\mathcal N\) is the line of columns \(c\,(1, 1)\), the wagons move by the same distance, and this is a rigid-body displacement (definition 1.11). By theorem 10.23, \(z = 1\), \(\lambda_1 = 0\) and \(\vek\varphi_1 = (1, 1)/\sqrt{m_1 + m_2} = 0{,}0044721\,(1, 1)\) kg\(^{-1/2}\). By the third point the second mode must be \(\mat M\)-orthogonal to it, \(m_1 q_1 + m_2 q_2 = 0\), so \(q_2/q_1 = -m_1/m_2 = -2/3\): the wagons move in opposite directions, the lighter wagon moves \(3/2\) times as far as the heavier one, because the sum \(m_1 q_1 + m_2 q_2\) must be zero. The column \(\vek q = (m_2, -m_1)\) is \(\mat M\)-orthogonal to \(\vek\varphi_1\), since \(m_2 m_1 - m_1 m_2 = 0\). By corollary 10.14, \(\vek q = c_1\vek\varphi_1 + c_2\vek\varphi_2\) with \(c_1 = \vek\varphi_1^\T\mat M\,\vek q = 0\), so \(\vek q = c_2\vek\varphi_2\), and by the first point of the theorem (in step 1 of the proof \(\vek\varphi_2^\T\mat K\,\vek\varphi_2 = \lambda_2\) with \(\vek\varphi_2^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi_2 = 1\))

\[\lambda_2 = \frac{\vek q^\T\mat K\,\vek q}{\vek q^\T\mat M\,\vek q} = \frac{k\,(m_1 + m_2)^2}{m_1 m_2^2 + m_2 m_1^2} = \frac{k\,(m_1 + m_2)}{m_1 m_2} = \frac{2\cdot 10^6\cdot 5\cdot 10^4}{6\cdot 10^8}\ \text{s}^{-2} = 166{,}67\ \text{s}^{-2} ,\]

where \(c_2^2\) cancels. Let us also check the eigenvalue equation: the first row of \(\mat K\,\vek q = \lambda_2\mat M\,\vek q\) is \(k\,(m_2 + m_1) = \lambda_2 m_1 m_2\), which is exactly the expression above, and the second row gives the same. The circular frequency is \(12{,}910\) s\(^{-1}\), the frequency \(2{,}0547\) Hz, the period \(0{,}4867\) s. The \(\mat M\)-normalized mode is \(\vek\varphi_2 = (m_2, -m_1)/\sqrt{m_1 m_2\,(m_1 + m_2)} = (0{,}0054772,\ -0{,}0036515)\) kg\(^{-1/2}\).

Figure 10.16 shows both modes. In the elastic mode a pattern appears: the centre of mass, where \(m_1 q_1 + m_2 q_2 = 0\), is at rest. This is no accident, the cause is the third point of the theorem: the condition of \(\mat M\)-orthogonality to the rigid-body mode \((1, 1)\) is exactly the condition that the sum \(m_1 q_1 + m_2 q_2\) be zero.

Two initial states show what the fourth point says. The first: wagon 1 moves at \(1\) m/s towards the resting wagon 2, the buffer is unstretched, \(\vek q(0) = \vek 0\), \(\dot{\vek q}(0) = (1, 0)\) m/s. We get \(\dot\eta_1(0) = \vek\varphi_1^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}(0) = m_1\cdot 1/\sqrt{m_1 + m_2} = 89{,}44\) m kg\(^{1/2}\)/s and \(\eta_1 = \dot\eta_1(0)\,t\), so the rigid part is \(\vek\varphi_1\eta_1 = 0{,}4\,t\,(1, 1)\) m: both wagons move at the speed \(m_1\cdot 1/(m_1 + m_2) = 0{,}4\) m/s. The elastic part has \(\dot\eta_2(0) = \vek\varphi_2^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}(0) = 109{,}54\) m kg\(^{1/2}\)/s and \(\eta_2 = \dot\eta_2(0)\sin(\omega_2 t)/\omega_2\). The buffer shortens and stretches between \(\pm 77{,}46\) mm, which is exactly the relative speed \(1\) m/s divided by \(\omega_2 = 12{,}910\) s\(^{-1}\), and the largest force in the buffer is \(154{,}9\) kN. Of the initial kinetic energy \(\tfrac12 m_1\cdot 1^2 = 10\,000\) J, \(\tfrac12(m_1 + m_2)\,0{,}4^2 = 4000\) J is in the rigid part (a share \(m_1/(m_1 + m_2) = 40\ \%\)) and \(6000\) J in the oscillation.

The second: a locomotive pulls wagon 2 with a constant force \(F = 60\) kN, \(\vek Q = (0, F)\), from rest. The static computation \(\mat K\,\vek q = \vek Q\) has no solution, since \(\vek\varphi_1^\T\mat K\,\vek q = (\mat K\,\vek\varphi_1)^\T\vek q = 0\) for every \(\vek q\) while \(\vek\varphi_1^\T\vek Q = F/\sqrt{m_1 + m_2}\) is not zero: a force with a component in the rigid-body mode accelerates the whole system. By equation (10.28), \(\ddot\eta_1 = \vek\varphi_1^\T\vek Q\), so \(\vek\varphi_1\eta_1 = \tfrac12 a\,t^2\,(1, 1)\) with the acceleration \(a = F/(m_1 + m_2) = 1{,}2\) m/s\(^2\): in \(1\) s both wagons move by \(0{,}6\) m. The elastic part \(\ddot\eta_2 + \lambda_2\eta_2 = \vek\varphi_2^\T\vek Q = -219{,}09\) m kg\(^{1/2}\)/s\(^2\) has the constant solution \(\eta_2 = -219{,}09/166{,}67 = -1{,}3145\) m kg\(^{1/2}\), so the displacements are \(\vek\varphi_2\eta_2 = (-7{,}2,\ 4{,}8)\) mm: the buffer is stretched by \(12\) mm and pulls wagon 1 with the force \(k\cdot 0{,}012\ \text{m} = 24\) kN, which is exactly \(m_1 a = 20\,000\cdot 1{,}2\) N. The elastic part is thus the static stretch that the acceleration demands, and its shape is the elastic mode. Since the wagons start from rest, \(\eta_2 = -1{,}3145\,(1 - \cos\omega_2 t)\) m kg\(^{1/2}\) and the stretch of the buffer oscillates between \(0\) and \(24\) mm. A computation for the full system in NumPy (fourth-order Runge-Kutta) confirms the centre of mass at \(0{,}6000\) m at \(1\) s.

For the wagons the centre of mass is at rest in the elastic mode and moves by Newton's law in the rigid-body mode. Does this also hold for several point masses on a line? The answer is a corollary of the theorem.

Corollary 10.24 The centre of mass is at rest in the elastic modes (težišče miruje v prožnih načinih)

Let \(m_1, \dots, m_{\mathrm{n}}\) be the masses of points on a line, \(q_{\mathrm{i}}\) the displacement of the \(i\)-th point along the line, \(\mat M = \mathrm{diag}(m_1, \dots, m_{\mathrm{n}})\), \(\mat K\) a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix with \(\mat K\,\vek s = \vek 0\) for \(\vek s = (1, \dots, 1)\) (all points move by the same distance), and \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek Q(t)\), where \(Q_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the force on the \(i\)-th point. The displacement of the centre of mass (definition 8.73, for point masses a sum instead of an integral) is \(q_{\mathrm{T}} = \vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek q/m_\mathrm{c}\) with the total mass \(m_\mathrm{c} = \vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek s = \sum m_{\mathrm{i}}\). Then:

  1. \(m_\mathrm{c}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{T}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} Q_{\mathrm{i}}\): the centre of mass moves like a single point of mass \(m_\mathrm{c}\) under the sum of the external forces.
  2. For every elastic mode \(\vek\varphi\) (definition 10.22), \(\vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi = 0\): the centre of mass is at rest in such an oscillation.
Proof

We must show two equations, the first from the equation of motion, the second from the third point of theorem 10.23.

  1. Multiply the equation of motion by \(\vek s^\T\) from the left: \(\vek s^\T\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + \vek s^\T\mat K\,\vek q = \vek s^\T\vek Q\). By symmetry \(\vek s^\T\mat K = (\mat K\,\vek s)^\T = \vek 0^\T\), so \(\vek s^\T\mat M\ddot{\vek q} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} Q_{\mathrm{i}}\), and the left side is \(m_\mathrm{c}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{T}}\).
  2. The column \(\vek s\) lies in \(\mathcal N\) of theorem 10.23, since \(\mat K\,\vek s = \vek 0\). By the third point of the theorem, \(\vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi = 0\) for every elastic mode \(\vek\varphi\).

Source: CK §9.4 (PDF p. 286): "there is a node point (i.e., a point that remains stationary)", for the two-mass system of Example 9.7 (PDF p. 285-286), not for wagons. The centre of mass is ours. The general form is from theorem 10.23, the proof is not from the source.

The centre of mass thus follows only the rigid-body part of the motion, and all internal oscillations are in elastic modes, in which it is at rest. For a mass matrix that is not diagonal, for example for a bar with finite elements (section 10.7), the same conclusion \(\vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek\varphi = 0\) still holds by the third point of the theorem, except that \(\vek s^\T\mat M\,\vek q\) is no longer a sum of masses multiplied by the displacements of single points, and the centre of mass is explained separately there.

Zero frequencies return in later sections. A bar with finite elements that has both ends free (section 10.7) has \(\mat K\,(1, \dots, 1)^\T = \vek 0\), so a zero frequency with a rigid-body mode in which all nodes move by the same distance, and elastic modes in which the centre of mass is at rest by corollary 10.24. For a truss with supports that prevent every rigid-body displacement and every mechanism, \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite (theorem 7.33) and all frequencies are greater than zero.
