Chapter 16 Torsion

16.7 Shear centre (strižno središče)

The section on thin strips ended with a channel, where a question came up: through which point must a transverse force pass so that the section does not twist? Chapter 5 took the force to act in a plane of symmetry of the section for bending, and promised the shear centre for other sections. Chapter 12 claimed that for a section with two axes of symmetry the centroid is also the shear centre, and postponed the explanation to this chapter. The guess is natural: we bend a beam with a force through the centroid, so the centroid should be the right point. This section shows that the guess is right for the rectangle and wrong for the channel, and finds the right point with a number.

First let us say what we look for. Let us draw the section of the prism of definition 16.1. The prism can also carry a transverse force \(V\) that is the same in every section, for example a force at the free end of a cantilever. Its line of action \(\ell\) can be put anywhere parallel to the axis \(y\) or \(z\). What happens to the section depends on the position of \(\ell\): when \(\ell\) passes by a certain point \(S\), the section twists as well as bends, and the point \(S\) is the one through which it does not twist.

Definition 16.44 Shear centre (strižno središče)

Let a prism with the axis \(x\) through the centroids of the sections, as in chapter 5, be clamped at one end and loaded at the other with a transverse force \(V\) along the axis \(y\) or the axis \(z\) in the plane of the section. The shear centre of the section is the point \(S\) in the plane of the section with the property that a force whose line of action passes through \(S\) does not twist the section, for a force in the direction \(y\) and for a force in the direction \(z\). That the section at \(x\) does not twist means that the mean rotation of the section about the axis \(x\)

\[\bar\theta(x) = \frac1A\int_{\mathrm{A}} \tfrac12\Big(\frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}}{\partial y} - \frac{\partial u_{\mathrm{y}}}{\partial z}\Big)\mathrm{d}A = -\frac1A\int_{\mathrm{A}} \omega_{\mathrm{yz}}\,\mathrm{d}A \tag{16.47}\]

is zero, where \(\omega_{\mathrm{yz}}\) is an element of the small rotation tensor of definition 1.122 and \(A\) the area of the section. In the torsion of a prism of definition 16.1 \(u_{\mathrm{y}} = -\alpha xz\) and \(u_{\mathrm{z}} = \alpha xy\), so \(\bar\theta(x) = \alpha x\), and the condition means \(\alpha = 0\).

Source: ONATE2 section 4.2.6, equations (4.17) and (4.18) (PDF p. 189) and section 4.2.7 (PDF p. 190-191), SADD section 9.8, equation (9.8.21) and the text after it, where SADD also calls the point the centre of flexure (PDF p. 277). The existence of the point \(S\) for a general section is taken from the sources and not proved. The letter \(S\) is a point in this section, not the section as a set, so the section is named in words. The point about which the section turns in torsion is, by ONATE2 (PDF p. 191), the elastic axis through \(S\), the equality of the two points is cited here and not proved.

The point \(S\) depends only on the shape of the section, not on the size of the force, and it can be computed from equilibrium without solving Prandtl's equation. How, the following consideration shows.

Let the section be thin and open, made of rectangular walls of thickness \(t\), like the channel of section 16.6. We split the transverse force \(V\) as in chapter 5: from the moment \(M(x)\) we have a longitudinal stress \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\) that changes along the axis, and a piece of the beam is in equilibrium only if a shear stress on the cut holds it. Jourawski's theorem 5.39 did this for a horizontal cut across a section with a plane of symmetry. Here we cut across a wall, which can be a flange, so the cut has another direction, and the section has no plane of symmetry \(x\)-\(y\). The theorem therefore does not cover our cut, and we must repeat the same consideration, which is the lemma on the shear flow. We take the second moment \(I\) about the axis \(z\) through the centroid of the section and require that the product moment \(\int yz\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\), which for our profiles the symmetry about the axis \(z\) ensures. Then for bending in the plane \(x\)-\(y\) we still have \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -My/I\) as in theorem 5.35, since the theorem needs only that \(y\) and \(z\) are principal axes. Chapter 5 proves this for a section with symmetry about the axis \(y\). For a section with the other axis of symmetry we take the statement from ONATE2 (equations (4.12) to (4.14), PDF p. 189) and do not prove it.

A cut across a wall is not unique, so first let us say what we measure. The mean line \(s\) of a wall is the straight or broken line along the middle of the walls, the beginning \(s = 0\) is at the free end of the wall. The cut is the plane that crosses the wall perpendicular to the mean line at \(s\) and separates from the section the part \(A^*(s)\) between the free end and the cut. We denote

\[Q(s) = \int_{\mathrm{A}^*(\mathrm{s})} y\,\mathrm{d}A . \tag{16.48}\]

This is the first moment of a partial area of definition 5.38, only that we now measure it on the part bounded by a cut across a wall. The letter \(Q\) is used by SADD and EP, in this book the same thing is \(S\), but the letter \(S\) is the shear centre in this section. The shear flow (strižni tok) is the integral of the component \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xs}}\) across the thickness of the wall, \(f(s) = \int \sigma_{\mathrm{xs}}\,\mathrm{d}n\), where \(\hat s\) is the tangent to the mean line in the direction of increasing \(s\) and \(n\) the distance from the mean line. On the cut, \(f\) is the force in the direction \(x\) per unit length of the axis with which the wall beyond the cut acts on the part \(A^*(s)\). Since \(\sigma_{\mathrm{sx}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{xs}}\) (theorem 2.41), the same \(f\) is, in the section, the force in the direction \(\hat s\) per unit length of the mean line.

Before we prove the lemma, let us cut out a piece. We draw the piece \(P\) of the slice of the beam between the sections \(x\) and \(x + \Delta x\) beyond the cut. The axis \(x\) runs along the beam. On the left face and the right face act the longitudinal stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\), on the cut across the wall the shear flow \(f\), and the two lateral faces are free.

Lemma 16.45 Shear flow in a thin wall (strižni tok v tanki steni)

Let a prism have the axis \(x\) through the centroids of the sections, let the section be made of walls whose lateral faces are free, let \(\int y\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) and \(\int yz\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) and \(I = \int y^2\,\mathrm{d}A\). Let \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -My/I\) of theorem 5.35 hold, let the transverse force \(V\) be in the direction \(y\), the load without a component in the direction \(x\) (\(q_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), \(N = 0\)), and the stress continuous in \(x\). Then the shear flow in every wall at \(s\) is

\[f(s) = -\frac{V\,Q(s)}{I} . \tag{16.49}\]

The flow points toward the free end of the wall when \(V\,Q > 0\).

Idea

The part of the piece of the beam between two sections that lies beyond the cut across the wall is in equilibrium. What the longitudinal stresses on its faces do not balance, the shear flow on the cut must balance. The calculation is the same as for theorem 5.39, only that the cut is not horizontal.

Proof

We must show equation (16.49). We write the equilibrium of forces in the direction \(x\) for the part beyond the cut and divide by \(\Delta x\).

  1. Let \(P\) be the part of the slice between the sections \(x\) and \(x + \Delta x\) with the cross-section \(A^*(s)\). It is a part of a body in equilibrium, so by definition 2.3 the sum of forces on it is zero. The force on a face is the stress vector multiplied by the area, by Cauchy's formula (theorem 2.26).
  2. The faces of \(P\) are the right one at \(x + \Delta x\) with the normal \(+\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), the left one at \(x\) with the normal \(-\vek e_{\mathrm{x}}\), the cut with the normal \(\hat s\) (it points from \(P\) into the wall toward larger \(s\)) and the free lateral faces of the wall, on which the stress vector is zero. The load has no component \(x\), so the remaining terms in the direction \(x\) vanish.
  3. The component \(x\) of the stress vector is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}\) on the right and left faces and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{sx}}\) on the cut. So
    \[N^*(x + \Delta x) - N^*(x) + \int_{\mathrm{x}}^{x + \Delta x}\! f(\xi, s)\,\mathrm{d}\xi = 0 , \qquad N^*(x) = \int_{\mathrm{A}^*(\mathrm{s})}\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}}(x, y, z)\,\mathrm{d}A ,\]
    where by the symmetry of stress (theorem 2.41) \(\sigma_{\mathrm{sx}} = \sigma_{\mathrm{xs}}\), and the inner integral across the thickness is \(f\).
  4. We divide by \(\Delta x\) and take \(\Delta x \to 0\). By the fundamental theorem of calculus (theorem 3.55, item 2) and the continuity of the stress, \(N^*\) is differentiable and \((N^*)'(x) = -f(x, s)\).
  5. We insert \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xx}} = -My/I\). The section does not change along the axis, so \(N^*(x) = -M(x)\,Q(s)/I\) and \((N^*)' = -M'\,Q/I\).
  6. By corollary 5.11 \(M' = -V\), so \((N^*)' = V\,Q/I\). From steps 4 and 6, \(f = -V\,Q/I\).
Explanation

There is no assumption of uniformity across the thickness, since the flow \(f\) by definition already integrates across the thickness. In the torsion of a thin wall a stress appears that is odd across the thickness (equation (16.43), the stress \(2G\alpha|y|\) with the sign of \(y\)). Its integral across the thickness is zero, so torsion contributes nothing to the flow \(f\), and equation (16.49) holds for the whole stress field, also when the section twists at the same time, since there is no longitudinal stress in torsion. In the thin model we take that the field of flows alone does not twist the section and that the twist is caused only by the torsional part, which is odd across the thickness. This is an assumption (idealization) of this section, the lemma does not prove it.

Source: SADD (D.9) (PDF p. 579-580) for a horizontal cut, ONATE2 (PDF p. 190) says that the shear stresses for a general section are computed by the procedure of appendix D. The proof is an extension of the proof of theorem 5.39 to a cut across a wall and is not from a source. The sign follows from the convention for \(V\) of chapter 5.

We test the lemma on the channel of example 16.43, that is, on a profile with mean lines of height \(h = 40\) mm (the web) and length \(b = 20\) mm (the flanges), thickness \(t = 2\) mm and force \(V = 500\) N in the direction \(y\). We build the figure up part by part.

We draw the section, the letter U made of three rectangles, and in it the axes: \(y\) up along the web, \(z\) to the right along the flanges, with the origin at the centroid \(C\). The centroid lies, by the centroid of a composite figure (definition 5.21), at the distance \(z_{\mathrm{C}} = 2\,(b\,t)(b/2)/\big(t\,(h + 2b)\big) = b^2/(h + 2b) = 5{,}00\) mm from the mean line of the web, because the two flanges together have the area \(2bt\) with the centroid at \(b/2\) and the section has the area \(t\,(h + 2b)\). The second moment is, by theorem 5.28 (only the Steiner term, the own moment of a flange \(bt^3/12\) is \(0{,}06\,\%\) of the contribution),

\[I = \frac{t\,h^3}{12} + 2\,b\,t\left(\frac h2\right)^2 = \frac{t\,h^2\,(h + 6b)}{12} = 42\,667\ \text{mm}^4 . \tag{16.50}\]

The thin model replaces the section with the mean lines and ignores the overlap in the corners. The real section with the same mean lines has the outline \(42 \times 21\) mm (web \(2 \times 42\) mm, flanges \(19 \times 2\) mm from the face of the web on the side of the flanges to \(z = 20\) mm from its mean line) and the area \(160\) mm\(^2\). Its second moment is \(I = 2\cdot 42^3/12 + 2\cdot 19\,(21^3 - 19^3)/3 = 42\,773\) mm\(^4\), so the thin value is \(0{,}25\,\%\) smaller.

For a cut in the upper flange at the distance \(s\) from the free end, by definition (16.48) the part \(A^*\) is the rectangle \(t \times s\) with the centroid at the height \(h/2\), so \(Q = t\,s\,h/2\) and by lemma 16.45

\[f(s) = -\frac{V\,t\,h}{2I}\,s .\]

The flow in the upper flange is linear: zero at the free end and \(9{,}38\) N/mm (\(4{,}69\) MPa) at the web, since \(V\,t\,h\,b/(2I) = 500\cdot 2\cdot 40\cdot 20/(2\cdot 42\,667)\). The sign is minus, so it points toward the free end, in the figure from the web outward (\(+z\)) at \(V > 0\). For the lower flange \(y = -h/2\) and \(Q\) has the opposite sign, so it is the same triangle, and the flow points toward the web (\(-z\)). At the web the part \(A^*(y_0)\) is the upper flange and the part of the web above \(y_0\), so \(Q = b\,t\,h/2 + t\,(h^2/4 - y_0^2)/2\), which is \(800\) mm\(^3\) at the junction and \(1200\) mm\(^3\) in the middle. The flow there is \(V\,Q/I = 9{,}38\) and \(14{,}06\) N/mm and points up, because in the web the tangent \(\hat s\) points down. The stress in the middle of the web is \(14{,}06/2 = 7{,}03\) MPa. At the junction the flow continues from the web into the flange with the same value \(9{,}38\) N/mm.

Where does the sum of the flows point? We draw from the profile only the mean lines, because they carry the flows, and put the resultant of a flow on each. In a flange it is \(F = \int_0^b |f|\,\mathrm{d}s = V\,t\,h\,b^2/(4I) = 93{,}75\) N on a line parallel to \(z\) (for a linear flow the point of application is at \(b/3\) from the web). In the web the resultant is

\[\int_{-\mathrm{h}/2}^{h/2}\frac{V}{I}\Big(\frac{b\,t\,h}{2} + \frac{t\,(h^2/4 - y^2)}{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}y = \frac{V}{I}\cdot\frac{t\,h^2\,(h + 6b)}{12} = V ,\]

which by equation (16.50) is indeed \(V = 500{,}000\) N: the flows give exactly the transverse force. The flanges point in opposite directions at the heights \(\pm h/2\), so they are a couple with the moment \(F\,h\) about the axis \(x\) through the mean line of the web, and the web with the arm zero contributes nothing. The total moment of the flows is \(93{,}75\cdot 40 = 3750\) N mm.

The flows are therefore statically equivalent to one force \(V\) in the direction \(y\) on a line at the distance \(e\) from the mean line of the web. The moment of a force \(V\,\vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\) about \(x\) on a line with the coordinate \(z_\ell\) is, by the definition of the moment (lemma 5.9, \(\int(y\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}} - z\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A\)), equal to \(-V\,(z_\ell - z_{\mathrm{w}})\) with respect to the mean line of the web \(z = z_{\mathrm{w}}\), and this must be \(F\,h\). So \(z_\ell = z_{\mathrm{w}} - F\,h/V\): the line lies on the side of the web that is opposite to the flanges.

Theorem 16.46 Shear centre of a channel (strižno središče U-profila)

Let the section be a thin channel with mean lines: a web of height \(h\), flanges of length \(b\), thickness \(t\) everywhere, \(t \ll b\) and \(t \ll h\), symmetric about the axis \(z\) through the centroid. Let the section follow the thin model and lemma 16.45. Then a transverse force in the direction \(y\) does not twist the section if it acts on a line parallel to \(y\) at the distance

\[e = \frac{3\,b^2}{h + 6\,b} \tag{16.51}\]

from the mean line of the web, on the side opposite to the flanges, and the shear centre \(S\) lies on the axis of symmetry \(z\) through the centroid \(C\). The centroid is at the distance \(z_{\mathrm{C}} = b^2/(h + 2b)\) from the mean line of the web on the side of the flanges, so \(S \ne C\) and the distance is \(e + z_{\mathrm{C}}\). For \(h = 40\) mm, \(b = 20\) mm, \(t = 2\) mm, \(e = 7{,}50\) mm, \(z_{\mathrm{C}} = 5{,}00\) mm and the distance is \(12{,}50\) mm.

Idea

The flow in the flanges gives a couple that the web does not have. The line on which one force \(V\) with the same moment acts is where the resultant of the flows is.

Proof

We must show that the line of the resultant of the flows is at the distance \(e\) of equation (16.51) and that this is the line for which there is no twist.

  1. The second moment of the section in equation (16.50) is \(I = t\,h^2\,(h + 6b)/12\).
  2. In a flange, by lemma 16.45, the flow is \(|f| = V\,t\,h\,s/(2I)\) and the force \(F = V\,t\,h\,b^2/(4I)\), and in the web \(\int f\,\mathrm{d}y = V\) by the calculation above.
  3. The moment of the flows about the mean line of the web is \(F\,h\) (the couple of the flanges with the arm \(h\)), since the web contributes nothing.
  4. A force \(V\) on a line at the distance \(e\) from the mean line of the web has the same moment \(V\,e\), so \(e = F\,h/V = t\,h^2\,b^2/(4I) = 3b^2/(h + 6b)\) by step 1.
  5. The section is symmetric about the axis \(z\), so (lemma 16.48 below) \(S\) lies on the axis \(z\), that is at the height \(y = 0\).
  6. The centroid: \(z_{\mathrm{C}} = b^2/(h + 2b)\) by the calculation above. For the given numbers \(e = 1200/160 = 7{,}50\) mm and \(z_{\mathrm{C}} = 400/80 = 5{,}00\) mm.
  7. The moment of the load about the axis \(x\) is the sum of the moment of the flows and the moment of the torsional part of the field, which is odd across the thickness. By the thin model of the explanation after lemma 16.45 the flows alone do not twist the section, the twist is caused only by the torsional part. This part carries the moment of the load minus the moment of the flows. If the force acts on the line of step 4, this difference is zero, the torsional part has the moment zero and by theorem 16.18 (\(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G\,J\,\alpha\)) its \(\alpha = 0\). The mean rotation of the section (16.47) is therefore zero, and by definition 16.44 this is the line through \(S\).
Explanation

The shear centre lies outside the profile, on the side of the web opposite to the flanges, at \(e = 7{,}50\) mm from the mean line of the web and \(12{,}50\) mm from the centroid. The same position is given by ONATE2 in figure 4.6 (PDF p. 190), which for a profile with thin walls gives the distance \(CA = 3b^2/(h + 6b)\) from the centre to the mean line of the web and \(AO = b^2/(h + 2b)\) from it to the centroid, which is the same as equation (16.51) and \(z_{\mathrm{C}}\) (the difference is zero in SymPy).

Source: ONATE2 section 4.2.6 and figure 4.6 (PDF p. 189-190). SADD section 9.8 (PDF p. 277) says that the centre does not necessarily fall into the section. Steps 2 to 4 are a calculation by the lemma and not a source. The error of the thin model is estimated only for \(I\), as \(0{,}25\,\%\) from the real section. For \(e\) we do not compute it, since in the real section both \(I\) and the force \(F\) in a flange change.

The figure has three drawings of the same section, with an equals sign and a plus sign between them. In the figure the force \(V\) on the line through \(S\) is drawn in the middle as a vertical line at the distance \(e\) from the mean line of the web and the dimensions \(e\) and \(h\) measure the distances. This force is statically equivalent to the sum of the resultants in the flanges and the resultant in the web: the same force, the same moment.

And a force that does not act on this line? The definition says only that there is no twist if it passes through \(S\), and not how large the twist is otherwise. The line of such a force is at the distance \(d\) from the line through \(S\).

Lemma 16.47 An offset force twists the section (izmaknjena sila zasuka prerez)

Let the transverse force \(V\) be in the direction \(y\) and let the line of its action \(\ell\) be parallel to the line \(\ell_{\mathrm{S}}\) through \(S\) and at the distance \(d\) from it. Then the stress field is the sum of the field for the force through \(S\) and the field of the torsion of a prism (definition 16.1) with the moment \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = V\,d\) about the axis through \(S\). The twist per unit length is \(\alpha = M_{\mathrm{x}}/(GJ)\) with \(J\) from theorem 16.18.

Proof

We must show that the difference of the two loadings gives the torsion of a prism.

  1. The loadings differ statically only by a couple: the force \(V\) on \(\ell\) and the force \(V\) on \(\ell_{\mathrm{S}}\) have the same resultant, and their moments about the axis \(x\) differ by \(V\,d\).
  2. For the force on \(\ell_{\mathrm{S}}\) the mean rotation of the section (16.47) is zero by definition 16.44. Elasticity is linear, so the field for \(\ell\) is the sum of this field and the field for the difference of the loadings, which is a couple with the moment \(V\,d\) at the end and nothing elsewhere.
  3. The difference of the loadings at the end has the resultant zero and the moment \(V\,d\) about the axis \(x\), so it is a torsional loading of definition 16.1. The exact field differs from the field of torsion only near the ends by Saint-Venant's principle (section 11.11), so far from the ends the field of the difference is torsion with \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = V\,d\). For thin open sections the zone at the clamp is long, because the warping is restrained there and the twist rate varies along the axis (ONATE2 section 4.10, PDF p. 227). We do not compute its length here.
  4. By theorem 16.18 \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G\,J\,\alpha\), which gives \(\alpha\).

Source: ONATE2 section 4.2.7 (PDF p. 190-191): external forces at an arbitrary point \(D\) are equivalent to forces through the shear centre and a torque about the elastic axis. Saint-Venant's principle is cited from chapter 11 and is not proved.

The lemma tells how large the twist is: \(\alpha = V\,d/(GJ)\), proportional to the force and to the distance \(d\) from \(S\).

A thin wall carries bending and torsion each in its own way. The lemma gives only the integral \(f\) of the flow across the thickness. In the thin model we take the stress from the flow to be uniform across the thickness and equal to \(f/t\), as it is uniform across the width in theorem 5.39. The torsional one is odd across the thickness and largest on the faces, \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\,t/J\) (theorem 16.41). Both are parallel to the wall, so on one face they add and on the other they subtract, and the largest total stress is their sum.

16.7.1 When the centroid is the shear centre

Where do the centroid and the shear centre coincide? A consideration of symmetry answers this without computing flows.

We draw the section with the axis of symmetry \(y\). The reflection \(z \mapsto -z\) maps it onto itself. The force \(V\) acts on the axis, the stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) at mirror points are equal, the stresses \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) opposite. With both axes of symmetry the axes \(y\) and \(z\) meet in one point, as the lemma below shows.

Lemma 16.48 Symmetry and the shear centre (simetrija in strižno središče)

Let the prism be isotropic, linearly elastic, clamped at one end and loaded at the other. Let the section be symmetric about the axis \(y\), that is about the reflection \(z \mapsto -z\), and let the transverse force \(V\) be in the direction \(y\) with such a distribution of forces at the end that it is symmetric about this reflection. Then the stress field is symmetric: \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}(y, -z) = \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}(y, z)\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}(y, -z) = -\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}(y, z)\). The moment of these stresses about the centroid is zero, the line of the force \(V\) is the axis \(y\) and the section does not twist. Therefore:

  1. For a section with the axis of symmetry \(y\) the line of the force in the direction \(y\) without twist lies on the axis \(y\). For a section with the axis of symmetry \(z\) the line of the force in the direction \(z\) without twist lies on the axis \(z\).
  2. For a section with both axes of symmetry both lines are the axes of symmetry and the shear centre is their intersection, that is the centroid of the section.
  3. For a section with one axis of symmetry \(S\) lies on this axis.
Proof

We must show that the solution is symmetric and that the moment about the centroid is zero.

  1. The reflection \(R: (x, y, z) \mapsto (x, y, -z)\) maps the body onto itself, because the section is symmetric. If \(\vek u\) is a solution, \(\vek u^R(\vek r) = R\,\vek u(R\vek r)\) is a solution for the reflected data, because Hooke's law of an isotropic material (theorem 3.19) does not choose a direction.
  2. The data are symmetric: the clamping is zero, the load (the force \(V\) on the axis) is symmetric by assumption. So the reflected data equal the original ones.
  3. By uniqueness (theorem 11.4, item 1) \(\vek u\) and \(\vek u^R\) have the same stresses. The clamped end admits only the rigid displacement zero, so by item 3 of the same theorem the displacements are equal too, \(\vek u^R = \vek u\). The components of stress transform under \(R\) as \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}^R(y, z) = \sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}(y, -z)\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}^R(y, z) = -\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}(y, -z)\). The equality gives the symmetry of the lemma.
  4. The moment about the axis \(x\) through the centroid is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = \int_{\mathrm{A}} (y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}} - z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}})\,\mathrm{d}A\). With \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) odd in \(z\), \(y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\) is odd in \(z\), with \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) even in \(z\), \(z\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}}\) is odd in \(z\). The section is symmetric in \(z\), so both integrals are zero (lemma 5.24 for the centroid, the integral of an odd function over a symmetric region). So \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\).
  5. A force \(V\) on a line with the coordinate \(z_\ell\) has the moment \(-z_\ell\,V\) about \(x\) by the formula in the section on flows (lemma 5.9). By equilibrium this equals \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), so \(z_\ell = 0\) and the line is the axis \(y\). From \(\vek u^R = \vek u\) we get \(u_{\mathrm{y}}(x, y, -z) = u_{\mathrm{y}}(x, y, z)\) and \(u_{\mathrm{z}}(x, y, -z) = -u_{\mathrm{z}}(x, y, z)\), so \(\partial u_{\mathrm{z}}/\partial y\) and \(\partial u_{\mathrm{y}}/\partial z\) are odd in \(z\). The integrand of the mean rotation (16.47) is therefore odd in \(z\), its integral over the symmetric section is zero, and by definition 16.44 the section does not twist.
  6. A reflection about the axis \(z\) gives the same for a force in the direction \(z\). With both axes \(S\) is the intersection of both axes, that is the centroid (lemma 5.24). With one axis, say \(z\), the line of the force in the direction \(z\) lies on this axis, and the line of the force in the direction \(y\) is somewhere parallel to \(y\). The intersection lies on the axis, so \(S\) lies on the axis.

Source: SADD section 9.8 (PDF p. 277): for a section with two perpendicular axes of symmetry \(S\) lies at the intersection and is the centroid, with one axis it lies on it. COOK (PDF p. 43) and ONATE2 figure 4.6 (PDF p. 190) for a section with one axis. The proof is ours and not from a source.

An example with two axes of symmetry that is not a rectangle is the I profile.

We draw the section, the letter I, which is symmetric about both axes, and in the web the resultant \(V\). In both half-flanges above and below we put the resultants of the flow \(F = 53{,}57\) N, by lemma 16.45 \(F = V\,t\,h\,b^2/(4I)\) with \(I = 74\,667\) mm\(^4\). The two halves of the same flange pull from the web outward and balance on the same line, so the total force of the flange is zero, the moment about the web is zero and \(S\) is at the centroid, as lemma 16.48 says (item 2). This is also the claim of chapter 12 for the rectangle \(20 \times 40\) mm: two axes of symmetry, so the shear centre is at the centroid.

16.7.2 The centroid is not the shear centre: the channel under a force

Now we use theorem 16.46 and lemma 16.47 for the twist. First the natural but wrong choice: we put the force \(V = 500\) N at the free end of the cantilever through the centroid \(C\).

Corollary 16.49 Twist of a channel under a force through the centroid (zasuk U-profila pod silo skozi težišče)

Let the channel of theorem 16.46 be of steel (\(G = 75{,}385\) GPa) and let the force \(V = 500\) N act through the centroid \(C\). Then \(d = e + z_{\mathrm{C}} = 12{,}50\) mm, the torsional moment is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = V\,d = 6{,}25\) N m, the torsion constant of the open section is \(J = (h + 2b)\,t^3/3 = 213{,}3\) mm\(^4\) by corollary 16.42, and

\[\alpha = \frac{V\,d}{G\,J} = \frac{6{,}25}{16{,}08}\ \mathrm{rad/m} = 0{,}3886\ \mathrm{rad/m} = 22{,}3\,^\circ/\mathrm{m} . \tag{16.52}\]

In uniform torsion, far from the clamp, the torsional stress in the walls is \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\,t/J = 58{,}6\) MPa, the shear stress from bending is at most \(7{,}03\) MPa, the ratio is \(8{,}3\), and the largest total shear stress is on the face of the web near the middle, \(65{,}6\) MPa. For a force through \(S\), \(\alpha = 0\) and the largest shear stress is \(7{,}03\) MPa.

Proof

We must show the numbers.

  1. The distance \(d\) between \(\ell\) (through \(C\)) and \(\ell_{\mathrm{S}}\) is \(e + z_{\mathrm{C}} = 7{,}50 + 5{,}00 = 12{,}50\) mm by theorem 16.46. So \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 500\cdot 0{,}0125 = 6{,}25\) N m by lemma 16.47.
  2. The total length of the mean lines is \(h + 2b = 80\) mm, so \(J = 80\cdot 8/3 = 213{,}3\) mm\(^4\) and \(GJ = 75\,385\cdot 213{,}3 = 1{,}608\cdot 10^{7}\) N mm\(^2\) \(= 16{,}08\) N m\(^2\).
  3. \(\alpha = 6{,}25/16{,}08 = 0{,}3886\) rad/m, so \(22{,}27\,^\circ\)/m.
  4. The stress from torsion by theorem 16.41: \(\tau_{\max} \approx M_{\mathrm{x}}\,t/J = 6250\cdot 2/213{,}3 = 58{,}6\) MPa. The stress from bending in the middle of the web is \(f/t = 14{,}06/2 = 7{,}03\) MPa, their sum \(65{,}6\) MPa, below the shear yield strength of steel \(110\) MPa by Tresca's criterion.

Source: The calculations are in outlines/ch-torzija/num-07.py. The model is uniform torsion with a freely warping section (definition 16.1), at the clamped end the warping is restrained and by ONATE2 (section 4.10, PDF p. 227) the twist rate there varies along the axis, which is not treated here (COOK, PDF p. 42-43, ONATE2 section 4.10, PDF p. 227-246 are only mentioned).

We draw first the section before loading as a grey dashed line, in it the centroid \(C\) and the shear centre \(S\) on the left, \(12{,}5\) mm from the centroid. In the left picture the force \(V\) acts through \(C\) upward. The section after loading (orange) is lifted and turned about \(S\), because the force is to the right of \(S\) and turns counterclockwise. The angle is \(\theta = \alpha L\) and is enlarged three times in the figure so that we can see it. In the right picture the force acts through \(S\) and the section after loading does not turn, it only moves up by the same distance \(\delta\). By equation (5.57) \(\delta = V L^3/(3EI) = 0{,}31\) mm at \(E = 196\) GPa, enlarged in the figure.

The twist is large because \(GJ\) of a thin open section is small: an offset of only \(12{,}5\) mm twists the profile by \(22{,}3\) degrees per metre. This is the twist rate of uniform torsion, far from the clamp. For a cantilever of length \(L = 0{,}25\) m the end would turn by \(\alpha L = 5{,}57^\circ\) if the section warped freely everywhere. At the clamp the warping is restrained and the twist rate varies along the axis (ONATE2 section 4.10, PDF p. 227), so \(5{,}57^\circ\) is not the true end rotation of this cantilever. We do not compute the true one here.

The guess that the centroid is the right point therefore does not pass the test for the channel: the largest shear stress for a force through \(S\) drops from \(65{,}6\) MPa to \(7{,}03\) MPa, and the twist to zero. An offset of \(12{,}5\) mm is already large for the channel, for the slit tube it will be larger.

We draw the ring with the slit on the right and in it the axis of the tube \(O\). Outside the ring the flow is a diagram that is zero at the slit and grows to the back. The force \(V\) is through \(S\), at the distance \(2r\) from \(O\) and outside the section. The shear centre therefore lies outside the material, which SADD explicitly allows (PDF p. 277: the centre does not necessarily lie in the section).

Example 16.50 Shear centre of a slit tube (strižno središče razrezane cevi)

The tube of example 16.43 has the mean radius \(r = 18\) mm, thickness \(t = 4\) mm and is cut along a generator. Let the slit be at \(\theta = 0\) on the axis \(z\) (\(\theta\) is the polar angle in the plane of the section, measured from the axis \(z\) toward the axis \(y\), as in chapter 11). Let \(V = 500\) N in the direction \(y\).

  1. The ring has the radii \(R_{\mathrm{i}} = 16\) mm and \(R_{\mathrm{o}} = 20\) mm. In polar coordinates (theorem 4.59, \(y = \rho\sin\theta\)) the exact second moment is \(I = \pi\,(R_{\mathrm{o}}^4 - R_{\mathrm{i}}^4)/4 = 74\,191\) mm\(^4\) (lemma 16.2), and for a thin ring \(\pi\,r^3\,t = 73\,287\) mm\(^4\), which is \(1{,}2\,\%\) less.
  2. The first moment of the part from the slit to \(\theta\) is, by the same theorem, \(Q = \int_0^\theta\!\int_{\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{i}}^{R_{\mathrm{o}}}\rho\sin\theta'\,\rho\,\mathrm{d}\rho\,\mathrm{d}\theta' = \dfrac{R_{\mathrm{o}}^3 - R_{\mathrm{i}}^3}{3}\,(1 - \cos\theta)\), and for a thin ring \(r^2\,t\,(1 - \cos\theta)\), which is \(0{,}4\,\%\) smaller (\(1296\) against \(1301{,}3\) mm\(^3\)). The shear flow by lemma 16.45 in the thin model has the magnitude \(f = V\,(1 - \cos\theta)/(\pi\,r)\), largest \(17{,}68\) N/mm at the back (\(\theta = \pi\)). At \(t/r = 0{,}22\) the remaining steps are estimates that do not hold to three digits.
  3. The flow runs tangentially in the direction of decreasing \(\theta\), with the unit vector \((\sin\theta, -\cos\theta)\) in \((z, y)\). The force in the direction \(y\) is \(-r\int_0^{2\pi} f\cos\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta = V\,\dfrac{1}{\pi}\,\pi = V\) and in the direction \(z\) zero.
  4. The moment of the flows about the axis of the tube, with the arm \(r\) of each piece of arc, is \(\int_0^{2\pi} f\,r^2\,\mathrm{d}\theta = V\,r\cdot 2 = 2Vr = 18\,000\) N mm. So the line of the force \(V\) is at the distance \(2r = 36\) mm from the axis of the tube on the side opposite to the slit. The outer radius is \(20\) mm, so the line is \(16\) mm outside the section.
  5. If the force passes through the axis of the tube: \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 18{,}0\) N m, \(J = 2\pi\,r\,t^3/3 = 2413\) mm\(^4\) (corollary 16.42), \(\alpha = 0{,}0990\) rad/m \(= 5{,}67\,^\circ\)/m and \(\tau = M_{\mathrm{x}}\,t/J = 29{,}8\) MPa, the stress from bending is \(17{,}68/4 = 4{,}42\) MPa.

The slit tube is the opposite of the tube without a slit of example 16.43: for an unbroken tube the section has two axes of symmetry and by lemma 16.48 the centre is on the axis. The cut takes it away by \(2r = 36\) mm.

The section ends where it began: with the question whether the same strip, so soft in the open form, would resist twisting more if we closed it. The answer is in the next section on hollow sections.
