Chapter 15 Continuum finite elements

15.8 Locking in nearly incompressible material (zaklepanje)

The elements of the previous sections computed well as long as Poisson's ratio of steel was \(\nu = 0{,}3\). What happens when the material hardly changes its volume, like rubber, or like steel with \(\nu\) near \(\tfrac12\)? Chapter 6 showed a phenomenon on the hanging bar that was called locking (zaklepanje): the trial field prevented a deformation that the body needs, and the solution came out too stiff. Chapter 12 explained it for the Timoshenko beam as a penalty. Here we meet it in the plane and in space. The name comes from COOK (PDF p. 509, read) as "the mesh locks": the mesh is locked like a door, and the solution can no longer move.

We test it on the tube of chapter 11 (theorem 11.27). The tube has inner radius \(a = 40\) mm, outer radius \(b = 60\) mm, inner pressure \(p = 30\) MPa, and the outer wall is free. The deformation is plane (definition 11.30). By symmetry we take only a quarter of the tube, the one in the first quadrant.

We draw the mesh of the quarter of the annulus. The outer and inner arcs of the mesh have radii \(a\) and \(b\), the mesh has four elements across the thickness and eight along the arc, each a quadrilateral with four nodes. By equation (11.35) the true displacement of the tube in plane strain has the direction \(\vek e_{\mathrm{r}}\), so a point on the horizontal axis moves only in the direction \(x\) and a point on the vertical axis only in the direction \(y\). We ask the same of the computation: the supports hold the horizontal axis in the direction \(y\) and the vertical axis in the direction \(x\). On the inner edge acts the pressure \(p\), a force per area pushing the edge outward, so the arrows stand in front of the edge and touch it with their tips. The element is Q4 (section 15.5) with the \(2 \times 2\) Gauss rule (section 15.6). For steel with \(E = 196\) GPa and \(\nu = 0{,}3\) the displacement of the inner edge is \(u_{\mathrm{r}}(a) = 16{,}873\) µm by the Lamé solution, and the computed one is \(0{,}9945\) of it. Now we keep the same Young's modulus \(E\) and the same material and change only Poisson's ratio. This is not steel but a test of the element: the idealizations are linear elasticity, small strains and isotropy, and \(\nu\) goes toward \(\tfrac12\), where the substance does not change its volume. At \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) the computed displacement falls to \(0{,}1765\) of the true one, while the Lamé solution itself changes by only \(2\) percent (from \(16{,}873\) to \(16{,}531\) µm, equation (11.35)).

15.8.1 Energy by volume and by shape (energija po prostornini in po obliki)

The first answer lies in the strain energy density. Chapter 3 showed that for an isotropic material

\[w = \tfrac12\,K\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}}^2 + G \sum_{\mathrm{i}}\sum_{\mathrm{j}} e_{\mathrm{ij}}^2 ,\]

where \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}}\) is the volumetric strain, \(e_{\mathrm{ij}}\) the deviatoric strain (equation (3.43)), \(K = E/\big(3(1 - 2\nu)\big)\) the bulk modulus (definition 3.7) and \(G = E/\big(2(1 + \nu)\big)\) the shear modulus. The first term is the energy of the change of volume and the second the energy of the change of shape. When \(\nu \to \tfrac12\) at constant \(E\), \(K \to \infty\) while \(G\) stays bounded, \(G \to E/3\). For our material \(K/G = 2(1 + \nu)/\big(3(1 - 2\nu)\big)\), that is \(2{,}17\) at \(\nu = 0{,}3\) and \(4999{,}7\) at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) (column \(K/G\) of the table). In price, a change of volume is thus thousands of times dearer than a change of shape. This is the term that the element changes: let us see how.

In plane strain \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \operatorname{div}\vek u\) for the displacement field \(\vek u = (u, v)\) in the plane. We put the strains in a column in Voigt notation (definition 3.18) in the order \(xx\), \(yy\), \(xy\), where the third entry is the shear strain \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = 2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}}\). The matrix \(\mat B\) is the matrix of strains from the nodal displacements of the element (section 15.4). The letter \(\mat D\) is in this section the material matrix, which the previous sections and chapter 3 call \(\mat C\), because here we need the letter \(\mat C\) for the matrix of constraints of chapter 12. The letter \(K\) without bold type is the bulk modulus, bold \(\mat K\) is a stiffness matrix.

Lemma 15.55 Volumetric part of the element stiffness matrix (prostorninski del togostne matrike elementa)

Let the material be isotropic with \(E\) and \(-1 < \nu < \tfrac12\) and the deformation plane. Let \(\mat m = (1, 1, 0)^\T\). Then:

  1. The energy density is \(w = \tfrac12\,\vek\varepsilon^\T\mat D\,\vek\varepsilon\) with the material matrix \(\mat D = \mat D_{\mathrm{G}} + K\,\mat m\,\mat m^\T\) and
    \[\mat D_{\mathrm{G}} = G \begin{pmatrix} 4/3 & -2/3 & 0 \\ -2/3 & 4/3 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix} . \tag{15.40}\]
    The matrix \(\mat D_{\mathrm{G}}\) depends on \(\nu\) only through \(G\).
  2. If \(\vek\varepsilon = \mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the strain from the nodal displacements \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) of the element and \(\mat b_{\mathrm{v}} = \mat m^\T\mat B\) a row of \(n\) numbers, then \(\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}\), and the element matrix by the Gauss rule with points \(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}}\) and weights \(w_{\mathrm{g}}\) is
    \[\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat k_{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{e}} + K\,\mat k_{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{e}} , \qquad \mat k_{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{e}} = \sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,t\,J(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\;\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})^\T\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}}) , \tag{15.41}\]
    where \(\mat k_{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{e}}\) is the same sum with \(\mat B^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{G}}\mat B\) in place of \(\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}^\T\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}\), \(t\) is the thickness and \(J\) the determinant of the Jacobian matrix of the element map.
Proof

We must show that the matrix \(\mat D\) gives the energy of chapter 3 and that \(\mat D - K\,\mat m\,\mat m^\T\) is the matrix (15.40).

  1. By equation (3.43) \(w = \tfrac12 K\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}}^2 + G \sum e_{\mathrm{ij}}^2\). In plane strain \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \mat m^\T\vek\varepsilon\), so the first term is \(\tfrac12 \vek\varepsilon^\T(K\,\mat m\,\mat m^\T)\vek\varepsilon\).
  2. For the deviatoric part \(e_{\mathrm{xx}} = (2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}})/3\), \(e_{\mathrm{yy}} = (2\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}})/3\), \(e_{\mathrm{zz}} = -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}}/3\) and \(e_{\mathrm{xy}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xy}}\), there are no other shear components. The sum of squares is \(\sum e_{\mathrm{ij}}^2 = e_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 + e_{\mathrm{yy}}^2 + e_{\mathrm{zz}}^2 + 2\,e_{\mathrm{xy}}^2 = \tfrac23\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}}^2) + \tfrac12\,\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\).
  3. Hence \(G \sum e_{\mathrm{ij}}^2 = \tfrac12\,\vek\varepsilon^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek\varepsilon\) with the matrix (15.40): the term \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}^2\) has the factor \(\tfrac12 \cdot \tfrac43\,G = \tfrac23\,G\), the product \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}}\) has \(2 \cdot \tfrac12 \cdot (-\tfrac23)\,G = -\tfrac23\,G\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}}^2\) has \(\tfrac12\,G\). This is item 1, and the matrix changes with \(\nu\) only through \(G\).
  4. Item 2: \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \mat m^\T\mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\). The element matrix is \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,t\,J\,\mat B^\T\mat D\,\mat B\) (section 15.6), we insert \(\mat D\) from item 1 and take out \(K\,\mat B^\T\mat m\,\mat m^\T\mat B = K\,\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}^\T\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}\).

Source: COOK §13.4 (PDF pp. 510-511, read): \([E] = G\,[E_{\mathrm{G}}] + B\,[E_{\mathrm{B}}]\) and \([k] = G\int [B]^T[E_{\mathrm{G}}][B]\,dV + B\int [B]^T[E_{\mathrm{B}}][B]\,dV\), "as \(\nu\) approaches 0.50, matrix \(B[K_{\mathrm{b}}]\) dominates". We write \(K\) for COOK's \(B\), since \(B\) is our strain matrix. Checked in num-08.py: \(\mat D_{\mathrm{G}} + K\,\mat m\,\mat m^\T\) is the plane strain matrix.

The proof showed two things. The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{e}}\) does not depend on \(\nu\) except through \(G\), which stays bounded, and all the large numbers are in the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{e}}\), which is a sum of squares of the numbers \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}(\vek x_{\mathrm{g}})\) at the Gauss points. This is exactly the form of the penalty of definition 12.27 of chapter 12.

15.8.2 Can the true field stand it (ali pravo polje to zdrži)

What does the true field do when \(\nu \to \tfrac12\)? The Lamé solution of theorem 11.27 with \(p_{\mathrm{a}} = p\), \(p_{\mathrm{b}} = 0\) and plane strain, \(\varepsilon_0 = 0\), that is \(S = 2\nu A\), has by equation (11.35) the displacement field \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = (1 + \nu)\big((1 - 2\nu)\,A\,r + B/r\big)/E\) with \(A = p\,a^2/(b^2 - a^2) = 24\) MPa. Its divergence is by corollary 11.21 \(\operatorname{div}\vek u = \partial u_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial r + u_{\mathrm{r}}/r = 2\,(1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu)\,A/E\), since the term \(B/r\) gives \(-B/r^2 + B/r^2 = 0\). At \(\nu = 0{,}3\) this is \(1{,}27 \cdot 10^{-4}\), at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) it is \(7{,}35 \cdot 10^{-8}\): the true field has an ever smaller divergence. The term \(K \operatorname{div}\vek u = 2\,(1 + \nu)\,A/3\) stays finite, \(20{,}8\) MPa at \(\nu = 0{,}3\) and \(23{,}998\) MPa at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) (num-08.py), because by definition 3.7 it is the mean normal stress \((\sigma_{\mathrm{rr}} + \sigma_{\theta\theta} + \sigma_{\mathrm{zz}})/3 = (2A + 2\nu A)/3\) from equation (11.36), which hardly depends on \(\nu\). The volumetric energy is \(\tfrac12 K\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}}^2\) at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) only \(0{,}88\) J/m³ against \(1324\) J/m³ at \(\nu = 0{,}3\): the factor \(K\) grows as \(1/(1 - 2\nu)\) while the square of the divergence falls as \((1 - 2\nu)^2\), so faster.

The true field thus escapes the price by having a divergence close to zero. The trial field of the finite elements must do the same. If it cannot, it pays \(K\) times the square of a divergence that is not zero. The theorem will say this.

15.8.3 Locking is a penalty (zaklepanje je kazen)

Recall what we found for the Timoshenko beam in lemma 12.35: the shear stiffness \(kGA\) is the penalty number, the constraints are the shear strains at the Gauss points. Here the penalty number is \(K/G\) and the constraints are the divergences at the Gauss points. Let the mesh have \(m\) elements, each with \(q\) Gauss points. We choose the matrix \(\mat C\) with the row \(\sqrt{t\,w_{\mathrm{g}}\,J(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})}\;\mat b_{\mathrm{v}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) for every pair \((e, g)\) (selection matrix \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\), definition 7.11, without the columns of the prescribed unknowns), so that \(|\mat C\,\vek d|^2\) is the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{e},\mathrm{g}} t\,w_{\mathrm{g}}\,J\,(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}(\vek x_{\mathrm{g}}))^2\). We assemble the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\) from the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{e}}/G\) by theorem 7.13 and write the reduced system (theorem 7.18). The load is a pressure or a body force that does not depend on \(\nu\), so the right side \(\vek f\) is independent as well.

Theorem 15.56 Locking is a penalty system (zaklepanje je kazenski sistem)

Let \(E\) be constant and \(-1 < \nu < \tfrac12\), \(G = E/\big(2(1 + \nu)\big)\), \(K = E/\big(3(1 - 2\nu)\big)\) and \(\alpha = K/G\). Let the reduced system of theorem 7.18 for a plane finite element with matrices \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\) and \(\mat C\) as above be

\[\big(G\,\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + K\,\mat C^\T\mat C\big)\,\vek d_\nu = \vek f , \tag{15.42}\]

with the right side \(\vek f\) independent of \(\nu\). Let condition (H) hold: \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d > 0\) for every \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\). Let \(V_{\mathrm{c}} = \{\vek d : \mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\}\) be the set of columns whose field has divergence zero at all Gauss points, \(\vek d^*\) the column of \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) at which the function \(\tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d - \vek f^\T\vek d\) takes its smallest value on \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\), and \(\mu\) the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \mat C^\T\mat C\). Then:

  1. For every \(\nu\) the system (15.42) has exactly one solution \(\vek d_\nu\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \mat C^\T\mat C\) is positive definite and \(\mu > 0\). There exist \(\vek d^*\) and a column \(\vek\lambda\), independent of \(\nu\), with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d^* = \vek f + \mat C^\T\vek\lambda\).
  2. The divergences at the Gauss points tend to zero: \(|\mat C\,\vek d_\nu| \le |\vek\lambda|/K\).
  3. For \(\nu \ge \tfrac18\) (so that \(\alpha \ge 1\)) \(|G\,\vek d_\nu - \vek d^*| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}\). The field \(\vek d_\nu\) therefore tends to \(\vek d^*/(E/3)\) as \(\nu \to \tfrac12\), and this field is the best field in \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) in the sense of energy.
  4. For \(\nu \ge \tfrac18\) the mesh locks (zaklene) if \(V_{\mathrm{c}} = \{\vek 0\}\) or if the load does no work on any column of \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\), \(\vek f^\T\vek d = 0\) for every \(\vek d \in V_{\mathrm{c}}\). Then \(\vek d^* = \vek 0\) and \(|\vek d_\nu| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,G\,K}\) tends to zero, while the true displacement field has a finite limit as \(\nu \to \tfrac12\) (as for the tube, section 15.8.2).
Proof

We must show that the system is a penalty system with the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\), constraints \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\) and penalty number \(\alpha\), and then repeat the estimates of theorem 12.28 that do not need independent rows of the matrix \(\mat C\).

  1. The matrix \(G\,\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + K\,\mat C^\T\mat C\) is symmetric, \(\vek d^\T(\cdot)\,\vek d = G\,\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d + K\,|\mat C\,\vek d|^2 \ge 0\), because \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\) is positive semidefinite: \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d\) is a sum over elements and rule points with positive weights \(w_{\mathrm{g}}\,t\,J\) and terms \((\mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}})^\T(\mat D_{\mathrm{G}}/G)(\mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}) = 2\sum e_{\mathrm{ij}}^2 \ge 0\) (steps 2 and 3 of lemma 15.55). If the sum is zero, both terms are zero, \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek 0\) and \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d = 0\), and by (H) \(\vek d = \vek 0\). The matrix is positive definite and the solution is unique (theorem 6.29). In the same way \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \mat C^\T\mat C\) is positive definite, \(\mu > 0\) (theorem 10.26 with \(\mat M = \mat I\)).
  2. From the rows of the matrix \(\mat C\) choose a largest independent set, the matrix \(\mat C'\), so that every row of \(\mat C\) is a linear combination of them. Then \(\ker\mat C' = \ker\mat C = V_{\mathrm{c}}\). The hypotheses of theorem 12.26 hold (\(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\) positive semidefinite, (H), \(\vek g = \vek 0\)), so there are exactly one \(\vek d^*\) and exactly one \(\vek\lambda'\) with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d^* = \vek f + \mat C'^\T\vek\lambda'\). If we put \(\vek\lambda'\) at the places of the chosen rows and zero elsewhere, we get \(\vek\lambda\) with \(\mat C'^\T\vek\lambda' = \mat C^\T\vek\lambda\) and \(|\vek\lambda| = |\vek\lambda'|\). Since \(\vek d^*\) and \(\vek\lambda\) come from \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\), \(\mat C\) and \(\vek f\), they do not depend on \(\nu\).
  3. Divide (15.42) by \(G\): \((\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\,\vek d = \vek f/G\). So \(\tilde{\vek d} = G\,\vek d_\nu\) solves \((\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\,\tilde{\vek d} = \vek f\), a penalty system with the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\) and \(g = 0\) (definition 12.27). The error \(\vek e = \tilde{\vek d} - \vek d^*\) with \(\mat C\,\vek d^* = \vek 0\) and item 2 solves \((\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\,\vek e = -\mat C^\T\vek\lambda\) (as equation (12.30)).
  4. Multiply by \(\vek e^\T\): \(\vek e^\T(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\vek e = -(\mat C\,\vek e)\cdot\vek\lambda \le |\mat C\,\vek e|\,|\vek\lambda|\) (Cauchy-Schwarz). The left side is at least \(\alpha\,|\mat C\,\vek e|^2\), since \(\vek e^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek e \ge 0\), so \(|\mat C\,\vek e| \le |\vek\lambda|/\alpha\). Since \(\mat C\,\vek d^* = \vek 0\), \(\mat C\,\vek e = \mat C\,\tilde{\vek d} = G\,\mat C\,\vek d_\nu\), and \(|\mat C\,\vek d_\nu| \le |\vek\lambda|/(G\,\alpha) = |\vek\lambda|/K\). This is item 2.
  5. By step 4 also \(\vek e^\T(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\vek e \le |\vek\lambda|^2/\alpha\). For \(\alpha \ge 1\) the left side is at least \(\vek e^\T(\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + \mat C^\T\mat C)\vek e \ge \mu\,|\vek e|^2\), so \(|\vek e| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}\), and \(\vek e = G\,\vek d_\nu - \vek d^*\). This is item 3.
  6. For \(V_{\mathrm{c}} = \{\vek 0\}\), \(\vek d^* = \vek 0\). If \(\vek f^\T\vek d = 0\) for every \(\vek d \in V_{\mathrm{c}}\), the function on \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) equals \(\tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{G}}\,\vek d\), which by (H) is positive for \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\), so its smallest value is at \(\vek d^* = \vek 0\). In both cases, for \(\nu \ge \tfrac18\) (\(\alpha \ge 1\)), by step 5 \(|G\,\vek d_\nu| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}\), hence \(|\vek d_\nu| \le |\vek\lambda|/(G\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}) = |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,G\,K}\), which tends to zero with \(K \to \infty\). By section 15.8.2 the true field has a finite limit as \(\nu \to \tfrac12\). This is item 4.

Source: COOK §13.4-13.5 (PDF pp. 511-514, read): "As \(\nu\) approaches 0.50, matrix \(B[K_{\mathrm{b}}]\) dominates the equation, thus acting as a penalty constraint and, unless \([K_{\mathrm{B}}]\) is singular, locking the mesh", and the condition \(H = 0\) at the Gauss points. The proof of the estimates is not from the source, it follows theorem 12.28 of chapter 12 without the assumption of independent rows.

The theorem does not say whether a mesh locks, but when and why. A mesh locks when among the fields with divergence zero at all Gauss points there is none on which the load does work. In the limit \(\nu \to \tfrac12\) the solution is the best of these fields (item 3). For the tube the space \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) of these fields has dimension \(8\) in the Q4 mesh \(4 \times 8\) (section 15.8.5), but the pressure does no work on any of these eight fields, \(\vek f^\T\vek d = 0\) for every \(\vek d \in V_{\mathrm{c}}\) (NumPy). By item 4 of the theorem, \(\vek d^* = \vek 0\) and the computed displacement tends to zero. The ratio of the computed to the true displacement of the inner edge is \(0{,}1765\) at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\), \(0{,}0210\) at \(\nu = 0{,}49999\) and \(0{,}0021\) at \(\nu = 0{,}499999\). What does an element allow with one Gauss point, and what with four?

15.8.4 What Q4 cannot bend (kaj Q4 ne zna ukriviti)

Let us look at one element. Take a rectangle \(2a \times 2b\) with its centre at the origin and the field that bending causes. In bending with curvature \(\kappa\) the longitudinal displacement at the point \((x, y)\) is \(u = \kappa\,x\,y\), because layers above the axis stretch and below it shorten, and the transverse displacement is \(v = -\kappa\,x^2/2\). The Q4 element has a bilinear displacement field (section 15.5), so it cannot represent \(x^2\). Its nodes have the value \(-\kappa a^2/2\), and a bilinear function with four equal values is constant, \(v = -\kappa a^2/2\). The constant part is a rigid motion, so we leave it out of the drawing.

We draw the original position of the element. In the field \(u = \kappa\,x\,y\) the upper edge stretches, the lower one shortens and the sides tilt: the deformed position is an isosceles trapezoid. The strains are \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \partial u/\partial x = \kappa\,y\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \partial v/\partial y = 0\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \partial u/\partial y + \partial v/\partial x = \kappa\,x\), so \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = \kappa\,y\). True bending strain at \(\nu = \tfrac12\) would have \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = 0\) everywhere, but in Q4 \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}}\) is independent of \(y\), because \(v\) is only linear in \(y\), so the element cannot reduce it. The volumetric strain \(\kappa\,y\) is zero on the midline and of opposite sign above and below. In the four Gauss points it is \(\pm 0{,}58\,\kappa b\) (from \(\pm 1/\sqrt3\)), in the centre it is zero. The energy of the volumetric part over the element is \(\tfrac12 K \int \kappa^2 y^2 = \tfrac23\,K\,\kappa^2 a\,b^3\), the energy of shape for the same field by equation (3.43) is \(\tfrac29\,G\,\kappa^2 a\,b\,(3a^2 + 4b^2)\) (num-08.py, SymPy). For \(a = b\) the ratio is \(3K/(7G)\): \(0{,}93\) at \(\nu = 0{,}3\) and \(2143\) at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\). An element that is supposed to bend pays thousands of times more for a volume that it does not need. COOK states this for Q4 in plane strain: bending "produces volumetric strain ... which is strongly resisted as \(\nu\) approaches 0.50" and "there are locations in elements where the unwanted strains are zero: ... at the center of the Q4 element" (PDF p. 512, read).

This leads to counting. If the constraints \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) are at four points, bending is forced to zero in the field, if the constraint is only at the centre, bending stays free. We prove it exactly for a rectangular mesh.

15.8.5 Counting constraints (štetje vezi)

Every element of the mesh brings unknowns and constraints, figure 15.32.

We draw a large part of a Q4 mesh, every element has nodes at its corners. Since neighbours share nodes, in a large mesh one node comes per element, for example the upper right one, which has two unknowns, the displacements \(u\) and \(v\). With the full rule the element has four Gauss points and in each the constraint \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), so four constraints for every two unknowns, the ratio \(2 : 4\). With the rule of one point there is one constraint, the ratio \(2 : 1\). COOK (PDF p. 515, read) counts this for a Q4 mesh in plane strain: "there are \(4N_{\mathrm{els}}\) penalty constraints as \(\nu\) approaches 0.50, and only \(2N_{\mathrm{els}}\) active d.o.f., so the mesh is locked", with one point \(r = 2/1\), and states the conjecture that the best ratio equals the number of dimensions. For the eight-node element \(6\) unknowns come per element (\(1\) corner and \(2\) midside nodes with \(2\) unknowns each), with the \(2\times2\) rule \(r = 6/4 = 3/2\) (COOK, PDF p. 515), with the \(3\times3\) rule \(r = 6/9\), less than one (our count). The count is approximate because the rows are not independent, so we also count the independent constraints (a constraint is independent if it is not a linear combination of the others, which num-08.py finds by elimination).

For the tube with the Q4 mesh \(4 \times 8\) there are \(80\) free unknowns. The full rule gives \(32 \cdot 4 = 128\) constraints, of which \(72\) are independent, so only \(80 - 72 = 8\) fields remain in \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\). The rule with one point gives \(32\) constraints, all independent, so \(48\) fields (num-08.py). The dimension of \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) for square meshes with all boundary nodes clamped is shown by the table.

For Q4 on a rectangular mesh we prove this for every mesh of rectangles.

Theorem 15.57 Q4 on a rectangular mesh locks (Q4 na pravokotni mreži zaklene)

Let the body be a rectangle \([0, X] \times [0, Y]\) cut into rectangles with sides parallel to the axes, let every element be Q4 and let the matrix \(\mat C\) have its rows at the points of the \(2 \times 2\) rule. Let all boundary nodes be clamped, \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = v_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\). Then:

  1. A field of \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) is affine on every element, has \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), and on the whole body has the form \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = c\,x + g(y)\), \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = -c\,y + h(x)\) with a number \(c\) and continuous piecewise linear functions \(g\) and \(h\).
  2. With the clamped boundary \(V_{\mathrm{c}} = \{\vek 0\}\): the mesh locks, and by theorem 15.56 \(\vek d_\nu \to \vek 0\) as \(\nu \to \tfrac12\).
Proof

We must show that the constraint at four points forces every element into an affine field, then that the affine fields of neighbouring elements agree only as in item 1, and that the boundary condition leaves only zero. On an element with centre \((x_{\mathrm{c}}, y_{\mathrm{c}})\) and half-sides \(a\), \(b\) we have \(x = x_{\mathrm{c}} + a\,\xi\), \(y = y_{\mathrm{c}} + b\,\eta\) and \(\vek\xi \in [-1, 1]^2\) (section 15.5).

  1. The bilinear displacements are \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = \alpha_0 + \alpha_1\xi + \alpha_2\eta + \alpha_3\xi\eta\) and \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \beta_0 + \beta_1\xi + \beta_2\eta + \beta_3\xi\eta\). The divergence is \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = (\alpha_1 + \alpha_3\eta)/a + (\beta_2 + \beta_3\xi)/b = c_0 + (\alpha_3/a)\,\eta + (\beta_3/b)\,\xi\) with \(c_0 = \alpha_1/a + \beta_2/b\).
  2. The Gauss points of the \(2\times2\) rule are \((\xi, \eta) = (\pm g, \pm g)\) with \(g = 1/\sqrt3\). The constraint at all four gives four equations \(c_0 \pm (\alpha_3/a)\,g \pm (\beta_3/b)\,g = 0\) for all combinations of signs. The sum of all four gives \(4\,c_0 = 0\), the difference of the first two and the last two in \(\eta\) gives \(\alpha_3 = 0\), in \(\xi\) it gives \(\beta_3 = 0\). So the field on the element is affine and \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} \equiv 0\).
  3. Let two elements share a vertical edge \(x = x_0\). On it both fields are linear functions of \(y\) with the same values at both nodes, so \(\partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial y\) and \(\partial v_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial y\) agree. Since \(\partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x = -\partial v_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial y\) on both elements (step 2), \(\partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x\) agrees as well. For a horizontal edge it is the same: \(\partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x\) and \(\partial v_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x\) agree. So \(\partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x\) is the same number \(c\) on the whole connected mesh and \(\partial v_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial y = -c\).
  4. The function \(w = u_{\mathrm{h}} - c\,x\) has \(\partial w/\partial x = 0\) on every element, so it depends on \(y\) only. In one row of elements the functions agree as functions of \(y\) because of continuity at the vertical edges, between rows because of continuity at the horizontal edges. So \(w = g(y)\), a continuous piecewise linear function. In the same way \(v_{\mathrm{h}} + c\,y = h(x)\). This is item 1.
  5. With the clamped boundary \(u_{\mathrm{h}}(0, y) = g(y) = 0\) for all \(y \in [0, Y]\), so \(g = 0\), and \(u_{\mathrm{h}}(X, y) = c\,X = 0\) gives \(c = 0\). In the same way \(h = 0\). So \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = v_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) and \(V_{\mathrm{c}} = \{\vek 0\}\). Hypothesis (H) of theorem 15.56 holds because \(\ker\mat C\) is trivial, so item 4 of the theorem gives \(\vek d_\nu \to \vek 0\).

Source: The proof is not from the source. COOK (PDF pp. 514-515, read) states such a result for the counting example, without proof.

The theorem is special: rectangles and a clamped boundary. For the annulus of figure 15.29 the elements are distorted, the boundary is only partly clamped, and \(8\) fields remain, not \(0\), but the pressure does no work on any of them, so the mesh locks all the same (theorem 15.56, item 4). But the proof shows where the fault is: when the constraint is at four points, the element must have \(\alpha_3 = \beta_3 = 0\), that is no terms \(\xi\eta\), which are the ones that bend the element, as in figure 15.31.

15.8.6 Selective integration (izbirna integracija)

Let us look for a way out. The constraint \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) is required only by the penalty term, not by the term of shape. If we integrate it with fewer points, there will be fewer constraints, and the shape part keeps the full rule that we need for stability.

Definition 15.58 Selective integration (izbirna integracija)

Let a plane element have the matrix (15.41). Selective integration (izbirna integracija) computes the shape part \(\mat k_{\mathrm{G},\mathrm{e}}\) with the full Gauss rule (for Q4 \(2 \times 2\)) and the volumetric part \(\mat k_{\mathrm{K},\mathrm{e}}\) with fewer points (for Q4 \(1 \times 1\), for Q8 \(2 \times 2\) instead of \(3 \times 3\)). A rule that chooses a different number of points for two parts of the same expression is called selective, because we choose which part gets which rule.

Source: COOK §13.5 (PDF pp. 513-515, read): "If \(n = m\) and sampling points \(i\) and \(j\) are the same, the integration scheme is uniform, otherwise it is selective." The definition in our form.

The system matrix is then \(G\,\mat K_{\mathrm{G}} + K\,\mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\) with the matrix \(\mat C_1\) that has rows only at these fewer points. This is again a system of theorem 15.56, only with a shorter matrix of constraints, and the theorem holds unchanged if hypothesis (H) holds: the only field with divergence zero at these points and with zero energy of shape is zero. This does not follow from the theorem, so in the computations of the table we checked that the reduced matrix of the system is positive definite (its smallest eigenvalue is positive, num-08.py). There are fewer constraints, so \(V_{\mathrm{c}}\) is larger. For the tube of the first paragraph the dimension \(8\) with the full rule grew to \(48\) with the one-point rule (the count above), and in the table the ratio at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) is no longer \(0{,}1765\) but \(0{,}9955\). The limit as \(\nu \to \tfrac12\), the field \(\vek d^*/(E/3)\) of item 3 of the theorem, also gives the ratio \(0{,}9955\) (NumPy). The table also shows Q8 with the full rule for the shape part, \(3\times3\), and the volumetric part with \(2 \times 2\): the ratio is \(1{,}0000\) at all \(\nu\).

The price is that the volumetric term is no longer integrated exactly. For the element of figure 15.31 the energy of the volumetric part is \(\tfrac23\,K\,\kappa^2 a\,b^3\), and the one-point rule in the centre estimates it as zero, because \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{v}} = 0\) there. The computed functional is no longer the energy of the body, so the estimate of theorem 6.39 of chapter 6 does not hold for it, as it speaks about the exactly integrated energy. The confidence in the result comes from the table, not from a theorem: \(0{,}9955\) for Q4. We do not claim that selective integration never fails: COOK (PDF p. 516, read) says that "reduced integration may have to be accompanied by an element stabilization device in order to avoid spurious modes", and section 15.6 shows that the Q8 element with the \(2\times2\) rule has four zero eigenvalues, so the shape part always gets the full rule. Elements with a mixed formulation (COOK PDF pp. 517-518) are a topic of their own and we only name them here.

15.8.7 Plane stress does not lock (ravninsko napetostno stanje ne zaklene)

Theorem 15.56 claims that \(K \to \infty\) causes locking. Let us test whether there is no locking without it. Take the same tube in plane stress, as if it were a thin plate with a hole (definition 11.32), with the same material, \(\nu = 0{,}4999\). The Q4 mesh with the full rule gives the ratio \(0{,}9940\), the Q8 mesh with the \(3\times3\) rule \(1{,}0000\) (num-08.py, Lamé solution of theorem 11.27 with \(S = 0\), \(u_{\mathrm{r}}(a) = 18{,}979\) µm). The mesh does not lock, although the material is the same.

The reason is corollary 11.34, item 2: plane stress of a material with \(E\), \(\nu\) solves the same plane system as plane strain with \(E_{\mathrm{p}} = E\,(1 + 2\nu)/(1 + \nu)^2\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm{p}} = \nu/(1 + \nu)\). At \(\nu = 0{,}4999\), \(\nu_{\mathrm{p}} = 0{,}33329\) and \(E_{\mathrm{p}} = 174{,}2\) GPa, so the bulk modulus of the replacement plane material is \(E_{\mathrm{p}}/\big(3\,(1 - 2\nu_{\mathrm{p}})\big) = 174{,}2\) GPa, finite, and the shear modulus stays \(G = 65{,}34\) GPa. The ratio \(K_{\mathrm{p}}/G = 2{,}67\) is as small as for steel. Where is the volume? At \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) the true field has in the plane \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} + \varepsilon_{\theta\theta} = 1{,}2247 \cdot 10^{-4}\), but the plate becomes thinner, \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = -1{,}2242 \cdot 10^{-4}\), and the sum of the three is \(4{,}9 \cdot 10^{-8}\): the thinning in the direction \(z\) takes over the volume that the plane must not change. Plane strain prevented this with \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\). COOK (PDF p. 516, read) says the same: "For plane stress conditions, there is no locking or ill-conditioning even when \(\nu = 0{,}50\)." The hypothesis \(K \to \infty\) of theorem 15.56 therefore does not hold for plane stress, and neither does the conclusion, the constraints \(\operatorname{div}\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} \to 0\).

15.8.8 The same phenomenon in bar and beam (isti pojav pri palici in nosilcu)

The locking that we found is not new. Chapter 6 chose for the hanging bar in space the family of fields \(u_{\mathrm{y}} = u_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\), which does not allow thinning, and obtained the ratio \(E/(\lambda + 2\mu) = (1 + \nu)(1 - 2\nu)/(1 - \nu) = 0{,}7429\) at \(\nu = 0{,}3\). That is a constraint chosen by the family of fields, not by the mesh: at \(\nu = 0{,}4999\) the ratio is \(0{,}0006\) (num-08.py), and as \(K \to \infty\) it points to the same compulsion. For the Timoshenko beam (lemma 12.35) the penalty number is \(kGA\), the constraint is the shear strain at the Gauss points, with the full rule, \(2\) points per element, only zero remains (lemma 12.36), and with one point bending stays free (lemma 12.37). Here the penalty number is \(K/G\), the constraint the divergence, the full \(2\times2\) rule for Q4 leaves only zero (theorem 15.57), and the one-point rule allows bending. For axisymmetric elements (section 15.9) the volumetric strain is \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rr}} + \varepsilon_{\mathrm{zz}} + \varepsilon_{\theta\theta}\) and the same theorem and the same remedy apply, for three-dimensional elements (section 15.10) the constraint is of the same kind, and COOK (PDF p. 515, read) gives \(3/1\) as the favourable ratio for the trilinear element with one point.

Locking is therefore not a fault of the mesh but the consequence of two things: a large number in the material and too small a space of fields that can carry that number. We remove it by the choice of the rule, at the cost of one of these two. The table shows that at \(\nu\) near \(\tfrac12\) the eight-node element with the \(2\times2\) rule for the volumetric part and \(3\times3\) for the shape part computes the tube to four decimals, and the four-node one with one point to half a percent.
