Chapter 21 Modal reduction and eigenvalue solvers

21.3 The additional mass method (metoda dodatne mase)

In section 21.2 we solved base motion with relative coordinates: from the displacement of the structure we subtracted the rigid displacement carried by the support, and obtained an equation with the right-hand side \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) (theorem 21.5). That requires us to separate the support node from the other coordinates, to partition the matrices and to impose the motion on the support. Can we do without? The additional mass method takes the model as it is, adds a large mass to the support node and pushes it with a force known from the ground acceleration. One system of equations for all coordinates is then solved at once, without partitioning (GR section 2.9.6, PDF p. 129). This section shows how the method works, why it is only approximate, how large the error is and what the large mass costs.

The model is the frame with three floors from chapter 10. Each floor has mass \(m = 500\) kg, each storey has stiffness \(k = 501\,760\) N/m, the floors move only horizontally and there is no damping. The natural frequencies of the frame with a fixed support are \(f_{\mathrm{r}} = 2{,}2438\), \(6{,}2870\) and \(9{,}0850\) Hz. Here the support is a single node with horizontal displacement \(q_2\), on which the columns of the first storey stand. The ground moves with acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\) and the support node moves with it. In a real building a foundation carries the ground motion into the structure, and here we idealize it as a rigid node, since the flexibility of the foundation and the soil would change the model. In figure 21.8 the frame is drawn on the left.

We build the figure part by part. First three floors of mass \(m\), joined to each other and to the support by columns, as in chapter 10. The columns of the first storey stand on the support block, which is a node with horizontal displacement \(q_2\) and can roll on the ground, as a foundation moves together with the ground. If we wanted to move the block with acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) by prescription, we would have to partition the matrices and impose the displacement. Instead we load the block with an additional mass \(M_0\), which the structure does not have, and push it with the horizontal force \(M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\). The force has this value by Newton's law: a body of mass \(M_0\) with acceleration \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) needs a force equal to the product of the two. Since we push the block, the arrow ends at its edge. The name "additional mass" is therefore literal: the mass is added, it is not a mass of the structure, and its purpose is to force the acceleration of the block to the value \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\).

Why should the block really have an acceleration close to \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\)? The free-body diagram of the block answers (figure 21.8, right), where we cut the structure just above the body of the block. Two horizontal forces act on the body: the push \(M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) from the left and the reaction \(R\) of the structure, which pulls the block back when the columns hold it. It is called a reaction because it is the structure's answer to the force with which the block moves it (Newton's third law: the force of the block on the structure and the force of the structure on the block are equal and opposite). By Newton's second law \(M_0\,\ddot q_2 = M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} - R\), so

\[\ddot q_2 = \ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} - \frac{R}{M_0} . \tag{21.20}\]

The acceleration of the block differs from the ground acceleration by \(R/M_0\). In slow motion the reaction has to accelerate the whole structure, so by Newton \(R \approx 3m\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} = 1500\ \mathrm{kg}\cdot\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\), and the difference is small when \(M_0\) is much larger than this mass. How much larger is shown by theorem 21.14, and here is the plan of its proof: equation (21.20) holds at every time, and the theorem solves it for harmonic ground motion.

Now we write the method in matrices. We split the coordinates into a column \(\vek q_1\) of structure coordinates (for the frame the three floor displacements) and one support displacement \(q_2\), and split the mass and stiffness matrices accordingly,

\[\mat M = \begin{bmatrix} \mat M_{11} & \mat M_{12} \\ \mat M_{21} & M_{22} \end{bmatrix} , \qquad \mat K = \begin{bmatrix} \mat K_{11} & \mat K_{12} \\ \mat K_{21} & K_{22} \end{bmatrix} , \tag{21.21}\]

with \(\mat M_{21} = \mat M_{12}^\T\) and \(\mat K_{21} = \mat K_{12}^\T\). Index \(1\) means the structure, index \(2\) the support, as in GR section 2.9. For the frame \(\mat M_{11} = m\,\mat I\), \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(M_{22} = 0\), since we do not count the mass of the support in the structure.

Definition 21.11 Additional mass method (metoda dodatne mase)

Let \(\mat M\) be a symmetric positive semidefinite and \(\mat K\) a symmetric matrix, split as in (21.21), with \(\mat M_{11}\) and \(\mat K_{11}\) positive definite (definition 6.27). Let \(\vek s\) be a column with \(\mat K\,\vek r = \vek 0\) for \(\vek r = (\vek s,\ 1)\), that is

\[\mat K_{11}\,\vek s + \mat K_{12} = \vek 0 , \qquad \mat K_{21}\,\vek s + K_{22} = 0 . \tag{21.22}\]

The column \(\vek r\) is the rigid motion of the structure caused by a displacement of the support by \(1\) (frame: \(\vek s = (1, 1, 1)\)). Let \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\) be the prescribed acceleration of the support. We write

\[\vek h = \mat M_{11}\,\vek s + \mat M_{12} , \qquad m_\Sigma = \vek r^\T\mat M\,\vek r . \tag{21.23}\]

The column \(\vek h\) is the inertia column of theorem 21.5 and \(m_\Sigma\) the total mass of the structure and the support without the added mass, as in section 21.2. The prescribed problem is to find the motion of the structure for \(q_2(t) = u_{\mathrm{g}}(t)\). The additional mass method (metoda dodatne mase) with an added mass (dodana masa) \(M_0 > 0\) instead solves

\[\mat M^{0}\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \begin{bmatrix} \vek 0 \\ M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}} \end{bmatrix} , \qquad \mat M^{0} = \mat M + M_0\begin{bmatrix} \mat 0 & \vek 0 \\ \vek 0^\T & 1 \end{bmatrix} , \tag{21.24}\]

that is, the equation with the same stiffness matrix and a mass matrix to which \(M_0\) is added in the row of the support, and with the force \(M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) on the support. The matrix \(\mat M^0\) is positive definite.

Source: GR section 2.9.6, (2.195) to (2.197) (PDF pp. 128-129, read): the same model with a matrix \(\mat M^0_{22}\) for several support coordinates, we take one. Condition (21.22) and the symbols \(m_\Sigma\) and \(\vek h\) are ours.

The matrix \(\mat M^{0}\) is positive definite: if \(\vek v^\T\mat M^0\vek v = 0\), both nonnegative terms \(\vek v^\T\mat M\,\vek v\) and \(M_0\,v_2^2\) are zero, so \(v_2 = 0\) and \(\vek v_1^\T\mat M_{11}\vek v_1 = 0\), and since \(\mat M_{11}\) is positive definite, \(\vek v = \vek 0\). System (21.24) is the equation of theorem 10.2 without damping and with the force \((\vek 0,\ M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}})\).

How is the added mass related to the penalty method of chapter 12? The second row of system (21.24) is exactly equation (21.20), if we write \(R = \mat M_{21}\ddot{\vek q}_1 + M_{22}\,\ddot q_2 + \mat K_{21}\vek q_1 + K_{22}\,q_2\) for what the structure demands of the support (for the frame the sum of the column forces of the first storey, figure 21.8, reaction): \(R = -M_0\,(\ddot q_2 - \ddot u_{\mathrm{g}})\). In definition 12.27 the constraint \(\mat C\,\vek d = \vek g\) is penalized by the system \((\mat K + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\mat C)\,\vek d = \vek f + \alpha\,\mat C^\T\vek g\) and the penalty forces are \(\vek\lambda_\alpha = -\alpha\,(\mat C\,\vek d_\alpha - \vek g)\). Now the constraint is \(\ddot q_2 = \ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\), not a constraint on displacement, the penalty number is the added mass, \(\alpha = M_0\), and the "penalty" is the force \(-M_0\,(\ddot q_2 - \ddot u_{\mathrm{g}})\), which grows with how much the acceleration of the support differs from the prescribed one. The name is penalty because a mismatch costs a force, and the size \(M_0\) of the penalty decides how expensive the mismatch is. GR remarks that a penalty is usual for constraints on displacements, but not for constraints on accelerations (PDF p. 128). An exact constraint would be a Lagrange multiplier, which GR treats in chapter 1, section 1.6, and this section does not use it.

How large \(R/M_0\) is in (21.20) depends on the frequency of the motion. What force must the support give the structure if we move it with a harmonic acceleration of amplitude \(a\) and there is no other load? In slow motion it must accelerate the whole mass, so the force is \(m_\Sigma\,a\), as for a rigid body. In faster motion the floors lag or lead and the force is different. The ratio of the amplitude of the force to the amplitude of the acceleration will be called the dynamic mass.

Definition 21.12 Dynamic mass at the support (dinamična masa na podlagi)

Let \(\mat M\), \(\mat K\), \(\vek s\), \(\vek h\) and \(m_\Sigma\) be as in definition 21.11, \(\Omega \ge 0\), and \(\Omega^2\) not an eigenvalue of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\). Write \(\mat H(\Omega) = (\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11})^{-1}\). The dynamic mass at the support (dinamična masa na podlagi) is the number

\[m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega) = M_{22} + \mat M_{21}\,\vek s - \big(\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21}\big)\,\mat H(\Omega)\,\vek h . \tag{21.25}\]

Source: The name is ours. The notion is GR's support impedance \(\mat Z^*_{22}\) (2.164) (PDF p. 122): for a rigid motion \(\vek u_{2(\mathrm{i})}\) of the support, \(\vek u_{2(\mathrm{i})}^\T\mat Z^*_{22}(\Omega^2)\,\vek u_{2(\mathrm{i})} = -\Omega^2\,m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega)\). At \(\Omega = 0\) the result is the total mass, in GR \(m_\mathrm{T}\) of (2.192) (PDF p. 128), computed from the mass matrix \(\mat M^*_{22}\) (2.158b) condensed on the support. The mass \(m_\mathrm{S}\) of (2.193) is from the block \(\mat M_{22}\) without condensation.

The name has its reason in two words. "Mass", because the ratio of force to acceleration is a mass by Newton's second law, as item 1 of lemma 21.13 shows. "Dynamic", because the number depends on the frequency \(\Omega\), and at \(\Omega = 0\) it is the total mass \(m_\Sigma\) (item 3).

Lemma 21.13 Properties of the dynamic mass (lastnosti dinamične mase)

Let the hypotheses of definition 21.12 hold. Let the support oscillate harmonically, \(q_2 = \mathrm{Re}\big(Q_2\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t}\big)\), and let no force load the structure otherwise. Then:

  1. The displacements of the structure are \(\vek q_1 = \mathrm{Re}\big((\vek s\,Q_2 + \vek Y)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t}\big)\) with the relative part \(\vek Y = \Omega^2\,\mat H(\Omega)\,\vek h\,Q_2\), and the force that the support must give has amplitude \(-\Omega^2\,m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega)\,Q_2\), that is, \(m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega)\) times the amplitude \(-\Omega^2 Q_2\) of the support acceleration.
  2. \(m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega) = m_\Sigma + \Omega^2\,\vek h^\T\mat H(\Omega)\,\vek h\).
  3. Let \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) be the eigenvalues and modes of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\) and \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek h\). Then
    \[m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega) = m_\Sigma + \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{n_1}\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2\,\frac{\Omega^2}{\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \Omega^2} , \tag{21.26}\]
    \(m_{\mathrm{d}}(0) = m_\Sigma\), and \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) has a pole at every \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) with \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} \ne 0\).
Proof

We must show that the structure follows the support with the offset \(\vek Y\) (steps 1 and 2), that the force on the support really is \(-\Omega^2 m_{\mathrm{d}} Q_2\) (step 3), rewrite \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) with condition (21.22) (steps 4 and 5) and finally insert the modal sum (step 6). We write \(\vek q_1 = \mathrm{Re}(\vek Q_1\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t})\) and \(\vek Y = \vek Q_1 - \vek s\,Q_2\).

  1. The rows of the structure without load are \(\mat M_{11}\ddot{\vek q}_1 + \mat M_{12}\ddot q_2 + \mat K_{11}\vek q_1 + \mat K_{12}q_2 = \vek 0\). With \(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t}\), \(\ddot{\vek q}_1 \to -\Omega^2\vek Q_1\) (as in step 1 of the proof of theorem 10.53), so \((\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11})\vek Q_1 + (\mat K_{12} - \Omega^2\mat M_{12})\,Q_2 = \vek 0\).
  2. We insert \(\vek Q_1 = \vek Y + \vek s\,Q_2\) and use the first condition (21.22), \(\mat K_{11}\vek s = -\mat K_{12}\), and the definition of \(\vek h\) from (21.23): \((\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11})\,\vek Y = \Omega^2\,\vek h\,Q_2\). Since \(\mat H\) exists, \(\vek Y = \Omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,Q_2\). This is the first part of item 1.
  3. The row of the support without the added mass gives the force \(F = (\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\,\vek Q_1 + (K_{22} - \Omega^2 M_{22})\,Q_2\). With \(\vek Q_1 = \vek Y + \vek s\,Q_2\) and the second condition (21.22), \(\mat K_{21}\vek s + K_{22} = 0\), we get \(F = (\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\,\vek Y - \Omega^2\,(\mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22})\,Q_2\). With \(\vek Y\) from step 2, \(F = -\Omega^2\big[M_{22} + \mat M_{21}\vek s - (\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\mat H\,\vek h\big]Q_2 = -\Omega^2\,m_{\mathrm{d}}\,Q_2\). This is item 1.
  4. From symmetry and the first condition (21.22), \(\mat K_{21} = \mat K_{12}^\T = -\vek s^\T\mat K_{11}\). From (21.23), \(\vek h^\T = \vek s^\T\mat M_{11} + \mat M_{21}\), so \(\mat M_{21} = \vek h^\T - \vek s^\T\mat M_{11}\). Therefore \(\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21} = -\vek s^\T(\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11}) - \Omega^2\vek h^\T\) and \((\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\,\mat H\,\vek h = -\vek s^\T\vek h - \Omega^2\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h\).
  5. The total mass is \(m_\Sigma = \vek r^\T\mat M\,\vek r = \vek s^\T\mat M_{11}\vek s + \vek s^\T\mat M_{12} + \mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22} = \vek s^\T\vek h + \mat M_{21}\vek s + M_{22}\). From (21.25) and step 4, \(m_{\mathrm{d}} = M_{22} + \mat M_{21}\vek s + \vek s^\T\vek h + \Omega^2\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h = m_\Sigma + \Omega^2\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h\). This is item 2.
  6. Let \(\mat\Phi\) be the modal matrix of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\) (definition 10.15) and \(\mat\Lambda = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}})\). By lemma 10.16, \(\mat K_{11} - \Omega^2\mat M_{11} = \mat\Phi^{-\T}(\mat\Lambda - \Omega^2\mat I)\mat\Phi^{-1} = \mat M_{11}\mat\Phi\,(\mat\Lambda - \Omega^2\mat I)\,\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\) (as in step 2 of the proof of lemma 10.50), the diagonal elements \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2\) are nonzero, and with \(\mat\Phi^\T\mat M_{11}\mat\Phi = \mat I\) we get \(\mat H = \mat\Phi\,(\mat\Lambda - \Omega^2\mat I)^{-1}\mat\Phi^\T\). Hence \(\vek h^\T\mat H\,\vek h = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} \Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2/(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}} - \Omega^2)\) and item 2 gives (21.26). At \(\Omega = 0\) the sum is zero, \(m_{\mathrm{d}}(0) = m_\Sigma\). As \(\Omega \to \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) the terms with \(\omega_{\mathrm{s}} \ne \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) stay bounded. The terms with \(\omega_{\mathrm{s}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) all have the same sign, since \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{s}}^2 \ge 0\), and if \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}} \ne 0\) is among them, the absolute value of their sum grows beyond every bound. This is the pole.

Source: The proof is not from the sources. For a rigid motion of the support, item 3 is GR (2.164) (PDF p. 122) divided by \(-\Omega^2\), and the same modal expansion as in lemma 10.50, with \(\vek h\) in place of a force. Sum (21.26) is the same as the support force per unit acceleration (21.17) in corollary 21.10.

The lemma says that \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) at \(\Omega = 0\) is the total mass, as Newton demands for a rigid body, and that its absolute value grows beyond every bound when \(\Omega\) approaches a frequency of the structure with fixed support. For the frame \(\mat M_{12} = \vek 0\) and \(\vek h = m\,\vek s\), so the \(\Gamma_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) are the effective modal masses of section 21.2 (definition 21.8): \(1371{,}12\), \(112{,}32\) and \(16{,}57\) kg, together \(1500\) kg \(= m_\Sigma\). At \(f = 4\) Hz, that is \(\Omega = 25{,}133\) 1/s, with \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} = 14{,}098\), \(39{,}502\) and \(57{,}082\) 1/s, the terms of sum (21.26) are \(-2000{,}65\), \(76{,}38\) and \(3{,}98\) kg, and

\[m_{\mathrm{d}}(2\pi\cdot 4\ \mathrm{Hz}) = 1500 - 2000{,}65 + 76{,}38 + 3{,}98 = -420{,}29\ \mathrm{kg} = -0{,}8406\,m . \tag{21.27}\]

The first term is negative because \(\Omega > \omega_1\): above the first natural frequency the first mode oscillates in antiphase with the support and demands a force opposite to the acceleration. The computation is in num-03.py.

Now we have everything to say what motion the method gives. The theorem holds for harmonic ground motion, because that is the case where the dynamic mass can be computed.

Theorem 21.14 Accuracy of the additional mass method (natančnost metode dodatne mase)

Let the hypotheses of definition 21.11 hold, \(\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}(t) = \mathrm{Re}\big(A\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t}\big)\) with \(\Omega > 0\), let \(\Omega^2\) not be an eigenvalue of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\), and \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega) \ne 0\). Let \(U = -A/\Omega^2\) be the amplitude of the prescribed support displacement \(u_{\mathrm{g}}\) and \(\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}} = \Omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,U\) the amplitude of the relative displacement \(\vek q_1 - \vek s\,u_{\mathrm{g}}\) of the prescribed problem. Then:

  1. System (21.24) has one harmonic solution \(\vek q = \mathrm{Re}(\vek Q\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t})\) of this frequency, and with the factor \(\beta = M_0/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega))\)
    \[Q_2 = \beta\,U , \qquad \vek Q_1 - \vek s\,Q_2 = \beta\,\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}} . \tag{21.28}\]
  2. The relative error \(\varepsilon = \beta - 1 = -m_{\mathrm{d}}/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\) is the same for all coordinates of the relative displacement. For \(M_0 \ge 2\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}|\), \(|\varepsilon| \le 2\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}|/M_0\), and \(M_0\,\varepsilon \to -m_{\mathrm{d}}\) as \(M_0 \to \infty\).
  3. The natural circular frequencies \(\omega > 0\) of system (21.24) with \(\omega^2\) different from the eigenvalues of the pair \((\mat K_{11}, \mat M_{11})\) are exactly the solutions of \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\omega) = 0\).
Proof

We must show that the rows of system (21.24) with a harmonic ansatz give the same relative offset as in lemma 21.13 and an equation for \(Q_2\) in which \(M_0\) is only added to \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\). The first row does not contain \(M_0\), the second contains \(M_0\) on both sides.

  1. We insert \(\vek q = \mathrm{Re}(\vek Q\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Omega t})\), \(\ddot{\vek q} \to -\Omega^2\vek Q\). The rows of the structure are the same as in step 1 of lemma 21.13. With \(\vek Y = \vek Q_1 - \vek s\,Q_2\), step 2 of the lemma gives \(\vek Y = \Omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,Q_2\).
  2. The row of the support is \((\mat K_{21} - \Omega^2\mat M_{21})\,\vek Q_1 + (K_{22} - \Omega^2 M_{22})\,Q_2 - \Omega^2 M_0\,Q_2 = M_0\,A\). The first two terms together are, by step 3 of lemma 21.13, equal to \(-\Omega^2 m_{\mathrm{d}}\,Q_2\), because we computed \(\vek Y\) in step 1. Hence \(-\Omega^2\,(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\,Q_2 = M_0\,A\).
  3. Since \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}} \ne 0\), \(Q_2 = M_0 A/\big(-\Omega^2 (M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\big) = \beta\,U\) with \(U = -A/\Omega^2\), and \(\vek Y\) is determined uniquely by step 1. So the solution is unique.
  4. In the prescribed problem \(q_2 = u_{\mathrm{g}}\), so \(Q_2 = U\), and by step 1 (without the added mass) \(\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}} = \Omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,U\). Then by steps 1 and 3, \(\vek Y = \Omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,\beta\,U = \beta\,\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}}\). This proves item 1.
  5. From step 4, \(\vek Y - \vek Y_{\mathrm{t}} = (\beta - 1)\,\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}}\) and \(\beta - 1 = -m_{\mathrm{d}}/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\), the same factor for every coordinate. For \(M_0 \ge 2\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}|\), \(|M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}| \ge M_0 - |m_{\mathrm{d}}| \ge M_0/2\) and \(|\varepsilon| \le 2\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}|/M_0\). Also \(M_0\,\varepsilon = -m_{\mathrm{d}}\,M_0/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}) \to -m_{\mathrm{d}}\). This is item 2.
  6. Free vibration is a solution of (21.24) with \(A = 0\). Steps 1 and 2 give \(-\omega^2\,(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\omega))\,Q_2 = 0\) and \(\vek Y = \omega^2\mat H\,\vek h\,Q_2\). For \(Q_2 = 0\) also \(\vek Y = \vek 0\) and \(\vek Q = \vek 0\), which is not a mode. So \(Q_2 \ne 0\) and \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\omega) = 0\). Conversely, if \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}(\omega) = 0\), we take \(Q_2 = 1\), \(\vek Y\) from step 1 and \(\vek Q_1 = \vek Y + \vek s\), and step 2 gives the row of the support. This is item 3.

Source: GR section 2.9.6 (PDF p. 129): the method and the criterion that \(M_0\) should be large. Formula (21.28) and its proof are ours, GR does not compute the error. The theorem is proved only for harmonic ground motion, GR states the method for any motion.

What does the theorem say? Because of the added mass the support moves by the factor \(\beta\) of the prescribed displacement, and the structure follows the support with the same factor, so only the overall size of the motion is wrong, not its shape. Since the elastic forces of the structure depend only on the relative displacement \(\vek q_1 - \vek s\,q_2\) (a rigid motion produces none, \(\mat K\,\vek r = \vek 0\)), the forces in the columns have the same relative error \(\varepsilon\). The error is \(-m_{\mathrm{d}}/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\), about \(-m_{\mathrm{d}}/M_0\), so it is not "a small mass against a large mass" in general, but the dynamic mass at this frequency against \(M_0\). This is the measure that figure 21.9 shows.

Example 21.15 The frame at 4 Hz with masses \(M_0 = 10\,m\) to \(10^4\,m\)

The ground moves with acceleration of amplitude \(1\) m/s² at \(4\) Hz, so \(\Omega = 25{,}133\) 1/s and \(U = -1/\Omega^2 = -1{,}583\) mm. The prescribed problem (theorem 21.14, step 4) has amplitudes of relative displacements of the floors \(\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}}\) that for unit acceleration are \(0{,}8376\), \(2{,}1445\) and \(3{,}0980\) mm. The dynamic mass is, by (21.27), \(m_{\mathrm{d}} = -420{,}29\) kg. We solve system (21.24) for four values of \(M_0\) and in each case compute \(\beta\) from the theorem (column \(Q_2/U\)) and the error \(100\,\varepsilon\):

\(M_0/m\) \(M_0\) (kg) \(Q_2/U = \beta\) \(100\,\varepsilon\) (%) \(-100\,m_{\mathrm{d}}/M_0\) (%) first nonzero \(f\) (Hz) \(\kappa(\mat M^0)\)
10 5000 1,091771 9,177 8,406 2,5248 10
100 50 000 1,008477 0,8477 0,8406 2,2743 100
1000 500 000 1,000841 0,0841 0,0841 2,2469 1000
10 000 5 000 000 1,000084 0,0084 0,0084 2,2441 10 000

For all four \(M_0\) the ratio \(\vek Y/\vek Y_{\mathrm{t}}\) is the same for all three floors and equal to \(\beta\) to six digits (\(1{,}091771\) at \(M_0 = 10\,m\)), as item 1 says. The error falls to a tenth for every tenfold \(M_0\), because \(\varepsilon \approx -m_{\mathrm{d}}/M_0\) (\(|m_{\mathrm{d}}| = 0{,}84\,m\)). As \(M_0\) grows, the nonzero natural frequencies approach the natural frequencies \(2{,}2438\), \(6{,}2870\) and \(9{,}0850\) Hz of the frame with fixed support (at \(M_0 = 10\,m\) they are \(2{,}5248\), \(6{,}3596\) and \(9{,}1006\) Hz), and one frequency is zero, because the frame with the added mass is still a free body (theorem 10.23). The squares of the circular frequencies are the eigenvalues of the pair \((\mat K, \mat M^0)\), that is, of system (21.24) without the force. Inserted into \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}\) they give zero, as item 3 requires: at \(M_0 = 10\,m\), \(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}}\) at the computed frequency \(2{,}5248\) Hz is less than \(10^{-10}\) kg, which is zero up to the rounding of the computation. The computation is in num-03.py.

At \(M_0 = 10\,m\) the error is \(9{,}2\) %, as predicted: \(M_0\) is only \(11{,}9\) times larger than \(|m_{\mathrm{d}}| = 0{,}84\,m\) (\(5000\) kg against \(420\) kg), and the requirement "\(M_0\) much larger than \(|m_{\mathrm{d}}|\)" is barely met. This is the first near miss of the method. Since \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) is negative at \(4\) Hz, \(\varepsilon = |m_{\mathrm{d}}|/(M_0 - |m_{\mathrm{d}}|)\). An error of at most \(1\) % therefore requires \(M_0 - |m_{\mathrm{d}}| \ge 100\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}|\), so \(M_0 \ge 101\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}| = 42\,449\) kg \(= 84{,}9\,m\), and an error of at most \(0{,}1\) % requires \(M_0 \ge 1001\,|m_{\mathrm{d}}| = 841{,}4\,m\). The range given by GR is \(10^2\) to \(10^4\) times the norm of \(\mat M_{11}\) (PDF p. 129), for the frame \(100\,m\) to \(10\,000\,m\), and the error at \(4\) Hz is \(0{,}85\) % and \(0{,}0084\) % there.

The second near miss is the frequency. The theorem requires \(M_0 \gg |m_{\mathrm{d}}(\Omega)|\) at the frequency of the motion, and \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) has a pole at every natural frequency of the frame with fixed support (lemma 21.13, item 3). At \(\Omega = 0{,}5\,\omega_1\), \(0{,}9\,\omega_1\), \(0{,}99\,\omega_1\) and \(0{,}999\,\omega_1\) (\(f = 1{,}1219\), \(2{,}0194\), \(2{,}2214\) and \(2{,}2416\) Hz) \(m_{\mathrm{d}} = 1961\), \(7359\), \(69\,046\) and \(686\,049\) kg, and the error at \(M_0 = 100\,m\) is \(3{,}77\) %, \(12{,}8\) %, \(58{,}0\) % and \(93{,}2\) %. At \(M_0 = 10^4\,m\) the errors are \(0{,}039\) %, \(0{,}147\) %, \(1{,}36\) % and \(12{,}1\) %. The cause is in sum (21.26): at \(0{,}99\,\omega_1\), \(\Omega^2/(\omega_1^2 - \Omega^2) = 49{,}251\) and the first term is \(1371{,}12 \cdot 49{,}251 = 67\,529\) kg. Physically: near resonance the structure responds with large displacements, its reaction \(R \approx m_{\mathrm{d}}\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) becomes large and drags the heavy block, since \(R/M_0\) in (21.20) is large. For an error of at most \(1\) % at \(0{,}99\,\omega_1\) we would need \(M_0 \ge 99\,m_{\mathrm{d}} = 6{,}84 \cdot 10^6\) kg \(= 1{,}4 \cdot 10^4\,m\) (here \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) is positive and \(|\varepsilon| = m_{\mathrm{d}}/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\), hence \(99\) instead of \(101\)), which is already outside the range of GR. The blue curve in figure 21.9 shows the error at \(4\) Hz, the orange one at \(0{,}9\,\omega_1\) and the blackwhite one at \(0{,}99\,\omega_1\).

In the figure there are two axes with logarithmic scales. Drawn are the blue curve at \(4\) Hz, the orange one at \(0{,}9\,\omega_1\) and the blackwhite one at \(0{,}99\,\omega_1\), and the dots are the four rows of the table. The grey band marks the range recommended by GR, and the lower part of the figure (conditioning) shows the price. The curves fall with slope \(-1\), except near small \(M_0\) and at frequencies near resonance, where \(m_{\mathrm{d}}\) holds them up.

The price of the large mass is in the matrix \(\mat M^0\). The condition number (definition 6.31) shows how many digits of the solution we lose in the computation, and it grows with \(M_0\) like in the penalty method (lemma 12.29).

Lemma 21.16 Growth of the condition number with the added mass (rast števila občutljivosti z dodano maso)

Let the hypotheses of definition 21.11 hold and \(\mu_1\) be the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat M_{11}\). Then for every \(M_0 > 0\)

\[\kappa(\mat M^0) \ge \frac{M_0}{\mu_1} . \tag{21.29}\]
Proof

We must bound the largest eigenvalue from below and the smallest from above, both by the Rayleigh quotient (theorem 10.26 with \(\mat M = \mat I\)).

  1. The matrix \(\mat M^0\) is symmetric and positive definite (definition 21.11), so it has a condition number. The largest eigenvalue is at least the quotient at the column \(\vek e_2 = (\vek 0,\ 1)\): \(\vek e_2^\T\mat M^0\vek e_2 = M_{22} + M_0 \ge M_0\), because \(M_{22} \ge 0\) (a diagonal element of a positive semidefinite matrix).
  2. The smallest eigenvalue is at most the quotient at \((\vek v_1,\ 0)\), where \(\vek v_1\) is an eigencolumn of \(\mat M_{11}\) with eigenvalue \(\mu_1\): the quotient is \(\vek v_1^\T\mat M_{11}\vek v_1/|\vek v_1|^2 = \mu_1\), and does not depend on \(M_0\).
  3. The ratio of the two (definition 6.31) is at least \(M_0/\mu_1\).

Source: The proof is not from the sources. GR (PDF p. 129) limits \(\|\mat M^0_{22}\|\) to \(10^2\) to \(10^4\) times \(\|\mat M_{11}\|\), to keep the conditioning of the mass matrix.

For the frame \(\mu_1 = m\) and \(\mat M^0 = \mathrm{diag}(m, m, m, M_0)\), so for \(M_0 \ge m\) bound (21.29) is an equality: \(\kappa(\mat M^0) = M_0/m\), \(10\), \(10^2\), \(10^3\) and \(10^4\) in the table of example 21.15, the line in the lower part of figure 21.9. Rounding does not spoil the solution for this frame: the computed error of the relative displacements agrees with the formula of the theorem to three digits up to \(M_0 = 10^{12}\,m\) (num-03.out). The reason is the same as for the horizontal support in chapter 12: the large number stands on the diagonal only. For a mesh in which \(M_0\) takes part in off-diagonal terms we did not test this, and so the upper limit of GR remains a source and not a result of this section.

To sum up. The additional mass method replaces the prescribed motion of the support with a large mass and the force \(M_0\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\) on it (definition 21.11), and for harmonic ground motion the error of all relative displacements is the same factor \(\varepsilon = -m_{\mathrm{d}}/(M_0 + m_{\mathrm{d}})\) (theorem 21.14). The factor is small only when \(M_0\) is much larger than the dynamic mass at the frequency of the motion, which is large at frequencies near a natural frequency of the structure with fixed support (lemma 21.13). The price is a condition number that grows with \(M_0\) (lemma 21.16). What we proved is for harmonic motion and one support coordinate. For several support coordinates GR takes a matrix of added masses \(\mat M^0_{22}\) in place of a number (PDF p. 128), and for an arbitrary time dependence the theorem is not proved here. We do not use a Fourier series to split the frequencies. Sum (21.26) is the same as the support force per unit acceleration (21.17) of section 21.2, where we already shortened it to a few modes (approximations (21.18)), and the mode-acceleration correction is in section 21.1.
