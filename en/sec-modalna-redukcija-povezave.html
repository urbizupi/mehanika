Chapter 21 Modal reduction and eigenvalue solvers

21.10 Connections (povezave)

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

  • The Ritz idea behind every reduction: each reduced model of this chapter, that is the truncated modal computation, the Guyan basis, the Craig-Bampton basis, the subspace of subspace iteration and the Krylov subspace (Krilov podprostor), is a Ritz model with a basis \(\mat\Psi\) from definition 10.58. So by theorem 10.59 no eigenvalue of the reduced model lies below the eigenvalue of the full model with the same ordinal number. Why? The basis allows only shapes that are combinations of its columns. That is an added constraint, and a constraint can only stiffen a structure, never soften it. For the truncated modal computation they are equal, because the columns of the basis are modes of the full model. The numbers of section 21.4 confirm this: Guyan reduction of the cantilever with two elements has the frequencies \(3{,}52198\) and \(22{,}2790\,\omega_0\), and the full model with the same two elements \(3{,}51772\) and \(22{,}2215\,\omega_0\). The continuous cantilever has \(3{,}51602\) and \(22{,}0345\,\omega_0\). In figure 21.28 the Guyan values are the blue points at \(n = 2\), on the error axis \(0{,}17\) % and \(1{,}11\) % above the continuous cantilever. That the full model lies above it too is a second step, theorem 13.82, which for the cantilever holds under the assumption that the modes are complete (lemma 13.80 proves it only for the bar and for the simply supported beam). The choice of the basis is what separates the methods, and it is the question left open at the end of section 10.9 (DSK).
  • Truncation of the modal sum: bar C gave \(81{,}12\) % of the static tip deflection with one mode (section 10.10.5). In figure 21.8 this is the start of the dashed blue curve at \(\Omega = 0\), which lies below the start of the exact blackwhite one. Theorem 21.4 adds the static part of the dropped modes to the solution with the kept modes. At \(\Omega = 0\) the deflection is then exact, and at \(\Omega = 0{,}5\,\omega_1\) the error with one mode is \(0{,}240\) % instead of \(15{,}06\) %: at the first dot the orange curve with the correction covers the blackwhite one, while the dashed blue one stays below it. The correction holds only while the dropped modes answer statically. Why exactly this? A dropped mode \(r\) answers as a spring multiplied by the dynamic factor \(1/(1 - (\Omega/\omega_{\mathrm{r}})^2)\), and the correction takes the factor \(1\). At \(\Omega = 0{,}5\,\omega_1\) the factor of the second mode is \(1{,}03\) (because \(\omega_2 = 3{,}010\,\omega_1\)), so the assumption is good. At \(\Omega = 0{,}9\,\omega_2\) the factor is \(5{,}26\) and the assumption fails: section 21.1 has this counterexample with the error \(87{,}0\) % with one mode, in figure 21.9 these are the blackwhite and the orange dot far apart, while the curve with the accelerations of two modes again lies on the exact one. For a system without a support that floats, inertia relief (razbremenitev vztrajnosti), the chapter only names the source (CK §11.6, PDF pp. 371 to 376).
  • Effective modal mass and additional mass: by section 21.2 a moving base gives the load \(-\vek h\,\ddot u_{\mathrm{g}}\), where \(\vek h\) is the column of forces with which we would hold the structure if we moved it rigidly with the acceleration \(1\) (theorem 21.5). The effective masses add up to the total mass without the mass at the support, \(m_\Sigma - m_{\mathrm{S}}\) (theorem 21.9). For bar C the sum is \(98{,}20\) % of the mass \(\rho A L\): in figure 21.13 the cumulative sums of the bar stop at the dashed line \(98{,}20\), while the sums of the frame reach the line \(100\) %. The remaining \(1{,}80\) %, the gap between these two lines, is the mass at the support \(m_{\mathrm{S}}\), the part of the bar's mass attached to the support, which moves with it. The consistent mass matrix gives the clamped node a diagonal entry of \(2{,}08\) % of the mass, and the coupling to the first free node lowers it to \(1{,}80\) % (GR §2.9.4 to §2.9.5, PDF pp. 126 to 128). The additional mass method of section 21.3 is similar to the penalty method of definition 12.27, except that it penalizes the acceleration of the support instead of a displacement, and the penalty number is the added mass \(M_0\). The price is ill-conditioning of the same kind as in lemma 12.29: the condition number grows with \(M_0\) (lemma 21.16, lower part of figure 21.22), while the error falls with \(M_0\) (the upper curves). So the recommended range is only the grey band.
  • Guyan reduction and lemma 12.45: section 21.4 extends the exact condensation of massless rotations of chapter 12 to unknowns that do have mass. There the condensation is approximate and by theorem 21.19 its values are upper bounds. The exact condensation is nonlinear in \(\lambda\), and Guyan's is its tangent at \(\lambda = 0\) (theorem 21.20). The choice of kept coordinates decides the accuracy (CK §14.8.2, PDF p. 479, figure 21.28): if for the cantilever with \(n = 2\) the deflections are condensed instead of the rotations, the second frequency is \(37{,}6003\,\omega_0\) instead of \(22{,}2790\,\omega_0\), and the continuous cantilever has \(22{,}0345\,\omega_0\). In the figure this is, at \(n = 2\), the dashed orange curve (rotations kept) with the error \(70{,}6\) % against the dashed blue one (deflections kept) with the error \(1{,}11\) %. Why such a difference? A condensed coordinate follows the kept ones statically, as if it had no inertia. For the rotations this is nearly true, because the turning of a short piece of the beam carries little kinetic energy: the rotations are light and stiff and, with the deflections held still, vibrate on their own only at \(53{,}3\,\omega_0\), \(2{,}4\) times above \(\omega_2\). For the deflections it is not, because the transverse motion carries nearly all the kinetic energy: the deflections, with the rotations held still, vibrate on their own already at \(11{,}0\,\omega_0\), below \(\omega_2\), so they cannot follow statically (section 21.4). Reducing a large model to the coordinates measured in a test (test-analysis models, CK PDF p. 479) and the choice of the mesh are questions of modelling (AK, chapter 17).
  • Substructures: the Craig-Bampton basis of section 21.5 keeps all interface coordinates together as generalized coordinates. In figure 21.39 these are the deflection and the rotation of interface a with the constraint modes \(\vek\psi_{\mathrm{c}1}\) and \(\vek\psi_{\mathrm{c}2}\), and added to them are the fixed-interface modes of substructure \(1\) and substructure \(2\). With this Craig and Bampton simplified the older method of Hurty, which split the interface coordinates into rigid-body and redundant ones (CK §17.6, PDF p. 575). Lemma 21.25 shows that each substructure computes its own part of the basis and of the reduced matrices by itself, and the contributions to the interface coordinates add up as in the assembly of elements (theorem 7.13). The figure shows it like this: each fixed-interface mode is nonzero only on its own substructure, and they share only the interface. The same idea, but with the reference conditions of a moving body and three kinds of modes (rigid-body, reference and normal), is used for the coordinates of a flexible link (DSK, chapter 28, SHABANA §6.7, PDF pp. 309 to 311), for reduced models of shafts with disks in rotors (DSK, chapter 24) and for reduced models of long chains of links in engines (DSK, chapter 23).
  • Eigenvalue algorithms: sections 21.6, 21.7 and 21.8 are the procedures announced at the end of section 10.8.8. In inverse iteration the error of the shape after \(k\) steps is at most \((\lambda_1/\lambda_2)^k\) times the initial one, and the error of the Rayleigh quotient falls at least as fast as \((\lambda_1/\lambda_2)^{2k}\) (theorem 21.27). The square comes from the Rayleigh quotient being stationary at a mode: an error of size \(\varepsilon\) in the shape changes it only by the order \(\varepsilon^2\). In subspace iteration the error of the \(r\)-th value falls at least as fast as \((\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}/\lambda_{\mathrm{q}+1})^{2k}\) (theorem 21.33). In figure 21.60 these predictions are dashed (\(q = 3\), \(q = 6\), \(q = 11\)), and the computed errors are the solid lines (\(q = 3\), \(q = 6\), \(q = 11\)): a larger \(q\) means a larger \(\lambda_{\mathrm{q}+1}\), a smaller ratio and a steeper line. The Lanczos method builds a basis of the Krylov subspace with a three-term recurrence (theorem 21.37). Its lowest value with \(m\) vectors is not above the quotient of inverse iteration after \(m - 1\) steps from the same start (lemma 21.38): in figure 21.69 the solid blue line of \(\omega_1\) lies, wherever that one is drawn, below the dashed one with hollow circles, which is inverse iteration. All three procedures give upper bounds. The quotient of inverse iteration without a shift is not below \(\lambda_1\), the values of subspace iteration are Ritz values (lemma 21.32), and the Lanczos values lie between the true values and the Ritz values of the same basis (lemma 21.38). With a shift (corollary 21.30) the quotient is not necessarily an upper bound of the wanted value, because the Rayleigh quotient lies only above the lowest eigenvalue (theorem 10.42). For the frame of section 21.6 with the shift \(\lambda_0 = 3{,}0\) the quotients are \(2{,}0\), \(3{,}22222\) and \(3{,}24658\), all below the wanted \(\lambda_3 = 3{,}24698\) (in the unit \(k/\bar m\)). The QR, Householder, Jacobi and Sturm sequence methods are only named (CK §15.1, PDF pp. 487 to 489). The Lanczos method is in CK §15.5 (PDF p. 507), and §15.4 is the QR method (PDF p. 501).
  • Damping of reduced models: section 21.9 shows that \(\tilde{\mat C} = \mat\Psi^\T\mat C\mat\Psi\) (lemma 21.42) and that Rayleigh damping stays Rayleigh with the same numbers \(a_0\) and \(a_1\) (corollary 21.43). The reason is linearity: \(\mat\Psi^\T(a_0\mat M + a_1\mat K)\mat\Psi = a_0\tilde{\mat M} + a_1\tilde{\mat K}\). So in figure 21.79 the filled dots of the reduced model lie on the same curve \(\zeta(\omega)\) as the hollow circles of the full one, only at slightly higher frequencies. Modal damping of the full model does not stay diagonal under reduction (theorem 21.44), because each Ritz shape is a mixture of several exact modes (the squared coefficients of the fourth shape). With one interior mode per component the fourth reduced ratio is \(\tilde\zeta_4 = 1{,}631\) % instead of \(2\) %: the fourth blue bar in figure 21.80 stays below the dashed line \(\tilde\zeta_{\mathrm{s}} = \zeta\), while the bars for eight coordinates nearly reach it. The damping models themselves, that is equivalent viscous, hysteretic and viscoelastic damping, complex modes and the state-space form, belong to chapter 20 (DSK).
  • Continuous systems: a reduced model with the first modes is a finite sum of the expansion of theorem 13.72, with the sum of the dropped modes left out. The effective masses of bar C (\(0{,}81057\), \(0{,}09006\) and \(0{,}03240\) in units of \(\rho A L\)) are nearly equal to those of the continuous bar \(8/((2r-1)^2\pi^2)\) (\(0{,}81057\), \(0{,}09006\) and \(0{,}03242\)). In figure 21.13 these are the first three bars of the bar C panel. The difference grows with the ordinal number of the mode and is only \(0{,}06\) % at \(r = 3\). This is a comparison of two computations, not a proof of convergence (section 21.2).
  • Limits of the chapter: proved are the mode-acceleration correction for a harmonic force, the equation of motion relative to the support, the sum of the effective masses with the support mass and the support force by modes, the accuracy of the additional mass method for harmonic support motion and the growth of its condition number, the upper bounds of Guyan and Craig-Bampton reduction, exact condensation, the accuracy of the Craig-Bampton basis when all fixed-interface modes are kept, the assembly of substructures, the convergence of inverse iteration (also with a shift and with sweeping of the modes found), the convergence of the values of subspace iteration, the Lanczos recurrence with its comparison to inverse iteration, load-dependent Ritz vectors, and the damping matrix of the reduced model with Rayleigh and modal damping. Named but not proved are systems without a support and inertia relief, the mode-acceleration correction for a general time dependence of the force (CK (11.40), PDF p. 361), support motion with an arbitrary time dependence and with several support coordinates, the quadratic correction of Guyan reduction, free-interface modes and multilevel substructuring, the rate of the shapes in subspace iteration, the rate of the Lanczos method and the analysis of the loss of orthogonality in it. Reduced models of flexible links and of rotors are in chapters 28 and 24, and the damping models in chapter 20.
