Chapter 7 Basics of FEM for bars and trusses

7.6 Space truss (prostorsko paličje)

Cranes, masts and roofs are trusses whose bars do not lie in one plane. The plane truss of section 7.5 has two unknowns at each joint, while the joint of a crane can move in all three directions. What changes if we allow this? The answer is short: there is one more unknown at the joint and one more component in the unit vector of the bar. One thing does change, though. In space a joint with two bars does not stay in place, and why, we show in the last subsection.

Definition 7.34 Space truss (prostorsko paličje)

A space truss (prostorsko paličje) is a truss (definition 4.4) whose joints have coordinates \((x, y, z)\) in space and in which every joint has an unknown displacement with three components \((u_{\mathrm{x}}, u_{\mathrm{y}}, u_{\mathrm{z}})\). The joint with number \(i\) has the unknowns with numbers \(3i - 2\), \(3i - 1\) and \(3i\), so a truss with \(n_{\mathrm{v}}\) joints has \(3 n_{\mathrm{v}}\) unknowns.

Source: COOK §2.4 (PDF p. 45): "A bar element arbitrarily oriented in global coordinates xyz has a 6 by 6 stiffness matrix", acting on three displacements at each end.

We number the unknowns joint by joint, as in the plane (there \(2i - 1\) and \(2i\)), and for the same reason: the nonzero entries of the matrix then lie close to the diagonal, which will help when we solve large systems (a later section on sparse matrices).

7.6.1 A bar in space (palica v prostoru)

Take a bar from joint \(i\) to joint \(j\) with position vectors \(\vek x_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\vek x_{\mathrm{j}}\) and length \(L = |\vek x_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek x_{\mathrm{i}}|\). Its unit vector from the first joint towards the second is \(\vek n = (\vek x_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek x_{\mathrm{i}})/L\) and now has three components, \(\vek n = (n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}}, n_{\mathrm{z}})\). These are the direction cosines (smerni kosinusi) of the axis of the bar (definition 2.37): if we place a new axis \(\bar x\) along the bar, then \(\bar{\vek e}_{\mathrm{x}} = \vek n\) and the first row of the rotation matrix is \((n_{\mathrm{x}}, n_{\mathrm{y}}, n_{\mathrm{z}})\).

What has to change in the element matrix of lemma 7.28? Almost nothing. The proof of the lemma used only the energy of the bar, \(U_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac{EA}{2L}\big(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}})\big)^2\), in which the elongation of the bar is the projection of the difference of the end displacements onto \(\vek n\) (equation (4.48)). The projection has the same form with two and with three components. So the same lemma holds, only \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}}\) are now columns with three components each and the element matrix has size \(6 \times 6\):

\[\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{EA}{L}\begin{bmatrix} \vek n\vek n^\T & -\vek n\vek n^\T \\ -\vek n\vek n^\T & \vek n\vek n^\T \end{bmatrix} , \qquad \vek n\vek n^\T = \begin{bmatrix} n_{\mathrm{x}}^2 & n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} & n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{z}} \\ n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{y}} & n_{\mathrm{y}}^2 & n_{\mathrm{y}} n_{\mathrm{z}} \\ n_{\mathrm{x}} n_{\mathrm{z}} & n_{\mathrm{y}} n_{\mathrm{z}} & n_{\mathrm{z}}^2 \end{bmatrix} . \tag{7.37}\]

The rows and columns belong to the displacements \(u_{\mathrm{ix}}, u_{\mathrm{iy}}, u_{\mathrm{iz}}, u_{\mathrm{jx}}, u_{\mathrm{jy}}, u_{\mathrm{jz}}\) (COOK (2.4-6), (2.4-7), PDF p. 45). For \(n_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\) the matrix of section 7.5.1 remains in the plane \(x\)-\(y\). Assembly, supports and reactions proceed as for the wall truss in the plane (section 7.5.2): \(\mat K = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\), only the selection matrix \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) has six rows. Theorem 7.13 holds for any number of unknowns per node, so in space we need nothing new: the selection matrix picks six displacements out of the column of all displacements.

What does change is the number of rigid motions (togi gibi). A plane truss has three, two translations and one rotation. In space there are six rigid motions, three translations and three rotations about the coordinate axes. We describe a rotation by a vector \(\vek\omega\) that points along the axis of rotation and whose length is the angle of rotation in radians. A point \(\vek x\) moves under a small rotation about an axis through the origin by \(\vek\omega\times\vek x\) (to first order): the length of this vector is \(|\vek\omega|\) times the distance of the point from the axis, that is the length of the circular arc, and its direction is perpendicular to the axis and to that distance.

Lemma 7.35 Rigid motions of a space truss (togi gibi prostorskega paličja)

Let \(\mat K\) be the stiffness matrix of a space truss, \(\vek a\) a vector and \(\vek\omega\) a rotation vector. Let the displacements of the joints be \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek a + \vek\omega\times\vek x_{\mathrm{i}}\) and let \(\vek d\) be the column of all these displacements. Then the length of no bar changes (to first order), \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = 0\) and \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek 0\). If the joints do not all lie on one line, there are six independent such columns: all displacements \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}}\) are zero only for \(\vek a = \vek 0\) and \(\vek\omega = \vek 0\).

Proof

We must show that the elongation of every bar is zero and then use the lemma on the kernel of a semidefinite matrix.

  1. For the bar from \(i\) to \(j\) we have \(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek\omega\times(\vek x_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek x_{\mathrm{i}}) = L\,\vek\omega\times\vek n\) (points 2 and 3 of lemma 2.31, the vector \(\vek a\) cancels).
  2. The elongation is \(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}}) = L\,\vek n\cdot(\vek\omega\times\vek n) = 0\) by point 4 of the same lemma.
  3. By theorem 7.33 (equation (7.36)), \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \tfrac{EA}{L}\,\big(\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}})\big)^2 = 0\), since every term is zero.
  4. The matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric, because every \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) is (lemma 7.28), and by equation (7.36) \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d \ge 0\) for every \(\vek d\), so it is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15). Hence \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = 0\) gives \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek 0\) by lemma 7.16.
  5. Independence: let \(\vek a + \vek\omega\times\vek x_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek 0\) for all joints and \(\vek\omega \ne \vek 0\). The difference for two joints gives \(\vek\omega\times\vek\Delta = \vek 0\) for \(\vek\Delta = \vek x_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek x_{\mathrm{i}}\). By point 5 of lemma 2.31, \(0 = |\vek\omega\times\vek\Delta|^2 = |\vek\omega|^2|\vek\Delta|^2 - (\vek\omega\cdot\vek\Delta)^2\). For the unit vector \(\vek e = \vek\omega/|\vek\omega|\) this is \(|\vek\Delta|^2 = (\vek\Delta\cdot\vek e)^2\), so by point 3 of lemma 2.14 the squared length of the vector \(\vek\Delta - (\vek\Delta\cdot\vek e)\,\vek e\) is zero. Hence \(\vek\Delta = (\vek\Delta\cdot\vek e)\,\vek e\) is a multiple of \(\vek\omega\). This holds for every pair of joints, so all joints would lie on a line in the direction \(\vek\omega\), which we excluded. Therefore \(\vek\omega = \vek 0\) and from \(\vek a + \vek\omega\times\vek x_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek 0\) also \(\vek a = \vek 0\).

Source: COOK §2.6 (PDF p. 53): "six rigid-body motions must be suppressed to achieve adequate support of a structure" (the sentence is about a structure with six degrees of freedom per node, and a space truss also has the six rigid motions of a body in space). The proof is not from the source.

The lemma says what the supports must do: they must prevent all six motions. Otherwise there would be a rigid motion that is zero at all prescribed unknowns and not zero everywhere. For it \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d = 0\) would hold, the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) would not be positive definite (theorem 7.33), and the system \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) would have infinitely many solutions or none. A space truss therefore needs at least six prescribed unknowns, arranged so that no rigid motion remains. In the next subsection all three displacement components are prescribed at three joints, nine unknowns.

7.6.2 The space bracket (prostorska konzola)

The wall truss of chapter 4 has a horizontal bar \(AC\) on the wall. We replace it by two bars fixed to the wall at two points, like two legs (nogi) of the support of a crane. Such a bracket can also carry a lateral force, which the plane truss cannot. The space bracket has the joints \(A_1 = (-4, 0, 1{,}5)\) m, \(A_2 = (-4, 0, -1{,}5)\) m, \(B = (-4, 3, 0)\) m and \(C = (0, 0, 0)\) (figure 7.22). The pins \(A_1\), \(A_2\) and \(B\) are fixed to the wall \(x = -4\) m and allow no displacement, the bars can turn around them. The bars are \(A_1C\), \(A_2C\) and \(BC\), made of steel (\(E = 196\) GPa), each with cross-section \(A = 1\ \text{cm}^2\), as in chapter 4. At the joint \(C\) acts the load \(F = 10\) kN downwards, the weight of the bars is negligible, as definition 4.4 requires. The axis \(y\) points up, the axes \(x\) and \(z\) are horizontal.

The unit vectors from the wall towards \(C\) are \(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = (4, 0, -1{,}5)/L\), \(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = (4, 0, 1{,}5)/L\) and \(\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}} = (4, -3, 0)/5\), with lengths \(L_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = L_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = \sqrt{16 + 2{,}25}\ \text{m} = 4{,}272\) m and \(L_{\mathrm{BC}} = 5\) m. The direction cosines of the legs are \((0{,}9363,\ 0,\ \mp 0{,}3511)\) and of the bar \(BC\) \((0{,}8,\ -0{,}6,\ 0)\). The stiffnesses \(EA/L\) are \(4{,}588 \cdot 10^6\) N/m for the legs and \(3{,}92 \cdot 10^6\) N/m for the bar \(BC\). We number the joints \(A_1 = 1\), \(A_2 = 2\), \(B = 3\), \(C = 4\), so that \(C\) has the unknowns \(10\), \(11\), \(12\), and the connectivity table (definition 7.10) has the rows \((1, 4)\), \((2, 4)\), \((3, 4)\). The matrix \(\mat K\) has size \(12 \times 12\) and we assemble it as the sum of three element matrices (7.37) (section 7.5.2). Each inserts the block \(\tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n\vek n^\T\) at the places \((e, e)\) and \((4, 4)\) and the block \(-\tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n\vek n^\T\) at the places \((e, 4)\) and \((4, e)\), where \(e\) is the joint on the wall (figure 7.23).

There are only three unknowns, the displacement of the joint \(C\), \(\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = (u, v, w)\), because all the others are prescribed and equal to zero. By theorem 7.18 the reduced system is \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\), where \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is the block of joint \(4\), that is the sum of three contributions \(\tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n\vek n^\T\). The contribution of the leg \(A_1C\) and of the leg \(A_2C\) (in units of \(10^6\) N/m) is

\[4{,}588\begin{bmatrix} 0{,}8767 & 0 & \mp 0{,}3288 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \mp 0{,}3288 & 0 & 0{,}1233 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4{,}0224 & 0 & \mp 1{,}5084 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \mp 1{,}5084 & 0 & 0{,}5657 \end{bmatrix} ,\]

and the contribution of the bar \(BC\) is \(3{,}92\,\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}^\T\),

\[3{,}92\begin{bmatrix} 0{,}64 & -0{,}48 & 0 \\ -0{,}48 & 0{,}36 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2{,}5088 & -1{,}8816 & 0 \\ -1{,}8816 & 1{,}4112 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} .\]

The upper sign \(\mp\) holds for the leg \(A_1C\) and the lower for \(A_2C\), because their components \(n_{\mathrm{z}}\) have opposite signs, so in the sum the terms \(\mp 1{,}5084\) cancel and

\[\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \begin{bmatrix} 10{,}5535 & -1{,}8816 & 0 \\ -1{,}8816 & 1{,}4112 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1{,}1313 \end{bmatrix} \cdot 10^6\ \text{N/m} . \tag{7.38}\]

The displacement \(w\) does not couple with \(u\) and \(v\) precisely because of this cancellation. The entry \(K_{\mathrm{zz}} = 2 \cdot 4{,}588 \cdot 0{,}1233 \cdot 10^6 = 1{,}1313 \cdot 10^6\) N/m is contributed only by the legs, not by the bar \(BC\), which has no component \(z\).

Load downwards. For \(\vek F_1 = (0, -10\,000, 0)\) N the third equation is \(1{,}1313 \cdot 10^6\,w = 0\), so \(w = 0\). What remains is a \(2 \times 2\) system, which we solve by elimination. The first pivot is \(10{,}5535 \cdot 10^6\), the second \(1{,}4112 - 1{,}8816^2/10{,}5535 = 1{,}0757\) (in \(10^6\) N/m), both positive. The solution is

\[\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = (-1{,}657,\ -9{,}296,\ 0)\ \text{mm} .\]

The axial force of a bar is \(N = \tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}})\) (section 7.5.2), here \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek 0\) on the wall and \(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} = \vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\). We get \(N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = N_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = 4{,}588 \cdot 10^6 \cdot 0{,}9363 \cdot (-1{,}657 \cdot 10^{-3}) = -7{,}12\) kN and \(N_{\mathrm{BC}} = 3{,}92 \cdot 10^6 \cdot \big(0{,}8 \cdot (-1{,}657) + (-0{,}6)(-9{,}296)\big) \cdot 10^{-3} = 16{,}67\) kN.

We check with the equilibrium of the joint \(C\) as in section 4.1.4, by hand. The force of a bar on the joint is \(-N\,\vek n\). Component \(y\): \(-F + \tfrac35 N_{\mathrm{BC}} = 0\), since only the bar \(BC\) has a component \(y\), so \(N_{\mathrm{BC}} = \tfrac53 F = 16{,}67\) kN, as in the plane. Component \(x\): \(-2 \cdot 0{,}9363\,N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} - 0{,}8 \cdot 16\,667 = 0\) for equal forces in the legs, so \(N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = -13\,333/(2 \cdot 0{,}9363) = -7{,}12\) kN. Component \(z\): \(0{,}3511\,N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} - 0{,}3511\,N_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = 0\). The horizontal force \(13{,}33\) kN, which in the plane was carried by one horizontal bar, is now carried by the two legs together: each is compressed by \(7{,}12\) kN, and their components \(x\) give \(2 \cdot 0{,}9363 \cdot 7{,}12 = 13{,}33\) kN. By Clapeyron's theorem (theorem 4.86) the energy is \(U = \tfrac12\,F\,|v| = \tfrac12 \cdot 10\,000 \cdot 9{,}296 \cdot 10^{-3} = 46{,}48\) J, and it equals the sum \(\sum N^2 L/(2EA) = 46{,}48\) J (equation (4.5)). The reactions in the pins are \((6667, 0, -2500)\) N in \(A_1\), \((6667, 0, 2500)\) N in \(A_2\) and \((-13\,333, 10\,000, 0)\) N in \(B\). Their sum is \((0, 10\,000, 0)\) N, which balances the load, and the sum of moments about \(C\) is zero. This must hold by lemma 7.35: lemma 7.30 is proved for the plane, but its steps 4 and 5 hold in space with lemma 7.35 for the six rigid motions \(\vek d_{\mathrm{k}}\). From \(\mat K\,\vek d_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek 0\) and the symmetry of \(\mat K\) it follows that \(\vek d_{\mathrm{k}}^\T(\vek f + \vek r) = 0\), and these are the sums of forces in the directions \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) and the sums of moments about the three axes.

Lateral force. For \(\vek F_2 = (0, 0, 1000)\) N, 1 kN in the direction \(z\), the first two equations are homogeneous, and the \(2 \times 2\) matrix in the upper left corner of the matrix (7.38) is positive definite (pivots \(10{,}55\) and \(1{,}08\)), so \(u = v = 0\). The third gives \(w = 1000/(1{,}1313 \cdot 10^6) = 0{,}884\) mm. The elongations of the legs are \(\vek n\cdot\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \mp 0{,}3511 \cdot 0{,}884\) mm and the forces \(N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = -1{,}424\) kN, \(N_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = +1{,}424\) kN. The bar \(BC\) has no component \(z\), so \(N_{\mathrm{BC}} = 0\). The joint moves towards \(A_1\) (which has \(z = +1{,}5\) m), the leg \(A_1C\) shortens and is compressed, the leg \(A_2C\) lengthens and is in tension.

The solutions have an interesting form: under the load downwards the forces in the legs are equal, under the lateral force opposite. This is no accident, it has a cause.

7.6.3 Mirror symmetry of the bracket (zrcalna simetrija konzole)

The bracket has the plane of symmetry \(z = 0\): the reflection in it swaps the joints \(A_1\) and \(A_2\), leaves the joints \(B\) and \(C\) in place, and swaps the legs with equal stiffnesses. The load \(\vek F_1\) lies in the plane and does not change under the reflection, while the load \(\vek F_2\) turns into \(-\vek F_2\). What follows? COOK §2.11 (PDF p. 69) says: under a symmetric load of a symmetric structure the results are symmetric. We prove this for our bracket with the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).

Let \(\mat S = \mathrm{diag}(1, 1, -1)\) be the matrix of the reflection \(z \to -z\), acting on the displacement of the joint \(C\).

Lemma 7.36 Mirror symmetry of the space bracket (zrcalna simetrija prostorske konzole)

For the space bracket \(\mat S\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mat S = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) holds. Let \(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) be the load at the joint \(C\) and \(\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) the solution of the system \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\). Then:

  1. if \(\mat S\,\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) (the load lies in the plane \(z = 0\)), then \(\mat S\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\), so \(w = 0\), and \(N_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\),
  2. if \(\mat S\,\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} = -\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) (the load is perpendicular to the plane), then \(\mat S\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = -\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\), so \(u = v = 0\), and \(N_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}} = -N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\).
Proof

We must show that \(\mat S\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) solves the same system as \(\pm\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) and use uniqueness.

  1. The reflection swaps the vectors \(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\) and \(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}\), \(\mat S\,\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}} = \vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}\), and leaves \(\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}\) in place, \(\mat S\,\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}} = \vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}\), because \(\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}\) has the component \(z\) equal to zero.
  2. Since \(\mat S^\T = \mat S\), we have \(\mat S\,(\vek n\vek n^\T)\,\mat S = (\mat S\vek n)(\mat S\vek n)^\T\), so by step 1 and by \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\) we get \(\mat S\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mat S = \tfrac{EA}{L}\big(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}^\T + \vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}^\T\big) + \tfrac{EA}{L}\big|_{\mathrm{BC}}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}^\T = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\), since the legs have equal stiffnesses.
  3. From \(\mat S^2 = \mat I\) and step 2 it follows that \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mat S = \mat S\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).
  4. Let \(\mat S\,\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} = \pm\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\). Then \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,(\mat S\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}) = \mat S\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \mat S\,\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} = \pm\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\), so \(\pm\mat S\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) solves the same system as \(\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\).
  5. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite (theorem 7.33, or directly from (7.38)), so the system has exactly one solution (theorem 6.29). Hence \(\pm\mat S\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\), which is the claim about the displacements.
  6. The force in the leg \(A_2C\) is (the displacement of the wall is zero) \(\tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}\cdot\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \tfrac{EA}{L}\,(\mat S\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}})\cdot\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \tfrac{EA}{L}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\cdot(\mat S\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}) = \pm N_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\) (\(\mat S\) is a symmetric matrix, so \((\mat S\vek a)\cdot\vek b = \vek a\cdot(\mat S\vek b)\)).

Source: COOK §2.11 (PDF p. 69): rules S1 and A1, "Translational motion has no component normal to a plane of symmetry" and "no component in a plane of antisymmetry". The proof is not from the source.

The lemma agrees with the results: under \(\vek F_1\) we have \(w = 0\) and the forces in the legs are equal (\(-7{,}12\) kN), under \(\vek F_2\) we have \(u = v = 0\) and the forces are opposite (\(\mp 1{,}424\) kN). It also tells us why the blocks \(x\)-\(y\) and \(z\) of the matrix (7.38) are separate: in step 2 the contributions of the legs in the entries \(K_{\mathrm{xz}}\) cancel. An arbitrary load \((F_{\mathrm{x}}, F_{\mathrm{y}}, F_{\mathrm{z}})\) is split into the symmetric part \((F_{\mathrm{x}}, F_{\mathrm{y}}, 0)\) and the antisymmetric part \((0, 0, F_{\mathrm{z}})\) (COOK does the same for a plane frame in figure 2.11-3, PDF p. 70, splitting the load into a symmetric and an antisymmetric part), and the solution separates into a \(2 \times 2\) system and one equation.

7.6.4 Why at least three bars (zakaj vsaj tri palice)

In the plane two bars that do not lie on one line hold a joint in place. In space this is not enough. We remove the bar \(BC\) and leave only the two legs. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is then \(\mathrm{diag}(8{,}0447,\ 0,\ 1{,}1313) \cdot 10^6\) N/m: the entries \(y\) are zero, since no leg has a component \(y\) (figure 7.25). The displacement \(\vek d = (0, 1, 0)\) has zero energy, \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d = 0\). The joint \(C\) can turn about the line \(A_1A_2\) like a door about its hinges. To first order the lengths of the legs do not change, to second order they do: if \(C\) moves by \(1\) mm in the direction \(y\), a leg lengthens only by \(d^2/(2L) = 1{,}2 \cdot 10^{-7}\) m, as in step 3 of the derivation of equation (4.48). Linear theory does not see this, just as it does not see the sag of a rope (section 7.5.3).

When is a joint with several bars therefore not a mechanism? Let \(m\) bars meet at the joint \(C\), with their other ends fixed, with unit vectors \(\vek n_1, \dots, \vek n_{\mathrm{m}}\) from the fixed end towards \(C\) and with stiffnesses \(k_{\mathrm{e}} = EA/L > 0\). The contribution of these bars to the block of joint \(C\) is \(\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\) (as for the bracket).

Lemma 7.37 A joint in space needs three bars (vozlišče v prostoru potrebuje tri palice)

For \(\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e} = 1}^m k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\) the following holds:

  1. \(\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}}\) is positive definite if and only if there is no nonzero vector \(\vek d\) perpendicular to all \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\),
  2. for \(m \le 2\) such a vector always exists,
  3. for \(m = 3\) there is none if and only if the scalar triple product (mešani produkt) \(\vek n_1\cdot(\vek n_2\times\vek n_3) \ne 0\), that is, if the bars do not lie in one plane through \(C\).
Proof

We must show the claims one after another, all from the quadratic form \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}}\,\vek d\) and the lemma on the zero determinant.

  1. Since \(\vek d^\T\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek d = (\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek d)^2\), we have \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}}\,\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} k_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek d)^2 \ge 0\), and it is zero if and only if \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek d = 0\) for all \(e\). The matrix is symmetric. So it is positive definite (definition 6.27) if and only if no such nonzero \(\vek d\) exists. This is point 1.
  2. Let \(\mat N\) be the \(3 \times 3\) matrix whose rows are \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\), the remaining rows (if \(m < 3\)) being zero. The condition \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek d = 0\) for all \(e\) is \(\mat N\vek d = \vek 0\). By point 6 of lemma 2.51 and by definition 2.50, \(\det\mat N = \det\mat N^\T = \vek n_1\cdot(\vek n_2\times\vek n_3)\), where the missing vectors are zero. For \(m \le 2\) at least one of \(\vek n_2\), \(\vek n_3\) is therefore zero, the vector product with a zero vector is zero (point 3 of lemma 2.31, \(k = 0\)) and \(\det\mat N = 0\). By lemma 2.53 there is a \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat N\vek d = \vek 0\).
  3. For \(m = 3\) we have \(\det\mat N = \vek n_1\cdot(\vek n_2\times\vek n_3)\) (the same computation, definition 2.46). By lemma 2.53 there is a \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat N\vek d = \vek 0\) if and only if \(\det\mat N = 0\).
  4. Such a \(\vek d\) means \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek d = 0\) for all three \(e\), that is, all three \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\) lie in the plane through \(C\) with normal \(\vek d\). Conversely, if they all lie in some plane through \(C\), its normal \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) is perpendicular to all of them. So \(\det\mat N = 0\) if and only if the bars lie in one plane through \(C\), which is point 3.

Source: COOK §2.6 (PDF pp. 52-53): an inadequately supported structure has a singular stiffness matrix. The proof is not from the source.

With more than three bars it suffices that three of them have a nonzero triple product, since \(\mat N\vek d = \vek 0\) for these three gives \(\vek d = \vek 0\). Our bracket has it: \(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_1\mathrm{C}}\cdot(\vek n_{\mathrm{A}_2\mathrm{C}}\times\vek n_{\mathrm{BC}}) = 1{,}2 \cdot 0{,}9363 \cdot 0{,}3511 = 0{,}3945 \ne 0\), and all three pivots of the matrix (7.38) (\(10{,}55\), \(1{,}08\) and \(1{,}13\), in \(10^6\) N/m) are positive. If, however, we place the third bar in the plane of the two legs, for example to \(B' = (-4, 0, 0)\), the triple product is zero, the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{CC}}\) has a row \(y\) of zeros only and the joint remains a mechanism. The lemma holds only for a joint whose neighbouring bars are fixed. For several free joints connected to each other, theorem 7.33 decides: no nonzero displacement of the free joints may leave all bars unchanged.

In the plane the same lemma holds with vectors with two components: two bars suffice if and only if \(\det[\vek n_1\ \vek n_2] \ne 0\), that is, if they do not lie on one line. This is why two bars sufficed for the joint \(C\) of the wall truss, while in space three are needed.
