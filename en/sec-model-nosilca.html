Chapter 5 Beam theory (teorija nosilcev)

5.1 Beams and their model (nosilec in njegov model)

In this section we answer the question how to describe a slender body that carries a load across it, what holds it and what loads it, and which numbers are enough to describe it. With the answer we set up the model that the chapter will refine, and we choose two bodies on which we shall try every new idea with numbers. The axis \(x\) points along the body from its left end, the axis \(y\) vertically up, the axis \(z\) toward the reader, so that the triple \(x\), \(y\), \(z\) is right-handed, as in chapters 1 and 2.

5.1.1 Line structures (linijske konstrukcije)

Structures are divided by shape (figure 5.1). A line structure (linijska konstrukcija) is made of bodies that are much longer in one direction than in the other two. We already know the bar: forces act only at its ends or along its axis, so it carries only axial force (definition 4.4). A cable (vrv, kabel) is a very flexible bar that carries only tension. A slender body that also carries load across it, as we shall see in the definition below, is the subject of this chapter. Several such bodies that are rigidly joined to each other make a structure of rigidly joined bodies, which one of the last sections of this chapter treats. A surface structure (ploskovna konstrukcija) has one dimension, the thickness, much smaller than the other two. If it is flat we call it a plate (plošča), if it is curved a shell (lupina). Both have their own chapters (18, 22). When all three dimensions are comparable we have a massive body (masivno telo), such as a cube or the base of a machine. For such a body we must solve the equations of elasticity in all three directions at once. The division follows EP §2.5.1 (pp. 71-72).

For us the slender body that carries a load across it is the most important. In the model we shall replace the cross section by a point on the axis, so we must first say what the axis is. A cross section (prečni prerez) is here the plane surface that we get by cutting the body perpendicular to its axis, as in chapter 2 (figure 2.5). For a symmetric cross section, such as a rectangle or a double T, the centre of the cross section lies where its axes of symmetry meet. The general rule, valid for every cross section, we shall state later in this chapter, when we can compute the mean position of the points of the cross section. For now we take bodies with a symmetric cross section and the axis through the centres of the cross sections.

Definition 5.1 Beam (nosilec)

A beam (nosilec) is a body with a straight axis that has length \(L\) along it and the same cross section everywhere, and the dimensions of the cross section are much smaller than \(L\). The axis (os) is the line through the centres of the cross sections, the cross section (prečni prerez) is the plane perpendicular to it. The beam is loaded by forces that also have a component perpendicular to the axis, and supported at its ends or at several places.

Source: EP §2.5.1 (p. 71), ONATE2 §1.2 (PDF p. 33), SADD §D.3 (PDF p. 574).

How long must a beam be compared with its cross section? EP says that a beam has a very large ratio of length to the dimensions of the cross section, "say, 10 to 100 or more" (EP §2.5.1, p. 71). The ratio \(L/h\), where \(h\) is the height of the cross section in the plane in which the beam bends, we call slenderness (vitkost). The beams of the chapter will have \(L/h = 25\) and \(L/h = 50\) (figure 5.2).

Why does slenderness allow a simpler model? The forces across a cross section gave three numbers in chapter 2: the force along the axis, the force across it and the moment (figure 2.5). The model will replace the whole cross section by them and, about how the cross section deforms, will only assume that it stays plane (we study this in the next section), instead of solving the equations of elasticity in all three directions. How close this is to the truth depends on slenderness. For our steel strip we shall compute later in the chapter, at the Timoshenko beam, that because of the cross sections, which in truth do not stay quite plane, the displacement of the end of the cantilever grows by 0,125 % at \(L/h = 25\) and by 19,5 % at \(L/h = 2\). The share falls with the square of the ratio \(h/L\). EP summarizes that such theories give results "accurate enough for engineering analysis and design" (EP §2.5.1, p. 72). One cost is already visible. The cross section is a point on the axis in the model, so the model does not say how the forces are distributed over the cross section near a support or near the place where a load acts. The exact solution of the plane problem of a beam is also not necessarily valid near the ends of the beam (SADD example 8.2, PDF p. 171).

Besides slenderness the model makes one more restriction. All loads and supports lie in the plane \(xy\) and the cross section is symmetric about the axis \(y\). Then the beam only bends in this plane, without twisting about the axis and without bending sideways (EP §2.5.1, p. 72, ONATE2 §1.2.1, PDF p. 33). What happens with unsymmetric cross sections and with beams loaded in two planes we state as a limit of the model in the last section of the chapter.

5.1.2 Supports and loads (podpore in obremenitve)

Loads first, then supports. Three kinds of loads act on a beam (figure 5.3). A point force (točkovna sila) acts at one point of the axis. For it we already have the force vector (definition 2.1), here with components \(F_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{y}}\). But how do we describe a load that acts over the whole length or over part of it, like weight or snow on a roof? Let \(q(x)\) be the force per unit length of the beam in the direction \(y\) at the place \(x\). With it we write how much force a small piece of the beam takes, and write the sum as an integral. We did similarly for a bar with the axial load (definition 4.74).

Definition 5.2 Distributed transverse load (porazdeljena prečna obtežba)

Let the beam lie along the axis \(x\) from \(0\) to \(L\). A distributed transverse load (porazdeljena prečna obtežba) is a continuous function \(q\) on the interval \([0, L]\) whose input is the place \(x\) on the axis and whose output is the force per unit length of the beam in the direction of the axis \(y\) at that place, in newtons per metre. The load is positive when it points up. On the part of the beam between the places \(x_1 < x_2\) acts, in the direction of the axis \(y\), the force

\[\int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2} q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t , \tag{5.1}\]

and its moment about the point \((0, 0, 0)\) has the component \(z\)

\[\int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2} t\,q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t . \tag{5.2}\]

Source: ONATE1 §2.2 (PDF pp. 66-67), EP §2.5.2 (p. 72, figure 2.5.2), where the symbol is \(q\). The moment follows from definition 2.32 applied to the forces \(q(t_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{i}}\) at the points \((t_{\mathrm{i}}, 0, 0)\), and from the limit of Riemann sums.

The moment of the load is the limit of the sums of the moments of the forces \(q(t_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{i}}\) on pieces of length \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{i}}\), each at the point \((t_{\mathrm{i}}, 0, 0)\). For \(\vek r = (t_{\mathrm{i}}, 0, 0)\) and \(\vek F = (0, q(t_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{i}}, 0)\) the vector product is \(\vek r\times\vek F = (0, 0, t_{\mathrm{i}}\,q(t_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta t_{\mathrm{i}})\), the other two components are zero. Since \(t\,q(t)\) is a continuous function, the sums are Riemann sums and their limit is (5.2). The weight of the beam is an example. Steel has density \(\rho\), the beam has cross-section area \(A\), so the mass of a unit of length is \(\rho A\) and the weight of a unit of length is \(\rho A g\). The weight points down, so \(q = -\rho g A\) (a constant along the whole length for a beam with the same cross section). The uniform load \(q(x) = -q_0\) with \(q_0 > 0\) has by (5.1) the resultant \(-q_0 L\) and by (5.2) the moment \(-q_0 L^2/2\), equal to the moment of the force \(-q_0 L\) at the point \(x = L/2\).

The third kind of load is a pair of opposite forces. The forces \(\vek F\) and \(-\vek F\) at the ends of a ruler, across it, have zero resultant, yet they turn the ruler (figure 2.3). A lever welded across a beam loads it in the same way if two opposite forces push it. Such a pair is named in the definition.

Definition 5.3 Couple (dvojica sil)

A couple (dvojica sil) is a pair of forces \(\vek F\) and \(-\vek F\) that act at different points \(A_1\) and \(A_2\) and do not lie on the same line.

Source: ONATE2 §1.2.1 (PDF p. 33), where point forces and moments act on a plane beam. The definition of a couple is ours.

Since the resultant is zero, by lemma 2.34 on the independence of the moment of the point, the moment of such a pair of forces does not depend on the point about which we compute it. Let us state it with one vector.

Lemma 5.4 Moment of a couple (moment dvojice)

Let the forces \(\vek F\) and \(-\vek F\) act at the points \(A_1\) and \(A_2\). Then their total moment about every point \(O\) equals

\[\vek M_0 = \overrightarrow{A_2 A_1}\times\vek F , \tag{5.3}\]

the vector from the point where \(-\vek F\) acts to the point where \(\vek F\) acts, vector-multiplied by the force \(\vek F\).

Proof

We shall show that we may choose the point \(O\) to be \(A_2\), and then a single term remains.

  1. The resultant of the forces is \(\vek F + (-\vek F) = \vek 0\), so by lemma 2.34 the total moment does not depend on the point about which we compute it. We take \(O = A_2\).
  2. The force \(-\vek F\) acts at the point \(A_2\), the vector from \(A_2\) to \(A_2\) is zero, and its moment is \(\vek 0\times(-\vek F) = \vek 0\) (item 3 of lemma 2.31, used with the number zero).
  3. The force \(\vek F\) acts at the point \(A_1\), its moment about \(A_2\) is by definition 2.32 equal to \(\overrightarrow{A_2 A_1}\times\vek F\). This is the total moment.

Source: The proof is not from a source.

So a couple acts only through the vector \(\vek M_0\), not through where exactly the forces are. Take the forces \(\vek F = (0, 500, 0)\) N at the point \(A_1 = (0{,}05, 0, 0)\) m and \(-\vek F\) at \(A_2 = (-0{,}05, 0, 0)\) m. The vector from \(A_2\) to \(A_1\) is \((0{,}1, 0, 0)\) m, so by (5.3)

\[\vek M_0 = (0{,}1, 0, 0)\times(0, 500, 0) = (0, 0, 50)\ \text{N m} .\]

The couple has only the component \(z\), because both forces lie in the plane \(xy\). We get the same vector if we double the force and halve the distance, since the product does not change. The vector \(\vek M_0\) points in the positive direction of the axis \(z\), toward the reader, and the couple turns the beam counterclockwise as seen by a reader looking toward the negative axis \(z\). A point couple (točkovna dvojica) \(M_0\) on a beam is the limit of such a pair of forces when the distance between the forces shrinks to zero and the forces grow so that \(M_0\) stays the same. Then it loads the beam only with the moment \(M_0 = (\vek M_0)_{\mathrm{z}}\) and with zero resultant (ONATE2 §1.2.1, PDF p. 33).

How is a beam supported? A beam lies on a wall, lies on columns, or is clamped in a wall. A support prevents some displacements and therefore acts on the beam with forces or couples that we call reactions (reakcije). They are external forces, since they act from outside, and they are not known in advance, because the support produces just as much of them as it takes to prevent the displacement. For a plane beam we know three typical supports (figure 5.4).

Definition 5.5 Beam supports (podpore nosilca)

The supports of a plane beam in the plane \(xy\) are three idealizations.

  1. A clamp (vpetje, fixed support) prevents displacement in the directions \(x\) and \(y\) and rotation in the plane. The reactions are a force \((R_{\mathrm{x}}, R_{\mathrm{y}})\) and a couple \(M_{\mathrm{A}}\) (component \(z\)).
  2. A fixed pin support (nepomična členkasta podpora, pin) prevents displacement in the directions \(x\) and \(y\) and allows rotation. The reactions are the components of a force \((R_{\mathrm{x}}, R_{\mathrm{y}})\).
  3. A roller support (pomična členkasta podpora, roller) prevents displacement only in the direction perpendicular to the base along which the support rolls, here the direction \(y\). It allows displacement along the base and rotation. The reaction is one force \(R_{\mathrm{y}}\).

Source: SADD §D.3, figure D.5 (PDF p. 575). The symbols of the reactions are ours.

Each of the three is an idealization and each has a real counterpart. A clamp is the end of a beam welded to a thick plate or built into a wall. A real beam turns a little in the clamp, because the base bends a little, and here we set this rotation to zero. A pin support is a bolt in a hole that allows rotation, but has play and friction. A roller support is a beam lying on rollers or on a greased plate, and the rollers give no force at all along the beam. Which idealization is more suitable depends on the structure. The differences for real supports we estimate in the last section of the chapter, where we shall have numbers.

The reactions are unknown. When can we compute them from equilibrium? The whole beam is in equilibrium with the external forces and the reactions (definition 2.3). For a beam in the plane \(xy\) the force and position vectors lie in the plane, so the moment \(\vek r\times\vek F\) has only the component \(z\), and the two equations, (2.2) and (2.35), give together three equations: the components \(x\) and \(y\) of the forces and the component \(z\) of the moments about a chosen point. The choice of the point for the moments does not change the number of equations, since by the first equation the resultant is zero and by lemma 2.34 the moment does not depend on the point.

Definition 5.6 Statically determinate beam (statično določen nosilec)

A plane beam is statically determinate (statično določen) when the three equations of equilibrium of the whole beam determine all the reactions of the supports uniquely. It is statically indeterminate (statično nedoločen) when it has more unknown reactions than there are independent equations of equilibrium, and for given loads the equations have infinitely many solutions. It is a mechanism (mehanizem) when the supports allow a rigid displacement, so equilibrium for general loads is not possible.

Source: EP example 3.2.6 (p. 122) says of a frame "this is a determinate structure", because all the external reactions follow from the equilibrium of the whole structure, and EP example 3.2.3 (pp. 115-116) says of a shaft clamped between two rigid walls "this is an indeterminate structure", because there is one equation of equilibrium and two unknown torques. The two examples use the words only in a worked example, the definition for a beam in the plane is ours, of the same kind as for the truss (chapter 4, section 4.7.5).

Counting the reactions says where the beam belongs, if the supports prevent all rigid displacements. A rigid displacement (definition 1.11) of a plane body has three free parts: displacement in the direction \(x\), displacement in the direction \(y\) and rotation. If the supports prevent all three and the beam has exactly three unknown reactions, it is statically determinate. If it has more than three, it is statically indeterminate. If the supports do not prevent some rigid displacement, it is a mechanism, even if there are not few reactions. Such is a beam on two roller supports. Both give only a force in the direction \(y\), so no reaction balances a horizontal force and the beam slides along its axis (figure 5.5). We saw the same idea for the truss in section 4.7.5: three unknown forces in the bars, but only two equations of equilibrium of the joint, so equilibrium itself could not tell the true forces.

5.1.3 Two beams that will stay with us (dva nosilca, ki nas bosta spremljala)

Now let us choose concrete beams that we shall use throughout the chapter. They have the same cross section and the same material, they differ in the supports. The cross section is a steel strip of width \(b = 20\) mm in the direction \(z\) and height \(h = 40\) mm in the direction \(y\), standing on its edge (figure 5.6). The area is \(A = b\,h = 800\ \text{mm}^2 = 8\cdot 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2\). We choose the rectangle because we know how to integrate it. The material is the steel of the book: modulus of elasticity \(E = 196\) GPa (section 3.1.2), yield strength \(\sigma_{\mathrm{Y}} = 220\) MPa (definition 2.7) and density \(\rho = 7800\ \text{kg/m}^3\). The weight per unit length is by definition 5.2 \(\rho g A = 7800 \cdot 9{,}81 \cdot 8\cdot 10^{-4} = 61{,}2\) N/m.

The first beam is a cantilever (konzola, cantilever). Its length is \(L = 1\) m, so \(L/h = 25\). At \(x = 0\) it is clamped, and at the free end \(x = L\) a force \(F = 500\) N pushes it down (figure 5.7). The weight of the beam is \(61{,}2\) N, which is 12 % of the force. So we treat the weight separately, at the deflection, which we shall compute later in the chapter. The reactions of the clamp we compute from the equilibrium of the whole beam.

The unknowns are \(R_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(R_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{A}}\). The force \(\vek F = (0, -500, 0)\) N acts at the point \(B = (1, 0, 0)\) m, the clamp is at the point \(A = (0, 0, 0)\).

  1. Equilibrium of forces ((2.2)): \((R_{\mathrm{x}},\ R_{\mathrm{y}} - 500,\ 0) = \vek 0\) N. So \(R_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) and \(R_{\mathrm{y}} = 500\) N.
  2. Equilibrium of moments about \(A\) ((2.35)). The moment of the force \(\vek F\) is by definition 2.32 \((1, 0, 0)\times(0, -500, 0) = (0, 0, -500)\) N m. The moment of the reactions is \((0, 0, M_{\mathrm{A}})\), since the force \(R_{\mathrm{y}}\) acts at \(A\), the vector from \(A\) to \(A\) is zero, and the force \(R_{\mathrm{x}}\) is zero.
  3. Component \(z\): \(M_{\mathrm{A}} - 500 = 0\), so \(M_{\mathrm{A}} = 500\) N m.

So the clamp holds the beam with a force of 500 N up and a couple of 500 N m counterclockwise. The direction says what the clamp does: the load pushes the free end down and tries to turn the beam clockwise, and the clamp turns it back. We got the reactions from equilibrium alone, without any datum about the material, so the cantilever is statically determinate (definition 5.6).

The second beam is the simply supported beam (prostoležeči nosilec, simply supported beam). Its length is \(L = 2\) m, so \(L/h = 50\). At \(x = 0\) it lies on a fixed pin support, at \(x = L\) on a roller support (figure 5.8). We load it in two ways. In the first there is a uniform load \(q = -q_0\) with \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m, which is \(1000\) N/m down along the whole length. In the second there is a force \(F = 1\) kN down at the middle.

The unknowns are \(R_{\mathrm{Ax}}\), \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}}\) at the pin support and \(R_{\mathrm{By}}\) at the roller support. Let us compute the reactions for the uniform load.

  1. Forces, component \(x\): \(R_{\mathrm{Ax}} = 0\), since there is no other force in the direction \(x\). Component \(y\): \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}} + R_{\mathrm{By}} - q_0 L = 0\) by (5.1), since the resultant of the load is \(-q_0 L = -2000\) N.
  2. Moments about \(A\), component \(z\). The force \(R_{\mathrm{By}}\) acts at \((L, 0, 0)\), its moment is \((L, 0, 0)\times(0, R_{\mathrm{By}}, 0) = (0, 0, L\,R_{\mathrm{By}})\). The moment of the load is by (5.2) \(-q_0 L^2/2 = -2000\) N m. The force \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}}\) acts at \(A\) and has no moment. So \(L\,R_{\mathrm{By}} - q_0 L^2/2 = 0\).
  3. From step 2, \(R_{\mathrm{By}} = q_0 L/2 = 1000\) N, and from step 1, \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}} = q_0 L - R_{\mathrm{By}} = 1000\) N.

Each support carries half of the load, because the load lies symmetrically about the middle. For the force \(F = 1\) kN at the middle the moment about \(A\) is \(L\,R_{\mathrm{By}} - F L/2 = 0\), so \(R_{\mathrm{By}} = F/2 = 500\) N and \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}} = 500\) N. Here too the reason is symmetry. The beam is statically determinate.

For comparison take a propped cantilever (podprta konzola, propped cantilever), the same beam with \(L = 2\) m, clamped at \(x = 0\) and supported on a roller support at \(x = L\), loaded by the uniform load \(q_0 = 1\) kN/m down. The reactions are \(R_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(R_{\mathrm{y}}\), \(M_{\mathrm{A}}\) and \(R_{\mathrm{B}}\), four unknowns. Equilibrium gives \(R_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), \(R_{\mathrm{y}} = q_0 L - R_{\mathrm{B}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{A}} = q_0 L^2/2 - L\,R_{\mathrm{B}}\), so three equations for four unknowns. For every choice of the number \(R_{\mathrm{B}}\) the remaining reactions are such that the beam is in equilibrium:

\[\begin{aligned} R_{\mathrm{B}} &= 0 \text{ N}: & R_{\mathrm{y}} &= 2000\ \text{N} , & M_{\mathrm{A}} &= 2000\ \text{N m} , \\ R_{\mathrm{B}} &= 1000\ \text{N}: & R_{\mathrm{y}} &= 1000\ \text{N} , & M_{\mathrm{A}} &= 0 . \end{aligned}\]

The first case is an ordinary cantilever with the load along the whole length, the second is a simply supported beam with a clamp without a couple. Equilibrium does not know which number \(R_{\mathrm{B}}\) is the true one. The deformation of the beam will tell, in three ways: with the equation for the deflection, with virtual forces and with energy, all later in this chapter. We shall then check the result: \(R_{\mathrm{B}} = 750\) N, \(R_{\mathrm{y}} = 1250\) N, \(M_{\mathrm{A}} = 500\) N m.

The question of the section was what a beam is, what holds it and what loads it. The answer is a model: a straight axis, a slender beam, a plane problem, three idealized supports and three kinds of loads. The equilibrium of the whole beam gives the reactions for the cantilever and the simply supported beam, but not for the propped cantilever. And what happens inside the beam? Equilibrium of the whole does not say this, and the next section answers it.
