Chapter 16 Torsion

16.6 Thin strips and open sections (tanki trakovi in odprti prerezi)

The frame of chapter 12 had a section of \(20 \times 40\) mm, a rectangle, and for its torsion constant \(J\) we took the series of theorem 16.30. Structures, however, are often built from thin sheet profiles: an angle, a channel, an I-beam, a turbine blade. Their section is not a rectangle, so the series cannot be inserted, and a new boundary problem for every profile would be too much work. The question of this section is whether the theorem for one rectangle gives a simple calculation for a profile made of several rectangles. The answer is yes if the walls are thin enough, and the section says how thin, with a number.

We begin with one wall. The section is the rectangle \(S = \{(y, z) : |y| \le a,\ |z| \le b\}\) of theorem 16.30, and we now call it a strip (trak): the thickness is \(t = 2a\) along \(y\), the length is \(h = 2b \ge t\) along \(z\). The letter \(t\) is the thickness here, since time does not appear in this chapter, and \(h\) is the length of the strip, not the height of the beam of chapter 5. The model is the torsion of a prism of definition 16.1: an isotropic linear elastic material, the axis \(x\) through the middle, twist per unit length \(\alpha\), and the section warps freely.

We draw the section, a narrow rectangle. We write its thickness \(2a\) and length \(2b\) as two measures, because the ratio \(h/t = b/a\) will decide. In it we place the axes: \(y\) across the thickness and \(z\) along the length. By theorem 16.16 the Prandtl function \(\Phi\) is the height of the membrane above the section (corollary 16.26), which is zero on the boundary and has \(\nabla^2\Phi = \Phi_{\mathrm{yy}} + \Phi_{\mathrm{zz}} = -2G\alpha\). What does the membrane do across the thickness? Let us first see what we get if along most of the length it did not depend on \(z\). Then \(\Phi_{\mathrm{zz}} = 0\), so \(\Phi_{\mathrm{yy}} = -2G\alpha\), the solution of this equation is \(\Phi = -G\alpha\,y^2 + c_1 y + c_0\), and \(\Phi(\pm a) = 0\) gives \(c_1 = 0\), \(c_0 = G\alpha a^2\):

\[\Phi(y) = G\alpha\,(a^2 - y^2) . \tag{16.34}\]

This is the parabola below the section, with the vertex \(G\alpha a^2\) in the middle and zeros on the faces \(y = \pm a\).

Is the membrane really independent of \(z\)? The answer is in the series for the rectangle. From it \(\Phi(0, z)\) is computed on the right: in the middle it is \(0{,}9992\) of the value \(G\alpha a^2\), at \(z = 0{,}6\,b\) it is \(0{,}955\), at the end it drops to zero. The reason for the plateau is the harmonic correction in the series: the terms \(\cosh(n\pi z/t)/\cosh(n\pi h/(2t))\) at the distance \(d\) from the end fall as \(\mathrm e^{-n\pi d/t}\), the first term has the ratio \(32/\pi^3 = 1{,}03\), so at \(d = t\) the deviation is \(1{,}03\,\mathrm e^{-\pi} \approx 4{,}5\,\%\) and at \(d = 1{,}5\,t\) only \(0{,}9\,\%\), which the computed series confirms (\(0{,}955\) and \(0{,}991\)). The end of the strip where the plateau does not hold is thus about \(t\) long. At \(h = 5t\) the two ends take \(2t\) out of \(5t\), at \(h = 20t\) only \(2t\) out of \(20t\), so the guess from the parabola is the better the larger \(h/t\) is.

The stress follows from the function \(\Phi\). By lemma 16.20 the stress is tangent to the level lines of \(\Phi\) and has the magnitude \(|\nabla\Phi|\). The level lines of the parabola (16.34) are parallel to the axis \(z\), so the stress is parallel to \(z\) with the magnitude \(|\Phi_{\mathrm{y}}| = 2G\alpha|y|\): zero in the middle and \(G\alpha t\) on the faces. The direction comes from equation (16.19): \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}} = -\Phi_{\mathrm{y}} = 2G\alpha y\), so the arrows point to \(+z\) at \(y > 0\) and to \(-z\) at \(y < 0\). By lemma 5.9 this stress along the faces gives the moment \(\int_{\mathrm{S}} y\,\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}}\,\mathrm{d}A = 2G\alpha\cdot 2b\int_{-\mathrm{a}}^{a}y^2\,\mathrm{d}y = G\alpha\,ht^3/6\), but this is only half. The other half is carried by the flow that turns at the ends of the strip, where \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \Phi_{\mathrm{z}} \ne 0\) and the parabola does not hold. The whole moment is given by theorem 16.16, \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\), which includes the ends too, hence

\[M_{\mathrm{x}} \approx 2\,G\alpha\cdot 2b\int_{-\mathrm{a}}^{a}(a^2 - y^2)\,\mathrm{d}y = 4\,G\alpha\,b\cdot\frac{4a^3}{3} = G\alpha\,\frac{h\,t^3}{3} . \tag{16.35}\]

Comparison with \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ\alpha\) gives \(J \approx ht^3/3\) and \(\tau \approx G\alpha t = M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\) on the faces. This is the guess from the picture. The theorem says how well it holds.

Theorem 16.33 Thin strip (tanek trak)

Let the section be the strip \(S = \{|y| \le a,\ |z| \le b\}\) with thickness \(t = 2a\), length \(h = 2b \ge t\), and let \(\alpha\) be the twist per unit length as in theorem 16.30. Then:

  1. The torsion constant is
    \[J = \frac{h\,t^3}{3} - \frac{64\,t^4}{\pi^5}\sum_{\mathrm{n} = 1, 3, 5, \dots}\frac{1}{n^5}\tanh\frac{n\pi h}{2t} , \tag{16.36}\]
    and \(\Big(1 - 0{,}631\,\dfrac{t}{h}\Big)\dfrac{h\,t^3}{3} \le J < \dfrac{h\,t^3}{3}\).
  2. The shear stress at the middle of the longer face \((\pm a, 0)\) is \(\tau(\pm a, 0) = f\,G\alpha\,t\) with
    \[1 - \frac{1}{\cosh\dfrac{\pi h}{2t}} \le f < 1 , \tag{16.37}\]
    and therefore \(\tau(\pm a, 0) < M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\).
Proof

We must show the two estimates for \(J\) and \(f\). Both follow from the series for the rectangle with two facts: \(\tanh\) lies between \(0\) and \(1\), and we can bound the sums \(\sum 1/n^5\) and \(\sum 1/n^2\) over odd \(n\).

  1. The series for \(J\) of theorem 16.30 has \(16a^3b/3 = ht^3/3\) and \(1024\,a^4 = 64\,t^4\) at \(a = t/2\), \(b = h/2\), which is equation (16.36).
  2. For \(x > 0\) we have \(\sinh x = (\mathrm e^{x} - \mathrm e^{-x})/2 > 0\), because the exponential function increases, and by lemma 9.42 (item 4 with \(a = 0\), \(b = 1\)) \(\cosh x - \sinh x = \mathrm e^{-x} > 0\). So \(0 < \sinh x < \cosh x\) and \(0 < \tanh x < 1\). Every term of the sum in (16.36) is therefore positive and smaller than \(1/n^5\), which gives \(J < ht^3/3\).
  3. Let \(S_5 = \sum_{\mathrm{n}\ \mathrm{odd}} 1/n^5\). The first three terms are \(1 + 1/243 + 1/3125 = 1{,}004435\). We estimate the remainder for \(n \ge 7\) with the decrease of the function \(x \mapsto x^{-5}\). On the interval \([n-2, n]\) we have \(x^{-5} \ge n^{-5}\), so by lemma 6.10 (items 2 and 3, for the nonnegative difference \(x^{-5} - n^{-5}\)) \(2\,n^{-5} \le \int_{\mathrm{n}-2}^{n}x^{-5}\,\mathrm{d}x\). The intervals \([5, 7], [7, 9], \dots\) do not overlap, so the sum over odd \(n \ge 7\) is at most \(\tfrac12\int_5^\infty x^{-5}\,\mathrm{d}x = 1/(8\cdot 5^4) = 0{,}0002\). So \(S_5 \le 1{,}004635\).
  4. Hence the sum in (16.36) is smaller than \(S_5\), and the correction in (16.36) is smaller than \(\dfrac{64\,t^4 S_5}{\pi^5} = \dfrac{192\,S_5}{\pi^5}\,\dfrac{t}{h}\cdot\dfrac{h\,t^3}{3}\). Numerically \(192\,S_5/\pi^5 \le 192\cdot 1{,}004635/306{,}0197 = 0{,}6303 < 0{,}631\), which gives the lower bound for \(J\).
  5. By corollary 16.31 the stress at the middle of the longer face is \(\tau(\pm a, 0) = 2a\,G\alpha\Big[1 - \dfrac{8}{\pi^2}\sum_{\mathrm{n}\ \mathrm{odd}}\dfrac{1}{n^2\cosh(n\pi b/2a)}\Big]\). With \(2a = t\) and \(b/a = h/t\) this is \(f\,G\alpha\,t\), where \(f = 1 - \dfrac{8}{\pi^2}\sum_{\mathrm{n}\ \mathrm{odd}}\dfrac{1}{n^2\cosh(n\pi h/(2t))}\).
  6. Every term of the sum is positive, so \(f < 1\). The function \(\cosh\) increases on \([0, \infty)\), because its derivative is \(\sinh \ge 0\) (lemma 9.42, items 1 and 3), so \(\cosh(n\pi h/(2t)) \ge \cosh(\pi h/(2t))\). With the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{n}\ \mathrm{odd}}1/n^2 = \pi^2/8\) (lemma 16.29, step 5) the sum in \(f\) is at most \(\dfrac{\pi^2}{8\cosh(\pi h/(2t))}\), so \(f \ge 1 - 1/\cosh(\pi h/(2t))\), which is the estimate (16.37).
  7. From \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ\alpha\) we have \(G\alpha t = M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\), so \(\tau(\pm a, 0) = f\,M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J < M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\).

Source: Series and upper bound: SADD section 9.5, equation (9.5.12) (PDF p. 264) and (9.5.15) (PDF p. 266), BARBER section 16.4, equation (16.41) (PDF p. 258). The lower bound \(1 - 0{,}631\,t/h\) and the stress estimate from the function \(1/\cosh\) are not from the sources, the computation is in num-06.py. That the stress at the middle of the longer face is the largest in the section is cited from SADD (9.5.13) (PDF p. 264-265) and visible from the membrane in section 16.4, it is not proved here.

How good is the bound? The table and figure 16.23 show that the ratio \(J/(ht^3/3)\) and the lower bound of theorem 16.33 nearly agree already at \(h/t = 5\).

We draw the axis \(h/t\) and on it the ratio \(J/(ht^3/3)\), which by theorem 16.33 is below \(1\). The curve is the ratio computed with the series. The bound \(1 - 0{,}631\,t/h\) lies below it, but only slightly, because at \(h/t \ge 5\) every \(\tanh(n\pi h/(2t))\) is larger than \(1 - 10^{-6}\) (the smallest is at \(n = 1\)) and the sum of equation (16.36) hardly differs from \(S_5\). The four points are examples: \(h/t = 1\), \(2\), \(5\), \(10\) with the ratios \(0{,}42\), \(0{,}69\), \(0{,}87\), \(0{,}94\). The constant \(J\) is certainly at least \(95\,\%\) of the thin value \(ht^3/3\) only for \(h/t \ge 12{,}7\) (the threshold): the bound \(1 - 0{,}631\,t/h = 0{,}95\) gives \(h/t = 12{,}62\), rounded up \(12{,}7\). The thin value is then at most \(1/0{,}95 = 1{,}053\) times \(J\), so at most \(5{,}3\,\%\) too large. For \(J\) to be at least \(99\,\%\) of the thin value we need \(h/t \ge 64\).

The thin value thus fails exactly for the sections we know. The square \(20 \times 20\) mm has by the series \(J = 22\,492\) mm\(^4\), while the formula \(ht^3/3\) gives \(53\,333\) mm\(^4\), \(2{,}4\) times too much. At \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 50\) N m this means a twist of \(0{,}0124\) rad/m instead of \(0{,}0295\) rad/m. The strip \(20 \times 40\) mm of chapter 12 (\(h/t = 2\)) has \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) against \(106\,667\) mm\(^4\), so \(0{,}69\): the twist would be \(0{,}00622\) rad/m instead of \(0{,}00906\) rad/m. The stress \(M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\) at \(50\) N m is \(13{,}67\) MPa with the true \(J\) and \(9{,}38\) MPa with the thin value, the exact stress of the theorem is \(f\,G\alpha t = 0{,}930\cdot 13{,}67 = 12{,}71\) MPa. The thin value for \(J\) thus underestimates the stress by \(26\,\%\), while the formula \(M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J\) with the true \(J\) overestimates it by \(7{,}5\,\%\) (\(1/f = 1{,}075\)). The conclusion: for a strip with \(h/t < 12{,}7\) we use the series, and for \(h/t \ge 12{,}7\) the thin calculation, for which \(J\) is at least \(95\,\%\) of the value \(ht^3/3\).

Now a profile made of several strips.

Take a channel of sheet metal, measured along the mean lines: the web is \(h_1 = 40\) mm long, the flanges \(h_2 = h_3 = 20\) mm, the thickness is \(t = 2\) mm everywhere. The mean line of the section is a curve without a loop and the section has no hole: such a section is called open (odprt prerez). If we cut it into three strips \(h_{\mathrm{i}} \times t\), each has \(h_{\mathrm{i}}/t = 20\) or \(10\). By theorem 16.33 the ratio \(J_{\mathrm{i}}/(h_{\mathrm{i}}t^3/3)\) is at least \(0{,}968\) at \(h_{\mathrm{i}}/t = 20\) and at least \(0{,}936\) at \(h_{\mathrm{i}}/t = 10\). Let us therefore try to treat each strip as if it were alone. A slit tube with \(r_{\mathrm{m}} = 18\) mm and \(t = 4\) mm is likewise one strip, only bent: we unroll it into a strip of length \(\ell = 2\pi r_{\mathrm{m}}\), because BARBER (PDF p. 256) takes a mean line of arbitrary shape as long as the thickness varies slowly along it. At \(t/r_{\mathrm{m}} = 0{,}22\) the curvature is not negligible, so the calculation is poorer here.

Corollary 16.34 Open section of thin strips (odprt prerez iz tankih trakov)

Let the section consist of strips \(S_{\mathrm{i}}\), \(i = 1, \dots, N\), with thicknesses \(t_{\mathrm{i}}\) and mean-line lengths \(h_{\mathrm{i}} \ge t_{\mathrm{i}}\), each with \(h_{\mathrm{i}}/t_{\mathrm{i}}\) large enough for theorem 16.33. Let (H) be: on the whole strip \(S_{\mathrm{i}}\) the Prandtl function equals the parabola of the isolated strip, \(G\alpha(t_{\mathrm{i}}^2/4 - n_{\mathrm{i}}^2)\), where \(n_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the distance from the mean line of strip \(i\). (H) is an approximation that neglects the bands at the junctions of the strips and at the free ends, where the parabola does not hold. Then, approximately:

  1. \(J \approx \displaystyle\sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{N}\frac{h_{\mathrm{i}}\,t_{\mathrm{i}}^3}{3}\).
  2. All strips have the same \(\alpha\), strip \(i\) carries the share \(M_{\mathrm{i}}/M_{\mathrm{x}} = h_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}}^3/\sum_{\mathrm{j}} h_{\mathrm{j}}t_{\mathrm{j}}^3\) of the moment, where \(M_{\mathrm{i}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}_\mathrm{i}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\) is its part of the integral of theorem 16.16, and has on its faces the stress \(\tau_{\mathrm{i}} \approx G\alpha\,t_{\mathrm{i}} = M_{\mathrm{x}} t_{\mathrm{i}}/J\). Away from the junctions and the free ends the stress is largest in the thickest strip. At a sharp re-entrant corner of a junction the stress is unbounded (source below).
  3. For a variable thickness \(t(s)\) along the mean line of length \(\ell\) we have \(J \approx \tfrac13\int_0^\ell t(s)^3\,\mathrm{d}s\) (without proof).
Proof

We must show that (H) and theorem 16.33 give items 1 and 2. Item 3 is quoted, not proved.

  1. By theorem 16.16 \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\). The integral over \(S\) is the sum of the integrals over the strips (additivity of the integral), and by (H) the integral over strip \(i\) is the integral of the parabola of the isolated strip, which by equation (16.35) equals \(G\alpha\,h_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}}^3/6\) (half of \(2\int_{\mathrm{S}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\)), so \(M_{\mathrm{x}} \approx G\alpha\sum_{\mathrm{i}} h_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}}^3/3\). Comparison with \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = GJ\alpha\) gives item 1.
  2. The section turns as a whole (definition 16.1), so \(\alpha\) is the same for all strips. The share of the strip \(M_{\mathrm{i}} = 2\int_{\mathrm{S}_\mathrm{i}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}A\) is by (H) and equation (16.35) equal to \(G\alpha\,h_{\mathrm{i}}t_{\mathrm{i}}^3/3\), which after division by \(M_{\mathrm{x}}\) gives the share. By equation (16.34) the stress on the faces is \(G\alpha t_{\mathrm{i}}\), which is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} t_{\mathrm{i}}/J\) because \(G\alpha = M_{\mathrm{x}}/J\), and it is largest at the largest \(t_{\mathrm{i}}\).

Source: BARBER section 16.3, equations (16.33) to (16.39) (PDF p. 256-257): item 3 is there too, and the remark that the stresses at re-entrant corners are unbounded if the corner is sharp (PDF p. 256). SADD section 9.5, equations (9.5.15) and (9.5.16) (PDF p. 266). SADD says there that the largest stress is estimated with the narrowest rectangle, which is an error: \(\tau_{\mathrm{i}} = 2\mu\alpha a_{\mathrm{i}}\) by (9.5.15) grows with the thickness, so it is largest in the thickest strip, as BARBER says (PDF p. 257). The proof from (H) is not from the sources. ONATE2 section 4.10.7 (PDF p. 241-242) also treats the case of restrained warping, which we do not treat here.

We shall check the hypothesis (H) on an example, because we do not prove it here: the junctions of the strips and the free ends cannot be estimated with theorem 16.33.

Example 16.35 Steel channel and slit tube (jeklen U-profil in razrezana cev)

For the channel of figure 16.24 with \(G = 75{,}385\) GPa and \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = 6{,}25\) N m:

  1. The sum over the three strips is \(J \approx (40 + 20 + 20)\cdot 2^3/3 = 213{,}3\) mm\(^4\) by item 1 of corollary 16.34, so \(GJ = 16{,}08\) N m\(^2\).
  2. \(\alpha = M_{\mathrm{x}}/(GJ) = 0{,}3886\) rad/m, which is \(22{,}3\,^\circ\)/m.
  3. The share of the web is \(40/80 = 50\,\%\), of each flange \(25\,\%\), so \(3{,}125\) N m and \(1{,}563\) N m each.
  4. The stress in all strips is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J = 6250\cdot 2/213{,}3 = 58{,}6\) MPa, because \(t\) is the same everywhere. This is below the shear yield limit of steel, \(110\) MPa by the Tresca criterion.

Let us check (H). We solved the Prandtl equation for the channel with its outer outline (\(42 \times 21\) mm: the web \(2 \times 42\) mm, the flanges of thickness \(2\) mm reach from the outer face of the web to \(20\) mm from the mean line of the web) on a mesh (finite differences): at the step \(0{,}125\), \(0{,}1\) and \(0{,}0625\) mm we get \(J = 211{,}3\), \(211{,}6\) and \(212{,}0\) mm\(^4\). The value still grows, but slowly, and for the step going to zero we estimate \(J \approx 212{,}3\) mm\(^4\). The thin sum \(213{,}3\) mm\(^4\) is thus \(0{,}5\,\%\) larger than the exact value. This is the error of (H), which neglects the ends and the junctions. The stress at the junctions of the flanges and the web, however, the thin calculation does not capture: a sharp corner has by BARBER (PDF p. 256) an unbounded stress, in a real profile a fillet limits it, but by how much we do not compute here. The slit tube has by item 3 of corollary 16.34 with \(t = 4\) mm the constant \(J \approx \ell t^3/3 = 2412{,}7\) mm\(^4\). At \(50\) N m we get \(\alpha = 0{,}2749\) rad/m and \(\tau = M_{\mathrm{x}} t/J = 82{,}9\) MPa. At \(t/r_{\mathrm{m}} = 0{,}22\) this is an estimate that we do not trust to three digits.

Open sections are thus very soft in torsion: \(J\) is proportional to \(t^3\). Whether the same strip in a different shape, without the slit, would resist the twist more, we shall check in the section on hollow sections. For the channel, however, another question arises: through which point must the shear force pass so that the section does not twist. The answer is in the next section.
