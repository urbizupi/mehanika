Chapter 26 Belts as axially moving continua

26.8 Finite elements for the moving string

In section 26.5 we turned the moving string (gibajoča se struna) into a system of ordinary equations with the Galerkin method, where the trial functions were the sines of chapter 13. A sine reaches across the whole string, so every entry of the matrices is an integral over the full length, and at the speed \(v/c = 0{,}9\) two sines gave the second frequency \(5{,}65\) instead of \(1{,}19\). Chapters 7 and 12 showed another way: small pieces that we assemble, where each piece contributes its own matrix, computed once. The question of this section is whether that way also works when the equation is no longer symmetric because of the mixed derivative term \(w_{\mathrm{xt}}\). The answer has two parts. The matrix of this term is assembled from elements like the mass and stiffness matrices, and its symmetric part cancels at the interior nodes (figure 26.25), so after the two eyelets hold the ends it is antisymmetric. Therefore the system of elements inherits everything that we proved for the system of sines: the eigenvalues are purely imaginary and the energy is conserved.

Take the steel band of section 26.1: \(\mu = 0{,}3510\) kg/m, tension \(T = 4500\) N, wave speed \(c = 113{,}23\) m/s, span \(L = 1\) m. The dimensions of the band, the tension and the speeds are our own choice, no source in the books gives belt data (section 26.1). Let the band run at \(v = c/2 = 56{,}61\) m/s. By theorem 26.13 the lowest natural frequency is \(f_1 = \frac{c}{2L}\left(1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}\right) = 42{,}46\) Hz. A model of \(n = 4\), \(8\), \(16\) and \(32\) equal elements gives (the numbers are in the table below) \(f_1 = 45{,}32\), \(43{,}16\), \(42{,}64\) and \(42{,}50\) Hz (figure 26.26). All of them are above the true value and approach it. What of this we can prove and what we cannot, the section shows step by step.

The path is as follows, its steps are in figures 26.24, 26.25 and 26.26. First we show that the true solution satisfies a weak equation in which only first derivatives appear (lemma 26.39), because a trial function made of hat functions has kinks at the nodes and no second derivative. Then we compute the three element matrices (lemma 26.40). We assemble them and show that the total gyroscopic matrix is antisymmetric (corollary 26.41). At the end we try an end that no eyelet holds, and show that the energy is then no longer conserved (lemma 26.42).

26.8.1 The weak form

The model is the same as in section 26.1 (the elements are in figure 26.25): a string of mass \(\mu\) per length and tension \(T\) that moves along a fixed track with constant speed \(v\) through two eyelets at \(x = 0\) and \(x = L\), which are fixed in space and hold the displacement at zero, \(w(0, t) = w(L, t) = 0\). The idealizations of section 26.1 apply, the track speed is below critical, \(v < c\) with \(c = \sqrt{T/\mu}\) (definition 13.4). We write \(w\) for the displacement, as in chapter 13, and the letter \(v\) is always the track speed in this chapter, never the displacement of chapter 7. The nodal displacements of an element are written \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) as there. By theorem 26.4 the equation of motion is

\[\mu\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + (\mu v^2 - T)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0 . \tag{26.51}\]

A trial function made of hat functions (definition 6.25, one is in figure 26.24) is piecewise linear, has a kink at the nodes, and there the second derivative \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) does not exist. We cannot insert it into equation (26.51). As in chapter 6 we multiply the equation by a test function and integrate by parts, so that one derivative passes to the test function.

Lemma 26.39 Weak form of the moving string (šibka oblika gibajoče se strune)

Let \(w\) be a twice continuously differentiable solution of equation (26.51) on \([0, L]\) with \(w(0, t) = w(L, t) = 0\), and let \(\psi\) be a piecewise continuously differentiable function on \([0, L]\) (a test function) with \(\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0\). Then for every time \(t\)

\[\mu\int_0^L \psi\,w_{\mathrm{tt}}\,\mathrm{d}x + 2\mu v\int_0^L \psi\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\,\mathrm{d}x + (T - \mu v^2)\int_0^L \psi'\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x = 0 . \tag{26.52}\]
Proof

We must show that equation (26.52) follows from equation (26.51) when we multiply it by \(\psi\) and integrate, and that the last term gets a first derivative instead of a second.

  1. Multiply equation (26.51) at a chosen time by \(\psi(x)\) and integrate over \([0, L]\). The integral is linear (lemma 6.10), so \(\mu\int\psi\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2\mu v\int\psi\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + (\mu v^2 - T)\int\psi\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\).
  2. The function \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) is continuously differentiable because \(w\) is twice continuously differentiable, and \(\psi\) is piecewise continuously differentiable. By lemma 6.13 with \(N = \psi\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) in place of \(w\) we have \(\int_0^L \psi\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,\mathrm{d}x = \psi(L)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(L, t) - \psi(0)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(0, t) - \int_0^L \psi'\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  3. Because \(\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0\), both boundary terms are zero and \(\int\psi\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -\int\psi'\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\). Insert into step 1: the term \((\mu v^2 - T)\int\psi\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) becomes \((T - \mu v^2)\int\psi'\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\), which is equation (26.52).

Source: WM1990 (1) (PDF p. 2) for the equation, step 2 is lemma 6.13. We derived the weak form ourselves, WM1990 does not treat elements (PDF p. 6 mentions only global discretizations).

The lemma states only one direction: the true solution satisfies equation (26.52). We do not need the converse. The Galerkin method (definition 6.42) demands that an approximation, not the true solution, satisfies equation (26.52), for every test function from the same family as the trial functions. The term with \(w_{\mathrm{tt}}\) is the mass term, the term with \(\psi' w_{\mathrm{x}}\) the stiffness term as in chapter 6, and the term with \(w_{\mathrm{xt}}\) is the one that chapter 7 did not have. Its coefficient \(2\mu v\) is from the lemma on the acceleration of a material particle (lemma 26.1), where the same term arose from the chain rule.

26.8.2 Element matrices

We make the mesh as in chapter 7: \(n\) equal elements of length \(h = L/n\), nodes \(x_{\mathrm{k}} = kh\) for \(k = 0, \dots, n\), on each element the shape functions \(N_1 = (x_{\mathrm{b}} - x)/h\) and \(N_2 = (x - x_{\mathrm{a}})/h\) (definition 7.2). The displacements of nodes \(0\) and \(n\) are zero because of the eyelets, the unknowns are \(d_1(t), \dots, d_{\mathrm{n}-1}(t)\), and the trial function is \(w_{\mathrm{h}}(x, t) = \sum_{\mathrm{j}=1}^{n-1} d_{\mathrm{j}}(t)\,\psi_{\mathrm{j}}(x)\), where \(\psi_{\mathrm{j}}\) is the hat function of node \(j\).

Each term of equation (26.52) gives one of three integrals over the element. We draw the element as in chapter 7 (figure 26.24). First we take the element of length \(h\) between nodes \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(x_{\mathrm{b}}\), in one step the horizontal axis and the two vertical lines that the nodes bound. On it we draw the function \(N_1\), which is \(1\) at the left node and \(0\) at the right, and the function \(N_2\), which is the other way round. Under each of them is a triangle with base \(h\) and height \(1\), its area is \(h/2\). We need the area because the integral of the product \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\,N_{\mathrm{j}}'\) is exactly the area under \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) times the value of the derivative \(N_{\mathrm{j}}'\), since the derivative is constant on the element: \(N_1' = -1/h\) and \(N_2' = 1/h\).

Lemma 26.40 Element matrices of the moving string (matrike elementa gibajoče se strune)

Let \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) be an element of length \(h\) with nodes \(1\) and \(2\), \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = (d_1, d_2)^\T\) the nodal displacements, \(\mu\), \(T\) and \(v\) constant on the element, and \(N_1\), \(N_2\) the shape functions. The integrals of the terms of equation (26.52) over the element are

\[\mat m_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{\mu h}{6}\begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mat g_{\mathrm{e}} = \mu v\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{T - \mu v^2}{h}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \tag{26.53}\]

where \((\mat m_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = \mu\int N_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{j}}\,\mathrm{d}x\), \((\mat g_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = 2\mu v\int N_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x\) and \((\mat k_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = (T - \mu v^2)\int N_{\mathrm{i}}' N_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x\). Moreover:

  1. The sum \(\mat g_{\mathrm{e}} + \mat g_{\mathrm{e}}^\T = \mu v\,\mathrm{diag}(-2, 2)\) is diagonal, its entry \((1, 1)\) is \(-2\mu v\) and its entry \((2, 2)\) is \(+2\mu v\). It does not depend on \(h\).
  2. For nodal displacements \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) we have \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat g_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \mu v\,(d_2^2 - d_1^2)\).
Proof

We must show three integrals and two consequences.

  1. By lemma 10.31 with \(\rho A = \mu\) the mass matrix is \(\frac{\mu h}{6}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
  2. The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the integral of the constant matrix \(\mat b_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat b_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac1{h^2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\) times a constant coefficient, which is here \(T - \mu v^2\) instead of \(EA\). The proof of lemma 7.5 (steps 2 and 3) uses only that the coefficient is constant on the element, so \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{T - \mu v^2}{h}\left(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
  3. The derivatives are \(N_1' = -1/h\) and \(N_2' = 1/h\) (lemma 1.81), constant on the element. Hence \(\int N_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x = N_{\mathrm{j}}'\int N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\mathrm{d}x\). The integral \(\int_0^h (1 - s/h)\,\mathrm{d}s = h - h/2 = h/2\) with \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}}\), and equally \(\int_0^h s/h\,\mathrm{d}s = h/2\).
  4. So \(\int N_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x = \frac h2 N_{\mathrm{j}}'\), which is \(-\tfrac12\) for \(j = 1\) and \(+\tfrac12\) for \(j = 2\), for both rows \(i\). Multiplication by \(2\mu v\) gives \(\mat g_{\mathrm{e}} = \mu v\left(\begin{smallmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
  5. Item 1: \(\mat g_{\mathrm{e}}^\T = \mu v\left(\begin{smallmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\), the sum is \(\mu v\left(\begin{smallmatrix} -2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
  6. Item 2: \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat g_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \mu v\,(-d_1^2 + d_1 d_2 - d_1 d_2 + d_2^2) = \mu v\,(d_2^2 - d_1^2)\).

Source: The mass and stiffness matrices as in lemmas 10.31 and 7.5 (COOK (11.3-4) PDF p. 392, COOK (2.2-1) PDF p. 34). The gyroscopic matrix of the element is our own computation (SymPy, outlines/ch-jermeni/num-08.out A), the sources do not give it.

The proof showed why the matrix \(\mat g_{\mathrm{e}}\) differs from \(\mat m_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\): it contains \(N_{\mathrm{j}}'\) and not \(N_{\mathrm{j}}\) or \(N_{\mathrm{i}}'\), so it is not symmetric. Its two rows are equal because the two shape functions have equal areas \(h/2\) under them. Item 1 is what we need: the symmetric part, which is the one that disturbs us, since in the equation \(\mat m\ddot{\vek d} + \mat g\dot{\vek d} + \mat k\vek d = \vek 0\) it would do work or take energy away, is only on the diagonal, namely \(-\mu v\) at the left and \(+\mu v\) at the right node of the element. Item 2 says the same in words: the term \(\vek d^\T\mat g\,\vek d = \mu v\,\big(w(x_{\mathrm{b}})^2 - w(x_{\mathrm{a}})^2\big)\) depends only on the displacements at the edges of the element, nothing comes from the interior. So it equals the integral of a derivative, \(2\mu v\int w\,w_{\mathrm{x}} = \mu v\int (w^2)_{\mathrm{x}}\), which the fundamental theorem computes as a difference of boundary values.

26.8.3 Assembly and antisymmetry

We assemble the elements as theorem 7.13 taught us. Let us see what happens to the diagonal of the gyroscopic matrix (figure 26.25). We draw \(n = 4\) elements with nodes \(0\) to \(4\) along the track. At every node one element reaches in from the right and one from the left. The element on the right of a node has it as its left node and contributes \(-\mu v\) to the diagonal, next to the node stands \(-1\) in units of \(\mu v\). The element on the left has it as its right node and contributes \(+\mu v\). The sum at interior nodes is \(0\), and at the end nodes \(-1\) stays at node \(0\) and \(+1\) at node \(4\). Nodes \(0\) and \(n\) are held by the eyelets, the displacement there is zero and their rows and columns drop out, so the symmetric part drops out of the system of unknowns \(d_1, \dots, d_{\mathrm{n}-1}\) too.

Corollary 26.41 The assembled gyroscopic matrix is antisymmetric (skupna žiroskopska matrika je antisimetrična)

Let the string of section 26.8.1 be divided into \(n\) equal elements with nodes \(0, \dots, n\) and let \(v < c\). Let \(\mat M\), \(\mat G\), \(\mat K\) be matrices of order \(n - 1\) with entries

\[M_{\mathrm{ij}} = \mu\int_0^L \psi_{\mathrm{i}}\psi_{\mathrm{j}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad G_{\mathrm{ij}} = 2\mu v\int_0^L \psi_{\mathrm{i}}\psi_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad K_{\mathrm{ij}} = (T - \mu v^2)\int_0^L \psi_{\mathrm{i}}'\psi_{\mathrm{j}}'\,\mathrm{d}x \tag{26.54}\]

for \(i, j = 1, \dots, n-1\), and let the Galerkin system be \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek d} + \mat G\,\dot{\vek d} + \mat K\,\vek d = \vek 0\) (equation (26.52) with \(\psi = \psi_{\mathrm{i}}\)). Then:

  1. The matrices \(\mat M\), \(\mat G\), \(\mat K\) are the sums over the elements of the element matrices of lemma 26.40, placed as in theorem 7.13.
  2. The matrix \(\tilde{\mat G}\) with all nodes \(0, \dots, n\) has \(\tilde{\mat G} + \tilde{\mat G}^\T = \mu v\,\mathrm{diag}(-2, 0, \dots, 0, 2)\), which is nonzero only in the first and last entry. The matrix \(\mat G\) is antisymmetric, \(G_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{i}+1} = \mu v\), \(G_{\mathrm{i}+1,\mathrm{i}} = -\mu v\) and all other entries are zero.
  3. The matrices \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) are symmetric and positive definite.
  4. All eigenvalues of the system are purely imaginary, \(\lambda = \mathrm{i}\omega\) with real \(\omega\), and \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{h}} = \tfrac12\dot{\vek d}^\T\mat M\dot{\vek d} + \tfrac12\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d\) is constant.
Proof

We must show that the assembly happens as for the bar, where the diagonal cancels, and then that the properties of section 26.5 apply.

  1. By lemma 6.10 the integrals in equation (26.54) are sums of integrals over the elements. On an element that is not in the row of the connectivity table of node \(i\), \(\psi_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\) (theorem 7.13, step 4), on the others \(\psi_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the shape function of the element (step 5 of the same theorem). The piece of element \(e\) is therefore the entry of the element matrix of lemma 26.40 at the place that the connectivity table gives, which is item 1.
  2. Element \(e\) lies between nodes \(e - 1\) and \(e\). The diagonal entry \(\tilde G_{\mathrm{kk}}\) receives \(-\mu v\) from element \(k + 1\) (node \(k\) is its first) and \(+\mu v\) from element \(k\) (node \(k\) is its second), for \(k = 1, \dots, n-1\), and the sum is \(0\). Node \(0\) has only element \(1\) and gets \(-\mu v\), node \(n\) has only element \(n\) and gets \(+\mu v\). Off the diagonal, element \(e\) contributes its entry \((1, 2)\) at place \((e-1, e)\) with value \(\mu v\) and its entry \((2, 1)\) at place \((e, e-1)\) with value \(-\mu v\), no place gets two contributions, so \(\tilde{\mat G} + \tilde{\mat G}^\T\) is zero off the diagonal. Deleting rows and columns \(0\) and \(n\) removes the only nonzero entries, and \(\mat G\) is antisymmetric with the stated entries.
  3. By item 2 of theorem 7.13, \(\vek d^\T\mat M\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat m_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), every term is by lemma 10.31 nonnegative and positive for \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} \ne \vek 0\). The sum is zero only if every \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\), so \(\vek d = \vek 0\). The same theorem gives \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \frac{T - \mu v^2}{h}\sum_{\mathrm{e}=1}^n (d_{\mathrm{e}} - d_{\mathrm{e}-1})^2\) with \(d_0 = d_{\mathrm{n}} = 0\) (equation (7.14)). For \(v < c\) we have \(T - \mu v^2 = \mu(c^2 - v^2) > 0\), so the sum is zero only if all differences are zero, and so all \(d_{\mathrm{k}}\) equal \(d_0 = 0\).
  4. By items 2 and 3 we have real matrices \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) symmetric and positive definite and \(\mat G\) antisymmetric, which are the assumptions of theorem 26.27, giving purely imaginary eigenvalues, and of lemma 26.26, giving the conservation of \(\mathcal E_{\mathrm{h}}\).

Source: Assembly by theorem 7.13, the structure of the system (symmetric \(\mat M\), \(\mat K\), antisymmetric \(\mat G\)) as in GR §2.11 (PDF pp. 137 and 139). Items 1 to 3 are our own proof, the sources do not treat discrete models of moving strings.

The proof showed where the antisymmetry comes from: at every interior node the two neighbours cancel the symmetric part, since the node sees the same entry \(\mu v\) once with a plus and once with a minus sign. The same can be read from the definition: \(G_{\mathrm{ij}} + G_{\mathrm{ji}} = 2\mu v\int (\psi_{\mathrm{i}}\psi_{\mathrm{j}})'\,\mathrm{d}x = 2\mu v\,\big[\psi_{\mathrm{i}}\psi_{\mathrm{j}}\big]_0^L\), which is zero because all test functions vanish at the ends. For hat functions this means the same as item 2. This computation shows that for the antisymmetry only the eyelets holding the ends matter, not the shape of the elements, so the same holds for other meshes.

We try the corollary on numbers. At \(v/c = 0{,}5\) and \(n = 8\) the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat M\) (\(L = c = \mu = 1\)) is \(0{,}0448\) and of the matrix \(\mat K\) it is \(0{,}913\), at \(v/c = 0{,}9\) the matrix \(\mat K\) is still positive definite with smallest value \(0{,}231\) and at \(v/c = 0{,}99\) with \(0{,}0242\). The coefficient \(T - \mu v^2\) falls towards zero as \(v\) approaches \(c\), and \(\mat K\) becomes almost singular. At \(v = c\) we have \(\mat K = \mat 0\), which is the critical speed of section 26.4. The largest absolute real part of the eigenvalues is below \(3 \cdot 10^{-15}\) at all three speeds, so zero up to rounding.

26.8.4 Numbers and convergence

Figure 26.26 shows the error of the frequencies of the element model against the number of elements \(n\). We draw the axes: \(n\) on the horizontal axis with equal spacing per doubling and the frequency error \(\Delta\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{h}} - \omega\) on the vertical axis with decades. For \(v/c = 0{,}5\) the error of the first frequency is \(0{,}1586\), \(0{,}0390\), \(0{,}0097\), \(0{,}0024\) and \(0{,}0006\) at \(n = 4, 8, 16, 32, 64\). The error of the second frequency is \(1{,}288\), \(0{,}317\), \(0{,}078\), \(0{,}019\) and \(0{,}005\). Each time we double the elements the error is divided by \(4{,}06\), \(4{,}02\), \(4{,}00\) and \(4{,}00\) (first frequency), so it goes as \(h^2\), which is the dashed line of slope \(-2\). At \(v/c = 0{,}9\) the error of the second frequency is \(1{,}826\) at \(n = 4\) and still \(0{,}242\) at \(n = 8\). At the doublings from \(n = 4\) to \(n = 64\) it is divided by \(7{,}54\), \(4{,}63\), \(4{,}14\) and \(4{,}03\). The order \(h^2\) is the same, but at the higher speed the factor \(4\) settles only with more elements.

The errors are positive in all computed cases: the frequencies of the model are above the true ones. We observed this and did not prove it. In chapter 10 theorem 10.42 gives an upper bound when the system \(\mat M\ddot{\vek d} + \mat K\vek d = \vek 0\) has no gyroscopic term, but here \(\mat G \ne \mat 0\) and its proof does not apply. We also only measured the order \(h^2\). A proof would need an error estimate for a system with an antisymmetric term, which we did not find in the books that we use.

The table connects the numbers with the band from the start of the section (\(v = c/2 = 56{,}61\) m/s, \(L = 1\) m). The angular frequency \(\omega\) computed with \(L = c = \mu = 1\) is in units of \(c/L\), so the frequency of the band is \(f = \omega\,c/(2\pi L)\) with \(c/(2\pi L) = 18{,}02\) Hz:

We also compare with section 26.5 at \(v/c = 0{,}9\) with the same number of unknowns. Two sines give \(\omega_1 = 0{,}663\) and \(\omega_2 = 5{,}655\), three elements (two unknowns) \(\omega_1 = 0{,}851\) and \(\omega_2 = 5{,}393\), the true values are \(0{,}597\) and \(1{,}194\). With two unknowns both families are poor and the elements are no better. For the sines of the stationary string WM1990 (PDF p. 6) says the same: at high speed they describe the motion poorly. The advantage of the elements is that the error falls with the number of elements as figure 26.26 shows.

26.8.5 An end that nothing holds

The antisymmetry of the matrix \(\mat G\) came from the eyelets. We try what happens without the right eyelet. The lower half of figure 26.25 has the same elements, contributions from the right and from the left and sum, only that node \(n\) is not held and its \(+1\) stays. We try it as a model of the mechanism, not as a description of a real end of a belt. A real free end, where the track leaves the span, is not treated in this chapter and the sources do not derive it. Node \(n\) is now an unknown and the test function \(\psi_{\mathrm{n}}\), which equals \(1\) at \(x = L\), is allowed. In the derivation of the weak form in lemma 26.39 the boundary term \((\mu v^2 - T)\,\psi(L)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(L, t)\) remains. If we set it to zero for every \(\psi(L)\) we demand the boundary condition \((T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(L, t) = 0\). At \(x = 0\) the eyelet is still there, \(w(0, t) = 0\).

Lemma 26.42 Energy at an unheld end (energija pri koncu, ki ni držan)

Let the model of section 26.8.1 have an eyelet at \(x = 0\) and the boundary condition \((T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(L, t) = 0\) at \(x = L\), let \(v < c\) and let \(w\) be three times continuously differentiable. Let \(\mathcal E^*(t) = \frac12\int_0^L\big(\mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2 + (T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\) be the conserved quantity of section 26.4. Then:

  1. \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal E^*}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{t}}(L, t)^2 \le 0\).
  2. In the Galerkin system with elements, where node \(n\) is an unknown, the symmetric part of the matrix \(\mat G\) is \(\mat G + \mat G^\T = \mu v\,\mathrm{diag}(0, \dots, 0, 2)\), \(\dot{\vek d}^\T\mat G\,\dot{\vek d} = \mu v\,\dot d_{\mathrm{n}}^2\) and \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal E_{\mathrm{h}}}{\mathrm{d}t} = -\mu v\,\dot d_{\mathrm{n}}^2 \le 0\).
Proof

We must show that the energy is not conserved at an end that nothing holds, and why.

  1. Differentiate in time under the integral (\(w\) is smooth enough): \(\dot{\mathcal E}^* = \int_0^L\big(\mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}}\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + (T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\big)\mathrm{d}x\).
  2. From equation (26.51), \(\mu\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} = -2\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + (T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\). Insert: the integrand is \(-2\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{xt}} + (T - \mu v^2)\big(w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{xt}}\big)\).
  3. By the product rule, \(2 w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{xt}} = (w_{\mathrm{t}}^2)_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{xx}} + w_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{xt}} = (w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{x}})_{\mathrm{x}}\). The fundamental theorem gives \(\dot{\mathcal E}^* = -\mu v\,\big[w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\big]_0^L + (T - \mu v^2)\big[w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{x}}\big]_0^L\).
  4. At \(x = 0\) we have \(w = 0\) at all times, so \(w_{\mathrm{t}}(0, t) = 0\). At \(x = L\) we have \((T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{x}}(L, t) = 0\). What remains is \(-\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{t}}(L, t)^2\), which is item 1. With an eyelet at \(L\) we would also have \(w_{\mathrm{t}}(L, t) = 0\) and the quantity would be conserved.
  5. Item 2: the matrix of element \(n\) from lemma 26.40 has its entry \((2, 2)\) equal to \(+\mu v\) at place \((n, n)\), and node \(n\) has no neighbour on the right to cancel it (figure 26.25 below). The other entries cancel as in corollary 26.41. So \(\mat G + \mat G^\T = \mu v\,\mathrm{diag}(0, \dots, 0, 2)\) and \(\dot{\vek d}^\T\mat G\,\dot{\vek d} = \tfrac12\dot{\vek d}^\T(\mat G + \mat G^\T)\dot{\vek d} = \mu v\,\dot d_{\mathrm{n}}^2\).
  6. Multiply the system \(\mat M\ddot{\vek d} + \mat G\dot{\vek d} + \mat K\vek d = \vek 0\) from the left by \(\dot{\vek d}^\T\): \(\dot{\vek d}^\T\mat M\ddot{\vek d} + \dot{\vek d}^\T\mat K\vek d = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal E_{\mathrm{h}} = -\dot{\vek d}^\T\mat G\dot{\vek d} = -\mu v\,\dot d_{\mathrm{n}}^2\), because \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) are symmetric.

Source: The derivation is our own. That the energy of the span is not conserved because particles carry energy through the eyelets is in CHEN2005 §2.5 (PDF p. 6) and in equation (32) (PDF p. 7), see lemma 26.23.

The lemma says what would happen: the energy would not be conserved but would fall at the rate \(\mu v\,\dot d_{\mathrm{n}}^2\). The term \(-\mu v\,w_{\mathrm{t}}(L, t)^2\) is cancelled only by an eyelet that holds \(w_{\mathrm{t}}(L, t) = 0\). We also compute numbers (\(L = c = \mu = 1\), \(v/c = 0{,}5\)). At \(n = 4, 8, 16\) the matrix \(\mat G + \mat G^\T\) has a single nonzero entry, of value \(1\), which is \(2\mu v\). The largest real part of the eigenvalues is \(-0{,}43\), \(-0{,}42\) and \(-0{,}21\), so all eigenvalues are left of the imaginary axis, which is something quite different from less than \(3 \cdot 10^{-15}\) at the held end. These numbers change with the mesh, so we do not conclude a final decay rate from them. Only that the energy does not grow is proved (item 2 of lemma 26.42). That all eigenvalues lie left of the imaginary axis we merely observed.

26.8.6 What we showed and what we did not

We proved three things. First: the weak form of the moving string has three integrals and the element matrices are these three, among them the gyroscopic matrix \(\mu v\left(\begin{smallmatrix} -1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right)\), which does not depend on the length of the element (lemma 26.40). Second: after assembly its symmetric part remains only at the end nodes, the eyelets remove them, so the system of elements is of the same kind as the system of sines and has purely imaginary eigenvalues and a constant energy (corollary 26.41). Third: when an end is not held this fails, because energy flows out through the end (lemma 26.42).

We observed, but did not prove, that the frequencies of the model converge from above with an error of order \(h^2\). We do not know whether this holds for every \(v < c\) and every mesh, and from the sources that we have we cannot check it. We did not treat unequal meshes, higher elements, or the moving beam with bending stiffness. Those need the Hermite elements of chapter 12 and we do not derive them here. The link between eyelets fixed in space and the pulleys that a belt wraps comes in the section on the belt drive.
