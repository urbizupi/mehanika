Chapter 6 Weak form, the Ritz and Galerkin method (šibka oblika, Ritzova in Galerkinova metoda)

6.5 How good is the approximation

Chapter 4 tried a line and a parabola on the hanging bar and noted two findings for the line. The error of the best line has the smallest strain energy, 97,76 µJ, and the displacement of the lower end is exactly right (section 4.6.8). The Ritz method (definition 6.32) now allows as many parameters as we like. We ask three questions. Does the Ritz method always choose the field whose error has the smallest energy? When is the displacement of a chosen point exact, and not only approximate? How fast does the error fall as we improve the trial family? Each question is followed by a theorem, which we test on the hanging bar and on the bar with a step.

The notation is that of section 6.4. For a bar the bilinear form \(B(u, w)\) (definition 6.4) is \(\int_0^L EA\,u'\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x\), the linear form \(\ell(w)\) is the work of the given loads on the field \(w\), \(u\) is the solution of the weak form (definition 6.6), \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) is the trial space (poskusni prostor), that is all sums \(c_1\psi_1 + \dots + c_{\mathrm{n}}\psi_{\mathrm{n}}\) of trial functions that satisfy the homogeneous essential conditions, \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) is the Ritz solution in \(\psi_0 + \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(e_{\mathrm{n}} = u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\) is its error. The energy of a field \(w\) is \(U(w) = \tfrac12 B(w, w)\) and its energy norm is \(\|w\|_{\mathrm{E}} = \sqrt{B(w, w)}\) (definition 6.18). The energy of the error \(U(e_{\mathrm{n}})\) is therefore \(\tfrac12\|e_{\mathrm{n}}\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\).

6.5.1 The error is orthogonal to the trial fields

For the best line the error \(a\,(Lx - x^2)\) was orthogonal to the trial field \(x\) in the energy inner product, \(B(e_1, x) = \int_0^L EA\,a\,(L - 2x)\,\mathrm{d}x = 0\) (section 6.3.2). Does this hold only for a line? The idea of the proof is simple. The Ritz solution is the lowest point of the energy on the trial family. If we move it in the direction of one of the trial functions, the energy must not decrease, and to first order it changes by \(B(u_{\mathrm{n}}, \psi_{\mathrm{i}}) - \ell(\psi_{\mathrm{i}})\). This is exactly the \(i\)-th Ritz equation. From it and from the weak form for the true field it follows that the error is orthogonal to all trial functions.

Lemma 6.36 Galerkin orthogonality (Galerkinova ortogonalnost)

Let \(B\) and \(\ell\) be the bilinear and the linear form of a bar, a beam or a body. Let \(u\) be the solution of the weak form, \(B(u, w) = \ell(w)\) for every test function \(w\), and let \(u_{\mathrm{n}} = \psi_0 + c_1\psi_1 + \dots + c_{\mathrm{n}}\psi_{\mathrm{n}}\) be the Ritz solution with the trial functions of definition 6.32. Then

\[B(u - u_{\mathrm{n}}, w) = 0 \quad \text{for every } w \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}} . \tag{6.20}\]
Proof

We must show that at every trial field the error does the same work as the true field, so that the difference is zero.

  1. Every \(w \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) is a linear combination of the test functions \(\psi_{\mathrm{i}}\) and is therefore a test function, because these form a vector space \(W\) (definition 6.6). The weak form says \(B(u, w) = \ell(w)\).
  2. The Ritz equations are \(K c = f\) with \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} = B(\psi_{\mathrm{j}}, \psi_{\mathrm{i}})\) and \(f_{\mathrm{i}} = \ell(\psi_{\mathrm{i}}) - B(\psi_0, \psi_{\mathrm{i}})\) (definition 6.32). Written row by row they say \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}} B(\psi_{\mathrm{j}}, \psi_{\mathrm{i}})\,c_{\mathrm{j}} = \ell(\psi_{\mathrm{i}}) - B(\psi_0, \psi_{\mathrm{i}})\). By linearity of \(B\) in the first argument, \(B(u_{\mathrm{n}}, \psi_{\mathrm{i}}) = B(\psi_0, \psi_{\mathrm{i}}) + \sum_{\mathrm{j}} c_{\mathrm{j}} B(\psi_{\mathrm{j}}, \psi_{\mathrm{i}}) = \ell(\psi_{\mathrm{i}})\) for \(i = 1, \dots, n\).
  3. Let \(w = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} d_{\mathrm{i}} \psi_{\mathrm{i}}\). We multiply the equation of step 2 by \(d_{\mathrm{i}}\) and sum over \(i\). By linearity of \(B\) in the second argument and of \(\ell\), \(B(u_{\mathrm{n}}, w) = \ell(w)\).
  4. We subtract the equation of step 3 from the equation of step 1. By linearity of \(B\) in the first argument, \(B(u, w) - B(u_{\mathrm{n}}, w) = B(u - u_{\mathrm{n}}, w) = \ell(w) - \ell(w) = 0\).

Source: BATHE (4.86) to (4.88), property 1 (PDF p. 258), where the orthogonality (4.86) in \(a(\cdot, \cdot)\) is proved by subtracting the equations of the principle of virtual work. The proof in steps is ours.

The proof showed that the orthogonality of the error says the same as the Ritz equations, only written differently. The name comes from the method of the next section, which has the same equations and therefore the same orthogonality also where there is no energy.

We check it on the hanging bar with the cubic trial field \(\psi = 3L^2 x - x^3\) from section 6.4.2. The Ritz solution is \(u_1 = c\,\psi\) with \(c = \tfrac{25}{96}\,q/(EA\,L)\), where \(q/(EA) = 2a\) and \(a = \rho g/(2E)\) (chapter 4). Hence \(c = \tfrac{25}{48}\,a/L\). The derivatives are \(u' = 2a\,(L - x)\) and \(u_1' = c\,\psi' = \tfrac{25}{16}\,(a/L)\,(L^2 - x^2)\), and the error has the derivative

\[e_1' = a\,(L - x)\Big(2 - \frac{25\,(L + x)}{16\,L}\Big) = \frac{a\,(L - x)(7L - 25x)}{16\,L} .\]

Since \(\psi' = 3\,(L - x)(L + x)\),

\[B(e_1, \psi) = \frac{3\,EA\,a}{16\,L} \int_0^L (L - x)^2 (L + x)(7L - 25x)\,\mathrm{d}x .\]

For \(L = 1\) the product under the integral is \((1 - x)^2(1 + x)(7 - 25x) = 7 - 32x + 18x^2 + 32x^3 - 25x^4\) and the integral is \(7 - 16 + 6 + 8 - 5 = 0\). The orthogonality holds here too (checked with SymPy). The Pythagorean theorem for energies (lemma 6.21) applied to \(e_1 \perp u_1\) gives \(U(u) = U(u_1) + U(e_1)\), that is \(381{,}87 + 9{,}165 = 391{,}03\) µJ. The same as for the line: \(293{,}27 + 97{,}76 = 391{,}03\) µJ.

By theorem 6.34 the energy of the error \(U(e_1) = 9{,}165\) µJ is also the difference of the energies \(\Pi(u_1) - \Pi(u)\) (section 6.4.4). What remains is the question whether this is the smallest error in the family.

Theorem 6.37 Best approximation in the energy norm (najboljši približek v energijski normi)

Let \(B\) be a symmetric bilinear form that is positive on the test functions, that is \(B(w, w) > 0\) for every test function \(w \ne 0\) (lemma 6.7). Let \(u\) and \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) be as in lemma 6.36. Then for every \(v \in \psi_0 + \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\)

\[\|u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\|_{\mathrm{E}} \le \|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}} , \tag{6.21}\]

with equality only for \(v = u_{\mathrm{n}}\). More precisely, \(\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = \|u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 + \|u_{\mathrm{n}} - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\).

Proof

We must show that the error of an arbitrary field of the family splits into the error of the Ritz solution and a part inside the family, and that the two are orthogonal.

  1. Let \(e = u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(d = u_{\mathrm{n}} - v\). The fields \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(v\) have the same \(\psi_0\), which cancels in the difference, so \(d\) is a sum of functions \(\psi_{\mathrm{i}}\), that is \(d \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\).
  2. We have \(u - v = e + d\). By bilinearity and symmetry of \(B\), \(\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = B(e + d, e + d) = B(e, e) + 2B(e, d) + B(d, d)\).
  3. By lemma 6.36, \(B(e, d) = 0\), because \(d \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\). Hence \(\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = \|e\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 + \|d\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\).
  4. Since \(\|d\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = B(d, d) \ge 0\), we have \(\|e\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 \le \|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\), and the square root of both sides gives equation (6.21).
  5. If \(\|e\|_{\mathrm{E}} = \|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}\), then by step 3 \(\|d\|_{\mathrm{E}} = 0\), so \(d = 0\) by the positivity of the form \(B\) and \(v = u_{\mathrm{n}}\).

Source: BATHE (4.91)-(4.94), property 3 (PDF p. 258-259): "the strain energy corresponding to \(u - u_{\mathrm{h}}\) is the minimum". The proof in steps is ours.

The proof showed that among all fields of the family the Ritz method chooses the one whose error has the smallest energy, and that every other field is worse by exactly the energy of its difference from the Ritz field (figure 6.19). For the line we only observed this (section 4.6.8).

We test it on the cubic family \(v = c\,\psi\) with \(c\) in units of \(q/(EA\,L)\). By the theorem \(U(u - v) = U(e_1) + U(u_1 - v)\) must hold, as the table shows.

\(c\) \(U(u - v)\) (µJ) \(U(u_1 - v)\) (µJ) end (µm)
\(25/96\) (Ritz) 9,165 0 20,33
\(1/4\) (least squares, next section) 9,776 0,611 19,52
\(1/3\) (collocation, next section) 39,10 29,94 26,03
\(1/2\) 332,4 323,2 39,04

In every row \(9{,}165 + U(u_1 - v) = U(u - v)\) (SymPy), for example \(9{,}165 + 0{,}611 = 9{,}776\) µJ. The energy of the difference is quadratic in \(c\), \(U(u_1 - v) = 5631\,(c - 25/96)^2\) µJ, a parabola with its lowest point at the Ritz value. The end displacement is not so orderly: least squares give the exact displacement 19,52 µm and a larger energy of the error than Ritz. The best in energy therefore does not mean the best at every point. In the next section we shall see when the displacement at a point is nevertheless exact.

6.5.2 When the displacement at a point is exact

Chapter 4 found for the best line that the displacement of the lower end is exact, and wrote that this is no coincidence. The reason given was that the line is the average of the true strain. Now we can pose the general question: for which points and which families is the Ritz displacement exact? Theorem 6.37 measures the error by the energy over the whole bar and says nothing about a single point. The answer comes from the work of a force. The displacement of a point \(P\) in the direction of a force is the work of a unit force at \(P\) on that displacement. The same work can be expressed by the strain energy of the field that the unit force causes.

Take a bar with axial stiffness \(EA\), positive and piecewise continuous (as for the bar with a step), fixed at \(x = 0\). Let \(P\) be the point \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) and \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) the displacement of this bar when only a unit force \(1\) N acts on it at \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) in the direction of the \(x\) axis. The axial force between the fixing and the point \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) is \(1\) N (tension), since the fixing must hold this force, and beyond \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) it is zero, because there are no loads. Therefore

\[\begin{aligned} G_{\mathrm{P}}(x) &= \int_0^{\min(x,\, x_{\mathrm{P}})} \frac{1}{EA(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t , \\ G_{\mathrm{P}}' &= \frac{1}{EA} \ \text{ for } x < x_{\mathrm{P}} , \qquad G_{\mathrm{P}}' = 0 \ \text{ for } x > x_{\mathrm{P}} . \end{aligned} \tag{6.22}\]

The field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) increases up to the point \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) and is constant beyond it (figure 6.20). It is continuous and piecewise continuously differentiable (definition 6.11), it has a kink at \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\), and \(G_{\mathrm{P}}(0) = 0\).

Theorem 6.38 Exactness of the displacement at a point (točnost pomika v točki)

Let the bar be fixed at \(x = 0\), with axial stiffness \(EA > 0\), piecewise continuous, and with arbitrary given loads. Let \(u\) be the solution of the weak form and \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) the Ritz solution in \(\psi_0 + \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) with the essential condition \(u(0) = 0\) (or \(u(0)\) given, the same for both). Let \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) be the field of the unit force at the point \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) from equation (6.22). If \(G_{\mathrm{P}} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\), then

\[u_{\mathrm{n}}(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = u(x_{\mathrm{P}}) .\]
Proof

We must show that the error at the point \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) is the work of a unit force on the error, and that this work is zero by the orthogonality of the error to the trial fields.

  1. For every admissible \(w\) with \(w(0) = 0\), \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}, w) = \int_0^L EA\,G_{\mathrm{P}}'\,w'\,\mathrm{d}x\). By equation (6.22), \(EA\,G_{\mathrm{P}}' = 1\) on \((0, x_{\mathrm{P}})\) and \(0\) on \((x_{\mathrm{P}}, L)\), so \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}, w) = \int_0^{x_{\mathrm{P}}} w'\,\mathrm{d}x = w(x_{\mathrm{P}}) - w(0) = w(x_{\mathrm{P}})\) by lemma 6.12.
  2. The error \(e = u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\) is a test function \(w\), since \(u\) and \(u_{\mathrm{n}}\) are piecewise continuously differentiable and have the same value at \(x = 0\), so \(e(0) = 0\). Step 1 with \(w = e\) gives \(e(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = B(G_{\mathrm{P}}, e)\).
  3. The form \(B\) is symmetric, \(B(G_{\mathrm{P}}, e) = B(e, G_{\mathrm{P}})\), because the integrand \(EA\,G_{\mathrm{P}}'\,e'\) is symmetric in the two fields (definition 6.4).
  4. Since \(G_{\mathrm{P}} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\), by lemma 6.36 \(B(e, G_{\mathrm{P}}) = 0\).
  5. From steps 2 to 4, \(e(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = 0\), so \(u_{\mathrm{n}}(x_{\mathrm{P}}) = u(x_{\mathrm{P}})\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. The theorem explains observations that sources state without a reason: exact nodal values for two-node bars (ONATE1, PDF p. 363, "the displacement solution for 2-noded bar elements is exact at the nodes").

The proof showed that the error at a point is the work of a unit force on the error (the principle of virtual displacements for the field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) with the virtual displacement \(e\)) and that the orthogonality cancels this work when \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) is in the family. The exchange of the roles of the two fields in step 3 is for a body stated by Betti's theorem (theorem 4.137), and for a bar the symmetry of the form \(B\) gives it here already. We test the theorem on four examples.

The line on the hanging bar. The family \(\{c\,x\}\) contains \(G_{\mathrm{L}} = x/EA\), the field of the unit force at the lower end (\(x_{\mathrm{P}} = L\), \(\min(x, L) = x\) and \(EA\) is constant). Hence \(u_1(L) = u(L) = 19{,}52\) µm. This explains the phenomenon from chapter 4. At the middle of the bar (\(x_{\mathrm{P}} = L/2\)), however, the field \(G_{\mathrm{L}/2} = \min(x, L/2)/EA\) is not a line, since it has a kink, and indeed the line gives 9,760 µm instead of 14,64 µm.

The cubic trial field. For \(\psi = 3L^2 x - x^3\) the field \(G_{\mathrm{L}} = x/EA\) is not in the family \(\{c\,\psi\}\), and the theorem is silent. The end displacement is 20,33 µm instead of 19,52 µm. Step 2 of the proof still holds. The error of the end displacement is \(e(L) = -0{,}813\) µm and \(B(G_{\mathrm{L}}, e_1)\) is exactly this much (SymPy). This value is not zero because step 4 fails: \(G_{\mathrm{L}} \notin \mathcal{V}_1\), so \(B(e_1, G_{\mathrm{L}}) \ne 0\).

The bar with a step. The upper 5 m of the bar have a diameter of 10 mm, the lower 5 m a diameter of 5 mm, so \(EA\) drops to a quarter (section 6.1.3). The exact displacements are \(u(5) = 7{,}320\) µm and \(u(10) = 12{,}20\) µm. The field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) has the slope \(1/EA\), which increases fourfold at the step. Trial fields that are continuous and a line on each of two elements (hat functions, section 6.3.3) have exactly such a kink when the node is at the step. Two elements with a node at \(x = 5\) m contain \(G_5\) and \(G_{10}\), so both nodal values are exact (SymPy: 7,320 and 12,20 µm). With a single element over the whole bar \(G_{10}\) is not in the family and the end displacement is 13,66 µm, 12 % too much (section 6.2.2). The node must therefore be where \(EA\) changes. The same reason holds for every node: where \(EA\) is constant on each element, \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) for a node \(x_{\mathrm{P}}\) is a line on each element, so it is in the family. For the hanging bar with \(n\) elements the nodal values are therefore exact for every \(n\) (SymPy for \(n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\)), for two elements 14,64 and 19,52 µm.

The cantilever under its own weight. The theorem holds also for a beam with a force at the free end, with the same four steps. Let the cantilever of length \(L\) be clamped at \(x = 0\) and let a unit force act upward at the free end. The field \(G(x) = x^2\,(3L - x)/(6EI)\) has \(EI\,G'' = L - x\) and \(G(0) = G'(0) = 0\). For every admissible \(w\) with \(w(0) = w'(0) = 0\), integration by parts gives \(B(G, w) = \int_0^L (L - x)\,w''\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[(L - x)\,w'\big]_0^L + \int_0^L w'\,\mathrm{d}x = w(L)\), which is step 1. For \(L = 1\) m and \(EI = 20\,906{,}7\ \text{N m}^2\), \(G = (3x^2 - x^3)/125\,440\) and it lies in the family \(\{x^2, x^3\}\), but not in the family \(\{x^2\}\). Therefore the end displacement with two terms is exact, \(-0{,}3660\) mm, and with one term it is \(-0{,}2440\) mm, because the error is \(e(L) = B(G, e) = -0{,}1220\) mm. The clamping moment with two terms is not exact, however: it is \(-25{,}51\) N m instead of \(-30{,}61\) N m (17 % too little). The theorem is silent about this, because the moment is the second derivative of the field, not its value at a point.

The theorem has limits. First, the field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) belongs to one point. For two elements on the hanging bar the nodal values are exact, but at the middle of an element the error is \(1{,}220\) µm, because the field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) for this point has a kink in the middle of an element and is not in the family. This error is \(a\,h^2/4\) with \(h = 5\) m, and in section 6.5.4 we shall see how it falls with \(h\). Second, the theorem says nothing about quantities that are not the value of a displacement at a point, for example the moment or the stress. Third, in a plane or spatial body the displacement under a point force is infinite (COOK, PDF p. 361: "Infinite displacement and infinite stress where P acts on a 2D or 3D solid"). The field \(G_{\mathrm{P}}\) has no finite energy there and cannot be in a family of fields with finite energy, so the theorem is not usable for a point force. Exactness at a point is a property of the one-dimensional model.

6.5.3 Cea's lemma for a bar

Theorem 6.37 measures the error in the energy norm, which contains the stiffness \(EA\). Often we are interested in the strain itself, that is the derivative \(u'\) and its error \(u' - u_{\mathrm{n}}'\), which is not weighted by \(EA\). Is the Ritz solution close to the best field of the family here too? For constant stiffness the answer is yes, since the energy norm is then \(\sqrt{EA}\) times the norm of the derivative. For the bar with a step the stiffness changes and the estimate needs bounds. We denote by \(\|f\| = \sqrt{\int_0^L f^2\,\mathrm{d}x}\) the norm of the inner product \((f, g) = \int_0^L f\,g\,\mathrm{d}x\) (definition 6.16).

Theorem 6.39 Cea's lemma for a bar (Ceajeva lema za palico)

Let the bar be fixed at \(x = 0\), \(EA\) piecewise continuous and \(m \le EA(x) \le M\) with \(m > 0\). Let \(u\) be the solution of the weak form and \(u_{\mathrm{n}} \in \psi_0 + \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) a field with \(B(u - u_{\mathrm{n}}, w) = 0\) for every \(w \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\), for example the Ritz solution (lemma 6.36). Then for every \(v \in \psi_0 + \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\)

\[\|u' - u_{\mathrm{n}}'\| \le \frac{M}{m}\,\|u' - v'\| . \tag{6.23}\]

Since \(B\) is symmetric, even

\[\|u' - u_{\mathrm{n}}'\| \le \sqrt{\frac{M}{m}}\,\|u' - v'\| . \tag{6.24}\]
Proof

We must show that the energy of the error bounds the norm of the derivative of the error from below with \(m\), and that, thanks to the orthogonality, the energy of the error does not exceed the part of the error of an arbitrary field, with \(M\), from above.

  1. Let \(e = u - u_{\mathrm{n}}\). The field \(e\) is a test function, \(e(0) = 0\). We split \(e = (u - v) + (v - u_{\mathrm{n}})\), where \(v - u_{\mathrm{n}} \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\). By lemma 6.36, \(B(e, v - u_{\mathrm{n}}) = 0\), so \(B(e, e) = B(e, u - v)\).
  2. Since \(EA \ge m\) and \(e'^2 \ge 0\), by nonnegativity and linearity of the integral of piecewise continuous functions (lemma 6.10, points 2 and 3, for the difference \((EA - m)\,e'^2 \ge 0\)), \(B(e, e) = \int_0^L EA\,e'^2\,\mathrm{d}x \ge m\,\|e'\|^2\).
  3. At every point \(EA\,e'\,(u - v)' \le M\,|e'|\,|(u - v)'|\), so by the same lemma, applied to the difference \(M\,|e'|\,|(u - v)'| - EA\,e'\,(u - v)' \ge 0\), \(B(e, u - v) \le M \int_0^L |e'|\,|(u - v)'|\,\mathrm{d}x = M\,(|e'|, |(u - v)'|)\). By lemma 6.19 this is at most \(M\,\|e'\|\,\|(u - v)'\|\). The lemma applies here because its proof uses only linearity in both arguments, symmetry and \((f, f) \ge 0\), which \((f, g) = \int_0^L f\,g\,\mathrm{d}x\) has for piecewise continuous functions (lemma 6.10).
  4. From steps 1 to 3, \(m\,\|e'\|^2 \le M\,\|e'\|\,\|(u - v)'\|\). If \(\|e'\| = 0\) the claim holds, otherwise we divide by \(m\,\|e'\|\) and obtain equation (6.23).
  5. For the symmetric case we take from step 2 the lower bound \(m\,\|e'\|^2 \le \|e\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\). As in steps 1 to 3 of the proof of theorem 6.37 (they use only orthogonality and symmetry, not the Ritz definition), \(u_{\mathrm{n}} - v \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = \|e\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 + \|u_{\mathrm{n}} - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 \ge \|e\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\). By the upper bound \(EA \le M\), \(\|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 \le M\,\|(u - v)'\|^2\). The chain of inequalities \(m\,\|e'\|^2 \le M\,\|(u - v)'\|^2\) gives, after division by \(m\) and the square root, equation (6.24).

Source: BATHE (4.95) (PDF p. 259): "This result is referred to as Cea's lemma", the constant is independent of \(h\) and dependent on the material properties. The proof in steps for a bar is ours.

The proof showed that the error of the Ritz method is at most a fixed number times the error of the best field of the family, and that this number depends only on the ratio of the extreme stiffnesses, not on the mesh. The first estimate (6.23) does not use symmetry, so it holds also for methods without energy, which we shall meet in the next section. For the second estimate symmetry saves a square root. BATHE (PDF p. 259, footnote 13) warns that for a body the constant grows when the bulk modulus is very large, and the estimate loses its meaning. This is another sign of locking (section 6.4.4), which chapters 12 and 15 treat.

We test it on the bar with a step, where \(M/m = 4\), so the constant of (6.23) is \(4\) and the constant of (6.24) is \(2\). The trial family is one element over the whole bar, \(\{c\,x\}\). The Ritz equation with the test function \(x\) gives \(c\int_0^L EA\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^L EA\,u'\,\mathrm{d}x\), so \(c\) is the average of the true strain \(u'\) weighted by the stiffness: the upper part has four times the weight. The true field has the average derivative \(u(10)/10 = 1{,}220\) µm/m, the Ritz \(c = 1{,}366\) µm/m, 12 % more, which equals the ratio of the end displacements 13,66 and 12,20 µm. The best line in the norm of the derivative is the one with the plain average \(1{,}220\) µm/m. The ratio of norms is \(\|u' - c\|/\|u' - v'\| = 1{,}028\) (SymPy), far below the bounds 2 and 4 (figure 6.21). The bound holds for every load and every bar with the same \(M/m\), so it is loose here. For constant stiffness (\(M/m = 1\), the hanging bar) the constant is 1 and the Ritz solution is the best also in the norm of the derivative, which is theorem 6.37.

6.5.4 Error of piecewise linear interpolation

Cea's lemma and the theorem on the best approximation lead the error of the Ritz method back to the error of the best field of the family. We can estimate this if we can name some field of the family. For a family of fields that are a line on each element, such a field is the interpolant. Let \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) be an element of length \(h = x_{\mathrm{b}} - x_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(u\) a field on it. The interpolant \(Iu\) is the line through the points \((x_{\mathrm{a}}, u(x_{\mathrm{a}}))\) and \((x_{\mathrm{b}}, u(x_{\mathrm{b}}))\) (figure 6.22). How much do \(u\) and \(u'\) differ from \(Iu\) and \((Iu)'\)? The answer must point to \(u''\): if \(u\) is a line the error is zero, and the larger the curvature, the larger the error.

Lemma 6.40 Error of piecewise linear interpolation (napaka odsekoma linearne interpolacije)

Let \(u\) have a continuous second derivative on \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) (one-sided at the end points), \(h = x_{\mathrm{b}} - x_{\mathrm{a}}\), \(\mu = \max |u''|\) on this interval and \(Iu\) the line through the points \((x_{\mathrm{a}}, u(x_{\mathrm{a}}))\) and \((x_{\mathrm{b}}, u(x_{\mathrm{b}}))\). Then for every \(x \in [x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\)

  1. \(|u'(x) - (Iu)'| \le h\,\mu\),
  2. \(|u(x) - Iu(x)| \le \tfrac18\,h^2\,\mu\).
Proof

We must show that the derivative of the error changes by at most \(h\,\mu\) and that the error itself behaves like a parabola with zeros at the end points.

  1. The slope of the line \(Iu\) is \(k = (u(x_{\mathrm{b}}) - u(x_{\mathrm{a}}))/h\). The field \(u\) is continuous on \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) and differentiable, so by the mean value theorem (theorem 1.84) there is \(\xi \in (x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}})\) with \(u'(\xi) = k\).
  2. The derivative \(u'\) is differentiable with derivative \(u''\), continuous on \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\), so by the same theorem for \(u'\) between \(\xi\) and \(x\) there is \(\eta\) with \(u'(x) - u'(\xi) = u''(\eta)\,(x - \xi)\). Since \(|x - \xi| \le h\) and \(|u''(\eta)| \le \mu\), \(|u'(x) - (Iu)'| = |u'(x) - u'(\xi)| \le h\,\mu\). This is point 1.
  3. For point 2 let \(x \in (x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}})\) (at the end points the error is zero). Let \(K = (u(x) - Iu(x))/((x - x_{\mathrm{a}})(x - x_{\mathrm{b}}))\) and \(g(t) = u(t) - Iu(t) - K\,(t - x_{\mathrm{a}})(t - x_{\mathrm{b}})\). The field \(g\) is continuous, twice differentiable and has zeros at \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\), \(x\) and \(x_{\mathrm{b}}\).
  4. By Rolle's theorem (theorem 1.83), \(g'\) has a zero in \((x_{\mathrm{a}}, x)\) and a zero in \((x, x_{\mathrm{b}})\). By the same theorem for \(g'\) between these zeros there is \(\zeta \in (x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}})\) with \(g''(\zeta) = 0\).
  5. Since \(Iu\) is a line, \((Iu)'' = 0\), so \(g'' = u'' - 2K\) and \(K = u''(\zeta)/2\).
  6. Hence \(u(x) - Iu(x) = \tfrac12\,u''(\zeta)\,(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})(x - x_{\mathrm{b}})\). With \(s = x - (x_{\mathrm{a}} + x_{\mathrm{b}})/2\), \(|(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})(x - x_{\mathrm{b}})| = h^2/4 - s^2 \le h^2/4\), so \(|u(x) - Iu(x)| \le \tfrac12\,\mu\,h^2/4 = \tfrac18\,h^2\,\mu\). This is point 2.

Source: KREYSZIG §19.3, (5) (PDF p. 837), theorem 1 and example 3 (PDF p. 838) for the error of interpolation with a polynomial. The proof in steps is ours.

The proof showed that the error of the derivative follows from two uses of the mean value theorem and the error of the field from two uses of Rolle's theorem. A test on the hanging bar: \(u'' = -2a\) is constant, so \(\mu = 2a\), and point 2 gives \(\tfrac18\,h^2 \cdot 2a = a h^2/4\). The actual error is \(\tfrac12 \cdot 2a\,(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})(x_{\mathrm{b}} - x)\), largest at the middle of the element, \(a h^2/4\), so equality holds. For \(h = 5\) m, \(a h^2/4 = 1{,}220\) µm, and for \(h = 10\) m, \(4{,}880\) µm (SymPy). Point 1, however, is twice too large for the same field. The actual \(u' - (Iu)' = u''\,(x - x_{\text{middle}})\) reaches \(a h\) at the end points, and the estimate is \(2 a h\).

Now we can estimate the error of the Ritz method with hat functions. Let the bar be fixed at \(x = 0\) and let \(EA \le M\). We divide it by the nodes \(0 = x_0 < x_1 < \dots < x_{\mathrm{n}} = L\) into the elements \([x_{\mathrm{k}-1}, x_{\mathrm{k}}]\), the longest element having length \(h\). The trial space \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{h}}\) consists of continuous fields that are a line on each element and have the value zero at \(x = 0\) (hat functions, section 6.3.3). We denote the Ritz solution in it by \(u_{\mathrm{h}}\).

Theorem 6.41 Convergence of piecewise linear fields (konvergenca odsekoma linearnih polj)

Let \(u\) be the solution of the weak form of a bar fixed at \(x = 0\), with piecewise continuous \(EA \le M\), and let \(u\) have a continuous second derivative on each element (the nodes are at the jumps of \(EA\)). Let \(\mu\) be the largest \(|u''|\) over all elements. Then

\[\|u - u_{\mathrm{h}}\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 \le M\,L\,h^2\,\mu^2 , \qquad \|u - u_{\mathrm{h}}\|_{\mathrm{E}} \le \sqrt{M L}\,h\,\mu , \tag{6.25}\]
\[0 \le \Pi(u_{\mathrm{h}}) - \Pi(u) \le \tfrac12\,M\,L\,h^2\,\mu^2 . \tag{6.26}\]
Proof

We must show that the family contains a field whose error is estimated by the interpolation lemma, and that the Ritz error is at most that.

  1. Let \(v = Iu\) be the field that is on each element the interpolant of \(u\). It is continuous, because at a node it equals \(u(x_{\mathrm{k}})\) from both sides, it is a line on each element and \(v(0) = u(0) = 0\). So \(v \in \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{h}}\).
  2. By theorem 6.37, \(\|u - u_{\mathrm{h}}\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 \le \|u - v\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2 = \int_0^L EA\,((u - v)')^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  3. On each element, by point 1 of lemma 6.40, \(|(u - v)'| \le h\,\mu\), since the length of the element is at most \(h\).
  4. Hence, because of \(EA \le M\) and nonnegativity of the integral (lemma 6.10, points 2 and 3, for the nonnegative difference \((M - EA)\,((u - v)')^2\)), \(\int_0^L EA\,((u - v)')^2\,\mathrm{d}x \le M\,h^2\,\mu^2\,L\). With step 2 this is the first inequality in equation (6.25), and the square root gives the second.
  5. By theorem 6.34, \(\Pi(u_{\mathrm{h}}) - \Pi(u) = \tfrac12\,\|u - u_{\mathrm{h}}\|_{\mathrm{E}}^2\). This is not negative, and by step 4 it is not larger than \(\tfrac12\,M L\,h^2\,\mu^2\).

Source: BATHE §4.3.5, rate of convergence (PDF p. 261), where the rate is related to the order of the polynomials. The proof in steps is ours.

The proof showed that the error in energy falls as \(h^2\) and in the energy norm as \(h\), because the estimate of the derivative of the error grows as \(h\). At the same time this is the completeness of hat functions that theorem 6.35 needs: for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a mesh with \(\sqrt{M L}\,h\,\mu < \varepsilon\), so a field in the family with an error below \(\varepsilon\).

A test on the hanging bar (\(M = EA\), \(\mu = 2a\)) with uniform meshes \(n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\) (table). The energy of the error is exactly \(97{,}76/n^2\) µJ, and the estimate (6.26) is \(1173/n^2\) µJ, for every \(n\) exactly 12 times more (SymPy).

When we halve \(h\), the energy of the error is divided by 4 (figure 6.23). The reason is \(h^2\). The Ritz solution is here just the interpolant, \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = Iu\): the field \(G_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{i}}\) of the unit force at a node is continuous and a line on each element (the stiffness is constant), so it lies in \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{h}}\), and the nodal values of \(u_{\mathrm{h}}\) are therefore exact by theorem 6.38, while the line on an element is determined by its end points. For quadratic \(u\), \(u' - u_{\mathrm{h}}' = u''\,(x - x_{\text{middle}})\), the energy of the error on one element is therefore \(\tfrac12\,EA\,u''^2\,h^3/12\) and over \(n = L/h\) elements \(EA\,u''^2\,L\,h^2/24\). For \(u'' = -2a\) this is \(\tfrac16\,EA\,a^2 L\,h^2\), which for \(h = L/n\) gives \(97{,}76/n^2\) µJ. The factor 12 between the estimate and the actual error has two sources: point 1 of the lemma is twice too large (the actual largest derivative of the error is \(u''\,h/2\)), which is a factor 4 in the square, and the estimate takes the largest value instead of the mean square, which is \(u''\,h/\sqrt{12}\) against \(u''\,h/2\), a further factor 3. Together \(4 \cdot 3 = 12\). The estimate is therefore reliable but loose by a constant factor, while the order \(h^2\) is exact.

Hence step 2 of the proof of theorem 6.41 is here an equality. The estimate (6.26) holds for each mesh separately, also for the meshes with 2 and 3 elements, which are not one inside the other and about which theorem 6.35 says nothing (section 6.4.5).

Now we have three solid statements for the Ritz method. The error is orthogonal to the trial fields and the Ritz field is the best in the energy norm (lemma 6.36 and theorem 6.37, shown by linearity and positivity of the energy). The displacement at a point is exact when the field of the unit force lies in the family (theorem 6.38, shown by the work of the unit force and orthogonality). For a piecewise linear family the error falls as \(h^2\) in energy (theorem 6.41, shown by interpolation and Rolle's theorem). What remains is the question of what to do where there is no energy, and the answer is in the next section.
