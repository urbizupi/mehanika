Chapter 28 Flexible robot links

28.7 Centrifugal stiffening (sredobežna ojačitev)

We linearized the link on the hub of section 28.6 at rest (corollary 28.22): the hub with inertia \(J_{\mathrm{h}}\) stood still and the link vibrated only transversely. Now let a driver turn the hub at a constant angular velocity \(\Omega\), as it is in an arm that follows a circular arc, or in a turbine blade. What happens to the first transverse frequency of the link? For the steel strip of example A (\(L = 1\) m, cross-section \(40 \times 6\) mm, \(EI = 141{,}12\) N m\(^2\), \(\rho A = 1{,}872\) kg/m, first cantilever frequency \(\omega_1 = 30{,}53\) rad/s, that is \(4{,}86\) Hz) the speed \(\Omega = 10\) rad/s is about \(95\) revolutions per minute, which a joint reaches easily. The answer we shall get is short, and the model of section 28.6 misses it: under spin the frequency rises, it does not fall. The result is drawn in figure 28.20.

The name of the section has two parts. Centrifugal (sredobežna) is the force that seems to a driver to pull him away from the centre of a bend: the word is built from the Latin for centre and fleeing, and in turning axes every piece of matter "flees" from the centre. In non-turning axes there is no such force, the piece only needs a real force that holds it on its circle, and we shall compute that force. Stiffening (ojačitev) means that the link becomes stiffer: the tensile force in the link that this effect causes raises the bending frequency, just as a taut string gives a higher note. The piece of the link that this force holds is shown in figure 28.18.

The path is this. First we look at what the model of section 28.6 says at constant spin and why we cannot accept it. Then we compute the axial force of a spinning link. Then we compute the work of this force on the shortening of the axis in bending, and so obtain the missing term in the energy. At the end we test the theorem on example A and on cases where its condition fails.

Notation. The angular velocity of the hub is \(\Omega\), but in chapter 9 \(\Omega\) is the frequency of excitation, another quantity with the same symbol. The angle of the hub is \(\theta\) as in chapter 8 (not the Timoshenko rotation of the cross-section of chapter 5, which we do not use here). The axial force \(N(x)\) is the one of definition 2.5 (tension positive). We have already met the matrix \(\mat G\) of section 28.7.3 in chapter 12 as the geometric stiffness matrix (lemma 12.50), there for a constant axial force, here for a force that varies along the link.

28.7.1 What the model of the equations of motion of the link says

The link is as in section 28.2: the transverse elastic displacement is \(v(x, t) = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} \psi_{\mathrm{k}}(x)\,q_{\mathrm{k}}\) in a floating frame (plavajoči koordinatni sistem) with its origin at the hub and the axis \(x\) along the link in the direction of the tangent at the joint (clamped reference conditions, definition 28.5). The model is Euler-Bernoulli, the axis is inextensible and the hub radius is zero. The last two assumptions are idealizations: in a real joint the axis of rotation lies a hub radius before the start of the strip, which we also compute in section 28.7.4. The equations of the link on the hub from corollary 28.21 have a row for the angle \(\theta\) and rows for the elastic coordinates. The driver now prescribes \(\theta(t) = \Omega t\), so we do not look for the moment \(\tau\), which is the moment that holds the speed. Such a link is shown in figure 28.19.

Lemma 28.27 A link at constant spin in the model of the equations of motion (člen pri stalnem vrtenju v modelu gibalnih enačb)

Let the link be as in corollary 28.21 (figure 28.19) with clamped reference conditions and \(\theta(t) = \Omega t\) with constant \(\Omega\). Let \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) be the circular frequency of the link at \(\Omega = 0\) with a clamped end (for modal shapes the cantilever frequency of theorem 13.37). Then the elastic coordinates move by the equation

\[\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} + \big(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} - \Omega^2\,\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\big)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0 , \tag{28.38}\]

and every harmonic solution of it, \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \hat{\vek q}\cos\omega t\) with \(\omega > 0\), has a circular frequency \(\omega\) with \(\omega^2 = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \Omega^2\) for some \(r\) with \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} > \Omega\).

Proof

We must show that the row of equations for the elastic coordinates turns into (28.38) and that the relation between the frequencies follows from it.

  1. From \(\theta = \Omega t\) we have \(\dot\theta = \Omega\) and \(\ddot\theta = 0\).
  2. In the row for \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) of corollary 28.21 the term with the angular acceleration \(\vek b\,\ddot\theta\) is therefore zero and \(\dot\theta^2 = \Omega^2\). Equation (28.38) remains. The row for \(\theta\) gives the moment \(\tau\) that holds the speed, and we do not need it.
  3. Let \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \hat{\vek q}\cos\omega t\) with \(\hat{\vek q} \ne \vek 0\). Substitution gives \(\big(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} - (\omega^2 + \Omega^2)\,\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\big)\hat{\vek q} = \vek 0\), so \(\omega^2 + \Omega^2\) is an eigenvalue of the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}, \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) (theorem 10.13, \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is positive definite by theorem 28.13). The eigenvalues of this pair are \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\), the squares of the frequencies of the link with a clamped end.

Source: The proof is not from the source. The equation is a consequence of the equation \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{f}} + \mat K\vek q_{\mathrm{f}} = \ldots\) of SHABANA (5.144), PDF p. 237, with the Coriolis terms neglected, section 28.6.

The lemma says that in this model spin only lowers the frequency. For the link of example A at \(\Omega = 10\) rad/s the first frequency is \(\sqrt{30{,}528^2 - 10^2} = 28{,}843\) rad/s, for \(\Omega = 20\) rad/s it is \(23{,}064\) rad/s, and at \(\Omega = \omega_1 = 30{,}53\) rad/s the first frequency is zero (NumPy, num-07.out), the orange curve in figure 28.20. At larger \(\Omega\) the model predicts even more:

Corollary 28.28 The predicted instability at \(\Omega \ge \omega_1\) (napoved nestabilnosti pri \(\Omega \ge \omega_1\))

Let \(\omega_1\) be the smallest of the frequencies \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) of lemma 28.27 and \(\Omega > \omega_1\). Then equation (28.38) has the solution \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}(t) = \vek\varphi_1\big(a\,\mathrm{e}^{\sigma t} + c\,\mathrm{e}^{-\sigma t}\big)\) with \(\sigma = \sqrt{\Omega^2 - \omega_1^2} > 0\), which for \(a \ne 0\) grows beyond all bounds. At \(\Omega = \omega_1\) the solution is \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek\varphi_1\,(a + c\,t)\), which grows linearly.

Proof

We must show that the given formula solves (28.38).

  1. Let \(\vek\varphi_1\) be the mode with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_1 = \omega_1^2\,\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_1\) (theorem 10.13) and \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = f(t)\,\vek\varphi_1\). Substitution into (28.38) gives \(\big(\ddot f + (\omega_1^2 - \Omega^2) f\big)\,\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_1 = \vek 0\), so \(\ddot f = \sigma^2 f\) with \(\sigma^2 = \Omega^2 - \omega_1^2\).
  2. For \(\sigma > 0\) this equation is solved by \(\mathrm{e}^{\pm\sigma t}\), for \(\sigma = 0\) (\(\ddot f = 0\)) by \(1\) and \(t\).

Source: The proof is not from the source.

Is this prediction true? No. By it the steel strip of length \(1\) m would at \(30{,}53\) rad/s (about \(292\) revolutions per minute) begin to vibrate with a growing amplitude. FPGL in section 10.8 (PDF p. 471) reports for a blade on a turning hub the opposite: the natural frequencies rise with spin. In this section we derive that and prove (corollary 28.32) that the frequency of a straight link at no \(\Omega\) is below \(\omega_1\). At \(\Omega = 10\) rad/s the model of section 28.6 predicts \(28{,}843\) rad/s, that is \(-5{,}5\) % from \(30{,}528\), and the true answer is \(+1{,}0\) % in the plane of rotation. Not only the size is wrong, the sign is wrong. The curves are in figure 28.20.

What is wrong in the model? The model moves a point of the link with material coordinate \(x\) only transversely, from \((x, 0)\) to \((x, v(x))\) (figure 28.19 below). Then the point would be farther from the centre \(\mathrm{O}\) than \(x\), but the axis is inextensible and the piece \([0, x]\) stays of length \(x\). In reality the end of the piece moves transversely and at the same time toward the hub by the shortening (skrček) \(s(x)\). The centrifugal force points away from the centre, but the points of the link move toward the centre in bending: the work is against the force and the energy grows. This work is missing from the model. We compute it with the force and the shortening.

28.7.2 Axial force of a spinning link

We begin with the straight link, before it bends. This spinning link is a straight bar with mass \(\rho A\) per length, of length \(L\), and turns about its end \(\mathrm{O}\) with constant angular velocity \(\Omega\). Every cross-section of it is on a circle about \(\mathrm{O}\), so a force must act on the cross-section that holds it on the circle: just as a stone on a string that we whirl needs the pull of the string toward the hand. The pull in the cross-section \(x\) is the axial force \(N(x)\) of definition 2.5. To compute it we cut the link at \(x\) and take the piece \([x, L]\) out as a free body. On the piece acts the rest of the link with the axial force \(N(x)\), which starts in the cut and points toward the hub, the other end of the piece is free. Newton's second law for the piece says that the sum of forces on it equals the sum of the products of mass and acceleration of its parts. A point at distance \(\xi\) from \(\mathrm{O}\) has at constant angular velocity an acceleration of size \(\Omega^2\xi\) toward \(\mathrm{O}\), like a point we know from circular motion. The force on the piece is therefore the sum of these accelerations multiplied by the mass, and the graph below shows it as a function of \(x\).

Lemma 28.29 Axial force of a spinning link (osna sila vrtečega se člena)

Let a straight prismatic bar of length \(L\) (figure 28.18) with mass \(\rho A\) per length turn about one end \(\mathrm{O}\) with constant angular velocity \(\Omega\) without bending, the hub radius being zero. Then the axial force (definition 2.5) in the cross-section \(x \in [0, L]\) is

\[N(x) = \rho A\,\Omega^2\,\frac{L^2 - x^2}{2} . \tag{28.39}\]

It is tension (\(N \ge 0\)), largest at \(\mathrm{O}\), where \(N(0) = \rho A\,\Omega^2 L^2/2\), and zero at the free end.

Proof

We must show that \(N(x)\) is the sum of the forces that hold the piece \([x, L]\) on circles.

  1. A point of the axis at distance \(\xi\) from \(\mathrm{O}\) has at time \(t\) the position \(\xi\,(\cos\Omega t, \sin\Omega t)\). The second derivative with respect to time is \(-\Omega^2\,\xi\,(\cos\Omega t, \sin\Omega t)\): the acceleration of size \(\Omega^2\xi\) points toward \(\mathrm{O}\) (the velocity \(\Omega\xi\) is perpendicular to the axis, as in lemma 8.78). The acceleration is shown by the grey arrow.
  2. On the piece \([x, L]\) act the forces of the rest of the link in the cross-section \(x\). The free end at \(L\) has no force. The acceleration has a component only along the axis, so the transverse force is zero and the axial force \(N(x)\) remains, directed toward \(\mathrm{O}\) (tension is positive, definition 2.5). The force is the orange arrow on the piece.
  3. Newton's second law projected on the axis toward \(\mathrm{O}\): \(N(x) = \int_{\mathrm{x}}^L \rho A\,\Omega^2\,\xi\,\mathrm{d}\xi\). The acceleration and the force are in figure 28.18.
  4. By the fundamental theorem (theorem 3.40) the integral is \(\rho A\,\Omega^2\,(L^2 - x^2)/2\).

Source: GR (4.267) and (4.268), PDF p. 294, at hub radius \(e = 0\): there the axial force in this same cross-section is the integral of \(m\Omega^2(e + \xi)\) over the piece from \(x\) to the end. The proof with the free body is ours, the source gives only the formula.

For example A at \(\Omega = 10\) rad/s we have \(N(0) = 1{,}872 \cdot 100 \cdot 1/2 = 93{,}6\) N, and at \(\Omega = \omega_1 = 30{,}528\) rad/s it is \(872{,}3\) N (NumPy). At \(x = L/2\) at this speed it is \(654{,}2\) N, which is \(0{,}75\) of the value at the hub, and at the end zero. We check also the piece \([0{,}4, 1]\) m: it has mass \(1{,}1232\) kg and its centre of mass at \(0{,}70\) m, so mass times acceleration is \(1{,}1232 \cdot 30{,}528^2 \cdot 0{,}70 = 732{,}7\) N, and formula (28.39) gives \(1{,}872 \cdot 30{,}528^2 \cdot (1 - 0{,}16)/2 = 732{,}7\) N. The graph of the axial force is in figure 28.18.

The force of \(872\) N is large, but the extension is small: at cross-section \(240\) mm\(^2\) and \(E = 196\) GPa we have \(N(0)/(EA) = 1{,}9 \cdot 10^{-5}\). An inextensible axis is therefore a good idealization. The force is large, however, when we multiply it by the displacement that bending will cause, and that is the energy term we are looking for. The only displacement along the axis is the shortening of figure 28.19.

28.7.3 Shortening of the axis and the energy of the force

We draw the link in the plane of rotation, which we watch in turning axes. First the axis \(x\), the transverse axis \(v\) and the dashed link before bending, the origin \(\mathrm{O}\) is in the hub. Then the bent link, which has the shape of the first cantilever mode. We choose the point with material coordinate \(x\) at \(x = 0{,}6\,L\) and mark the piece of the axis \([0, x]\), which because of the inextensible axis has length \(x\) also after bending. Its end is the point \(\mathrm{P}\). The model of the equations of motion would leave the same point on the vertical above \(x\), in the empty dot \((x, v(x))\). The tilted piece has a horizontal projection shorter than \(x\) and the difference is the shortening \(s(x)\), at the end of the link the shortening \(s(L)\). Such geometry was already computed by lemma 12.48 of chapter 12.

Lemma 12.48 is proved for an element on which \(u(x)\) and \(v(x)\) are continuously differentiable, the axis is inextensible and \(q = \max|v'|\) with \(q^2 \le 10^{-2}\). Its steps 1 to 3 hold for every upper limit \(x \in [0, h]\) and here we have \(u(0) = 0\) (the hub is at \(\mathrm{O}\)). Therefore the point \(\mathrm{P}\) with coordinate \(x\) is in the floating frame at

\[\big(x - s(x, t),\ v(x, t)\big) , \qquad s(x, t) = \int_0^x \Big(1 - \sqrt{1 - v'^2}\Big)\mathrm{d}\xi , \quad \tfrac12\int_0^x v'^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi \le s \le 1{,}0026\cdot\tfrac12\int_0^x v'^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi , \tag{28.40}\]

where the upper bound is from equation (12.53) at \(q^2 = 10^{-2}\). For example A with tip displacement \(10\) mm in the first mode we have \(\max|v'| = 0{,}0138\), so \(q^2 = 1{,}9 \cdot 10^{-4}\) and the factor is \(1{,}00005\), the shortening at the end is \(s(L) = 0{,}0581\) mm, at \(x = L/2\) it is \(0{,}0144\) mm and at \(x = L/4\) it is \(0{,}0025\) mm (NumPy). The shortening is zero at the clamp and grows fast toward the end.

The shortening is small, but the axial force at \(\Omega = \omega_1\) is up to \(872\) N: the product is of the order \(872 \cdot 0{,}0581 \cdot 10^{-3} = 0{,}05\) J, the same order as the elastic energy \(0{,}0218\) J at the same displacement. The work of the force on the shortening must therefore be taken into account. The shortening is shown in figure 28.19.

Theorem 28.30 Centrifugal stiffening of a link (sredobežna ojačitev člena)

Let the link be that of section 28.2 (figure 28.19) with clamped reference conditions, \(v(x, t) = \sum_{\mathrm{k}=1}^n \psi_{\mathrm{k}}(x)\,q_{\mathrm{k}}(t)\) with independent \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}(0) = \psi_{\mathrm{k}}'(0) = 0\), constant \(\rho A\) and \(EI\), an inextensible axis, hub radius zero and \(\theta(t) = \Omega t\) with constant \(\Omega\), and let \(v'^2 \le 10^{-2}\) on the link. Let \((\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})_{\mathrm{kl}} = \int_0^L \rho A\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\psi_{\mathrm{l}}\,\mathrm{d}x\), \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}})_{\mathrm{kl}} = \int_0^L EI\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}}''\psi_{\mathrm{l}}''\,\mathrm{d}x\) and

\[\mat G_{\mathrm{kl}} = \int_0^L \rho A\,\frac{L^2 - x^2}{2}\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}}'\psi_{\mathrm{l}}'\,\mathrm{d}x . \tag{28.41}\]

Then the elastic coordinates satisfy the following two equations, where terms of third and higher order in \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) are left out of the energy (steps 3 and 4). If the elastic displacement lies in the plane of rotation, then

\[\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} + \big(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2\,(\mat G - \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\big)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0 , \tag{28.42}\]

and if the displacement \(w\) is perpendicular to the plane of rotation, along the axis of the joint, which here is the axis of rotation, then

\[\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} + \big(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2\,\mat G\big)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0 . \tag{28.43}\]
Proof

We must show that the kinetic energy of the link in turning axes is to second order \(T = \text{constant} + \tfrac12\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} + \tfrac12\Omega^2\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} - \mat G)\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) plus a total time derivative, and then apply Lagrange's equations.

  1. In non-turning axes the point of the axis with coordinate \(x\) has the position \(\vek r = \mat R(\Omega t)\,\bar{\vek r}\) with \(\bar{\vek r} = (x - s,\ v)\) by equation (28.40) and the rotation matrix \(\mat R\) (lemma 8.40). The time derivative of the matrix is \(\dot{\mat R} = \Omega\,\mat R\,\mat J\) with \(\mat J = \begin{pmatrix}0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix}\) (lemma 8.76, as in lemma 28.8), so the velocity is \(\dot{\vek r} = \mat R\,\big(\dot{\bar{\vek r}} + \Omega\,\mat J\bar{\vek r}\big)\). The point is \(\mathrm{P}\) in figure 28.19.
  2. The matrix \(\mat R\) preserves lengths (lemma 2.40), so \(|\dot{\vek r}|^2 = |\dot{\bar{\vek r}}|^2 + 2\Omega\,\dot{\bar{\vek r}}\cdot\mat J\bar{\vek r} + \Omega^2|\bar{\vek r}|^2\). With \(\dot{\bar{\vek r}} = (-\dot s, \dot v)\) and \(\mat J\bar{\vek r} = (-v, x - s)\) the three terms are \(\dot s^2 + \dot v^2\), \(2\Omega\,\big(\dot s\,v + \dot v\,(x - s)\big)\) and \(\Omega^2\big((x - s)^2 + v^2\big)\). The kinetic energy is \(T = \tfrac12\int_0^L\rho A\,|\dot{\vek r}|^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) (definition 8.79) and by theorem 8.86 it splits into parts \(T_2 + T_1 + T_0\) by the degree in the velocities.
  3. Since \(s\) is of second order in \(v\) (equation (28.40)), to second order \(T_2 = \tfrac12\int\rho A\,\dot v^2 = \tfrac12\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}\) and \(T_1 = \Omega\int\rho A\,x\,\dot v\,\mathrm{d}x = \Omega\,\vek b^\T\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}\) with \(b_{\mathrm{k}} = \int\rho A\,x\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\,\mathrm{d}x\). The terms \(\dot s\,v\) and \(-s\,\dot v\) of \(T_1\) are of third order and we leave them out, and so \(\dot s^2\) and \(s^2\) (fourth order). The term \(\Omega\,\vek b^\T\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}\) is the total time derivative \(\mathrm{d}(\Omega\,\vek b^\T\vek q_{\mathrm{d}})/\mathrm{d}t\) at constant \(\Omega\) and drops out of Lagrange's equation: \(\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}t\,(\partial T_1/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{k}}) = \mathrm{d}(\Omega b_{\mathrm{k}})/\mathrm{d}t = 0\) and \(\partial T_1/\partial q_{\mathrm{k}} = 0\). The shortening in figure 28.19 is small.
  4. What remains is \(T_0 = \tfrac12\Omega^2\int\rho A\,\big((x - s)^2 + v^2\big)\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac12\Omega^2\int\rho A\,x^2\,\mathrm{d}x - \Omega^2\int\rho A\,x\,s\,\mathrm{d}x + \tfrac12\Omega^2\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\), where the term \(\tfrac12\Omega^2\int\rho A\,s^2\) (fourth order) is left out. The first term is a constant.
  5. The integral \(\int_0^L\rho A\,x\,s\,\mathrm{d}x\) we compute by parts (corollary 3.41) with \(P(x) = \rho A\,(L^2 - x^2)/2\): \(P' = -\rho A\,x\), \(P(L) = 0\), \(s(0) = 0\), so \(\int\rho A\,x\,s = -[P\,s]_0^L + \int P\,s' = \int_0^L P\,s'\,\mathrm{d}x\). By the fundamental theorem (theorem 3.40) \(s' = 1 - \sqrt{1 - v'^2}\), and by equation (28.40) \(s'\) lies between \(\tfrac12 v'^2\) and \(1{,}0026\cdot\tfrac12 v'^2\), so with a relative error of at most \(0{,}26\) %. Therefore \(\int\rho A\,x\,s\,\mathrm{d}x \approx \tfrac12\int_0^L P\,v'^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac12\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat G\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\).
  6. Altogether \(T_0 = \text{constant} + \tfrac12\Omega^2\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} - \mat G)\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\). The potential energy of the elastic forces is \(U = \tfrac12\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) (theorem 28.18). The constraint \(\theta = \Omega t\) contains time (definition 8.42), so Lagrange's equations (theorem 8.88, \(Q_{\mathrm{k}} = -\partial U/\partial q_{\mathrm{k}}\)) give \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} - \Omega^2(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} - \mat G)\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} + \mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0\). This is (28.42).
  7. A displacement \(w\) perpendicular to the plane: \(\bar{\vek r} = (x - s, 0)\) and \(w\) in the direction of the axis of the joint. The velocity is \(\mat R\,(-\dot s, \Omega(x - s))\) in the plane and \(\dot w\) perpendicular to it, so \(|\dot{\vek r}|^2 = \dot s^2 + \dot w^2 + \Omega^2(x - s)^2\) without a cross term. The term \(\Omega^2 v^2\) drops out and \(T_0 = \text{constant} - \tfrac12\Omega^2\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat G\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) remains, from the same steps 4 and 5 with \(w\) instead of \(v\). Lagrange's equations give (28.43).

Source: The proof is not from the source. GR (4.269), PDF p. 294, writes equation (28.43) for a beam clamped at distance \(e\) from the axis of rotation, with gyroscopic forces neglected (in our computation these are the terms with \(\dot s\), step 3). FPGL (10.41), PDF p. 471: \(\mat M\ddot{\vek q} + (\mat K + \Omega^2(\mat K_{\mathrm{c}} - \mat M_{\mathrm{t}}\sin^2\alpha))\vek q = \vek 0\) with \(\alpha = 0\) for a displacement out of the plane and \(\alpha = \pi/2\) for a displacement in the plane, where \(\mat K_{\mathrm{c}}\) plays the role of our \(\mat G\). FPGL PDF p. 470: Coriolis effects are neglected.

The theorem is derived with two approximations that we name. The first is the shortening \(s\) replaced by \(\tfrac12\int v'^2\) (step 5): the error is at most \(0{,}26\) % at \(v'^2 \le 10^{-2}\) and \(0{,}005\) % at the displacement \(10\) mm in example A. The second are the left-out terms of third order \(\dot s\,v - s\,\dot v\) of \(T_1\), that is the Coriolis term between the transverse motion and the motion of the point along the axis, which the shortening causes. For one shape (\(n = 1\), \(v = q_1\psi_1\), \(s = q_1^2 S\) with \(S(x) = \tfrac12\int_0^x\psi_1'^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi\)) we have \(\int\rho A\,(\dot s\,v - s\,\dot v)\,\mathrm{d}x = c\,q_1^2\dot q_1 = (c/3)\,\mathrm{d}(q_1^3)/\mathrm{d}t\) with \(c = \int\rho A\,S\,\psi_1\,\mathrm{d}x\) (for the shape with \(\psi_1(L) = 1\), \(c = 0{,}250\) kg/m), a total time derivative, so it drops out of the equation. For several shapes it is not a total derivative. An estimate that is not a bound: at tip displacement \(10\) mm and \(\Omega = \omega_1\) the first shape excites the second with an amplitude of the order \(6 \cdot 10^{-4}\) of the amplitude of the first (a factor \(3\) for the number of terms, NumPy). FPGL says that for blades the Coriolis influence on bending is negligible because of the large radial stiffness (PDF pp. 470 and 472). We measure both approximations by the smallness of the shortening in figure 28.19.

What the theorem says. The force \(N(x)\) is tension and pulls the link toward the hub, like a taut string (section 12.10.1, figure 12.31: a taut bar resists transverse displacement). Bending does not lengthen the axis but shortens it, and the shortening works against the centrifugal force and raises the energy by \(\tfrac12\Omega^2\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat G\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\). The softening \(-\tfrac12\Omega^2\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) is the work of the centrifugal force on the transverse displacement, and the model of the equations of motion of the link has only this. In the first mode of example A at \(\Omega = \omega_1\) and tip displacement \(10\) mm the elastic energy is \(\tfrac12\omega_1^2 q_1^2 = 0{,}0218\) J, the softening is \(-0{,}0218\) J and the stiffening \(+0{,}0260\) J (num-07.out). The stiffening exceeds the softening by \(0{,}0042\) J, so the sum of the energies is positive and there is no instability.

28.7.4 A test on example A and cases where the condition fails

Example 28.31 The steel strip spinning (jekleni trak pri vrtenju)

Now we prove that the prediction of instability is wrong.

Corollary 28.32 A spinning link does not fall below the cantilever frequency (frekvenca vrtečega se člena ni pod frekvenco konzole)

Let the link be as in theorem 28.30. For every column \(\vek q\) we have \(\vek q^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q \le \vek q^\T\mat G\,\vek q\). Therefore the matrices \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2(\mat G - \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2\mat G\) are positive definite at every \(\Omega\), all frequencies are real and the first frequency is at every \(\Omega\) at least \(\omega_1\). For the displacement out of the plane the square of the first frequency is at least \(\omega_1^2 + \Omega^2\).

Proof

We must show the estimate \(\int\rho A\,v^2 \le \int P\,v'^2\) with \(P = \rho A(L^2 - x^2)/2\) and then estimate the Rayleigh quotient.

  1. For \(v = \sum\psi_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}\) with \(v(0) = 0\) we have \(v(x) = \int_0^x v'\,\mathrm{d}\xi\). By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (lemma 6.19, the functions \(1\) and \(v'\) on \([0, x]\)) we have \(v(x)^2 \le x\int_0^x v'^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi\).
  2. We multiply by \(\rho A\) and integrate over \(x\). By parts (corollary 3.41) with \(F(x) = \int_0^x v'^2\,\mathrm{d}\xi\) we have \(\int_0^L x\,F\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{L^2}{2}F(L) - \int_0^L\tfrac{x^2}{2}\,v'^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \int_0^L\tfrac{L^2 - x^2}{2}\,v'^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), so \(\int\rho A\,v^2 \le \int P\,v'^2\), that is \(\vek q^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q \le \vek q^\T\mat G\,\vek q\).
  3. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is positive definite: from \(\vek q^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q = \int EI\,v''^2 = 0\) it follows that \(v'' = 0\), so \(v = a + bx\), and \(v(0) = v'(0) = 0\) gives \(v = 0\), that is \(\vek q = \vek 0\) for independent \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\). By step 2 we have \(\vek q^\T\big(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2(\mat G - \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\big)\vek q \ge \vek q^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q > 0\) for \(\vek q \ne \vek 0\), and even more for \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2\mat G\).
  4. The Rayleigh quotient of the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2(\mat G - \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}),\ \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) is by step 2 at every \(\vek q\) at least the quotient of the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}, \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\), and this is at least \(\omega_1^2\) (theorem 10.26). The smallest eigenvalue of the first pair is its smallest quotient (the same theorem), so \(\omega_1(\Omega)^2 \ge \omega_1^2\). The quotient of the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} + \Omega^2\mat G,\ \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) is by step 2 at least the quotient of the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}, \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) plus \(\Omega^2\), so the square of the first frequency out of the plane is at least \(\omega_1^2 + \Omega^2\).

Source: The proof is not from the source.

We test the inequality on the first shape of example A: \(G_{11} = 1{,}19334 \ge 1 = M_{11}\). The linear model is therefore not only quantitatively but in principle wrong: it predicts an instability that does not exist. The ratio is visible in figure 28.20 as the difference between the blue and the orange curve.

We test the theorem also on two cases where its condition fails.

The first case is the hub radius. In a real arm the axis of the joint can lie a hub radius \(e\) before the start of the strip. At hub radius \(e > 0\) the position of the point is \((e + x - s,\ v)\), the same steps 4 and 5 of the proof give the weight \(\big((L + e)^2 - (x + e)^2\big)/2\) instead of \((L^2 - x^2)/2\) in equation (28.41) and \(N(x) = \rho A\,\Omega^2\,\big((L + e)^2 - (x + e)^2\big)/2\), which is GR (4.268), PDF p. 294. For example A at \(e = 0\), \(0{,}1\), \(0{,}2\) m we have \(G_{11} = 1{,}19334\), \(1{,}35042\), \(1{,}50751\) and the first frequency in the plane at \(\Omega = 60\) rad/s is \(38{,}648\), \(45{,}224\), \(50{,}954\) rad/s (out of the plane \(71{,}370\), \(75{,}135\), \(78{,}717\)). The theorem therefore holds for \(e = 0\), at \(e > 0\) the stiffening is larger and formula (28.41) is no longer exact. At \(e > 0\) the graph in figure 28.18 would be higher: at \(\Omega = 20\) rad/s and \(e = 0{,}1\) m we have \(N(0) = 449{,}3\) N instead of \(374{,}4\) N (num-07.out).

A test with a rigid blade: the straight link of figure 28.18 is hinged at \(\mathrm{O}\) and tilts out of the plane or in the plane. GR (4.274), PDF p. 295, for a rigid blade hinged at radius \(e\) gives \(\omega^2 = \Omega^2\,(1 + 3e/(2L))\). Our formula with the function \(\psi = x\) and the weight for \(e\) gives the ratio \(\int\rho A\big((L + e)^2 - (x + e)^2\big)/2\,\mathrm{d}x\,/\int\rho A\,x^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), which is \(1{,}15000\) at \(e = 0{,}1\,L\) and \(1{,}75\) at \(0{,}5\,L\), equal to \(1 + 3e/(2L)\). At \(e = 0\) the ratio is \(1\), so \(\omega = \Omega\) out of the plane, and in the plane the ratio is \((G - M)/M = 0\): a rigid blade hinged at the axis of rotation is left neutral by the spin in the plane. The linear model would give \(-\Omega^2\), an instability at every speed. The shape \(\psi = x\) is not admissible under clamped reference conditions (\(\psi'(0) = 0\)), but steps 1 to 5 of the proof require only \(\psi(0) = 0\), so the test holds. It is also a case where the inequality of corollary 28.32 is an equality, since \(v'\) is constant.

The second case is the linear model itself at \(\Omega = \omega_1\). The linear model of section 28.7.1 predicts \(0\) rad/s, the theorem \(33{,}170\) rad/s and \(20\) elements \(33{,}164\) rad/s. When the work of the axial force is missing, the model agrees neither with the theorem nor with the elements. The difference is the difference between the orange and the blue curve in figure 28.20.

What have we shown? A link that spins has, besides the softening \(-\Omega^2\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) that the model of section 28.6 already knows, a stiffening \(+\Omega^2\mat G\): the matrix \(\mat G\) is the work of the axial force \(N(x)\) of lemma 28.29 on the shortening of the axis of equation (28.40). In the plane of rotation the stiffening beats the softening at every \(\Omega\) (corollary 28.32), out of the plane there is no softening. The same computation with an axial force that varies along the element gives nearly the same frequencies on \(20\) Hermite elements (example 28.31). For turbine blades FPGL uses the same term in section 10.8 (PDF p. 471). What stays neglected are the Coriolis term with the displacement along the axis (third order, estimate \(6 \cdot 10^{-4}\)), spatial terms and joints with several links, which chapter 27 treats. The result is shown in figure 28.20.
