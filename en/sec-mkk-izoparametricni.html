Chapter 15 Continuum finite elements

15.5 Isoparametric elements: the quadrilateral and the eight-node element

The triangle of section 15.4 has two weaknesses. Its displacement field is linear, so the strain in it is constant, while the true strain in bending grows linearly over the height, and the triangle cannot bend (the last subsection of that section). And its sides are straight, so a curved boundary is replaced by a polygonal line, while an element with a curved side can follow it. The remedy is already known in one dimension from chapter 7: the quadratic bar element has three nodes, and the map between the reference interval \([-1, 1]\) and the actual element (equation (7.28)) uses the same variable \(\xi\) as the shape functions. In the plane we do the same with two coordinates: a reference square is mapped onto a quadrilateral by the same functions with which we interpolate the displacements. Such an element is called isoparametric.

With the triangle the shape functions were already functions of the point \((x, y)\), so nothing had to be mapped. With an isoparametric element three questions arise. When is the map good, that is one to one and with a Jacobian determinant that is nowhere zero or negative, so that we may use theorem 4.59? How do we get the strain if we know the shape functions as functions of the coordinates \(\xi\) and \(\eta\), not of \(x\) and \(y\)? And does the field of the element stay continuous across shared sides, as the Ritz theorems of section 15.2 require, and does it contain the linear fields that the patch test of section 15.7 requires? The idealizations are as before: the material is linear elastic, the strains are small and the material is isotropic (steel, \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\)), but this section computes only geometry and shape functions, the material enters in the following ones.

15.5.1 The square and four shape functions

The reference element is the square \(K = [-1, 1]^2\) in the plane with natural coordinates (naravni koordinati) \(\xi\) and \(\eta\). Its four nodes are numbered counterclockwise from the lower left: \(1 = (-1, -1)\), \(2 = (1, -1)\), \(3 = (1, 1)\), \(4 = (-1, 1)\), and we write the coordinates of node \(i\) as \((\xi_{\mathrm{i}}, \eta_{\mathrm{i}})\).

We draw the square with side \(2\), so that its sides are always \(\xi = \pm 1\) and \(\eta = \pm 1\), whatever the shape of the actual element. Each node has one function that equals \(1\) in it and \(0\) in the other nodes. For node \(1\) this is a product of two linear functions, equal to \(1\) at \(\xi = \eta = -1\) and zero on the sides \(\xi = 1\) and \(\eta = 1\). The level lines of this function are hyperbolas, since the product \((1 - \xi)(1 - \eta)\) at a constant value is a hyperbola, and the value falls from \(1\) at node \(1\) to \(0\) on both opposite sides. On the right is the eight-node element Q8: the four corners are as for Q4, the four midpoints of the sides get the numbers \(5\) to \(8\), and together they make the eight nodes of the element. In general, for node \(i\) the following definition holds.

Definition 15.29 Shape functions of the four-node element (oblikovne funkcije štirikotnika)

The shape functions of the element with four nodes on the square \(K\), called Q4 (quadrilateral, four nodes), are

\[N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi, \eta) = \tfrac14\,(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\,\xi)\,(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\,\eta) , \qquad i = 1, 2, 3, 4 . \tag{15.26}\]

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF p. 220: shape functions of equation (6.2-3), each equal to \(1\) in its own node and \(0\) in the others. ONATE1, chapter 6, PDF p. 211. The name Q4 is used the same way there and in COOK.

Lemma 15.30 Properties of the four-node shape functions (lastnosti oblikovnih funkcij štirikotnika)

For the functions (15.26):

  1. \(N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi_{\mathrm{j}}, \eta_{\mathrm{j}}) = \delta_{\mathrm{ij}}\) for \(i, j = 1, \dots, 4\).
  2. \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\), \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\,N_{\mathrm{i}} = \xi\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\,N_{\mathrm{i}} = \eta\).
  3. On the side \(\eta = -1\), \(N_3 = N_4 = 0\) and \(N_1 = (1 - \xi)/2\), \(N_2 = (1 + \xi)/2\). The same holds for the other three sides: on each side only the functions of its two nodes are different from zero, and both are linear along the side.
  4. \(N_{\mathrm{i}} \ge 0\) everywhere on \(K\).
Proof

We must show four claims. The first by substitution, the second by splitting the sum over the nodes into a product of two sums, the third and fourth by writing the functions out.

  1. Let \(i \ne j\). Then \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}} \ne \xi_{\mathrm{j}}\) or \(\eta_{\mathrm{i}} \ne \eta_{\mathrm{j}}\), and each coordinate is \(\pm 1\), so \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}} = -1\) or \(\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}} = -1\) and one factor \(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}}\) or \(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}}\) is zero. For \(i = j\) each factor is \(1 + 1 = 2\) and \(N_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 1\).
  2. The four nodes have all four combinations \((\xi_{\mathrm{i}}, \eta_{\mathrm{i}}) \in \{\pm 1\}^2\), each exactly once. Hence the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14 \big(\sum_{\mathrm{a} = \pm 1}(1 + a\xi)\big)\big(\sum_{\mathrm{b} = \pm 1}(1 + b\eta)\big) = \tfrac14 \cdot 2 \cdot 2 = 1\).
  3. Similarly the weighted sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\,N_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14 \big(\sum_{\mathrm{a} = \pm 1} a\,(1 + a\xi)\big)\big(\sum_{\mathrm{b} = \pm 1}(1 + b\eta)\big) = \tfrac14 \cdot 2\xi \cdot 2 = \xi\), because \((1 + \xi) - (1 - \xi) = 2\xi\). For \(\eta\) the computation is the same.
  4. At \(\eta = -1\) every function with \(\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = +1\) has the factor \(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta = 0\), these are \(N_3\) and \(N_4\). The remaining two have the factor \(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta = 2\), so \(N_1 = \tfrac14 (1 - \xi) \cdot 2 = (1 - \xi)/2\) and \(N_2 = (1 + \xi)/2\). The sides \(\xi = 1\), \(\eta = 1\), \(\xi = -1\) are the same after exchanging the roles.
  5. On \(K\), \(|\xi| \le 1\) and \(|\eta| \le 1\), so both factors \(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi \ge 0\) and \(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta \ge 0\), and the product is not negative.

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF p. 220: the check that each function is \(1\) in its own node and \(0\) in the others. Points 2 to 4 and the proof are ours.

15.5.2 The same functions for position and for field

Now we say what isoparametric means. The element has nodes at positions \(\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = (x_{\mathrm{i}}, y_{\mathrm{i}})\) and displacements \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} = (u_{\mathrm{i}}, v_{\mathrm{i}})\). We use the functions \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\), defined on the square, twice, once for the position of a point of the element, once for its displacement.

Definition 15.31 Isoparametric element (izoparametrični element)

Let \(K\) be the reference region, in the plane the square \([-1, 1]^2\), in space the cube \([-1, 1]^3\) (section 15.10), and let \(N_1, \dots, N_{\mathrm{m}}\) be shape functions with \(N_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{j}}) = \delta_{\mathrm{ij}}\) in the nodes \(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{j}}\) of \(K\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\). The isoparametric element with nodes \(\vek p_1, \dots, \vek p_{\mathrm{m}}\) is the set of points \(\Phi(K)\) with the geometric map

\[\Phi(\xi, \eta) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{m} N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi, \eta)\,\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} , \tag{15.27}\]

with the displacement field that has the value

\[\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}\big(\Phi(\xi, \eta)\big) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{m} N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi, \eta)\,\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} \tag{15.28}\]

at the point \(\Phi(\xi, \eta)\), where \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}}\) are the nodal displacements. When \(\Phi\) is injective on \(K\) (for Q4 theorem 15.33), this makes \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}\) a function of the point of the element. For \(m = 4\) and the functions of definition 15.29 this is the quadrilateral Q4.

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF p. 220: the same shape functions interpolate coordinates and displacements of a point of the element from the values at the nodes. COOK, section 6.1, PDF p. 216: an element is isoparametric when the functions for position and for the field are identical, subparametric when those for position are of lower degree, superparametric when of higher degree.

History tells why this recipe arose and how it got its name. The isoparametric formulation began with four-node plane elements introduced by Taig in 1958, and publication outside company reports began with Irons in 1966. Irons developed elements with curved sides and is credited with the name "isoparametric" (COOK, PDF p. 217, ONATE1, PDF p. 212). The reason for curved sides is the same as ours: with triangles with straight sides a curved boundary is replaced by a polygonal line, while with an element with a curved side the boundary can follow the shape of the body.

Let us see on a concrete quadrilateral what the map does. Let the nodes be (in mm) \(\vek p_1 = (0, 0)\), \(\vek p_2 = (40, 0)\), \(\vek p_3 = (36, 30)\) and \(\vek p_4 = (4, 24)\), the area of this image will be \(972\) mm². By equation (15.26) the point \((\xi, \eta) = (\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)\) has the values \(N_1, \dots, N_4\) equal to \(0{,}1875\), \(0{,}5625\), \(0{,}1875\) and \(0{,}0625\), with sum \(1\) (lemma 15.30, point 2), and goes to \(x = 0{,}5625 \cdot 40 + 0{,}1875 \cdot 36 + 0{,}0625 \cdot 4 = 29{,}5\) mm and \(y = 0{,}1875 \cdot 30 + 0{,}0625 \cdot 24 = 7{,}125\) mm (num-05.out, part D). The centre \((0, 0)\) goes to \((20; 13{,}5)\) mm, which is not the centroid of the quadrilateral, since that lies at \((20{,}74; 13{,}06)\) mm.

First the square on the left with four numbered nodes, the axes \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) and six lines, blue at constant \(\xi\) and orange at constant \(\eta\). The arrow \(\Phi\) carries it to the right into the quadrilateral with the same node labels. The lines of the square went over into segments, which are no longer perpendicular and no longer equally spaced. Why segments, the next lemma says. The centre of the square fell on \((20; 13{,}5)\) mm, and the numbers at the corners and at the centre are values of the Jacobian determinant, which we define right away. The point \((\tfrac12, -\tfrac12)\) went to \((29{,}5; 7{,}125)\) mm, as computed.

The matrix of partial derivatives of the map, \(\mat F = \begin{bmatrix} x_\xi & x_\eta \\ y_\xi & y_\eta \end{bmatrix}\) (a subscript means a derivative, this is the matrix \(\mat F_\Phi\) of chapter 4), and its determinant \(J = \det\mat F\) are called the Jacobian matrix and the Jacobian determinant (Jacobijeva matrika in Jacobijeva determinanta). For the bar this was the factor \(\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}\xi = h/2\) of equation (7.28), in the plane it is a \(2 \times 2\) determinant. For the cross product of two plane vectors we write \(\vek u \times \vek v = u_{\mathrm{x}} v_{\mathrm{y}} - u_{\mathrm{y}} v_{\mathrm{x}}\), so \(J = \Phi_\xi \times \Phi_\eta\).

Why it is called an area factor is shown by figure 15.55. Take in the square the unit cell at node \(1\), with sides \(\Delta\xi = 1\) and \(\Delta\eta = 1\). The map pulls the side along \(\xi\) into the vector \(\Phi_\xi\,\Delta\xi\) and the side along \(\eta\) into \(\Phi_\eta\,\Delta\eta\). At node \(1\) these are the arrows \((20; 0)\) mm and \((2; 12)\) mm. If the map were linear in the cell, the image would be the parallelogram with these sides, and its area is exactly the cross product, \(20 \cdot 12 - 0 \cdot 2 = 240\) mm², that is \(J_1\) times the cell area \(1\). So \(J\) is the number by which the map multiplies a small area: \(\Delta A \approx J\,\Delta\xi\,\Delta\eta\). For the unit cell this is only an approximation, because the map is not linear (the assumption holds when the cell is small against the change of \(J\), and the error shows as the difference between the dashed edge and the true image). For our map \(J\) is an affine function, so the area of the image of a cell is exactly \(J\) at the cell centre times the cell area. Two cells of side \(\tfrac12\) have centres \((\tfrac34, -\tfrac34)\) and \((-\tfrac34, \tfrac34)\), there \(J = 285{,}75\) and \(200{,}25\) mm², so their images have areas \(\tfrac14 \cdot 285{,}75 = 71{,}4\) mm² and \(\tfrac14 \cdot 200{,}25 = 50{,}1\) mm². Two equal cells of the square thus give images of different size, and \(J\) tells by how much the area is stretched at that place (num-05.out, part B).

Lemma 15.32 Map and Jacobian determinant of the four-node element (preslikava in Jacobijeva determinanta štirikotnika)

Let \(\Phi\) be the map (15.27) of a quadrilateral Q4 with nodes \(\vek p_1, \dots, \vek p_4\) and \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek p_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \vek p_{\mathrm{i}}\) the side vectors (indices cyclic, \(\vek p_5 = \vek p_1\)). Let \(\vek a\), \(\vek b\), \(\vek c\) and \(\vek d\)

\[\vek a = \tfrac14(\vek p_1 + \vek p_2 + \vek p_3 + \vek p_4) , \quad \vek b = \tfrac14(-\vek p_1 + \vek p_2 + \vek p_3 - \vek p_4) , \quad \vek c = \tfrac14(-\vek p_1 - \vek p_2 + \vek p_3 + \vek p_4) , \quad \vek d = \tfrac14(\vek p_1 - \vek p_2 + \vek p_3 - \vek p_4) .\]

Then:

  1. \(\Phi(\xi, \eta) = \vek a + \vek b\,\xi + \vek c\,\eta + \vek d\,\xi\eta\). At constant \(\eta\) the function \(\xi \mapsto \Phi(\xi, \eta)\) is affine, at constant \(\xi\) the function \(\eta \mapsto \Phi(\xi, \eta)\) is affine. The sides of the square go over into the segments \(\vek p_1\vek p_2\), \(\vek p_2\vek p_3\), \(\vek p_3\vek p_4\), \(\vek p_4\vek p_1\).
  2. \(J(\xi, \eta) = \vek b\times\vek c + (\vek b\times\vek d)\,\xi + (\vek d\times\vek c)\,\eta\), so \(J\) is an affine function of \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).
  3. The value at corner \(i\) is \(J_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14\,\vek e_{\mathrm{i}-1}\times\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\), and \(J(0, 0) = \tfrac14 (J_1 + J_2 + J_3 + J_4)\).
Proof

We must show three points. The first by collecting terms, the second by differentiating, the third by computing at the four corners.

  1. In equation (15.27) we insert \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) from equation (15.26) and collect the terms in \(1\), \(\xi\), \(\eta\) and \(\xi\eta\). The coefficient of \(1\) is \(\tfrac14\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek a\), of \(\xi\) it is \(\tfrac14\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek b\), of \(\eta\) it is \(\tfrac14\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek c\) and of \(\xi\eta\) it is \(\tfrac14\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek d\), because \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = 1, -1, 1, -1\).
  2. At constant \(\eta\), \(\Phi = (\vek a + \vek c\eta) + (\vek b + \vek d\eta)\,\xi\), an affine function of \(\xi\), and the same at constant \(\xi\). At \(\eta = -1\) this is a segment that by lemma 15.30, point 1, has the values \(\vek p_1\) and \(\vek p_2\) at \(\xi = \mp 1\). The other three sides are the same.
  3. The derivatives are \(\Phi_\xi = \vek b + \vek d\eta\) and \(\Phi_\eta = \vek c + \vek d\xi\), so \(J = (\vek b + \vek d\eta)\times(\vek c + \vek d\xi) = \vek b\times\vek c + \xi\,\vek b\times\vek d + \eta\,\vek d\times\vek c + \xi\eta\,\vek d\times\vek d\), and \(\vek d\times\vek d = 0\).
  4. Corners. The computations of step 1 give \(\vek b - \vek d = \tfrac12\vek e_1\), \(\vek b + \vek d = -\tfrac12\vek e_3\), \(\vek c - \vek d = -\tfrac12\vek e_4\) and \(\vek c + \vek d = \tfrac12\vek e_2\). At \((-1, -1)\) we have \(\Phi_\xi = \vek b - \vek d\) and \(\Phi_\eta = \vek c - \vek d\), so \(J_1 = \tfrac12\vek e_1\times(-\tfrac12\vek e_4) = \tfrac14\vek e_4\times\vek e_1\). At \((1, -1)\), \(J_2 = \tfrac12\vek e_1\times\tfrac12\vek e_2\), at \((1, 1)\), \(J_3 = (-\tfrac12\vek e_3)\times\tfrac12\vek e_2 = \tfrac14\vek e_2\times\vek e_3\), and at \((-1, 1)\), \(J_4 = (-\tfrac12\vek e_3)\times(-\tfrac12\vek e_4) = \tfrac14\vek e_3\times\vek e_4\).
  5. For an affine function \(J = j_0 + j_1\xi + j_2\eta\) we have \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} J_{\mathrm{i}} = 4 j_0 + j_1\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{i}} + j_2\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = 4j_0 = 4J(0, 0)\), because \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\).

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF p. 221: \(J\) is the determinant of the Jacobian matrix, for a rectangle and a parallelogram it is constant, \(J = A/4\). The proof, the affinity of \(J\) and the values at the corners are ours.

For our quadrilateral \(\vek a = (20; 13{,}5)\) mm, \(\vek b = (18; 1{,}5)\) mm, \(\vek c = (0; 13{,}5)\) mm and \(\vek d = (-2; 1{,}5)\) mm, so \(\vek b\times\vek c = 243\), \(\vek b\times\vek d = 30\) and \(\vek d\times\vek c = -27\) mm². Hence \(J = 243 + 30\,\xi - 27\,\eta\) mm², at the corners \(240\), \(300\), \(246\) and \(186\) mm² and at the centre \(243\) mm², as figure 15.55 shows (num-05.out, part B). The mean of the four corner values is indeed \(243\). For a \(40 \times 30\) mm rectangle \(J = 300\) mm² everywhere, one quarter of the area \(1200\) mm², in agreement with the statement in COOK.

15.5.3 When a quadrilateral is a curved box

Theorem 4.59 requires the map to be injective on an open set around \(K\) and to have a nonzero Jacobian determinant, and definition 4.65 further requires it to be positive. For a quadrilateral this is simple, because \(J\) is an affine function.

Theorem 15.33 A quadrilateral with positive determinant at the corners is a curved box (štirikotnik s pozitivno determinanto v ogliščih je ukrivljen kvader)

Let \(\Phi\) be the map of a quadrilateral Q4 and let \(J_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) at all four corners (\(i = 1, \dots, 4\), lemma 15.32). Then:

  1. \(J > 0\) on the closed square \(K\) and on the open square \(K_\varepsilon = (-1 - \varepsilon, 1 + \varepsilon)^2\) for \(\varepsilon > 0\) small enough.
  2. The map \(\Phi\) is injective on \(K_\varepsilon\).
  3. The map \(\Phi\) satisfies conditions 1 to 3 of definition 4.65, written for the plane. The set \(Q = \Phi(K)\) is therefore a curved box in the plane, and for every continuous function \(f\) on \(Q\)
    \[\int_{\mathrm{Q}} f\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{K}} f\big(\Phi(\xi, \eta)\big)\,J(\xi, \eta)\,\mathrm{d}A . \tag{15.29}\]
  4. The area of the quadrilateral is \(A = 4J(0, 0) = J_1 + J_2 + J_3 + J_4\).
Proof

We must show four claims. The positivity of \(J\) follows from \(J\) being a weighted sum of the corner values with nonnegative weights. Injectivity follows from the difference of the images of two points being the Jacobian matrix at the midpoint times the difference of the points. Then we apply the change of variables theorem.

  1. The function \(J\) is affine (lemma 15.32, point 2), so by lemma 15.30, point 2, \(J = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} J_{\mathrm{i}}\,N_{\mathrm{i}}\). For affine \(J = j_0 + j_1\xi + j_2\eta\) the sum has the value \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} J_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = j_0\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} + j_1\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} + j_2\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\), which is \(j_0 + j_1\xi + j_2\eta\).
  2. By point 4 of lemma 15.30 the \(N_{\mathrm{i}} \ge 0\) and their sum is \(1\), so \(J \ge \min_{\mathrm{i}} J_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) on \(K\).
  3. Values on the open square. An affine function \(j_0 + j_1\xi + j_2\eta\) reaches its smallest value on a square at one of the corners, because each of the terms \(j_1\xi\) and \(j_2\eta\) reaches its smallest value at an end of its interval. The corners of the square \(K_\varepsilon\) are \((1 + \varepsilon)(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}, \eta_{\mathrm{i}})\) and \(J\big((1 + \varepsilon)(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}, \eta_{\mathrm{i}})\big) = J(0, 0) + (1 + \varepsilon)\,(J_{\mathrm{i}} - J(0, 0)) = J_{\mathrm{i}} + \varepsilon\,(J_{\mathrm{i}} - J(0, 0))\). At \(\varepsilon = 0\) this equals \(J_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) and it is continuous in \(\varepsilon\), so for small enough \(\varepsilon\) it is positive at all four corners and \(J > 0\) on the whole \(K_\varepsilon\).
  4. Let \(P = (\xi_{\mathrm{P}}, \eta_{\mathrm{P}})\) and \(Q' = (\xi_{\mathrm{Q}'}, \eta_{\mathrm{Q}'})\) be points of \(K_\varepsilon\) and \(\vek\delta = Q' - P\) (figure 15.57). By lemma 15.32, point 1, \(\Phi(Q') - \Phi(P) = \vek b\,\delta_\xi + \vek c\,\delta_\eta + \vek d\,(\xi_{\mathrm{Q}'}\eta_{\mathrm{Q}'} - \xi_{\mathrm{P}}\eta_{\mathrm{P}})\). We have \(\xi_{\mathrm{Q}'}\eta_{\mathrm{Q}'} - \xi_{\mathrm{P}}\eta_{\mathrm{P}} = \tfrac12\big[(\xi_{\mathrm{P}} + \xi_{\mathrm{Q}'})(\eta_{\mathrm{Q}'} - \eta_{\mathrm{P}}) + (\xi_{\mathrm{Q}'} - \xi_{\mathrm{P}})(\eta_{\mathrm{P}} + \eta_{\mathrm{Q}'})\big] = \xi_{\mathrm{M}}\,\delta_\eta + \eta_{\mathrm{M}}\,\delta_\xi\) with the midpoint \(M = (\xi_{\mathrm{M}}, \eta_{\mathrm{M}})\) of the segment \(PQ'\). So \(\Phi(Q') - \Phi(P) = (\vek b + \vek d\,\eta_{\mathrm{M}})\,\delta_\xi + (\vek c + \vek d\,\xi_{\mathrm{M}})\,\delta_\eta = \mat F(M)\,\vek\delta\).
  5. The square \(K_\varepsilon\) is convex, so \(M \in K_\varepsilon\) and \(\det\mat F(M) = J(M) > 0\) by step 3. A matrix with nonzero determinant is invertible (lemma 2.73), so for \(\vek\delta \ne \vek 0\) also \(\mat F(M)\,\vek\delta \ne \vek 0\) and \(\Phi(Q') \ne \Phi(P)\). This is point 2.
  6. The components of \(\Phi\) are polynomials, so they have continuous partial derivatives of all orders on the whole plane, \(\Phi\) is injective and \(J > 0\) on \(K_\varepsilon\). These are conditions 1 to 3 of definition 4.65. The square \(K\) is a set with area, so equation (15.29) holds by theorem 4.59 (in the plane), with \(|J| = J\).
  7. Area. For \(f = 1\) equation (15.29) gives \(A = \int_{\mathrm{K}} J\,\mathrm{d}A\). The integral of an affine function over the square is \(4j_0\), since \(\int_{\mathrm{K}} \xi\,\mathrm{d}A = \int_{\mathrm{K}} \eta\,\mathrm{d}A = 0\) by theorem 4.9, and \(4j_0 = 4J(0, 0) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} J_{\mathrm{i}}\) by step 5 of lemma 15.32.

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF pp. 221 and 222: \(J\) is the factor that multiplies \(\mathrm{d}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\eta\) into the area element, nodes must be numbered counterclockwise so that \(J\) is not negative. ONATE1, section 6.1, PDF p. 211: the Jacobian determinant must have a constant sign over the element, for the linear quadrilateral this is satisfied if no internal angle is equal to or larger than \(180^\circ\) (the source states the condition without proof). The proof is ours.

Step 4 of the proof we read on the figure. In the square we take two arbitrary points \(P\) and \(Q'\) of the grey square \(K_\varepsilon\), which is a little larger than \(K\), and the vector \(\vek\delta\) between them. The midpoint \(M\) of the segment lies in \(K_\varepsilon\) because the square is convex: this is the first assumption and it is needed, since in a non-convex region \(M\) could lie outside, where \(J\) is not positive. The arrow \(\Phi\) carries \(P\) and \(Q'\) to \(\Phi(P) = (8{,}96; 10{,}08)\) and \(\Phi(Q') = (29{,}84; 25{,}92)\) mm, into the quadrilateral and its enlarged outline. The segment \(PQ'\) does not go over into a segment but into a curved line, because \(\Phi\) is bilinear and not linear. The computation of step 4 shows, however, that the chord \(\Phi(Q') - \Phi(P) = (20{,}88; 15{,}84)\) mm is exactly \(\mat F(M)\,\vek\delta\), the tangent map at the midpoint. This holds because \(\Phi\) is a polynomial of degree two in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) together (the term \(\xi\eta\)), and it would fail for a polynomial of higher degree. For us \(J(M) = 234{,}9\) mm\(^2\) and \(\mat F(M)\,\vek\delta = (20{,}88; 15{,}84)\) mm, which agrees with the chord, and since \(J(M) \ne 0\) the chord is not zero: different points have different images. For the folded quadrilateral below \(J(M) = 0\) exactly and the chord vanishes (the points \(P\) and \(Q\) share an image).

The condition \(J_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) has a geometric meaning. By point 3 of lemma 15.32, \(J_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14|\vek e_{\mathrm{i}-1}||\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}|\sin\theta_{\mathrm{i}}\), where \(\theta_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the angle by which the vector \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}-1}\) turns counterclockwise into \(\vek e_{\mathrm{i}}\), that is the exterior angle at corner \(i\). A positive \(J_{\mathrm{i}}\) means that the boundary turns left at corner \(i\), the interior angle is smaller than \(180^\circ\). The condition of theorem 15.33 therefore means four turns to the left, that is a convex quadrilateral with nodes numbered counterclockwise (ONATE1, PDF p. 211, states the same condition for the interior angles). The example in the map of figure 15.55 has all four \(J_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) and is convex.

What happens if the condition is not met? Take a quadrilateral with nodes \((0, 0)\), \((40, 0)\), \((14, 8)\) and \((4, 24)\) mm, which differs from the previous one only in node \(3\), pulled inward. The polygon with these four vertices is an ordinary, nonconvex quadrilateral, and whoever draws it sees nothing wrong.

15.5.4 The strain from the inverse of the Jacobian matrix

The displacement field of the element (15.28) is a function of \((\xi, \eta)\), but we need the strains as derivatives with respect to \(x\) and \(y\). For the bar \(\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x = (2/h)\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}\xi\) was enough, because the ratio is constant. In the plane a matrix appears.

Lemma 15.35 Derivatives of the shape functions with respect to x and y (odvodi oblikovnih funkcij po x in y)

Let an isoparametric element be a curved box (\(\Phi\) injective on an open set \(D \supseteq K\) with \(J > 0\) on \(D\), for Q4 by theorem 15.33), \((\xi, \eta) \in K\), and let \(g(\xi, \eta)\) be a smooth function on \(D\) with \(g = f\circ\Phi\) for some function \(f(x, y)\) on \(\Phi(D)\). Then

\[\begin{pmatrix} f_{\mathrm{x}} \\ f_{\mathrm{y}} \end{pmatrix} = \mat F^{-\T}\begin{pmatrix} g_\xi \\ g_\eta \end{pmatrix} , \qquad \mat F^{-1} = \frac{1}{J}\begin{bmatrix} y_\eta & -x_\eta \\ -y_\xi & x_\xi \end{bmatrix} . \tag{15.30}\]

In particular \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x = (y_\eta N_{\mathrm{i},\xi} - y_\xi N_{\mathrm{i},\eta})/J\) and \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y = (-x_\eta N_{\mathrm{i},\xi} + x_\xi N_{\mathrm{i},\eta})/J\), and for the strain \(\vek\varepsilon = (\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}}, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}}, \gamma_{\mathrm{xy}})^\T = \mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = (u_1, v_1, \dots, u_4, v_4)^\T\) the matrix \(\mat B\) of size \(3 \times 8\) has in columns \(2i - 1\) and \(2i\) the block

\[\mat B_{\mathrm{i}} = \begin{bmatrix} \partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x & 0 \\ 0 & \partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y \\ \partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y & \partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x \end{bmatrix} . \tag{15.31}\]
Proof

We must show the relation between the derivatives, its inverse and the form of the matrix \(\mat B\).

  1. By the chain rule (lemma 4.43), \(g_\xi = f_{\mathrm{x}}\,x_\xi + f_{\mathrm{y}}\,y_\xi\) and \(g_\eta = f_{\mathrm{x}}\,x_\eta + f_{\mathrm{y}}\,y_\eta\), so \((g_\xi, g_\eta)^\T = \mat F^\T\,(f_{\mathrm{x}}, f_{\mathrm{y}})^\T\). The derivatives \(f_{\mathrm{x}}, f_{\mathrm{y}}\) exist, because by theorem 4.51 the inverse \(\Psi\) of \(\Phi\) is continuously differentiable on the open set \(\Phi(D)\) and \(f = g\circ\Psi\).
  2. The matrix \(\mat F^\T\) has determinant \(J \ne 0\), so it is invertible, and the inverse of a \(2 \times 2\) matrix is the adjugate matrix divided by the determinant. This gives the equations for \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x\) and \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y\) with \(g = N_{\mathrm{i}}\).
  3. The strains of the field \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\vek u_{\mathrm{i}}\) are \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{xx}} = \partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} (\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x)\,u_{\mathrm{i}}\), \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{yy}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} (\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y)\,v_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}} = \partial u_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial y + \partial v_{\mathrm{h}}/\partial x = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} (\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y\,u_{\mathrm{i}} + \partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x\,v_{\mathrm{i}})\). These are the rows of the product \(\mat B\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with the blocks \(\mat B_{\mathrm{i}}\).

Source: COOK, section 6.2, PDF pp. 220 to 222: the derivation of the derivatives with respect to \(x\) and \(y\) with the Jacobian matrix, equations (6.2-4) to (6.2-11), and the matrix \(\mat B\) as a product of three matrices. The proof is ours.

At the centre \(\mat F = \begin{bmatrix} 18 & 0 \\ 1{,}5 & 13{,}5 \end{bmatrix}\) mm and \(J = 243\) mm². The first value is \((13{,}5\cdot(-\tfrac14) - 1{,}5\cdot(-\tfrac14))/243 = -3/243\). The denominator \(J\) is in every entry of \(\mat B\). For the triangle \(\mat B\) of equation (15.22) was a constant matrix, here it depends on the point \((\xi, \eta)\) and is a ratio of polynomials, which will matter in section 15.6. On notation: \(\mat B\) is the same matrix as for the triangle (the matrix that gives the strain from the column of nodal displacements), only dependent on the point. The row \(\vek b_{\mathrm{e}}\) of chapter 7 is its one-dimensional form. The determinant \(J\) here is the \(J_\Phi\) of chapter 4.

The field \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}\) in the element is a polynomial in \(\xi\) and \(\eta\). In the coordinates \(x\) and \(y\) it is a polynomial for all nodal displacements if the map is affine, because then \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) are affine functions of \(x\) and \(y\). For a rectangle \(x = x_{\mathrm{c}} + a\,\xi\), \(y = y_{\mathrm{c}} + b\,\eta\) the field is bilinear in \(x\) and \(y\): it contains \(1\), \(x\), \(y\) and \(xy\), but not \(x^2\) or \(y^2\). For a general quadrilateral we get \(\xi\) and \(\eta\) from \(x\) and \(y\) by solving a quadratic equation, so they contain a square root and the field is in general not a polynomial in \(x\) and \(y\). For our quadrilateral, for example, \(\xi = x/40 + y/30 - 5 + \sqrt{9x^2 + 24xy + 16y^2 + 2880x - 4800y + 230\,400}/120\) (\(x\) and \(y\) in mm, SymPy, it gives \(-1\) at node \(1\) and \(1\) at node \(2\)). Because the field on a rectangle lacks the terms \(x^2\) and \(y^2\), Q4 does not describe bending exactly (section 15.7).

15.5.5 Linear fields and compatibility on shared sides

An isoparametric element must meet two conditions that the Ritz theory of section 15.2 demands. The field must be continuous across a shared side with another element (so that it lies in the space \(X\) of piecewise smooth fields, definition 15.13), and it must contain the linear fields, so that on any mesh the exact solution with constant strain is in the family.

Lemma 15.36 Linear fields belong to the isoparametric family (linearna polja so v družini izoparametričnih elementov)

Let an isoparametric element (definition 15.31) have nodal positions \(\vek p_{\mathrm{i}}\), and let \(\vek a_0 \in \mathbb{R}^2\) be a column and \(\mat G\) a \(2 \times 2\) matrix of constants. If the nodal displacements are \(\vek u_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek a_0 + \mat G\,\vek p_{\mathrm{i}}\), the field of the element at every point of the element is

\[\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}(\vek x) = \vek a_0 + \mat G\,\vek x . \tag{15.32}\]

The strain \(\vek\varepsilon = \tfrac12(\mat G + \mat G^\T)\) is then constant everywhere on the element and equal to the strain of this linear field.

Proof

We must show equation (15.32) and the strain.

  1. By equation (15.28), at the point \(\vek x = \Phi(\xi, \eta)\) the value is \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,(\vek a_0 + \mat G\,\vek p_{\mathrm{i}}) = \vek a_0\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} + \mat G\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\vek p_{\mathrm{i}}\).
  2. By definition \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\) and by equation (15.27) \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\vek p_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek x\). So \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}}(\vek x) = \vek a_0 + \mat G\,\vek x\).
  3. The derivatives of this field are \(\partial u/\partial x = G_{11}\), \(\partial u/\partial y = G_{12}\), \(\partial v/\partial x = G_{21}\), \(\partial v/\partial y = G_{22}\), which gives the strain \((G_{11}, G_{22}, G_{12} + G_{21})\), where the third component is \(\gamma_{\mathrm{xy}}\). This is the strain of the linear field, whatever the shape of the element.

The proof is ours.

Lemma 15.37 Compatibility of isoparametric elements along a shared side (skladnost izoparametričnih elementov na stiku)

Let two elements Q4 have a shared side with nodes \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(\vek p_{\mathrm{b}}\) and displacements \(\vek u_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(\vek u_{\mathrm{b}}\) at these nodes. Then their sides coincide as sets of points and the displacement fields have the same value at every point of this side.

Proof

We must show that the side and the field on it do not depend on the element.

  1. By lemma 15.30, point 3, on the side \(\eta = -1\) of the first element \(N_3 = N_4 = 0\) and \(N_1 = (1 - \xi)/2\), \(N_2 = (1 + \xi)/2\). With a numbering in which the shared side is exactly this one, the position is therefore \(\vek x = \tfrac{1 - \xi}{2}\vek p_{\mathrm{a}} + \tfrac{1 + \xi}{2}\vek p_{\mathrm{b}}\) and the displacement \(\vek u_{\mathrm{h}} = \tfrac{1 - \xi}{2}\vek u_{\mathrm{a}} + \tfrac{1 + \xi}{2}\vek u_{\mathrm{b}}\). The side is the segment from \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\) to \(\vek p_{\mathrm{b}}\) and the field on it is an affine function of the position along the segment.
  2. For another side or another element with the same side the computation is the same, with the roles of the nodes possibly exchanged, which means the replacement \(\xi \to -\xi\). The segment and the affine field between the values \(\vek u_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(\vek u_{\mathrm{b}}\) remain the same.
  3. The points of the segment are therefore the same in both elements and the values of the fields are equal at each, because an affine function on a segment is determined by the values at its ends.

15.5.6 The conductivity matrix of the quadrilateral

Section 15.3 used for the Q4 mesh a row of the conductivity matrix without derivation. Now we have all the ingredients. For a scalar field (temperature) the element Q4 replaces the matrix \(\mat B\) of equation (15.31) by a gradient row, and the element matrix is as for the triangle (lemma 15.17) the integral \((\mat k_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = \int k\,\nabla N_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\nabla N_{\mathrm{j}}\,t\,\mathrm{d}A\).

Lemma 15.38 The conductivity matrix of the four-node element on a square (prevodnostna matrika štirikotnika na kvadratu)

Let the element Q4 be a square with side \(h\), conductivity \(k\) and thickness \(t\), everything constant, with the nodes at the corners. Then the element matrix

\[\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{k\,t}{6}\begin{bmatrix} 4 & -1 & -2 & -1 \\ -1 & 4 & -1 & -2 \\ -2 & -1 & 4 & -1 \\ -1 & -2 & -1 & 4 \end{bmatrix} , \tag{15.33}\]

independent of \(h\). For a mesh of congruent squares with side \(h\) the row of the assembled matrix at an interior node \(I\) is \(\tfrac{k\,t}{3}\big(8\,T_{\mathrm{I}} - \sum_{\mathrm{J}} T_{\mathrm{J}}\big)\), where the sum runs over the eight neighbouring nodes.

Proof

We must show the element matrix and the row after assembly.

  1. For \(x = x_0 + h\,(1 + \xi)/2\) and \(y = y_0 + h\,(1 + \eta)/2\) we have \(\mat F = (h/2)\,\mat I\) and \(J = h^2/4\). By equation (15.30), \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x = \tfrac{2}{h}\,\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial\xi = \tfrac{\xi_{\mathrm{i}}}{2h}\,(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta)\) and \(\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial y = \tfrac{\eta_{\mathrm{i}}}{2h}\,(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi)\).
  2. The entry is \((\mat k_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = k\,t\,\tfrac{h^2}{4}\cdot\tfrac{1}{4h^2}\int_{\mathrm{K}} \big[\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}}(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta)(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{j}}\eta) + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}}(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi)(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{j}}\xi)\big]\mathrm{d}A\). Together \((\mat k_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{ij}} = \tfrac{k\,t}{16}\,\big[4(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}} + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}}) + \tfrac83\,\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}}\big]\), independent of \(h\).
  3. For \(i = j\), \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}} = \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}} = 1\) and the entry is \(\tfrac{k\,t}{16}(8 + \tfrac83) = \tfrac23\,k\,t\). For adjacent nodes (they differ in one coordinate) \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}} + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\) and \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{j}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{j}} = -1\), so \(-\tfrac{k\,t}{6}\). For opposite nodes the sum is \(-2\) and the product \(1\), so \(\tfrac{k\,t}{16}(-8 + \tfrac83) = -\tfrac13\,k\,t\). This is the matrix (15.33).
  4. An interior node belongs to four elements. By theorem 7.13 the diagonal entry is the sum \(4\cdot\tfrac23\,k\,t = \tfrac83\,k\,t\). Each of the four nodes along an edge shares two elements with \(I\), the entry is \(2\cdot(-\tfrac16)\,k\,t = -\tfrac13\,k\,t\). Each of the four diagonal neighbours shares one element, the entry is \(-\tfrac13\,k\,t\) (opposite node). So the row is \(\tfrac{k\,t}{3}\,(8\,T_{\mathrm{I}} - \sum_{\mathrm{J}} T_{\mathrm{J}})\) with eight neighbours, the sum of the coefficients is \(0\) as for every conductivity matrix.

15.5.7 The eight-node element

The linear side of the quadrilateral is replaced by a quadratic one: each side gets one more node and the element has eight nodes, this is the element Q8. As in the quadratic bar element each side has three nodes, and we arrange the eight nodes in the square so that the corners \(1\) to \(4\) are as before and the midpoints of the sides \(5\) to \(8\) are on the axes \(\xi\) and \(\eta\).

Definition 15.39 The eight-node element (osemvozliščni element)

On the square \(K\) the nodes \((\xi_{\mathrm{i}}, \eta_{\mathrm{i}})\) are the corners \(1\) to \(4\) as for Q4 and the midpoints \(5 = (0, -1)\), \(6 = (1, 0)\), \(7 = (0, 1)\), \(8 = (-1, 0)\). The shape functions of the element Q8 are

\[N_{\mathrm{i}} = \tfrac14(1 + \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi)(1 + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta)(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi + \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta - 1) \quad (i = 1, \dots, 4) ,\]
\[N_5 = \tfrac12(1 - \xi^2)(1 - \eta) , \; N_6 = \tfrac12(1 + \xi)(1 - \eta^2) , \; N_7 = \tfrac12(1 - \xi^2)(1 + \eta) , \; N_8 = \tfrac12(1 - \xi)(1 - \eta^2) . \tag{15.34}\]

The isoparametric element Q8 is the element of definition 15.31 with \(m = 8\).

Source: COOK, section 6.4, PDF pp. 227 and 228: the eight-node plane element, shape functions of equation (6.4-1) and their derivation by interpolation. COOK (PDF p. 229) calls this family "serendipity", the name is used in this book only here as a remark.

Lemma 15.40 Properties of the Q8 shape functions (lastnosti oblikovnih funkcij elementa Q8)

For the functions (15.34), \(N_{\mathrm{i}}(\xi_{\mathrm{j}}, \eta_{\mathrm{j}}) = \delta_{\mathrm{ij}}\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} N_{\mathrm{i}} = 1\). On the side \(\eta = -1\), \(N_3 = N_4 = N_6 = N_7 = N_8 = 0\) and \(N_1 = \xi(\xi - 1)/2\), \(N_5 = 1 - \xi^2\), \(N_2 = \xi(\xi + 1)/2\), so the same functions as for the quadratic bar element.

Proof

We must show three claims. The first by looking at where each function vanishes, the second by expanding a product, the third by inserting \(\eta = -1\).

  1. The function \(N_5\) vanishes where \(\xi = \pm 1\) (nodes \(1\) to \(4\), \(6\), \(8\)) or \(\eta = 1\) (node \(7\)), and at \((0, -1)\) it has the value \(\tfrac12\cdot 1\cdot 2 = 1\). Similarly for \(N_6\), \(N_7\), \(N_8\). The function \(N_1 = \tfrac14(1 - \xi)(1 - \eta)(-\xi - \eta - 1)\) vanishes at \(\xi = 1\) (nodes \(2\), \(3\), \(6\)) and \(\eta = 1\) (nodes \(3\), \(4\), \(7\)), at \((0, -1)\) and \((-1, 0)\) the third factor is zero, and at \((-1, -1)\) it is \(\tfrac14\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 1 = 1\). For \(N_2\), \(N_3\), \(N_4\) the computation is the same with the roles exchanged.
  2. The sum. For \(a = \xi_{\mathrm{i}}\xi\) and \(b = \eta_{\mathrm{i}}\eta\), \((1 + a)(1 + b)(a + b - 1) = a^2 + b^2 - 1 + ab + a^2 b + a b^2\). In the sum over the four corners the terms with odd powers of \(\xi_{\mathrm{i}}\) or \(\eta_{\mathrm{i}}\) (\(ab\), \(a^2b\), \(ab^2\)) vanish, because \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}}\xi_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}}\eta_{\mathrm{i}} = 0\) and all sign combinations occur once. What remains is \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}\le 4} N_{\mathrm{i}} = \xi^2 + \eta^2 - 1\).
  3. Midpoints: \(N_5 + N_7 = \tfrac12(1 - \xi^2)\big[(1 - \eta) + (1 + \eta)\big] = 1 - \xi^2\) and \(N_6 + N_8 = 1 - \eta^2\). Together \(\xi^2 + \eta^2 - 1 + 1 - \xi^2 + 1 - \eta^2 = 1\).
  4. At \(\eta = -1\) we insert: \(N_5 = 1 - \xi^2\), \(N_7 = 0\), \(N_6 = N_8 = 0\) (factor \(1 - \eta^2 = 0\)), \(N_3 = N_4 = 0\) (factor \(1 + \eta = 0\)), \(N_1 = \tfrac14(1 - \xi)\cdot 2\cdot(-\xi) = \xi(\xi - 1)/2\) and \(N_2 = \xi(\xi + 1)/2\).

Source: The lemma is not from a source. The functions along a side are ONATE1, equation (3.11), PDF p. 103, as chapter 7 cites them.

For Q8 lemmas 15.36 and 15.37 also hold (with the same proof, three nodes on a side instead of two), but the condition of theorem 15.33 does not carry over to Q8. For Q4 the \(J\) is affine, for Q8 it is not: for the element with corners \((0, 0)\), \((40, 0)\), \((36, 30)\), \((4, 24)\) mm and midpoints \((20, -6)\), \((38, 15)\), \((20, 27)\), \((2, 12)\) mm, \(J = 297 + 30\,\xi - 33\,\eta - 42\,\xi^2 - 6\,\xi^2\eta\) mm² (SymPy, num-05.out, part I). A check at the corners is therefore not enough. We check the values of \(J\) on a \(201 \times 201\) grid of points (smallest value \(186\) mm², num-05.out, part E) or, like COOK, at the points where we will integrate (section 15.6). Such a check is no proof that \(J > 0\) everywhere, so theorem 15.33 is not proved for Q8.

One thing we can prove, and it is close to a shortcut that is often used with Q8: pushing the midside node of a side toward the corner.

Lemma 15.41 A side of the Q8 element and the position of the midside node (stranica elementa Q8 in lega srednjega vozlišča)

Let a side of the element Q8 have nodes \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\), \(\vek p_{\mathrm{m}}\), \(\vek p_{\mathrm{b}}\) (corner, midside node, corner), let \(\vek e = \vek p_{\mathrm{b}} - \vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\) and \(\vek p_{\mathrm{m}} = \tfrac12(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}} + \vek p_{\mathrm{b}}) + s\,\tfrac12\vek e\) for a number \(s\), so \(\tfrac{1 + s}{2}\) is the fraction of the length from \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\) to \(\vek p_{\mathrm{m}}\) along \(\vek e\). The points of the side are \(\vek x(\tau) = \tfrac12(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}} + \vek p_{\mathrm{b}}) + \tfrac12\vek e\,\big[\tau + s\,(1 - \tau^2)\big]\), \(\tau \in [-1, 1]\). Then:

  1. The tangent is \(\vek x'(\tau) = \tfrac12\vek e\,(1 - 2s\tau)\).
  2. If \(|s| < \tfrac12\), then \(1 - 2s\tau > 0\) on \([-1, 1]\) and the tangent does not vanish. At \(s = \mp\tfrac12\), \(\vek x'(\mp 1) = \vek 0\), the tangent vanishes at one corner. For \(|s| > \tfrac12\) the tangent changes direction at the point \(\tau = 1/(2s) \in (-1, 1)\).
  3. For \(s = -0{,}6\) the points \(\tau = -1\) and \(\tau = -\tfrac23\) have the same position.
Proof

We must show three claims. The first by differentiating, the second by the sign of a factor, the third by solving a quadratic equation.

  1. By lemma 15.40, on the side \(\vek x = \vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\,\tfrac{\tau(\tau - 1)}{2} + \vek p_{\mathrm{m}}\,(1 - \tau^2) + \vek p_{\mathrm{b}}\,\tfrac{\tau(\tau + 1)}{2}\). We insert \(\vek p_{\mathrm{m}}\) and collect: \(\tfrac12(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}} + \vek p_{\mathrm{b}}) + \tfrac12\vek e\,\tau + \tfrac12 s\,\vek e\,(1 - \tau^2)\), which is the form in the statement. The derivative with respect to \(\tau\) is \(\tfrac12\vek e\,(1 - 2s\tau)\).
  2. The factor \(1 - 2s\tau\) is affine in \(\tau\), so it is positive on \([-1, 1]\) exactly when it is positive at both ends, that is \(1 - 2s > 0\) and \(1 + 2s > 0\), so \(|s| < \tfrac12\). At \(s = -\tfrac12\) the factor at \(\tau = -1\) equals \(1 + 2s = 0\), at \(s = \tfrac12\) at \(\tau = 1\) it equals \(1 - 2s = 0\). For \(|s| > \tfrac12\) the factor has the zero \(\tau = 1/(2s)\) inside \((-1, 1)\) and changes sign there, and with it the direction of the tangent.
  3. For \(s = -0{,}6\) the coordinate along \(\vek e\) is \(\tfrac12[\tau - 0{,}6\,(1 - \tau^2)]\). The value at \(\tau = -1\) is \(-\tfrac12\), so equal to that at \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\). The equation \(\tau - 0{,}6\,(1 - \tau^2) = -1\) gives \(0{,}6\,\tau^2 + \tau + 0{,}4 = 0\) with solutions \(\tau = (-1 \pm 0{,}2)/1{,}2\), so \(-1\) and \(-\tfrac23\). The smallest value of the coordinate is at \(\tau = -\tfrac56\) and it is \(-\tfrac{61}{120}\) of the length \(|\vek e|\), so the side went \(\tfrac1{60}\) of half the length beyond the corner.

Source: ONATE1, section 6.1, PDF p. 211: for a quadratic element the side nodes must lie within the "middle third" of the distance between adjacent corners (the source states the condition for positive \(J\) without proof). COOK, section 6.11, PDF p. 250, figure 6.11-2b: node \(7\) moved to corner \(3\) gives a negative \(J\) at a Gauss point. Our condition \(|s| < \tfrac12\), a fraction between \(\tfrac14\) and \(\tfrac34\), is necessary for a tangent without a zero on the side and is weaker than the "middle third" condition, which corresponds to \(|s| < \tfrac13\).

Let us look at the figure for a side with ends \(x = \mp 1\) (the unit is half the side) and the midside node at \(s\). The axes are \(\tau\) horizontally and the coordinate \(x\) along the side vertically, the dashed verticals and horizontals are the ends \(\mp 1\). The straight line at \(s = 0\) is the uniform map, the midside node is in the middle. At \(s = -0{,}5\) the midside node is at a quarter of the distance from \(\vek p_{\mathrm{a}}\) and the curve has a horizontal tangent at \(\tau = -1\): the coordinate does not change there. At \(s = -0{,}6\) the curve goes below the value at the end and then returns, so that the points \(\tau = -1\) and \(\tau = -\tfrac23\) land in the same place: the side folds back onto itself.

The same happens in the plane. For a \(40 \times 30\) mm rectangle whose lower midside node is at \(x_{\mathrm{m}}\) mm instead of at \(20\), \(J(-1, -1) = 30\,(x_{\mathrm{m}} - 10)\) and \(J(1, -1) = 30\,(30 - x_{\mathrm{m}})\) mm². At \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = 20, 15, 10, 8\) mm, \(J(-1, -1) = 300, 150, 0, -60\) mm² and \(J(1, -1) = 300, 450, 600, 660\) mm² (num-05.out, part E). At \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = 10\), that is at a quarter of the side length, \(J\) at the corner is zero, and at \(x_{\mathrm{m}} = 8\) mm it is negative. The reason: the left and right sides are vertical, so \(x_\eta = 0\) at both lower corners and \(J = x_\xi\,y_\eta\), with \(y_\eta = 15\) mm and \(x_\xi = 20\,(1 - 2s\tau)\) by lemma 15.41 (with \(\vek e = (40, 0)\) mm and \(s = (x_{\mathrm{m}} - 20)/20\)). At \(\tau = -1\) this is \(2x_{\mathrm{m}} - 20\), at \(\tau = 1\) it is \(60 - 2x_{\mathrm{m}}\).

The matrix \(\mat B\) has \(J\) in the denominator, so the integrand of the stiffness matrix is a rational function and cannot be integrated as a polynomial (section 15.6). The field contains the linear fields and is compatible across the shared sides, so the element must pass the patch test (section 15.7).
