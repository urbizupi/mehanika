Chapter 19 Symmetry, periodicity and constraints

19.3 Splitting a load into symmetric and antisymmetric parts

Mirror symmetry saved us half the work in sections 19.1 and 19.2, but only for a load that the mirror maps onto itself or onto its negative (definition 19.3). Such loads are not common. A typical case is a horizontal force on a frame (figure 19.15, left). Take a steel portal frame: two columns of length \(1\) m clamped at \(A\) and \(E\) and a beam of length \(1\) m between the nodes \(B\) and \(C\), all of a \(20 \times 40\) mm section (height \(40\) mm in the plane of the frame) with \(E = 196\) GPa. A force \(H = 500\) N acts on the node \(B\) to the right. The mirror that maps the frame onto itself maps this force to a force at the node \(C\) pointing left, a load the frame does not have. Does this make the symmetry useless?

It does not, because a law we know from every statics problem holds: if the model is linear (small displacements, Hooke's law, corollary 11.3), the response to a sum of loads is the sum of the responses. In our frame the displacements are below \(1{,}5\) mm in \(1\) m, which is less than \(0{,}15\) %, and the steel is far below yield. We can therefore write the load \(H\) as a sum of two loads, each symmetric or antisymmetric, solve each on a half of the frame and add the results. Instead of one problem with the whole frame we solve two with half frames. This is how COOK (section 2.11, PDF p. 70) solves a frame in which symmetry is not visible at first sight, and BARBER (section 5.2.3, PDF p. 81) a beam that is symmetric while its load is not symmetric about its axis. Figure 19.15 shows such a split.

Let us do this on the board. First we draw the frame, carried by the columns with clamps at \(A\) and \(E\), and the beam between the rigid corners \(B\) and \(C\). We mark the middle of the beam with the node \(D\), because it lies on the mirror plane that divides the frame into a left and a right half. Then we add the force \(H\) at \(B\). To the right of the equals sign we draw the same frame twice, each with a part of the force. In the first, the force \(H/2\) at \(B\) points right and the force \(H/2\) at \(C\) points left, a mirror pair that the mirror maps onto itself. In the second, both forces \(H/2\) point right, an antisymmetric pair, because the mirror maps the first force onto the second but with the opposite sign. The sum at \(B\) is \(H/2 + H/2 = H\) and at \(C\) it is \(-H/2 + H/2 = 0\), exactly our load. Next we cut the beam at \(D\) and draw what the halves exchange. In the symmetric part the forces \(H/2\) press the beam from both ends towards each other, so the right half acts on the left half with an axial force \(N\) pointing left, and the left half on the right half likewise. In the antisymmetric part both forces point right, the frame tilts, and the cut beam carries a shear force \(V\) that pulls the left half up and the right half down. Why choose exactly the halves? Because this is the only choice for which each part is symmetric or antisymmetric. The next lemma proves it for every load column and every mirror matrix.

Lemma 19.10 Split of a load into symmetric and antisymmetric parts (razcep bremena na simetrični in protisimetrični del)

Let \(\mat P\) be a matrix of size \(n \times n\) with \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\) (for instance a mirror matrix of definition 19.5), and \(\vek f \in \mathbb{R}^n\) a column of loads. Then:

  1. The columns
    \[\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} = \tfrac12\,(\vek f + \mat P\vek f) , \qquad \vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = \tfrac12\,(\vek f - \mat P\vek f) \tag{19.4}\]
    satisfy \(\vek f = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\), \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = -\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\). The column \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a symmetric load, \(\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\) an antisymmetric one.
  2. If \(\vek f = \vek g_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\) with \(\mat P\vek g_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek g_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\mat P\vek g_{\mathrm{a}} = -\vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\), then \(\vek g_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\vek g_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\).
Proof

We must show that the split exists (item 1) and that it is the only one (item 2). Existence is checked by computation, uniqueness comes from a trick we shall use several times: apply the matrix \(\mat P\) to the equation and add or subtract.

  1. Sum: \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = \tfrac12(\vek f + \mat P\vek f) + \tfrac12(\vek f - \mat P\vek f) = \vek f\).
  2. Since \(\mat P\) is linear and \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\), \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} = \tfrac12(\mat P\vek f + \mat P^2\vek f) = \tfrac12(\mat P\vek f + \vek f) = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\).
  3. Likewise \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = \tfrac12(\mat P\vek f - \mat P^2\vek f) = \tfrac12(\mat P\vek f - \vek f) = -\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\).
  4. Let \(\vek f = \vek g_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\) be another split. The equation multiplied by \(\mat P\) gives \(\mat P\vek f = \vek g_{\mathrm{s}} - \vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\), because \(\mat P\vek g_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek g_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\mat P\vek g_{\mathrm{a}} = -\vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\).
  5. Adding the equations of step 4 and the original equation gives \(\vek f + \mat P\vek f = 2\vek g_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(\vek g_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\). Subtracting gives \(\vek f - \mat P\vek f = 2\vek g_{\mathrm{a}}\), so \(\vek g_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\).

Source: COOK section 2.11 (PDF p. 70): the load is the "sum of symmetric and antisymmetric parts". BARBER section 5.2.3 (PDF p. 81): the same split for a continuous body. The formulas and the proof are not from the sources.

The lemma says what the split is, but not whether we may use it to compute displacements. The theorem says so, and it asks for the same as section 19.1: the structure is symmetric, that is \(\mat P\mat K\mat P = \mat K\) (theorem 19.6), and the supports are symmetric, so the mirror maps clamped unknowns onto clamped unknowns. Chapter 7 marked the free unknowns with the index \(f\), and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is the stiffness matrix after clamping. Here \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\) denotes the rows and columns of \(\mat P\) that belong to the free unknowns.

Theorem 19.11 Two half models instead of one (dva polovična modela namesto enega)

Let \(\mat K\) be the stiffness matrix, \(\mat P\) a signed permutation (definition 19.5) with \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat P\mat K\mat P = \mat K\) that maps clamped unknowns onto clamped ones. Let \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) be positive definite. Then items 1 to 3 hold, and for a frame with the mirror matrix \(\mat P\) also item 4:

  1. \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is a signed permutation (a permutation matrix with signs, definition 19.5) of the free unknowns, \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).
  2. The systems \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\) with the split (19.4) (for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\)) have one solution each, and \(\vek d = \vek d_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\) is the solution of the system \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\,\vek d = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\).
  3. \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{a}} = -\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\): the displacements of the symmetric load are symmetric, those of the antisymmetric load antisymmetric.
  4. The restriction of \(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\) to the left half and the plane solves the half model with symmetric conditions, that of \(\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\) the one with antisymmetric conditions (corollary 19.8, lemma 19.9) with the left-half load and half the plane load.
Proof

We must show four things. The first comes from splitting the unknowns into free and clamped, the second from linearity and uniqueness, the third from the same trick as in lemma 19.10, the fourth is lemma 19.9 applied to each part separately.

  1. The matrix \(\mat P\) maps clamped unknowns onto clamped ones, so free onto free. In the order free, clamped, \(\mat P = \operatorname{diag}(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}, \mat P_{\mathrm{cc}})\) (\(c\) for clamped, constrained). The block of \(\mat P^2\) is \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}^2 = \mat I\), the block of \(\mat P\mat K\mat P\) is \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).
  2. By lemma 19.10 (for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\)) \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\) exist. The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite, so each system has exactly one solution by theorem 6.29.
  3. Linearity: \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek d_{\mathrm{a}}) = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\). The solution of \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d = \vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\) is unique (step 2), so \(\vek d = \vek d_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\).
  4. Step 1 gives \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}^2\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\,(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}) = \mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).
  5. Hence \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}) = \mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{s}} = \mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) by lemma 19.10. The column \(\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\) solves the same system as \(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\), so by the uniqueness of step 2 it equals \(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\).
  6. Similarly \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}(-\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}) = -\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\), so \(-\mat P_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_{\mathrm{a}} = \vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\).
  7. The load \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) satisfies \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) (lemma 19.10), so by lemma 19.9 (\(+\)) \(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\) solves the half model with \(u = \varphi = 0\) on the plane (corollary 19.8). Also \(\mat P\vek f_{\mathrm{a}} = -\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\), so \(\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\) does with \(v = 0\) (\(-\)).

Source: COOK section 2.11 (PDF p. 70): the solution of the original problem is the "superposition of solutions" of the symmetric and antisymmetric case, one analysis of the whole structure is exchanged for two analyses of half of it. The proof is not from the source.

The theorem says that the split loses nothing: the sum of the solutions of two half models is the exact solution, not an approximation. It asks for two things that the example tests on both sides: that both halves are used, and that the structure is really symmetric.

Example 19.12 Splitting the force \(H\) on the portal frame (razcep sile \(H\) na portalnem okviru)

The model is the frame of figure 19.15: every element has \(EA = 1{,}568 \cdot 10^{8}\) N and \(EI = 20\,907\) N m², the columns have four elements each and the beam eight, four on each half \(BD\) and \(DC\), clamps at \(A\) and \(E\), \(H = 500\) N at \(B\). The mirror \(x \mapsto 1 - x\) maps the node \((x, y)\) to \((1 - x, y)\) and changes the sign of the components \(u\) and \(\varphi\) while \(v\) stays. A NumPy computation confirms \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat P\mat K\mat P = \mat K\).

The split (19.4) gives \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) with forces of \(250\) N at \(B\) to the right and at \(C\) to the left, and \(\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\) with forces of \(250\) N at \(B\) and \(C\) to the right, as the middle and right frame of figure 19.15 show. For the whole frame the displacement of the node \(B\) to the right is \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 1{,}4255\) mm.

  1. Symmetric part. The half model with the conditions \(u = 0\) and \(\varphi = 0\) at \(D\) (corollary 19.8) gives \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 0{,}0008\) mm and at \(D\) an axial force \(N = -249{,}90\) N, a moment of \(0{,}033\) N m and a shear force of zero (middle frame of figure 19.16, on this scale the blue line cannot be told from the grey one). The displacement is so small because the beam holds the nodes: a free column would under \(250\) N move \(250\ \text{N} \cdot (1\ \text{m})^3/(3 \cdot 20\,907\ \text{N m}^2) = 3{,}986\) mm, while the beam as a strut shortens by \(250\ \text{N} \cdot 0{,}5\ \text{m}/(1{,}568 \cdot 10^{8}\ \text{N}) = 0{,}0008\) mm. The force \(250\) N goes through the beam because the axial path is about \(5000\) times stiffer than the bending path (ratio \(3{,}986/0{,}0008 \approx 5000\)). A free-body diagram of the left part (force \(250\) N at \(B\) to the right, force of the right part \(-249{,}90\) N at \(D\) along the horizontal and the reaction at \(A\)) confirms it: \(\sum F_{\mathrm{x}} = 250 - 249{,}90 + R_{\mathrm{Ax}} = 0\) gives the horizontal reaction \(R_{\mathrm{Ax}} = -0{,}10\) N, and the moment about \(A\), \(M_{\mathrm{A}} - 1\ \text{m} \cdot 250\ \text{N} + 1\ \text{m} \cdot 249{,}90\ \text{N} + 0{,}033\ \text{N m} = 0\), gives \(M_{\mathrm{A}} = 0{,}067\) N m. The column carries only \(0{,}10\) N and so it does not bend.
  2. Antisymmetric part. The half model (right frame of figure 19.15) with the condition \(v = 0\) at \(D\) gives \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 1{,}4247\) mm, \(\varphi_{\mathrm{D}} = 0{,}000424\), at \(D\) a moment of zero, an axial force of zero and a shear force \(V = 214{,}19\) N. Free-body diagram of the left part: \(\sum F_{\mathrm{x}} = 250\ \text{N} + R_{\mathrm{Ax}} = 0\), so \(R_{\mathrm{Ax}} = -250\) N. \(\sum F_{\mathrm{y}} = V + R_{\mathrm{Ay}} = 0\), so \(R_{\mathrm{Ay}} = -214{,}19\) N. Moment about \(A\): \(M_{\mathrm{A}} - 1\ \text{m} \cdot 250\ \text{N} + 0{,}5\ \text{m} \cdot 214{,}19\ \text{N} = 0\), so \(M_{\mathrm{A}} = 142{,}91\) N m. The equilibrium of the whole frame confirms it: the clamps carry \(2 \cdot 142{,}91 = 285{,}81\) N m together and the pair of vertical reactions \(\mp 214{,}19\) N on the arm \(1\) m carries \(214{,}19\) N m, the sum is \(500\) N m, which is \(H \cdot 1\ \text{m}\).
  3. Sum (left frame of figure 19.16). At \(B\) it is \(0{,}0008 + 1{,}4247 = 1{,}4255\) mm and at \(C\) it is \(-0{,}0008 + 1{,}4247 = 1{,}4239\) mm, the displacements of the whole frame from the first computation. The symmetric part contributes \(0{,}056\) % of the displacement \(u_{\mathrm{B}}\). A horizontal force on a portal frame with an axially stiff beam is almost purely antisymmetric.

In the figure the grey frame is the undeformed frame and the blue line the frame after loading. For the whole frame and for the antisymmetric part both columns lean to the right and the beam takes the shape of an S, for the symmetric part nothing can be seen. The displacement \(\vek d\) is therefore almost equal to \(\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\).

Now the numbers on one picture, to one scale. First we draw the common scale from zero to \(1{,}4255\) mm. The displacement of the whole frame is the line \(u_{\mathrm{B}}\) up to the end of the scale. The displacement of the symmetric part is so small next to it, only \(0{,}0008\) mm, that we cannot see it at this scale, so we enlarge it a thousand times, to \(0{,}8\) mm, which at the scale of the figure is \(2{,}4\) cm. The displacement of the antisymmetric part is the rest, \(1{,}4247\) mm, which starts where the symmetric part ends and reaches the same line as the whole: \(0{,}0008 + 1{,}4247 = 1{,}4255\) mm, as superposition says in theorem 19.11.

We proved the theorem under the assumption \(\mat P\mat K\mat P = \mat K\), so let us test a case in which it does not hold.

Example 19.13 Splitting with a turned column (razcep ob obrnjenem stebru)

The same frame as in figure 19.15, but the right column turned by a right angle, so that it bends about its weak axis (\(I = 26\,667\) mm\(^4\) instead of \(106\,667\) mm\(^4\), the frame of section 19.1). The shape and supports are still symmetric, the stiffness is not: \(\mat P\mat K\mat P \ne \mat K\) (NumPy). We load the frame with a force of \(500\) N downward on the beam, at a quarter of its length from \(B\). The split (19.4) still holds, since lemma 19.10 does not depend on stiffness: \(\vek f_{\mathrm{s}}\) has \(250\) N downward at both quarter points, \(\vek f_{\mathrm{a}}\) has \(250\) N downward and \(250\) N upward. Also \(\vek d = \vek d_{\mathrm{s}} + \vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\) still holds, since it is only linearity (step 3 of the proof of theorem 19.11).

Steps 4 and 5 of the proof fail: \(\mat K\mat P \ne \mat P\mat K\) and \(\mat P\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\) does not solve the same system as \(\vek d_{\mathrm{s}}\). For the good frame (middle frame of figure 19.16) the symmetric part really is symmetric: \(u_{\mathrm{D}} = 0\), \(\varphi_{\mathrm{D}} = 0\) and the deflection of the quarter point is \(0{,}1106\) mm, as the half model with the conditions \(u = \varphi = 0\) at \(D\) gives. For the turned column \(u_{\mathrm{D}} = 0{,}1601\) mm, the nodes \(B\) and \(C\) rotate through different angles, \(\varphi_{\mathrm{B}} = -0{,}00056\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}00062\), and the frame as a whole moves sideways, \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 0{,}1602\) mm and \(u_{\mathrm{C}} = 0{,}1600\) mm. With equal columns the moment of the beam would move the top of the left column in one direction and the top of the right column in the opposite direction, but the beam is axially stiff and holds them at the same distance, so the displacements cancel. With columns whose bending stiffnesses differ by a factor of four they do not cancel. The deflection of the quarter point is \(0{,}1480\) mm, while the half model still gives \(0{,}1106\) mm and is wrong by \(25\) %. The half model with symmetric conditions is wrong for such a frame although the load is symmetric.

What if the structure has two mirror planes, for instance a rectangle with the axes \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\)? The mirror matrices \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\) are then signed permutations that commute. We apply lemma 19.10 twice, first for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\) and then to both parts for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\). We get four parts, each with a sign with respect to each plane. The corollary that follows proves only this split of the load. That each part is solved exactly by a quarter of the structure we do not prove here. The way to it is lemma 19.9 applied twice, first for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\) and then on the half model for \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\). The elements and the load on the second plane are halved again, and the load at the node where the planes meet is reduced to a quarter.

Corollary 19.14 Split of a load with respect to two planes (razcep bremena glede na dve ravnini)

Let \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\) be mirror matrices with \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}^2 = \mat P_{\mathrm{y}}^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\mat P_{\mathrm{y}} = \mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\). For \(\sigma, \tau \in \{1, -1\}\) put

\[\vek f_{\sigma\tau} = \tfrac14\,(\mat I + \sigma\mat P_{\mathrm{x}})(\mat I + \tau\mat P_{\mathrm{y}})\,\vek f . \tag{19.5}\]

Then \(\vek f = \sum_{\sigma,\tau}\vek f_{\sigma\tau}\), \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\vek f_{\sigma\tau} = \sigma\vek f_{\sigma\tau}\) and \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\vek f_{\sigma\tau} = \tau\vek f_{\sigma\tau}\).

Proof

We must show the sum and the sign for both matrices. The sum comes from adding over \(\sigma\) and over \(\tau\) separately, the sign from \(\sigma^2 = 1\).

  1. Adding over \(\sigma = \pm 1\) gives \(\sum_\sigma(\mat I + \sigma\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}) = 2\mat I\), and likewise over \(\tau\). So \(\sum_{\sigma,\tau}\vek f_{\sigma\tau} = \tfrac14\cdot 2\mat I \cdot 2\mat I\,\vek f = \vek f\).
  2. Since \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}^2 = \mat I\) and \(\sigma^2 = 1\), \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}(\mat I + \sigma\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}) = \mat P_{\mathrm{x}} + \sigma\mat I = \sigma(\mat I + \sigma\mat P_{\mathrm{x}})\).
  3. The matrix \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\) commutes with \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\), so \(\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}\vek f_{\sigma\tau} = \tfrac14\,\mat P_{\mathrm{x}}(\mat I + \sigma\mat P_{\mathrm{x}})(\mat I + \tau\mat P_{\mathrm{y}})\vek f = \sigma\vek f_{\sigma\tau}\) by step 2. For \(\mat P_{\mathrm{y}}\) the same holds with the roles exchanged.

Source: COOK section 2.11 (PDF p. 69): a structure with the mirror planes \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) (figure 2.11-1a), the force on the plane is halved, for equal forces one eighth is enough. The split into four parts is not from the source. The proof is not from the source, a NumPy computation confirms it on a random column.

If the two forces in COOK's figure 2.11-1a are equal (the book calls them \(P_{\mathrm{x}} = P_{\mathrm{y}}\)), the planes \(x = y\) and \(x = -y\) are also mirror planes and one eighth of the structure is enough (COOK, PDF p. 69). The force on a plane is halved in the half model, as for the bar on the mirror plane of the bridge in lemma 7.39. We have proved nothing new here, so we do not state the eighth as a theorem.

So far we always used the mirror. For a frame that has no mirror symmetry but is mapped onto itself by a rotation through half a turn, the mirror does not help, but the split still does. COOK calls this "skew" or "inversion" symmetry (PDF p. 70). We call it half-turn symmetry, because the name says what happens: we turn the structure through half a turn about an axis through the centre perpendicular to the plane, and it is the same as before. Two successive reflections, in the plane \(x = 0\) and in the plane \(y = 0\), give the same result as this rotation (COOK, PDF p. 70). Take the steel Z frame of \(20 \times 40\) mm shown in the first frame of figure 19.18: columns \(AB\) and \(CE\) of length \(1\) m and a slanted beam \(BC\) through the centre \(O\), clamped at \(A\) and \(E\). Two arcs around \(O\) show the half-turn that maps \(A\) onto \(E\) and \(B\) onto \(C\). The mirror \(x \mapsto 1 - x\) does not map the frame onto itself, \(\mat P\mat K\mat P \ne \mat K\) (NumPy), while the half-turn does.

In a half-turn (figure 19.18) the components \(u\) and \(v\) reverse, as every vector in the plane does, and the rotation \(\varphi\) does not change, because turning the body is only a motion together with its picture. The new matrix \(\mat R\) is thus a signed permutation that exchanges the node \((x, y)\) with \((2x_{\mathrm{O}} - x, 2y_{\mathrm{O}} - y)\), multiplies the components \(u\) and \(v\) by \(-1\) and \(\varphi\) by \(+1\). Since a half-turn done twice is the identity, \(\mat R^2 = \mat I\). Let \(\mat R\mat K\mat R = \mat K\) (the half-turn maps every element onto an element with the same \(EA\), \(EI\) and length, as the mirror does in section 19.1).

Corollary 19.15 Half-turn symmetry (simetrija pri polovičnem zasuku)

Let \(\mat R\) be the signed permutation of the half-turn about the centre \(O\) with \(\mat R^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat R\mat K\mat R = \mat K\), let it map clamped unknowns onto clamped ones and let \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) be positive definite. Then for every load \(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}}\):

  1. \(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} = \vek f_+ + \vek f_-\) with \(\vek f_\pm = \tfrac12(\vek f_{\mathrm{f}} \pm \mat R\vek f_{\mathrm{f}})\), \(\mat R\vek f_\pm = \pm\vek f_\pm\), and this split is unique.
  2. The solutions \(\vek d_\pm\) of the systems \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\vek d_\pm = \vek f_\pm\) satisfy \(\mat R\vek d_\pm = \pm\vek d_\pm\) and \(\vek d = \vek d_+ + \vek d_-\).
  3. At the centre \(O\) (if it is a node), \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = v_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\) for \(\vek d_+\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\) for \(\vek d_-\).
  4. If the frame splits into two halves meeting only at \(O\) and swapped by the half-turn, the restriction of the column \(\vek d_\pm\) solves one half with the conditions of item 3 and half the load at \(O\).
Proof

We must show that the proof of theorem 19.11 does not change when \(\mat P\) is replaced by \(\mat R\), that item 3 follows from the signs of the matrix \(\mat R\), and that the proof of lemma 19.9 gives item 4 when \(\mat P\) is replaced by \(\mat R\).

  1. In the proof of theorem 19.11 we needed only four properties of \(\mat P\): a signed permutation, \(\mat P^2 = \mat I\), \(\mat P\mat K\mat P = \mat K\) and mapping clamped onto clamped. \(\mat R\) has all four, so items 1 and 2 hold.
  2. The node \(O\) stays in place under the rotation, so \(\mat R\) acts on its unknowns only by signs: \(u_{\mathrm{O}} \mapsto -u_{\mathrm{O}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{O}} \mapsto -v_{\mathrm{O}}\), \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} \mapsto \varphi_{\mathrm{O}}\).
  3. For \(\mat R\vek d_+ = \vek d_+\) we have \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = -u_{\mathrm{O}}\) and \(v_{\mathrm{O}} = -v_{\mathrm{O}}\), so \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = v_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\). For \(\mat R\vek d_- = -\vek d_-\) we have \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = -\varphi_{\mathrm{O}}\), so \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\).
  4. Item 4 is lemma 19.9 with \(\mat R\) in place of \(\mat P\) and the node \(O\) in place of the plane. Its proof needs only the relation between the matrices of a pair of elements.
  5. The half-turn maps the element \(e_1\) with nodes \(i\), \(j\) to the element \(e_2\) with the nodes that \(i\) and \(j\) go to, with the same length, \(EA\) and \(EI\) and the opposite direction. In the matrix (12.11) \(c\) and \(s\) therefore change sign.
  6. The matrix (12.13) is therefore multiplied from the right by \(\mat T = \operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1, -1, -1, 1)\), and \(\mat k_2 = \mat T\mat k_1\mat T\).
  7. From \(\vek d_\pm = \pm\mat R\vek d_\pm\) follows \(\vek d_2 = \pm\mat T\vek d_1\), so \(\mat k_2\vek d_2 = \pm\mat T\mat k_1\vek d_1\): the force of \(e_2\) on \(O\) is \(\pm\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1)\) times the force of \(e_1\).
  8. The free unknowns at \(O\) are \(\varphi\) for \(+\) and \(u\) and \(v\) for \(-\), exactly those that \(\pm\operatorname{diag}(-1, -1, 1)\) multiplies by \(+1\). In step 2 of the lemma proof therefore again \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{R}} = \Sigma_{\mathrm{L}}\) and \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{L}} = \tfrac12 f_{\mathrm{Oc}}\).
  9. In step 5 \(e_2\) contributes the same energy as \(e_1\): \(\vek d_2^\T\mat k_2\vek d_2 = \vek d_1^\T\mat T\mat T\mat k_1\mat T\mat T\vek d_1 = \vek d_1^\T\mat k_1\vek d_1\). The other steps of the lemma stay.

Source: COOK section 2.11 (PDF p. 70-71): a half-turn (or two successive reflections) maps the structure onto itself, and with reversed loads, onto the other type. Half of the structure is enough, the conditions are in reference [2.22] of the book. The proof is not from the source.

Example 19.16 The force \(H\) on the Z frame (sila \(H\) na Z-okviru)

The Z frame has columns \(AB\) and \(CE\) of length \(1\) m and a beam \(BC\) of length \(\sqrt2\) m (\(4\) elements per member), clamped at \(A\) and \(E\), force \(H = 500\) N at \(B\) to the right. \(\mat R^2 = \mat I\) and \(\mat R\mat K\mat R = \mat K\) (NumPy). The split gives \(\vek f_+\) with forces of \(250\) N at \(B\) to the right and at \(C\) to the left (middle frame of figure 19.18) and \(\vek f_-\) with forces of \(250\) N at \(B\) and \(C\) to the right (right frame).

  1. The displacement \(u_{\mathrm{B}}\) (figure 19.18) of the whole frame is \(2{,}2386\) mm, that of the symmetric part \(\vek d_+\) only \(0{,}0038\) mm and that of the antisymmetric part \(\vek d_-\) is \(2{,}2348\) mm. The sum \(0{,}0038 + 2{,}2348 = 2{,}2386\) mm. The symmetric part presses the beam from \(B\) and \(C\), so it is carried by the axial stiffness of the beam, as in example 19.12.
  2. At the centre (figure 19.18), for \(\vek d_+\) \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = v_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = -5{,}4 \cdot 10^{-6}\), and for \(\vek d_-\) \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\) and \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = 1{,}6156\) mm, \(v_{\mathrm{O}} = -0{,}6191\) mm (item 3 of the corollary).
  3. The two half models are the part \(ABO\) with a condition at \(O\) (item 4 of the corollary, bottom frames of figure 19.18): at \(O\) in the first \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = v_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\), in the second \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = 0\). The first gives \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 0{,}0038\) mm and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{O}} = -5{,}4 \cdot 10^{-6}\), the second \(u_{\mathrm{B}} = 2{,}2348\) mm and \(u_{\mathrm{O}} = 1{,}6156\) mm. Both agree with the whole frame.

The mirror (figure 19.18) would not help here: \(\mat P\mat K\mat P \ne \mat K\), because the Z frame has no mirror symmetry. Without the half-turn we would have to solve the whole frame (13 nodes, \(33\) unknowns after clamping), with it two parts with \(7\) nodes.

The section has three results. We split a load \(\vek f\) into a symmetric and an antisymmetric part (lemma 19.10) and solve each on a half (theorem 19.11), and with two mirror planes we split it into four parts (corollary 19.14). The same split works for half-turn symmetry (corollary 19.15). The example with the turned column showed what happens when the stiffness is not symmetric: the split stays exact, but the half models do not. The split works as long as the structure is symmetric. In vibration and buckling a new difficulty appears, because we must solve both half models, not only one. This is the subject of the next section 19.4.
