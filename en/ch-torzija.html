The whole chapter on one page (loads more slowly)

We start with a bar that we turn about its axis, like a screw with a screwdriver. In chapter 5 we named such a moment as an internal quantity of the space frame, in chapter 12 we built it into the frame element, and in chapter 13 into the model of the shaft. Everywhere it was a formula taken from a source and not proved. Now we derive it from the equations of elasticity of chapter 11.

The word torsion (torzija) means twisting. When a moment turns a bar about its axis, the bar does not turn as a whole. Each cross section turns about the axis by a slightly different angle than its neighbour, so the bar is twisted, as a wrung cloth is. The name is therefore an honest picture of what happens: not the rotation of a body, but the twisting of its parts against one another.

Why does it matter? A shaft in a gearbox carries moment from the motor to the wheel. It must withstand the stress the moment causes and must not turn too far. A frame made of profiles is often loaded so that it is twisted, and then the shape of the section decides its stiffness. A tube twists much less than a tube of the same sheet with a slit along its wall, although both hold the same amount of metal.

The chapter promises four things. First, for the circle we will compute the twist and the stresses exactly, because there every step can be checked. Second, for any section we will find how the points of the section move along the axis when it is twisted, and write the stresses with a single function on the section. This also solves the rectangle that chapter 12 needs. Third, we will show why thin open sections and hollow sections behave so differently from solid ones, and through which point a transverse force must pass so that the section does not turn under it. Fourth, we will estimate how close an approximate calculation is to the true one, also with the finite element calculation of chapter 15. What we cannot prove from the book we will state with a source and say that it is stated.

First the circle, because it is the simplest.
