Chapter 29 Structural optimization

29.5 Minimum compliance (najmanjša podajnost)

We have \(300\ \text{cm}^3\) of steel and three places to put it. How much do we give to each place so that the structure under a given load is as stiff as possible? Take a wall and on it three points \(S_1\), \(S_2\) and \(S_3\) in a horizontal row, \(1\) m apart (supports). The letter \(S\) is the initial of the Slovenian word for support (podpora, in English support), since each of the points has that job. One metre below the middle point is the joint \(C\), free to move (joint \(C\)). We connect each point \(S_{\mathrm{e}}\) to the point \(C\) with a steel bar \(e\) (bars), \(e = 1, 2, 3\), and pull at \(C\) with the force \(F = 10\) kN (force), which makes the angle \(\theta\) with the downward direction (angle, figure 29.17). The bars need not all be equally thick. The thickness of a line is proportional to the cross-section of the bar, and a dashed thin line is a bar with cross-section zero, which is only still written down in the truss. Three directions of the force are drawn, \(\theta = 0^\circ\), \(30^\circ\) and \(60^\circ\), each with the best cross-sections that we shall find.

Such a truss is the model of section 7.5.2: the bars are joined with pins (definition 4.4), forces act only at the joints, displacements are small and the material is linearly elastic with \(E = 196\) GPa (definition 3.1). The joints and their connections were chosen in advance. Such a net is called the ground structure (izhodiščno paličje), because we start from it and because optimization takes the whole cross-section from some bars. The unknowns are the cross-sections \(A_1\), \(A_2\), \(A_3\), which we write in the column \(\vek A\), these are the design variables (projektne spremenljivke) of definition 29.1. Each bar \(e\) has a length \(L_{\mathrm{e}}\), and both numbers \(L_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) determine the volume of material \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\). The weight of the bars is negligible, as definition 4.4 requires: \(300\ \text{cm}^3\) of steel has the mass \(2{,}34\) kg and the weight \(22{,}96\) N, which is \(0{,}23\ \%\) of the force \(F\). We therefore do not count the weight among the loads, and the load does not depend on the design.

Before we ask which design is best, we must say what it means to be stiff. For one bar the answer is known: under the force \(F\) it extends by \(F L/(EA)\), and the smaller this displacement, the stiffer the bar. In a truss with several joints every joint has its own displacement, and we must choose one number.

29.5.1 Compliance of a truss (podajnost paličja)

First let us write how the cross-sections enter the stiffness. We collect the displacements of the free joints, in our case two numbers, the horizontal and the vertical displacement of the joint \(C\), in the column \(\vek u\), and the forces in their directions in the column \(\vek F\). The structure matrix \(\mat K\) in the free unknowns (theorem 7.18) is assembled from the bar matrices (theorem 7.13). The bar matrix in global axes (lemma 7.28) is multiplied by the cross-section \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\), and so the cross-section steps out of the assembly.

Lemma 29.31 Stiffness is linear in the areas (togost je linearna v presekih)

Let a truss have \(n\) bars with lengths \(L_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\) and modulus \(E\), and supports that prescribe zero displacement of some joints. Let \(\mat K(\vek A)\) be the structure matrix in the free unknowns (theorem 7.18) at cross-sections \(A_1, \dots, A_{\mathrm{n}} \ge 0\). Let \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the unit vector of bar \(e\) from the end \(i\) towards the end \(j\) and

\[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek v) = \frac{\vek n_{\mathrm{e}} \cdot (\vek v_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek v_{\mathrm{i}})}{L_{\mathrm{e}}} \tag{29.24}\]

the extension of the bar per length at displacements \(\vek v\) of the free unknowns, where the displacement of a supported end is zero. Then

\[\mat K(\vek A) = \sum_{\mathrm{e}=1}^{n} A_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{e}} , \qquad \vek v^\T \mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek v = E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek v)^2 , \tag{29.25}\]

where \(\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\) are symmetric matrices that do not depend on the cross-sections. The matrix \(\mat K(\vek A)\) is symmetric and \(\vek v^\T \mat K(\vek A)\,\vek v \ge 0\) for every \(\vek v\).

Proof

We must show that the bar matrix is linear in \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\), that the linearity carries over to the sum, and that the quadratic form of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\) gives the extension.

  1. By lemma 7.28, \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\mathrm{e}}\,\frac{E}{L_{\mathrm{e}}}\begin{bmatrix} \vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T & -\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T \\ -\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T & \vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}^\T \end{bmatrix} = A_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}^{0}\), where the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}^{0}\) does not contain the cross-section.
  2. By theorem 7.13, \(\mat K = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}^{0}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\). We denote \(\mat K_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\,\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}^{0}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) and obtain the first equation (29.25).
  3. By lemma 7.28, \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,\frac{EA_{\mathrm{e}}}{L_{\mathrm{e}}}\,e^2\) with the elongation \(e = \vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot(\vek u_{\mathrm{j}} - \vek u_{\mathrm{i}})\). At \(A_{\mathrm{e}} = 1\) we therefore have \(\vek v^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek v = \frac{E}{L_{\mathrm{e}}}\,e^2 = E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,(e/L_{\mathrm{e}})^2 = E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek v)^2\), where we used \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\vek v\) (point 2 of theorem 7.13).
  4. The symmetry and nonnegativity of the matrix \(\mat K(\vek A)\) are consequences of 7.14, since \(\vek v^\T \mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek v \ge 0\) by step 3 and \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\).

Source: The lemma is from ch7 and from CHK (5.7), (5.11), (5.12) (PDF p. 86-87), where \(\mat K = \sum_{\mathrm{j}} x_{\mathrm{j}}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{j}}^0\). The proof is ours, from chapter 7.

Here we also use that \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek v)\) is the extension of the bar per unit length, so the strain of bar \(e\) at displacements \(\vek v\), and that \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) is twice the strain energy density of the bar, since by lemma 4.5 the stress is \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}} = E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}\) and the energy per volume of the bar is \(\tfrac12\,\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\). This quantity will be at the centre of the section.

Definition 29.32 Compliance of a truss (podajnost paličja)

Let \(\mat K(\vek A)\) be the structure matrix of lemma 29.31 at cross-sections \(\vek A \ge 0\) and \(\vek F \ne \vek 0\) the column of loads in the directions of the free unknowns. If the system \(\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek u = \vek F\) has a solution, the compliance (podajnost) of the truss is the number

\[C(\vek A) = \vek F^\T\vek u , \tag{29.26}\]

where \(\vek u\) is any solution. If the system has no solution, that is if the load cannot be in equilibrium, we set \(C(\vek A) = +\infty\). The compliance is a number in joules, a function of the cross-sections \(\vek A\).

Source: CHK §5.1 (PDF p. 84): "the compliance \(C\) of the truss, i.e. \(F^T u\)". The comparison with \(C = +\infty\) and the independence of the solution are ours.

The name is from before: the compliance matrix (definition 4.138) gives displacements from forces, and for a positive definite matrix \(\mat K\) the number \(C = \vek F^\T\mat K^{-1}\vek F\) is its quadratic form, since \(\vek u = \mat K^{-1}\vek F\). For one bar \(C = F\cdot FL/(EA) = F^2/k\) with the stiffness \(k = EA/L\): the more a structure gives way under the force, the more compliant it is, and the smaller the compliance, the stiffer it is. In chapter 10 the letter \(C\) means the damping matrix, here it always means the number \(C(\vek A)\).

Why this number and not, for example, the horizontal displacement of the joint? The first reason is energy. By the lemma below, \(C\) is twice the strain energy, so what the structure stores. The second reason is that \(C\) is a convex function of the cross-sections (theorem 29.34), while the displacement of a joint in one direction is not (below with numbers), and the convexity from section 29.3 is what gives the optimum its reliability. The third reason is that in the best truss all bars with material have the same stress (corollary 29.38), which recalls the sizing by stresses of section 29.2.

Lemma 29.33 Compliance and energy (podajnost in energija)

Let \(\mat K = \mat K(\vek A)\) be the matrix of lemma 29.31 and \(\vek u\) a solution of the system \(\mat K\vek u = \vek F\). Then:

  1. the number \(\vek F^\T\vek u\) is the same for all solutions of the system,
  2. \(C = \vek F^\T\vek u = \vek u^\T\mat K\vek u = 2U\), where \(U = \tfrac12\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u\) is the strain energy of the truss,
  3. the total potential energy \(\Pi(\vek v) = \tfrac12\,\vek v^\T\mat K\vek v - \vek F^\T\vek v\) (definition 4.89) at \(\vek v = \vek u\) equals \(-\tfrac12\,C\) and \(\Pi(\vek v) \ge \Pi(\vek u)\) for every \(\vek v\),
  4. for every \(\vek v\)
    \[2\,\vek F^\T\vek v - \vek v^\T\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek v = C(\vek A) - (\vek v - \vek u)^\T\mat K(\vek A)\,(\vek v - \vek u) \le C(\vek A) , \tag{29.27}\]
    with equality at \(\vek v = \vek u\). So \(C(\vek A) = \max_{\vek{v}}\big[2\,\vek F^\T\vek v - \vek v^\T\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek v\big]\).
Proof

We must show four points, all from the symmetry of the matrix \(\mat K\) and from \(\vek w^\T\mat K\vek w \ge 0\) (lemma 29.31).

  1. Let \(\vek u\) and \(\vek u'\) be solutions. Then \(\mat K(\vek u' - \vek u) = \vek 0\), so \(\vek F^\T(\vek u' - \vek u) = (\mat K\vek u)^\T(\vek u' - \vek u) = \vek u^\T\mat K^\T(\vek u' - \vek u) = \vek u^\T\mat K(\vek u' - \vek u) = 0\), because \(\mat K\) is symmetric.
  2. From \(\vek F = \mat K\vek u\) we get \(\vek F^\T\vek u = \vek u^\T\mat K^\T\vek u = \vek u^\T\mat K\vek u\). By point 2 of theorem 7.13, \(\tfrac12\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u\) is the sum of the energies of the bars, that is \(U\).
  3. The value at \(\vek u\) is \(\Pi(\vek u) = \tfrac12\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u - \vek F^\T\vek u = \tfrac12\,C - C = -\tfrac12\,C\). From \(\vek F = \mat K\vek u\) it follows that \(\Pi(\vek v) - \Pi(\vek u) = \tfrac12\,\vek v^\T\mat K\vek v - \vek u^\T\mat K\vek v + \tfrac12\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u = \tfrac12\,(\vek v - \vek u)^\T\mat K(\vek v - \vek u) \ge 0\).
  4. With \(\vek F = \mat K\vek u\) we have \(2\,\vek F^\T\vek v - \vek v^\T\mat K\vek v = 2\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek v - \vek v^\T\mat K\vek v\). We add and subtract \(\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u = C\) and obtain \(C - (\vek v - \vek u)^\T\mat K(\vek v - \vek u)\), which by \(\vek w^\T\mat K\vek w \ge 0\) is at most \(C\).

Source: CHK (5.16)-(5.18) (PDF p. 88) for the energy, CHK §5.1 (PDF p. 97) for \(C = \vek F^\T\vek u = \vek u^\T\mat K\vek u\). Point 3 is the same principle as theorem 4.91 and corollary 4.93. The proof is ours.

What did we get? The compliance is twice the energy that the load \(\vek F\) stores in the truss, and also minus twice the smallest total potential energy. This is Clapeyron's theorem (theorem 4.86) for a truss with numbers: the work of a force that grows from zero is half of \(\vek F^\T\vek u\). Point 4 is new and we shall use it twice. It says that \(C\) is the largest value of the family of functions \(2\,\vek F^\T\vek v - \vek v^\T\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek v\), where every displacement \(\vek v\) gives one function.

Let us look at the family for one bar, the middle bar of our net, which has \(L = 1\) m and carries \(F = 10\) kN (axes, figure 29.18). The compliance is \(C(A) = F^2 L/(EA) = 510{,}2/A\) in joules, if \(A\) is in mm² (blue curve). The displacement \(v\) gives the function \(2Fv - EAv^2/L\), which at a chosen \(v\) is a straight line in \(A\). At \(A_0 = 100\ \text{mm}^2\) we have \(v = FL/(EA_0) = 0{,}5102\) mm and the line \(10{,}204 - 0{,}05102\,A\) in joules (first line), at \(300\ \text{mm}^2\) we have \(v = 0{,}1701\) mm and the line \(3{,}401 - 0{,}005669\,A\) (second), at \(600\ \text{mm}^2\) we have \(v = 0{,}0850\) mm and the line \(1{,}701 - 0{,}001417\,A\) (third). Each line touches the curve at its \(A_0\) (dots) and lies below it elsewhere. The curve is the upper envelope of the lines, and the upper envelope of lines cannot bend downwards. This is the idea of the next theorem.

Theorem 29.34 Convexity of the compliance (konveksnost podajnosti)

Let a truss be as in lemma 29.31 and \(\mathcal P = \{\vek A \in \mathbb R^n : \mat K(\vek A)\ \text{is positive definite}\}\) (definition 6.27). On \(\mathcal P\) we have \(\mat K(\vek A) = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\) also for cross-sections with negative components, and there the compliance is given by equation (29.26) with the same formula. Lemma 29.33 holds for them too, because its proof uses only the symmetry of the matrix \(\mat K\) and \(\vek w^\T\mat K\vek w \ge 0\). Then \(\mathcal P\) is a convex set and \(C\) is a convex function on \(\mathcal P\) (definitions 29.17 and 29.16), that is

\[C\big(t\vek A + (1 - t)\vek B\big) \le t\,C(\vek A) + (1 - t)\,C(\vek B) \qquad (\vek A, \vek B \in \mathcal P,\ 0 \le t \le 1) . \tag{29.28}\]
Idea

By point 4 of lemma 29.33, the compliance is the largest value of functions that are linear in the cross-sections. The largest value of linear functions is convex, as the upper envelope of lines in the figure.

Proof

We must show that \(\mathcal P\) is convex and that (29.28) holds for one function of the family of lemma 29.33.

  1. Let \(\vek A, \vek B \in \mathcal P\) and \(0 \le t \le 1\), \(\vek M = t\vek A + (1 - t)\vek B\). By lemma 29.31, \(\mat K(\vek M) = t\,\mat K(\vek A) + (1 - t)\,\mat K(\vek B)\).
  2. For \(\vek x \ne \vek 0\) we have \(\vek x^\T\mat K(\vek M)\,\vek x = t\,\vek x^\T\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek x + (1 - t)\,\vek x^\T\mat K(\vek B)\,\vek x > 0\), because both terms are positive or zero and at least one is positive (at least one of \(t\) and \(1 - t\) differs from zero). So \(\vek M \in \mathcal P\), and \(\mathcal P\) is convex.
  3. For \(\vek A \in \mathcal P\) the system \(\mat K(\vek A)\vek u = \vek F\) has a solution (theorem 6.29), so the compliance is a number. Let \(\vek w\) be the solution at \(\vek M\), that is \(\vek w = \vek u(\vek M)\).
  4. The function \(\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek X) = 2\,\vek F^\T\vek w - \vek w^\T\mat K(\vek X)\,\vek w\) at fixed \(\vek w\) is linear in \(\vek X\) with a constant term, because \(\mat K\) is linear by lemma 29.31. Therefore \(\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek M) = t\,\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek A) + (1 - t)\,\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek B)\).
  5. By point 4 of lemma 29.33, \(C(\vek M) = \varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek M)\) (equality at \(\vek v = \vek w\)) and \(\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek A) \le C(\vek A)\), \(\varphi_{\vek{w}}(\vek B) \le C(\vek B)\) (inequality at other cross-sections). Together with step 4, \(C(\vek M) \le t\,C(\vek A) + (1 - t)\,C(\vek B)\).

Source: CHK §5.2.1 (PDF p. 92-93) proves the theorem with the second derivative: it shows that the Hessian \(\nabla^2 C\) is positive semidefinite and uses theorem 3.2 (i). Our proof from lemma 29.33 is not from the source.

The theorem holds on \(\mathcal P\), so at cross-sections with a nonsingular, positive definite matrix \(\mat K\). What happens outside? Take \(\theta = 30^\circ\) and the cross-section \(1\) mm² in both outer bars next to the middle one with \(297{,}17\ \text{mm}^2\), which gives the same volume as above. The calculation gives \(C = 181{,}67\) J. At \(0{,}01\ \text{mm}^2\) of the outer bars it is \(18\,040\) J, and at \(10^{-4}\ \text{mm}^2\) it is \(1{,}80 \cdot 10^6\) J, always a hundred times more at a hundred times smaller cross-section. When the outer bars vanish, the middle one remains, and it cannot carry the horizontal force \(F\sin 30^\circ = 5\) kN. The matrix \(\mat K = \frac{EA_2}{L_2}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) is singular and \(\vek F\) is not in its column space, so the system has no solution and \(C = +\infty\) by definition 29.32. The theorem therefore speaks only about \(\mathcal P\). In the theorem on the optimum we shall prove the sufficient condition without inverting the matrix \(\mat K\), so that it also holds for a singular one.

The second reason for \(C\) was that the displacement of a joint is not convex. The horizontal displacement \(u_{\mathrm{x}}\) of the joint \(C\) at \(\theta = 30^\circ\) is \(-1{,}6404\) mm at the cross-sections \((512{,}9;\ 7{,}7;\ 2{,}0)\ \text{mm}^2\) and \(0{,}1258\) mm at the cross-sections \((135{,}3;\ 950{,}1;\ 674{,}5)\ \text{mm}^2\). At the average of the cross-sections it is \(0{,}1103\) mm, but the average of the displacements is \(-0{,}7573\) mm. Convexity ((29.28)) would require \(0{,}1103 \le -0{,}7573\), which does not hold. CHK §5.1 (PDF p. 84) states the same for \(\vek u^\T\vek u\). For the compliance we find no violation: with \(20\,000\) random pairs of cross-sections between \(1\) and \(1001\ \text{mm}^2\) there are \(0\) violations, which is a test, not a proof.

The compliance alone does not tell designs apart, because it decreases with larger cross-sections, so we compare designs at the same volume. How the compliance changes when all cross-sections are multiplied by the same number is told by this lemma.

Lemma 29.35 Scaling of the compliance (skaliranje podajnosti)

Let \(\vek A \ge \vek 0\) be a design with finite compliance \(C(\vek A)\) and \(\alpha > 0\). Then \(C(\alpha\vek A) = C(\vek A)/\alpha\) and \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\alpha A_{\mathrm{e}} = \alpha\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\). If \(\vek F \ne \vek 0\), then \(C(\vek A) > 0\).

Proof

We must show that the solution changes with \(1/\alpha\) and that the compliance is positive.

  1. By lemma 29.31, \(\mat K(\alpha\vek A) = \alpha\,\mat K(\vek A)\). If \(\mat K(\vek A)\vek u = \vek F\), then \(\mat K(\alpha\vek A)\,(\vek u/\alpha) = \vek F\) and \(C(\alpha\vek A) = \vek F^\T\vek u/\alpha = C(\vek A)/\alpha\).
  2. The volume is linear in the cross-sections, \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\alpha A_{\mathrm{e}} = \alpha\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  3. By point 2 of lemma 29.33, \(C = \vek u^\T\mat K\vek u \ge 0\). If \(C = 0\), then for every \(\vek w\) and number \(t\) we would have \(0 \le (\vek u + t\vek w)^\T\mat K(\vek u + t\vek w) = 2t\,\vek w^\T\mat K\vek u + t^2\,\vek w^\T\mat K\vek w\), which for small \(t\) of both signs is possible only if \(\vek w^\T\mat K\vek u = 0\). So \(\vek w^\T\vek F = 0\) for every \(\vek w\) and \(\vek F = \vek 0\), which contradicts the assumption.

Source: CHK lemma 5.1 (PDF p. 96). The proof is ours.

At doubled cross-sections the compliance is halved: twice the steel is twice as stiff, and a comparison makes sense only at equal volume.

29.5.2 The condition for the best design (pogoj najboljšega projekta)

The task is therefore: find the cross-sections \(\vek A\) that at volume \(V_0 = 300\ \text{cm}^3\) minimize \(C(\vek A)\). We write it as

\[\min_{\vek{A}}\ C(\vek A)\quad \text{subject to}\quad \sum_{\mathrm{e}=1}^{n} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}} \le V_0 ,\quad A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\ (e = 1, \dots, n) , \tag{29.29}\]

with the objective function \(C\), the design variables \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) and the constraints of definition 29.1. The feasible set (definition 29.5) is closed and bounded. The sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}} \le V_0\) is the volume of material, which is not larger than the available one.

We need the derivative of the function \(C\) with respect to the cross-sections to answer when a design is best. We shall compute it from lemma 29.33 and not by differentiating the solution of the system.

Lemma 29.36 Derivative of the compliance with respect to the cross-sections (odvod podajnosti po presekih)

Let \(\vek A \in \mathcal P\) and \(\vek u\) a solution of the system \(\mat K(\vek A)\vek u = \vek F\) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek u)\) (equation (29.24)). Then \(\mathcal P\) is an open set, the function \(C\) has continuous partial derivatives on \(\mathcal P\) and

\[\frac{\partial C}{\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}} = -\,\vek u^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek u = -\,E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le 0 \qquad (e = 1, \dots, n) . \tag{29.30}\]
Proof

We must show that \(\vek A + t\vek\Delta\) is in \(\mathcal P\) for small \(t\), and then bound the quotient \(\big(C(\vek A + t\vek\Delta) - C(\vek A)\big)/t\) from both sides. The direction is \(\vek\Delta\), and we choose \(\vek\Delta = \vek e_{\mathrm{e}}\), the unit column in the \(e\)-th direction.

  1. The function \(\vek x \mapsto \vek x^\T\mat K(\vek A)\vek x\) is continuous and positive on the unit sphere \(\{\vek x : |\vek x| = 1\}\), a closed and bounded set, so by theorem 8.26 it has a smallest value \(m > 0\). On the same sphere \(|\vek x^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek x| \le M\) for some number \(M\). For \(|t|\,M < m\) we have \(\vek x^\T\mat K(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}})\vek x \ge m - |t|M > 0\) for every \(\vek x\) with \(|\vek x| = 1\). Every \(\vek x \ne \vek 0\) is \(|\vek x|\) times such a vector, and the quadratic form is multiplied by \(|\vek x|^2 > 0\), so it is positive for every \(\vek x \ne \vek 0\) and \(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}} \in \mathcal P\). So \(\mathcal P\) is open.
  2. Let \(\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\) be the solution of the system at \(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}\). By Cramer's rule the components of \(\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\) are quotients of polynomials in \(t\) with the denominator \(\det\mat K(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}) \ne 0\), so \(\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\) is continuous in \(t\) and \(\vek w_{\mathrm{t}} \to \vek u\) as \(t \to 0\).
  3. By point 4 of lemma 29.33, applied to the design \(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(\vek v = \vek u\), we have \(C(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}) \ge 2\vek F^\T\vek u - \vek u^\T\mat K(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}})\vek u = C(\vek A) - t\,\vek u^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek u\).
  4. The same point, applied to the design \(\vek A\) with \(\vek v = \vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\), gives \(C(\vek A) \ge 2\vek F^\T\vek w_{\mathrm{t}} - \vek w_{\mathrm{t}}^\T\mat K(\vek A)\vek w_{\mathrm{t}} = C(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}) + t\,\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\).
  5. From points 3 and 4 it follows that \(-\vek u^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek u \le \dfrac{C(\vek A + t\vek e_{\mathrm{e}}) - C(\vek A)}{t} \le -\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek w_{\mathrm{t}}\) for \(t > 0\), and for \(t < 0\) we reverse the inequalities. By step 2 both bounds equal \(-\vek u^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{e}}\vek u\) in the limit \(t \to 0\), and this is the derivative. By equation (29.25) it equals \(-E L_{\mathrm{e}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\).
  6. The derivative is continuous in \(\vek A\), because \(\vek u\) is continuous by Cramer's rule and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}\) is linear in \(\vek u\).

Source: CHK (5.19)-(5.22) (PDF p. 92-93) and (5.24) (PDF p. 94), where the derivative is obtained by differentiating the equation \(\mat K\vek u = \vek F\). Our proof with lemma 29.33 does not need to differentiate the solution.

The derivative says: a larger cross-section of each bar reduces the compliance, and most where the bar has a large energy \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) per unit volume. Now we can write the condition for the best design.

Theorem 29.37 Condition of minimum compliance (pogoj najmanjše podajnosti)

Let \(\vek A^*\) be a feasible design of problem (29.29), \(\vek u^*\) a solution of the system \(\mat K(\vek A^*)\vek u^* = \vek F\) (which exists) and \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}} = \varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek u^*)\) (equation (29.24)) also for bars with cross-section zero.

  1. Sufficient condition. If \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = V_0\) and there is a number \(\lambda > 0\) such that
    \[E\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda \ \text{ for every bar with } A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 0 , \qquad E\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda \ \text{ for every bar with } A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = 0 , \tag{29.31}\]
    then \(\vek A^*\) is a solution of problem (29.29). Here \(\mat K(\vek A^*)\) need not be positive definite.
  2. Necessary condition. If \(\mat K(\vek A^*)\) is positive definite and \(\vek A^*\) is a solution of problem (29.29), then \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = V_0\) and there exists \(\lambda > 0\) for which (29.31) holds.
Idea

For point 1 we take the line of lemma 29.33 that touches the compliance at \(\vek A^*\), and show that the condition \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\) allows no feasible design to lie below it. Point 2 is theorem 29.29 for this problem.

Proof

We must show point 1 by a direct calculation, and point 2 with the necessary conditions of section 29.4.

Point 1. Let \(\vek A\) be a feasible design with finite compliance (otherwise \(C(\vek A) = +\infty \ge C(\vek A^*)\) and there is nothing to prove).

  1. By point 4 of lemma 29.33, applied to the design \(\vek A\) with \(\vek v = \vek u^*\), we have \(C(\vek A) \ge 2\,\vek F^\T\vek u^* - \vek u^{*\T}\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek u^*\).
  2. From \(\vek F = \mat K(\vek A^*)\vek u^*\) we have \(2\,\vek F^\T\vek u^* = 2\,\vek u^{*\T}\mat K(\vek A^*)\vek u^*\), so the right-hand side of step 1 equals \(\vek u^{*\T}\mat K(\vek A^*)\vek u^* - \vek u^{*\T}\big(\mat K(\vek A) - \mat K(\vek A^*)\big)\vek u^* = C(\vek A^*) - \sum_{\mathrm{e}} (A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^*)\,E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) (equation (29.25) and point 2 of lemma 29.33).
  3. Since \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\) for all bars and \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\), we have \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}\,E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}} \le \lambda V_0\).
  4. For bars with \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 0\) we have \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\), for the others \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = 0\). So \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^*\,E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = \lambda V_0\).
  5. From steps 3 and 4, \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} (A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^*)\,E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda V_0 - \lambda V_0 = 0\). By step 2, therefore, \(C(\vek A) \ge C(\vek A^*)\) for every feasible design \(\vek A\).

Point 2. The function \(C\) has continuous derivatives on the open set \(\mathcal P \ni \vek A^*\) (lemma 29.36), and the constraints are \(g_0(\vek A) = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}} - V_0 \le 0\), \(g_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek A) = -A_{\mathrm{e}} \le 0\).

  1. Let \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* < V_0\). Then for \(\alpha = V_0/\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 1\) the design \(\alpha\vek A^*\) satisfies all constraints, and by lemma 29.35 we have \(C(\alpha\vek A^*) = C(\vek A^*)/\alpha < C(\vek A^*)\), because \(C(\vek A^*) > 0\). This contradicts optimality, so \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = V_0\) and \(g_0\) is active (definition 29.27).
  2. The gradients of the active constraints are \(\vek L = (L_1, \dots, L_{\mathrm{n}})\) and \(-\vek e_{\mathrm{k}}\) for every \(k\) with \(A_{\mathrm{k}}^* = 0\). They are linearly independent: from \(c\,\vek L - \sum_{\mathrm{k}} d_{\mathrm{k}}\vek e_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek 0\) it follows, at any \(j\) with \(A_{\mathrm{j}}^* > 0\) (such a \(j\) exists because \(V_0 > 0\)), that \(c\,L_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\), so \(c = 0\) and then all \(d_{\mathrm{k}} = 0\).
  3. By theorem 29.29 there exist \(\lambda \ge 0\) and \(\mu_{\mathrm{k}} \ge 0\) with \(\mu_{\mathrm{k}} A_{\mathrm{k}}^* = 0\) such that \(\nabla C + \lambda\vek L - \sum_{\mathrm{k}} \mu_{\mathrm{k}}\vek e_{\mathrm{k}} = \vek 0\). By lemma 29.36 the component \(e\) of this condition is \(-E\,L_{\mathrm{e}}\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 + \lambda L_{\mathrm{e}} - \mu_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\), so \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda - \mu_{\mathrm{e}}/L_{\mathrm{e}}\). For \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 0\) we have \(\mu_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) and \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\), for \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = 0\) we have \(\mu_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\) and \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\).
  4. If \(\lambda = 0\), then \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le 0\) for all \(e\), so \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) and by equation (29.25) \(\vek u^{*\T}\mat K\vek u^* = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* E L_{\mathrm{e}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = 0\). This is compliance zero, which lemma 29.35 excludes. Therefore \(\lambda > 0\).

Source: CHK §5.2.2 (PDF p. 94-95): the KKT point of the problem for a convex problem and "all bars where the optimum cross-sectional area is strictly greater than the lower bound ... have the same magnitude of the stress" (PDF p. 95). The sufficiency proof without the matrix \(\mat K^{-1}\), and so also for singular \(\mat K\), is ours.

The condition (29.31) says what is different in an optimal truss from an ordinary one: it is not one bar that carries to the limit, but all bars with material have the same energy density, and bars without material no larger one. The number \(\lambda\) has a meaning. For bars with material, by equation (29.25), \(C^* = \vek u^{*\T}\mat K\vek u^* = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^*\,E L_{\mathrm{e}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = \lambda V_0\), so \(\lambda = C^*/V_0\). At \(\theta = 30^\circ\) this is \(3{,}1735\ \text{J}/(3\cdot 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^3) = 10{,}578\ \text{kJ/m}^3\), which really is the energy density of both bars with material. By lemma 29.35 the smallest compliance at volume \(V\) is \(C^*V_0/V\), since designs of volume \(V\) are obtained from designs of volume \(V_0\) by a multiplication that divides the compliance by the same number. Its derivative with respect to the volume at \(V_0\) is \(-C^*/V_0 = -\lambda\). The number \(\lambda\) is therefore the decrease of the smallest compliance per unit of added volume. The same multiplier \(\lambda\) showed the constraint force in chapter 8 (theorem 8.36), here it is the price of volume. The letter \(\lambda\) here is not the eigenvalue of chapter 10.

Corollary 29.38 Equal stress in all bars with material (enaka napetost v vseh palicah z materialom)

Let \(\vek A^*\) have the property (29.31) with \(\lambda > 0\) and let \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}} = E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the stress of bar \(e\) at displacements \(\vek u^*\). Then \(|\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}| = \sqrt{E\lambda}\) for every bar with \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 0\), and for bars with \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = 0\) we have \(|\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}| \le \sqrt{E\lambda}\) (in our mind we give them the stress they would have).

Proof

We must show that the magnitude of the stress follows from the energy density.

  1. By lemma 4.5 and Hooke's law, \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}} = E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}\), so \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \sigma_{\mathrm{e}}^2/E\).
  2. From equation (29.31), \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = E\lambda\) for \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* > 0\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le E\lambda\) for \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^* = 0\). The square root of both sides gives the claim.

Source: CHK §5.2.2 (PDF p. 95): "fully stressed design". The naming and the proof are ours.

The corollary is not a condition taken from a stress constraint, as in section 29.2. There we prescribed the stress \(\sigma_0\) and computed the cross-sections from it. Here nobody prescribes the common stress: it appears as a consequence of one condition, the volume \(V_0\), and nothing more.

29.5.3 Three bars: topology from sizing (topologija iz dimenzioniranja)

Let us test the theorem on the truss of figure 29.17, at \(\theta = 30^\circ\). The theorem does not say which bars have material. We try the sufficient condition. We guess that bars \(1\) and \(2\) have material and bar \(3\) does not, and check whether the condition (29.31) holds. Let \(\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the unit vector from \(S_{\mathrm{e}}\) towards \(C\). With \(\vek n_1 = (1, -1)/\sqrt2\), \(\vek n_2 = (0, -1)\) and \(\vek n_3 = (-1, -1)/\sqrt2\) (axis \(x\) horizontally to the right, axis \(y\) upwards, origin at \(C\)) the lengths are \(L_1 = L_3 = \sqrt2\) m and \(L_2 = 1\) m, and the force is \(\vek F = F\,(\sin\theta,\ -\cos\theta)\). A bar in tension pulls the joint \(C\) towards the wall, so the force of bar \(e\) on \(C\) is \(-N_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\).

  1. Equilibrium of the joint \(C\): \(\vek F - \sum_{\mathrm{e}} N_{\mathrm{e}}\,\vek n_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\). Without bar \(3\) these are two equations for \(N_1\) and \(N_2\): the horizontal one \(N_1/\sqrt2 = F\sin\theta\), the vertical one \(-N_1/\sqrt2 - N_2 = -F\cos\theta\). The solution is \(N_1 = \sqrt2\,F\sin\theta = 7{,}071\) kN and \(N_2 = F(\cos\theta - \sin\theta) = 3{,}660\) kN, both in tension.
  2. By corollary 29.38, \(|\sigma_{\mathrm{e}}|\) is equal in both bars, \(\sigma = N_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}\). The volume is \(V_0 = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}} = (L_1N_1 + L_2N_2)/\sigma\), so \(\sigma = (10{,}000 + 3{,}660)\ \text{kN m}/(3\cdot 10^{-4}\ \text{m}^3) = 45{,}53\) MPa and the cross-sections are \(A_1 = N_1/\sigma = 155{,}29\ \text{mm}^2\), \(A_2 = N_2/\sigma = 80{,}38\ \text{mm}^2\).
  3. The strain of both bars is \(\varepsilon = \sigma/E = 2{,}323\cdot 10^{-4}\). From \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek n_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\vek u/L_{\mathrm{e}}\) (equation (29.24), at \(\vek u_{\mathrm{S}_\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\)), with lengths in metres, \(\varepsilon_1 = (u_{\mathrm{x}} - u_{\mathrm{y}})/(2\ \text{m}) = \varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_2 = -u_{\mathrm{y}}/(1\ \text{m}) = \varepsilon\). The displacement of the joint is therefore \(\vek u = (\varepsilon,\ -\varepsilon) \cdot 1\ \text{m} = (0{,}2323;\ -0{,}2323)\) mm.
  4. Bar \(3\): \(\varepsilon_3 = -(u_{\mathrm{x}} + u_{\mathrm{y}})/(2\ \text{m}) = 0\). The condition \(E\varepsilon_3^2 = 0 \le \lambda\) holds, and \(\lambda = E\varepsilon^2 = 10{,}578\ \text{kJ/m}^3\) from both bars.
  5. The condition (29.31) is satisfied, and by point 1 of theorem 29.37, \(\vek A^* = (155{,}29;\ 80{,}38;\ 0)\ \text{mm}^2\) is a solution. The compliance is \(C = \vek F^\T\vek u = 10\ \text{kN}\,\big(0{,}5 \cdot 0{,}2323 + 0{,}866 \cdot 0{,}2323\big)\ \text{mm} = 3{,}1735\) J, and with equal cross-sections of \(78{,}36\ \text{mm}^2\) it is \(5{,}1625\) J.

Why is the third bar without stress? Figure 29.19 shows how the joint moves, strongly magnified. In the wall are the points \(S_1\), \(S_2\), \(S_3\), bars \(1\) and \(2\) before loading meet at \(C\), and after loading at \(C'\). The displacement \(\vek u\) (blue arrow) is perpendicular to bar \(3\) (right angle), since \(u_{\mathrm{x}} + u_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\). The joint therefore moves along the tangent of the circle around \(S_3\), and the length \(L_3\) does not change to first order. Bar \(3\) would carry nothing at such a displacement even if it had a cross-section, so we do not give it one. This is the topology that comes out of sizing: the ground structure had three bars, the optimum has two. Smallness: the displacement is \(0{,}23\) mm against lengths of \(1\) m, the ratio is \(2{,}3\cdot 10^{-4}\), and the stress \(45{,}5\) MPa is far below \(220\) MPa. The model of small displacements is therefore justified.

At \(\theta = 60^\circ\) the solution is different: bar \(3\) has material and bar \(2\) does not. Equilibrium gives \(N_1 = F(\cos\theta + \sin\theta)/\sqrt2 = 9{,}659\) kN and \(N_3 = F(\cos\theta - \sin\theta)/\sqrt2 = -2{,}588\) kN, bar \(3\) is in compression. The stress is \(\sigma = (9{,}659 + 2{,}588)\sqrt2\ \text{kN m}/V_0 = 57{,}74\) MPa and the cross-sections are \(A_1 = 167{,}30\), \(A_3 = 44{,}83\ \text{mm}^2\). From \(\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon_3 = -\varepsilon\) we get \(\vek u = (2\varepsilon,\ 0) = (0{,}5891;\ 0)\) mm, and bar \(2\) has \(\varepsilon_2 = -u_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\). The condition holds, \(C = 5{,}1020\) J (equal cross-sections: \(7{,}8594\) J). At \(\theta = 0\) only the middle bar remains: \(A_2 = V_0/L_2 = 300\ \text{mm}^2\), \(\vek u = (0,\ -0{,}1701)\) mm, the outer bars have \(E\varepsilon^2 = 1{,}417\ \text{kJ/m}^3 < \lambda = 5{,}669\ \text{kJ/m}^3\). The condition holds and \(C = F^2L_2^2/(EV_0) = 1{,}7007\) J, with equal cross-sections \(3{,}8140\) J. At \(\theta = 0\) the matrix \(\mat K = \frac{EA_2}{L_2}\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) is singular, the horizontal displacement is not determined, but \(\vek F\) has no horizontal component, so the system has a solution and \(C\) is finite. We chose the solution with \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), since point 1 of the theorem needs only one solution \(\vek u^*\) that satisfies the condition. Point 1 of theorem 29.37 holds here, while point 2 does not speak about such a design, because \(\mat K\) is not positive definite (figure 29.17, left).

At \(\theta = 60^\circ\) the compressed bar is a problem. The cross-section \(44{,}83\ \text{mm}^2\) means a round bar with diameter \(7{,}555\) mm. The Euler load by theorem 8.134 is \(\pi^2 EI/L_3^2 = 154{,}7\) N, while the compression is \(2588\) N. The bar would buckle at a force seventeen times smaller. The model of this section does not take buckling into account, which is a simplification, and at \(\theta = 60^\circ\) a buckling constraint would have to be added, as we did in section 29.2, and the solution would be different. At \(\theta = 30^\circ\) and \(0^\circ\) all bars are in tension.

Let us also test two designs that are close to the true solution in form, but are not it. The first is the uniform design \(\theta = 30^\circ\) with cross-sections \(78{,}36\ \text{mm}^2\): \(E\varepsilon^2\) is \(30{,}6\), \(21{,}4\) and \(0{,}8\) kJ/m³ (left graph, figure 29.20). We read the density as the height of a column above the horizontal axis, with the same scale in all three graphs. The densities differ, the condition (29.31) does not hold for any \(\lambda\) and the design is not optimal: \(C = 5{,}1625\) J against \(3{,}1735\) J of the optimum. The second is a design with such a choice of bars that two have equal stress. Bars \(2\) and \(3\) have at \(\theta = 30^\circ\) the cross-sections \(173{,}21\) and \(89{,}66\ \text{mm}^2\) and \(|\sigma| = 78{,}87\) MPa in both, so all bars with material carry equal stress. The compliance is \(C = 9{,}5205\) J, three times that of the optimum, and bar \(1\) without cross-section has \(E\varepsilon_1^2 = 126{,}9\ \text{kJ/m}^3\), which is more than \(\lambda = 31{,}7\ \text{kJ/m}^3\) of bars \(2\) and \(3\) (right graph). The inequality of the condition is violated. Equal stress in bars with material is therefore necessary for the optimum, but not sufficient: a bar without material must have an energy density of at most \(\lambda\).

We also checked with \(20\,000\) random designs of the same volume: the best has \(C = 3{,}1783\) J at \(\theta = 30^\circ\) and \(5{,}1081\) J at \(\theta = 60^\circ\), so none is below the optimum of \(3{,}1735\) and \(5{,}1020\) J respectively. This is a test, the proof is the theorem.

29.5.4 Compliance or stress (podajnost ali napetost)

Instead of the compliance at a given volume we could demand the smallest volume at a given compliance. Lemma 29.35 says that the solution of both tasks is the same design up to multiplication.

Corollary 29.39 Minimum compliance at a given volume is minimum volume at a given compliance (najmanjša podajnost pri dani prostornini je najmanjša prostornina pri dani podajnosti)

Let \(\vek A^*\) be a solution of problem (29.29) with volume \(V_0\) and finite compliance \(C^* = C(\vek A^*)\). Then for every design \(\vek B \ge \vek 0\) with \(C(\vek B) \le C^*\) we have \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} B_{\mathrm{e}} \ge V_0\). The design \(\alpha\vek A^*\) with \(\alpha = C^*/C_{\max}\) is therefore a solution of the task of minimum volume under the constraint \(C \le C_{\max}\).

Proof

We must show that a design with smaller volume and no larger compliance would contradict optimality.

  1. Let \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} B_{\mathrm{e}} < V_0\) and \(C(\vek B) \le C^*\). The compliance \(C(\vek B)\) is thus finite and by lemma 29.35 positive.
  2. For \(\alpha = V_0/\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} B_{\mathrm{e}} > 1\) the design \(\alpha\vek B\) has volume \(V_0\), and by lemma 29.35 \(C(\alpha\vek B) = C(\vek B)/\alpha < C(\vek B) \le C^*\). This contradicts the optimality of the design \(\vek A^*\).
  3. For the second part: by lemma 29.35, \(\alpha\vek A^*\) has compliance \(C^*/\alpha = C_{\max}\). A design \(\vek B\) with \(C(\vek B) \le C_{\max}\) has the design \(\vek B/\alpha\) with compliance \(C(\vek B/\alpha) = \alpha\,C(\vek B) \le C^*\), so volume \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} B_{\mathrm{e}}/\alpha \ge V_0\) by the first part, and \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}} B_{\mathrm{e}} \ge \alpha V_0\), which is the volume of the design \(\alpha\vek A^*\).

Source: CHK §5.2.3 (PDF p. 95-97) proves the equivalence of both tasks with equations for the KKT points, labelled (5.25) and (5.26). Our proof from scaling is shorter.

Let us look at this with our numbers. At \(\theta = 30^\circ\) the optimum at \(300\ \text{cm}^3\) has the stress \(45{,}53\) MPa. If we allow the stress \(\sigma_0 = 220\) MPa (without a safety factor, which is our simplification, because a factor only multiplies every cross-section), we must multiply the cross-sections by \(45{,}53/220\). We get \(A_1 = 32{,}14\ \text{mm}^2\), \(A_2 = 16{,}64\ \text{mm}^2\) and the volume \(62{,}09\ \text{cm}^3\), and the compliance grows by the factor \(220/45{,}53 = 4{,}83\). The truss of bars \(1\) and \(2\) is statically determinate, so by lemma 29.10 the fully stressed design is the smallest under a stress constraint, and its volume \((L_1N_1 + L_2N_2)/\sigma_0 = 62{,}09\ \text{cm}^3\) is the same. Minimum compliance and sizing by stresses are therefore here the same design. That this holds for every truss with one load, we do not claim here.

What have we shown? The compliance of a truss is twice the strain energy and a convex function of the cross-sections. A design at a given volume is best when all bars with material have the same energy density and bars without material no larger one, and the sufficient part of this holds also when the structure matrix is not positive definite. On three bars the topology came out of this condition: at every load one of the three bars got cross-section zero, and at \(\theta = 0\) two did. With this we have kept the promise from chapters 4 and 7: the principle of minimum potential energy and the assembly of the structure matrix were all we needed. How to find the optimum when we do not guess which bars have material is described by section 29.7 with the correction of the cross-sections \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \leftarrow A_{\mathrm{e}}\sqrt{E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2/\lambda}\), whose fixed point is the condition (29.31). We derived the derivative of the compliance here for this case (equation (29.30)), and general derivatives are treated in section 29.6.

Cited, not proved: that the solutions of the problem with a lower bound \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge A_{\min} > 0\) approach, as \(A_{\min} \to 0\), the solution with the bound zero (CHK §5.2, PDF p. 92: "It may be proven that as ε → 0"). Our sufficient condition from point 1 of theorem 29.37 does not need this restriction. It does not say, however, whether the optimum is unique.
