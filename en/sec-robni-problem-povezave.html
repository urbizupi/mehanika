Chapter 11 The boundary value problem of linear elasticity

11.16 Connections

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

  • The Navier-Lamé equations: theorem 11.6, which we solved in closed form in this chapter, is solved numerically for bodies of complicated shape with finite elements of the continuum, which start from the weak form of the equations (AK, VT, chapters 6 and 15). Thermal stresses, where the strain contains a temperature term, and the temperature field from heat conduction (AK, VT, chapter 14). Elastodynamics, where the inertial force \(\rho \ddot{\mathbf{u}}\) is added to the equilibrium in theorem 11.6 (DSK, chapter 13).
  • Orthogonal coordinates (cylindrical and spherical): shells with general curvilinear tensor calculus, where the basis vectors are no longer orthogonal (MK, chapter 22), circular plates as an axisymmetric problem in polar coordinates (MK, chapter 18), torsion of circular and prismatic shafts, antiplane strain and the Prandtl function (MK, VD, chapter 16).
  • Plane problems and the Airy function: plane and axisymmetric finite elements (AK, chapter 15), stress concentration in modelling and mesh refinement, where the Kirsch factor \(3\) of section 11.13 is a yardstick for a numerical result (AK, chapter 17), contact and the Flamant and Boussinesq solutions on a half-space (DSK, chapter 25), rotating discs (DSK, chapter 24), symmetry conditions (MK, AK, chapter 19).
  • Saint-Venant's principle: beam and plate theories are approximations that do not require the exact distribution of the load at the ends (MK, chapters 18 and 17). The curved beam of section 11.14 is an exact solution of elasticity with which we compared the elementary bending formula.
  • The thick-walled tube and sphere: the Lamé solutions of sections 11.7 and 11.8 are a test for axisymmetric finite elements (AK, chapter 15), and the axisymmetric Airy function of section 11.12 is the starting point for rotating discs (DSK, chapter 24).
  • Supports in several pieces: the support condition of the converse of the principle of virtual forces for several separate supported faces, which chapter 4 left open, was proved in section 11.15 for the rectangular strip with plane data, and for a body in space only under hypothesis (H), so for a general body it stays open, which concerns every model with several separate supported faces (AK, chapter 15).
