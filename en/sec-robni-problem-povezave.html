Chapter 11 The boundary value problem of linear elasticity

11.16 Connections

The abbreviations of subjects are the same as in section 1.19.

  • The Navier-Lamé equations: the equations of theorem 11.6, which we solved in closed form in this chapter, are solved numerically for bodies of complicated shape with finite elements of the continuum, which start from the weak form of the equations (AK, VT, chapters 6 and 15). Thermal stresses, where the strain contains a temperature term, and the temperature field from heat conduction (AK, VT, chapter 14). Elastodynamics, where the right-hand side of the equation of theorem 11.6 is the density times the acceleration, \(\rho\,\partial^2\vek u/\partial t^2\), instead of zero (DSK, chapter 13). Why exactly this? The force per unit volume that the stresses give a small cube, together with the body force \(\vek b\), is zero at rest, and in motion by Newton's second law it equals the mass of a unit volume, that is the density \(\rho\), times the acceleration.
  • Orthogonal coordinates (cylindrical and spherical): shells, where two coordinates on the surface in the middle of the wall are chosen along its directions of largest and smallest curvature and the third along the normal to it, so that they are orthogonal again and the strains are computed as in this chapter (MK, chapter 22), circular plates as an axisymmetric problem in polar coordinates (MK, chapter 18), torsion of circular and prismatic shafts, antiplane strain and the Prandtl function (MK, VD, chapter 16).
  • Plane problems and the Airy function: plane and axisymmetric finite elements (AK, chapter 15), stress concentration in modelling and mesh refinement, where the Kirsch factor \(3\) of section 11.13 is a yardstick for a numerical result (AK, chapter 17), contact, the Flamant solution on a half-plane and the Boussinesq solution on a half-space (DSK, chapter 25), symmetry conditions (MK, AK, chapter 19).
  • Saint-Venant's principle: the difference of two statically equivalent loads on the end of a strip (with the same resultant and the same moment) falls away from the end. For the first symmetric decaying term, on the section \(x = 2b\), one height of the strip from the end, the envelope of the stress is only \(0{,}0148\) of that at the end. The term is fixed only up to a complex factor \(\kappa\). The largest stress on the section is \(0{,}0205\) of the largest stress at the end for the \(\kappa\) of the figure, and at most \(0{,}051\) for every \(\kappa\) (section 11.11). This is why beam and plate theories are approximations that do not require the exact distribution of the load at the end of a beam or the edge of a plate (MK, chapters 18 and 17). The curved beam of section 11.14 has an exact solution of elasticity, with which we compared the elementary bending formula.
  • The thick-walled tube and sphere: the Lamé solutions of sections 11.7 and 11.8 are a test for axisymmetric finite elements (AK, chapter 15). A rotating disc is solved along the same route as the Lamé tube, with the displacement and the body force of rotation, and the homogeneous part of its stresses equals the stresses of the axisymmetric Airy function of section 11.12 without the term \(B\,r^2\ln r\), which the single-valued displacement removes (theorem 11.54) (DSK, chapter 24).
  • Supports in several pieces: the support condition of the converse of the principle of virtual forces for a body with several separate supported faces, which chapter 4 left open, was proved in section 11.15 for the rectangular strip with plane data, and for a body of cuboids in space only under hypothesis (H), that the traction problem with smooth forces on two rectangles, whose total resultant and moment are zero, has a solution with stresses continuously differentiable up to the surface. We do not prove this hypothesis. For a body with a re-entrant edge, such as the L-shaped body, it may be false, because the stress at such an edge may grow without bound, so for a general body the condition stays open. Finite elements of the continuum (AK, chapter 15) do not build on it, because in them the displacements at the supports are prescribed directly at the nodes.
