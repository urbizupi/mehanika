Chapter 29 Structural optimization

29.7 Sequential approximations (zaporedne aproksimacije)

We optimized the wall truss in section 29.2 by hand, because the forces in the bars are known from equilibrium before we know the areas. The displacement of joint \(C\) was a formula in the areas, and the least volume at a bounded displacement came from a calculation on paper. For a truss with a hundred bars and several load cases there is no such formula. Every value of the objective and of every constraint appears only after we solve the equilibrium \(\mat K(\vek A)\,\vek u = \vek F\), as chapter 7 taught us, and again for every new design. How do we attack the problem when solving the equilibrium costs more than everything else? Sequential approximations answer as follows: whatever we cannot compute quickly, we replace at each step by a function we can, solve the replaced problem, and repeat the step. This section explains three such replacements, sequential linear programming, convex linearization and the optimality criteria update, and tests each on trusses we already know.

29.7.1 A loop with an explicit subproblem (zanka z eksplicitno podnalogo)

The idea of accepting every design at once is risky, so we begin with the order of work. The problem has the form of definition 29.2: the objective \(f(\vek A)\) and the constraints \(g_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek A) \le 0\) are functions of the design variables \(A_1, \dots, A_{\mathrm{n}}\) (the bar areas) alone, and the state \(\vek u\) is eliminated by the equilibrium equation. Figure 29.23 shows the loop described by CHK in section 4.1 (PDF pp. 65-66).

We begin with the design \(\vek A^k\), where the index \(k = 0, 1, 2, \dots\) counts the rounds of the loop. For it we solve the equilibrium, as for the truss of chapter 7 (theorem 7.18). Once we know the displacements, we compute the values and derivatives of the functions \(f\) and \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\) at \(\vek A^k\), the derivatives by the methods of section 29.6. From these numbers we build the approximation, that is, a function written as a formula in \(\vek A\), which we therefore evaluate without solving the equilibrium. We solve the problem with it, which gives the new design \(\vek A^{k+1}\), and the arrow back closes the loop. We stop the loop when the design hardly changes between two rounds.

Definition 29.46 Explicit approximation and subproblem (eksplicitni približek in podnaloga)

Let the problem \(\min f(\vek A)\) subject to \(g_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek A) \le 0\) for \(i = 1, \dots, l\) be given in nested form (definition 29.2), with continuously differentiable \(f\) and \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\) on the set of designs with all areas \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\). Let \(\vek A^k\) be a design from this set. An explicit approximation of the function \(g\) at \(\vek A^k\) is a function \(\tilde g^{\,k}\) that we compute from the numbers \(g(\vek A^k)\) and \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\vek A^k)\) by a formula in the areas, without solving the equilibrium. The subproblem at \(\vek A^k\) is the problem

\[\min\ \tilde f^{\,k}(\vek A) \quad \text{subject to} \quad \tilde g_{\mathrm{i}}^{\,k}(\vek A) \le 0 , \quad i = 1, \dots, l , \tag{29.41}\]

and its solution is \(\vek A^{k+1}\).

Source: The loop and the terms are after CHK section 4.1 (PDF pp. 65-66, steps 1 to 6), there with the symbol \(x\) for the design variables. The wording of the definition is ours.

The word "sequential" says that we solve a sequence of subproblems \(k = 0, 1, \dots\), and "explicit" says that each subproblem is written with formulas in \(\vek A\). There are several approximations, and they differ in which function they take. The first is the simplest.

29.7.2 Sequential linear programming (zaporedno linearno programiranje)

The simplest approximation is the tangent plane, that is, linearization. When both sides of the subproblem are linear in \(\vek A\), we call the problem a linear program: the objective is linear and all constraints are linear inequalities. Any ordinary program solves such a problem quickly and reliably, and its solution lies at a vertex of the feasible polygon (as will be seen in figure 29.24).

Definition 29.47 Sequential linear programming, SLP (zaporedno linearno programiranje)

In the setting of definition 29.46 the tangent-plane approximations are

\[\tilde f^{\,k}(\vek A) = f(\vek A^k) + \sum_{\mathrm{e}=1}^{n} \frac{\partial f}{\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}}(\vek A^k)\,(A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^k) , \tag{29.42}\]

and likewise for \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\). The SLP subproblem is a linear program with two additional bounds for each area,

\[\underline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} \le A_{\mathrm{e}} \le \overline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} , \qquad e = 1, \dots, n . \tag{29.43}\]

The bounds \(\underline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k}\) and \(\overline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k}\) are the move limit: they ensure that in one round the design does not move too far from the point \(\vek A^k\) where the linearization holds. Without a move limit the bounds are simply the smallest and the largest admissible area \(A_{\min}\) and \(A_{\max}\). With a relative move limit \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}} > 0\) we have \(\underline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} = \max\{A_{\min},\ (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{p}})\,A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\}\) and \(\overline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} = \min\{A_{\max},\ (1 + \delta_{\mathrm{p}})\,A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\}\).

Source: CHK section 4.2 (PDF pp. 66-67): linearization at \(\vek x^k\), the limits \(l_{\mathrm{j}}^k\) and \(u_{\mathrm{j}}^k\), which are "updated according to some user-defined rule", and the statement that the subproblem is a linear program. The relative form of the limit and the symbol \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}}\) are ours. SIGMUND2001 (PDF p. 2) writes the same notion with \(m\), which in the chapters on dynamics is a mass.

The name says what we do: a sequence of linear programs (programming here is the old word for solving such problems by a plan, not writing computer code). We test it on a truss we know. The wall truss of section 4.1.4 has the joints \(A = (-4, 0)\) m and \(B = (-4, 3)\) m, the joint \(C = (0, 0)\) with the load \(F = 10\) kN downward, the bars \(AC\) and \(BC\) of lengths \(L_{\mathrm{AC}} = 4\) m and \(L_{\mathrm{BC}} = 5\) m, and \(E = 196\) GPa. The problem: the least volume \(V = L_{\mathrm{AC}}A_{\mathrm{AC}} + L_{\mathrm{BC}}A_{\mathrm{BC}}\) at a downward displacement of joint \(C\) with \(v_{\mathrm{C}} \le \delta = 2\) mm. The idealizations of straight bars, small displacements and a linearly elastic material from chapter 7 hold, and we neglect the weight of the bars. The areas lie between \(1\) mm² and \(10\,000\) mm².

The displacement \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\) is a formula in the areas, because the truss is statically determinate: the axial forces \(N_{\mathrm{AC}} = -13{,}33\) kN and \(N_{\mathrm{BC}} = 16{,}67\) kN are fixed by equilibrium and do not depend on the areas, and the unit forces are \(n_{\mathrm{AC}} = N_{\mathrm{AC}}/F = -4/3\) and \(n_{\mathrm{BC}} = 5/3\). By the unit force method (theorem 4.95, equation (4.64))

\[v_{\mathrm{C}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \hat N_{\mathrm{e}}\,\frac{N_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}}{E A_{\mathrm{e}}} = \frac{c_{\mathrm{AC}}}{A_{\mathrm{AC}}} + \frac{c_{\mathrm{BC}}}{A_{\mathrm{BC}}} , \qquad c_{\mathrm{e}} = \frac{N_{\mathrm{e}}\,n_{\mathrm{e}}\,L_{\mathrm{e}}}{E} , \tag{29.44}\]

with \(c_{\mathrm{AC}} = 3{,}628\cdot 10^{-7}\) m³ and \(c_{\mathrm{BC}} = 7{,}086\cdot 10^{-7}\) m³. At the areas \(100\) mm² of chapter 7 the formula gives \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 10{,}714\) mm, as we computed there. The derivative is \(\partial v_{\mathrm{C}}/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}} = -c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2\), at \(100\) mm² this is \(-36{,}28\) and \(-70{,}86\) m per m², as the unit force gives in section 29.6.

We start at \(\vek A^0 = (600, 600)\) mm², where \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 1{,}786\) mm (a feasible design) and \(V = 5400\) cm³. The derivatives there are \(-1{,}008\cdot 10^{-3}\) and \(-1{,}968\cdot 10^{-3}\) mm per mm². The first SLP subproblem is the linear program: the least \(V\) subject to \(v_{\mathrm{C}}(\vek A^0) + \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \partial v_{\mathrm{C}}/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^0) \le \delta\). Figure 29.24 shows the two areas as coordinates.

Look first at the left half of the figure. The axes are logarithmic, because without a move limit the SLP designs are scattered over three orders of magnitude. The blue curve is the set of designs with \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 2\) mm, from formula (29.44): the areas are tied by \(c_{\mathrm{AC}}/A_{\mathrm{AC}} + c_{\mathrm{BC}}/A_{\mathrm{BC}} = \delta\). The grey dashed line is the linearized constraint from \(\vek A^0\). Point 0 is the starting design. The linear program lies at a vertex, and since the linearization of the constraint knows no curvature, only the ratio of cost to benefit decides: bar \(e\) reduces the displacement by \(c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) per unit of area and costs \(L_{\mathrm{e}}\) per unit of area, so it is worth increasing the one with the smaller quotient \(L_{\mathrm{e}} A_{\mathrm{e}}^2/c_{\mathrm{e}}\). At \(\vek A^0\) this quotient is \(3{,}969\) m² for \(AC\) and \(2{,}540\) m² for \(BC\), so the program gives all the area to \(BC\) and lowers \(AC\) to its lower bound: point 1 is \((1{,}0;\ 797{,}8)\) mm². The linearized constraint there does give \(2{,}000\) mm, but the true displacement is \(363{,}7\) mm: bar \(AC\) with area \(1\) mm² is like a thread.

Lemma 29.48 The linearized SLP constraint admits too much (linearizirana omejitev SLP dopušča preveč)

Let \(g\) be a convex and continuously differentiable function on an open convex set of designs (definition 29.17) and \(\vek A^k\) a design from it. Then every design that satisfies \(g(\vek A) \le 0\) is also feasible for the linearized constraint \(g(\vek A^k) + \nabla g(\vek A^k)\cdot(\vek A - \vek A^k) \le 0\). Therefore, for a linear objective, the value of the SLP subproblem, measured on the same move limits, is at most the least value of the true problem.

Proof

We must show that the feasible set of the true problem is a subset of the feasible set of the subproblem. The key is the tangent plane of a convex function from section 29.3.

  1. By lemma 29.20 on the tangent plane, for convex \(g\) every \(\vek A\) in the set satisfies \(g(\vek A) \ge g(\vek A^k) + \nabla g(\vek A^k)\cdot(\vek A - \vek A^k)\).
  2. If \(g(\vek A) \le 0\), then by step 1 the right-hand side is at most \(g(\vek A) \le 0\), so the linearized constraint holds.
  3. The move limits are the same in both problems. The least value of a linear function \(f\) on a larger set is not larger than on a smaller one, and this is the second claim.

Source: The proof is ours, from lemma 29.20.

The lemma says in which direction the linearization deceives: a convex constraint is softened by linearization, so the program is too easy and pushes the design where the true constraint does not hold. The converse is not guaranteed, and point 1 in the figure is an example. Its values agree with the lemma: the volume \(3993\) cm³ is less than the true least volume \(4764{,}7\) cm³ (a lower bound), and the displacement \(363{,}7\) mm is more than ten times too large. So the lemma does not claim that the subproblem is feasible, and it does not claim that its solution is near the true one.

Example 29.49 Two failures of SLP on the wall truss (dva neuspeha SLP na stenskem paličju)

Without a move limit the designs do not settle. From point 1 the left part of figure 29.24 shows the sequence \((1{,}0;\ 797{,}8)\), \((2{,}0;\ 1{,}0)\), \((1{,}0;\ 2{,}4)\), \((3{,}3;\ 1{,}0)\) mm² and onward \((1{,}0;\ 2{,}3)\), \((3{,}3;\ 1{,}0)\), \((1{,}0;\ 2{,}3)\), \((3{,}3;\ 1{,}0)\). The displacements are \(363{,}7\), \(890{,}1\), \(660{,}4\), \(818{,}9\) mm and then between \(676\) and \(818\) mm, no design is feasible. Each subproblem puts all the material into the bar that looks cheaper at the current areas, and in the next design that bar looks expensive, because its quotient \(L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}^2/c_{\mathrm{e}}\) has changed.

With a move limit \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}} = 0{,}3\) the designs do not run away, but they do not settle either. The right half of figure 29.24 has linear axes, because the designs now stay between \(300\) and \(700\) mm². The blue curve is again the boundary \(v_{\mathrm{C}} = 2\) mm, and the grey dashed line is the linearized constraint from \(\vek A^0\), on which point 1 lies. From \(\vek A^0\) the designs go to \((420{,}0;\ 583{,}3)\), \((546{,}0;\ 496{,}6)\), \((382{,}2;\ 597{,}8)\), \((496{,}9;\ 522{,}1)\), \((347{,}8;\ 640{,}0)\), \((452{,}1;\ 546{,}0)\), \((316{,}5;\ 689{,}5)\), \((411{,}4;\ 575{,}6)\) mm². The displacements are between \(2{,}079\) and \(2{,}174\) mm, always above the limit of \(2\) mm, the volumes between \(4518\) and \(4713\) cm³. The empty circle is the optimum \((464{,}85;\ 581{,}07)\) mm², where the solid grey line \(V = 4764{,}7\) cm³ touches the blue curve. The designs jump back and forth across the optimum, never reach it and never settle there.

Why do they not settle? At the optimum the gradient of the objective must be parallel to the gradient of the constraint, which is the KKT condition of section 29.4: \(L_{\mathrm{e}} = \mu\,c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) for both bars with the same number \(\mu\). At the optimum we indeed have \(L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}^2/c_{\mathrm{e}} = 2{,}382\) m² for \(AC\) and \(BC\), and this is \(\mu\). A linear program whose objective is parallel to the constraint has no unique solution: every point on the line is equally good. Near the optimum the objective and the linearized constraint are nearly parallel, so a small difference of the quotients \(L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}^2/c_{\mathrm{e}}\) decides which bar is cheaper. The linear program thins the more expensive bar down to the lower move bound or thickens the cheaper one up to the upper bound, and the linearized constraint fixes the area of the other bar. The solution is thus a vertex of the feasible polygon with one area on a move bound and the other on the linearized constraint. In our sequence the area on the bound is always \(A_{\mathrm{AC}}\), which alternately shrinks and grows by \(30\) %. After the step the quotients swap, and the next step jumps back: at the points \(1, 3, 5, 7\) bar \(AC\) is the cheaper one, at the points \(2, 4, 6\) bar \(BC\). Since \(0{,}7 \cdot 1{,}3 = 0{,}91\), the area \(A_{\mathrm{AC}}\) is \(9\) % smaller after every pair of steps, so the designs in the figure creep up and to the left, and at point 8 bar \(AC\) is still the cheaper one. The displacement stays above \(2\) mm, because by lemma 29.48 the linearized constraint admits too much: the tangent plane of a convex function lies below it.

We do not prove the convergence of loops in this book. The test on this example is enough for the conclusion that SLP is not reliable without a rule for the move limit that shrinks with the rounds (CHK PDF p. 67 says that the choice of the move limits "affects the efficiency considerably").

29.7.3 Convex linearization (konveksna linearizacija)

Why does SLP describe the displacement so badly? Linearization in \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) would be exact if the displacement were a linear function of the areas, but by formula (29.44) the displacement is linear in \(1/A_{\mathrm{e}}\). Figure 29.25 shows this on one bar: we choose the variable in which the function is closer to a straight line.

The axes show the displacement \(v_{\mathrm{C}}\) of the joint as one area varies. The blue curve is \(c_{\mathrm{AC}}/A_{\mathrm{AC}} + c_{\mathrm{BC}}/A_{\mathrm{BC}}\) and is convex and decreasing. The point \(\vek A^k\) is the current design. The grey line is the tangent, that is, the SLP linearization. At \(A_{\mathrm{AC}} = 300\) mm² the difference is \(2{,}3904 - 2{,}0881 = 0{,}30\) mm: the tangent says \(2{,}088\) mm, but the true displacement is \(2{,}390\) mm. If we draw the same curve against \(1/A_{\mathrm{AC}}\), it is a straight line, and the tangent in this variable lies on the curve.

Definition 29.50 Convex linearization, CONLIN (konveksna linearizacija)

In the setting of definition 29.46 let all areas be strictly positive. An intervening variable is a function \(y_{\mathrm{e}}\) of the area \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) in which we expand the function linearly: \(y_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\mathrm{e}}\) or \(y_{\mathrm{e}} = 1/A_{\mathrm{e}}\). Let \(g\) be one of the functions \(f\), \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\Omega^+ = \{e : \partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek A^k) > 0\}\), \(\Omega^- = \{e : \partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek A^k) \le 0\}\). The CONLIN approximation of the function \(g\) at \(\vek A^k\) is

\[\tilde g^{\,k}(\vek A) = g(\vek A^k) + \sum_{\mathrm{e} \in \Omega^+} \frac{\partial g}{\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^k) + \sum_{\mathrm{e} \in \Omega^-} \frac{\partial g}{\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2\left(\frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{e}}^k} - \frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{e}}}\right) , \tag{29.45}\]

with the derivatives computed at \(\vek A^k\). The variables in which the derivative is positive are expanded in \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\), the others in \(1/A_{\mathrm{e}}\).

Source: CHK section 4.4 (PDF pp. 67-69): the choice between \(x_{\mathrm{j}}\) and \(1/x_{\mathrm{j}}\) by the sign of the derivative, equation (4.5) and the sets \(\Omega_+\), \(\Omega_-\) (PDF p. 69, read by hand from the crop of the equation). The term in \(1/A_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the expansion in \(y_{\mathrm{e}} = 1/A_{\mathrm{e}}\) with the derivative \(\partial g/\partial y_{\mathrm{e}} = -A_{\mathrm{e}}^2\,\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\) by the chain rule (lemma 8.29).

The name is short for "convex linearization" (CHK PDF p. 68), since the result is a convex function, which we prove at once.

Lemma 29.51 Properties of the CONLIN approximation (lastnosti približka CONLIN)

Let \(\tilde g^{\,k}\) be the CONLIN approximation of definition 29.50 for a continuously differentiable \(g\). Then:

  1. \(\tilde g^{\,k}(\vek A^k) = g(\vek A^k)\) and \(\partial\tilde g^{\,k}/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek A^k) = \partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}(\vek A^k)\) for every \(e\).
  2. \(\tilde g^{\,k}\) is a convex function on the set of designs with all \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\).
Proof

We must show that the approximation agrees with \(g\) in value and derivative and that it has a nonnegative Hessian matrix, so that by lemma 29.22 it is convex.

  1. At \(\vek A = \vek A^k\) all brackets \((A_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)\) and \((1/A_{\mathrm{e}}^k - 1/A_{\mathrm{e}})\) are zero, so \(\tilde g^{\,k}(\vek A^k) = g(\vek A^k)\).
  2. The derivative with respect to \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) for \(e \in \Omega^+\) is \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\). For \(e \in \Omega^-\) the derivative of the term \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2\,(1/A_{\mathrm{e}}^k - 1/A_{\mathrm{e}})\) with respect to \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) is \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k/A_{\mathrm{e}})^2\), because \(\mathrm{d}(-1/A_{\mathrm{e}})/\mathrm{d}A_{\mathrm{e}} = 1/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2\). At \(A_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\) this is again \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\), which completes item 1.
  3. Every term of the sum is a function of one variable, so the Hessian matrix (definition 8.61) is diagonal. For \(e \in \Omega^+\) the second derivative is \(0\), a linear term. For \(e \in \Omega^-\) the second derivative by step 2 is \(-2\,\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}}\,(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2/A_{\mathrm{e}}^3\), and it is \(\ge 0\), because \(\partial g/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}} \le 0\) and \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\).
  4. A diagonal matrix with nonnegative diagonal entries is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15), so \(\tilde g^{\,k}\) is convex by lemma 29.22.

Source: CHK section 4.4 (PDF pp. 70-71): the first property and the proof that \(g^{C,k}_{\mathrm{i}}\) is convex, as a sum of terms \(C(x_{\mathrm{j}} - x_{\mathrm{j}}^k)\) and \(A + B/x_{\mathrm{j}}\). The proof with the Hessian matrix, step by step, is ours.

Why the choice by sign? Let us try the opposite, on the volume of bar \(AC\), which is linear in \(A_{\mathrm{AC}}\) (the derivative \(L_{\mathrm{AC}} = 4\) m is positive). If we expanded it in \(1/A_{\mathrm{AC}}\) we would get \(V_{\mathrm{R}}(A) = L(A^k + (A^k)^2(1/A^k - 1/A))\), that is, \(4\,(1200 - 360\,000/A)\) cm³ for \(A\) in mm² and \(A^k = 600\) mm². The right half of figure 29.25 has axes with the area \(A_{\mathrm{AC}}\) horizontal and the volume vertical, negative values included. The blue line is the true volume \(4A\) cm³, the orange curve is \(V_{\mathrm{R}}\), and both pass through the point at \(A^k = 600\) mm² with the same slope \(L = 4\) m. The orange curve is concave: the coefficient \(-L(A^k)^2\) in front of \(1/A\) is negative, so the second derivative is \(-2L(A^k)^2/A^3 < 0\), and at \(A = 1\) mm² the approximation gives the volume \(-1{,}435\cdot 10^{6}\) cm³, a negative one. The subproblem would push the objective toward \(-\infty\) as the area goes to zero, and every bar would vanish. The attempt fails precisely at the point where the lemma demands the sign of the derivative. With the right choice the volume remains a straight line.

Lemma 29.52 CONLIN is exact for a statically determinate truss (CONLIN je točen za statično določeno paličje)

For a statically determinate truss (definition 4.4) with axial forces \(N_{\mathrm{e}}\), unit forces \(n_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(c_{\mathrm{e}} = N_{\mathrm{e}} n_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}/E\), the displacement is \(v = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}\), and its CONLIN approximation at every design with \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\) is exact if every \(c_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\).

Proof

We must show that the approximation agrees with the function everywhere, not only at \(\vek A^k\).

  1. For a statically determinate truss the equilibrium alone determines \(N_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(n_{\mathrm{e}}\), and they do not depend on the areas, so by theorem 4.95 \(v = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  2. Every derivative is \(\partial v/\partial A_{\mathrm{e}} = -c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le 0\), so all \(e\) lie in \(\Omega^-\).
  3. By formula (29.45), \(\tilde v^{\,k}(\vek A) = v(\vek A^k) + \sum_{\mathrm{e}} (-c_{\mathrm{e}}/(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2)(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2(1/A_{\mathrm{e}}^k - 1/A_{\mathrm{e}}) = v(\vek A^k) - \sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^k + \sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}\).

Source: The proof is ours. CHK section 4.4 (PDF p. 68) states the same for a statically determinate truss: linearization in \(1/A_{\mathrm{i}}\) is exact, because displacements and stresses are functions of \(1/A_{\mathrm{i}}\).

By lemma 29.52 the displacement of the wall truss is covered by the exact curve, which is the blue curve in the figure. A consequence: the CONLIN subproblem is in this case equal to the true problem. The displacement is exact, and the volume is already linear in the areas (\(L_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\), so all areas are in \(\Omega^+\) for \(f\)). The subproblem is \(\min L_{\mathrm{AC}}A_{\mathrm{AC}} + L_{\mathrm{BC}}A_{\mathrm{BC}}\) subject to \(c_{\mathrm{AC}}/A_{\mathrm{AC}} + c_{\mathrm{BC}}/A_{\mathrm{BC}} \le \delta\).

Example 29.53 One CONLIN step on the wall truss (en korak CONLIN na stenskem paličju)

We solve the subproblem with the multiplier \(\mu\). The problem is convex, since \(V\) is linear and by lemma 29.51 the displacement is convex, so by theorem 29.30 the KKT conditions are sufficient.

  1. Stationarity in \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) for the Lagrangian \(V + \mu\,(v - \delta)\): \(L_{\mathrm{e}} - \mu\,c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = 0\), so \(A_{\mathrm{e}} = \sqrt{\mu\,c_{\mathrm{e}}/L_{\mathrm{e}}}\).
  2. The displacement in the constraint: \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \sqrt{c_{\mathrm{e}}L_{\mathrm{e}}/\mu} = \delta\), so \(\sqrt\mu = \sum_{\mathrm{j}}\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{j}}L_{\mathrm{j}}}/\delta\). The numbers are \(\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{AC}}L_{\mathrm{AC}}} = 1{,}205\cdot 10^{-3}\) m², \(\sqrt{c_{\mathrm{BC}}L_{\mathrm{BC}}} = 1{,}882\cdot 10^{-3}\) m², \(\sqrt\mu = 1{,}5435\) and \(\mu = 2{,}382\) m².
  3. The areas \(A_{\mathrm{AC}} = \sqrt{\mu c_{\mathrm{AC}}/L_{\mathrm{AC}}} = 464{,}85\) mm² and \(A_{\mathrm{BC}} = 581{,}07\) mm², the volume \(4764{,}7\) cm³, the displacement \(2{,}0000\) mm. The multiplier is positive.

Since the subproblem at \(\vek A^0 = (600, 600)\) mm² equals the true problem, the result after one round is already the true optimum, and the starting design makes no difference to the areas from CONLIN. SLP did not settle in \(8\) rounds in the same circumstances. The stresses are \(N_{\mathrm{AC}}/A_{\mathrm{AC}} = -28{,}7\) MPa and \(N_{\mathrm{BC}}/A_{\mathrm{BC}} = 28{,}7\) MPa: both bars are equally loaded, which is a consequence, not an assumption.

The exactness is a property of static determinacy, not of CONLIN. In a statically indeterminate truss the forces \(N_{\mathrm{e}}\) themselves depend on the areas, so the formula \(v = \sum c_{\mathrm{e}}/A_{\mathrm{e}}\) does not hold and the CONLIN approximation only comes closer. An example is the truss with three bars: the joint \((0, 0)\), supports at \((-1, 1)\), \((0, 1)\) and \((1, 1)\) m, the force \(F = 10\) kN at \(30^\circ\) from the vertical, all areas equal to \(78{,}36\) mm² (volume \(300\) cm³). The compliance \(C\) (section 29.5) there is \(5{,}1625\) J, the derivatives with respect to the areas are \(-E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2L_{\mathrm{e}}\) (corollary 29.44), all negative, so all bars are in \(\Omega^-\). When the area of the first bar is multiplied by \(1{,}5\), the true calculation gives \(C = 3{,}9084\) J, the linear approximation \(3{,}4650\) J (\(-11{,}3\) %) and the CONLIN approximation \(4{,}0308\) J (\(+3{,}1\) %), at half the area (\(\times 0{,}5\)) the true \(7{,}7883\) J, the linear \(6{,}8599\) J (\(-11{,}9\) %) and CONLIN \(8{,}5574\) J (\(+9{,}9\) %). CONLIN is not exact, but in both cases it is closer and above the true value. The linear approximation is below it, as it must be for a convex function: the tangent underestimates it (lemma 29.20). CHK (PDF pp. 69-70) proves that CONLIN is always at least as conservative as SLP.

29.7.4 Moving asymptotes (premikajoče se asimptote)

The method of moving asymptotes (MMA) uses the intervening variables \(1/(A_{\mathrm{e}} - L_{\mathrm{e}})\) and \(1/(U_{\mathrm{e}} - A_{\mathrm{e}})\), where \(L_{\mathrm{e}} < A_{\mathrm{e}} < U_{\mathrm{e}}\) are asymptotes that move between rounds (CHK section 4.5, PDF pp. 74-76). The first applies to variables with derivative \(\le 0\), the second to those with derivative \(> 0\). The closer the asymptotes lie to the current design, the more conservative the approximation, and the farther away, the more it resembles the tangent plane (PDF pp. 76 and 77). CONLIN is obtained at \(L_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) and \(U_{\mathrm{e}} \to +\infty\), SLP at \(L_{\mathrm{e}} \to -\infty\) and \(U_{\mathrm{e}} \to +\infty\) (PDF p. 77). The asymptotes move by a rule: if the signs of the successive changes \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^k - A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k-1}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k-1} - A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k-2}\) differ, the design variable oscillates and the asymptotes come closer (PDF p. 76), which is exactly the phenomenon of example 29.49. We only state the exact formulas and the proof of convexity, we do not prove them (CHK PDF pp. 74-76). CHK itself mentions that CONLIN sometimes converges slowly or not at all (PDF p. 74), so MMA is an improvement on it.

29.7.5 The optimality criteria update (posodobitev po merilu optimalnosti)

For the problem of the least compliance of a truss at a bounded volume from section 29.5 there is an even simpler loop, which needs no solution of a subproblem. Theorem 29.37 tells us what the optimum looks like: for bars with \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\) we have \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\), for bars without material \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\), where \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the relative elongation of the bar, and \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) is by theorem 4.3 twice the strain energy per volume of the bar, since \(U = EA\varepsilon^2L/2\). The quantity \(\lambda\) here is the multiplier of the volume constraint (in chapter 10 it means an eigenvalue). A bar that stores more energy per volume than the average is too thin, since it has a large elongation, so we thicken it, and conversely. We write this idea as a rule.

Definition 29.54 The optimality criteria update, OC (posodobitev po merilu optimalnosti)

Let the problem be the least compliance of a truss subject to \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}} \le V_0\) and \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge A_{\min} > 0\) (section 29.5). Let \(\vek A^k\) be a design with equilibrium \(\mat K(\vek A^k)\vek u^k = \vek F\) and relative elongations \(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^k\), and let \(B_{\mathrm{e}}^k = E\,(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^k)^2\). Let \(\eta \in (0, 1]\) be a number (the damping) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}} > 0\) a move limit. The optimality criteria update is

\[A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k+1} = \min\Big\{\ \overline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k},\ \max\big\{\ \underline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k},\ A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\,(B_{\mathrm{e}}^k/\lambda)^{\eta}\ \big\}\Big\} , \tag{29.46}\]

with the bounds \(\underline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} = \max\{A_{\min},\ (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\}\) and \(\overline A_{\mathrm{e}}^{\,k} = (1 + \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\mathrm{e}}^k\), and with \(\lambda > 0\) chosen so that \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k+1} = V_0\).

Source: The form is after CHK section 9.1.2 (PDF p. 191, the equation with \(\eta\), there for a plate with thickness \(x_{\mathrm{e}}\) as the design variable) and SIGMUND2001 (PDF p. 2, equations (2) and (3), \(\eta = 1/2\), "numerical damping coefficient"). The adaptation to a truss with \(B_{\mathrm{e}} = E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) and volume \(L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}\) is ours: for a bar \(\vek u_{\mathrm{e}}^{\T}\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}^0\vek u_{\mathrm{e}} = E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2L_{\mathrm{e}}\), and dividing by \(a_{\mathrm{e}} = L_{\mathrm{e}}\) leaves \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2\).

The name is "criteria" because the rule comes from the criterion of optimality \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\) and not from an approximation of the whole problem. We find the value \(\lambda\) by bisection: every term in (29.46) is continuous and does not increase with increasing \(\lambda\), because \(\lambda^{-\eta}\) falls and min and max are not decreasing, so the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}}^{k+1}\) is also continuous and does not increase with \(\lambda\), and the root is found by halving the interval. At \(\eta = 1/2\) and \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}} = 0{,}2\) we test the rule on the truss with three bars. Figure 29.26 shows the designs and the quotients through \(30\) rounds.

We start with equal areas \(78{,}36\) mm² (the axes show the area against the round). Then \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = 30{,}6\), \(21{,}4\) and \(0{,}83\) kJ/m³, so the first bar is the most overloaded and the third almost idle. The first bar thickens, the second stays near its start, the third thins toward zero. In the right half the quotients of the first and second bar approach each other and settle at the dashed value \(\lambda \approx 10{,}6\) kJ/m³, and the third falls to zero, that is, below \(\lambda\). After \(30\) rounds the areas are \((155{,}2;\ 80{,}3;\ 0{,}10)\) mm², against the boundary areas of the optimum \((155{,}29;\ 80{,}38;\ 0)\) mm² and the compliance \(3{,}1735\) J (section 29.5). The compliance falls from \(5{,}1625\) J at equal areas to \(3{,}1735\) J, the first six values are \(5{,}1625\), \(4{,}5785\), \(4{,}1905\), \(3{,}9268\), \(3{,}7433\), \(3{,}6115\) J. We are within \(10^{-3}\) of the final value at \(k = 27\), within \(10^{-6}\) at \(k = 56\). Without a move limit (\(\delta_{\mathrm{p}}\) unbounded) \(5\) rounds sufficed: \(5{,}1625\), \(3{,}6129\), \(3{,}2879\), \(3{,}1870\), \(3{,}1738\), \(3{,}1735\) J. If we put the lower bound at \(1\) mm² instead of nearly zero, the third bar stays at \(1\) mm² and the compliance is \(3{,}1885\) J, which is \(0{,}5\) % larger.

Lemma 29.55 Fixed points of the OC update (negibne točke posodobitve OC)

Let \(A_{\min} > 0\), \(\eta \in (0, 1]\), \(\delta_{\mathrm{p}} > 0\) and let \(\vek A\) be a design with \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge A_{\min}\), \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}} L_{\mathrm{e}}A_{\mathrm{e}} = V_0\) and nonsingular \(\mat K(\vek A)\). Then:

  1. If the update (29.46) leaves \(\vek A\) unchanged, \(\vek A^{k+1} = \vek A^k = \vek A\), with some \(\lambda > 0\), then for every \(e\): \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > A_{\min}\) implies \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = \lambda\) and \(A_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\min}\) implies \(E\varepsilon_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \le \lambda\).
  2. If for some \(\lambda > 0\) these two conditions hold, \(\vek A\) is a fixed point of the rule.
  3. Such an \(\vek A\) is a global minimum of the least compliance problem.
Proof

We must show that the fixed points are exactly the points with the criteria of theorem 29.37. We distinguish two cases by bar.

  1. Let \(A_{\mathrm{e}} > A_{\min}\). Then \(\underline A_{\mathrm{e}} < A_{\mathrm{e}} < \overline A_{\mathrm{e}}\), because \(A_{\min} < A_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \((1 - \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\mathrm{e}} < A_{\mathrm{e}} < (1 + \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\mathrm{e}}\). The result \(\min\{\overline A_{\mathrm{e}}, \max\{\underline A_{\mathrm{e}}, X\}\}\) equals \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\) exactly when \(X = A_{\mathrm{e}}\): if \(X < \underline A_{\mathrm{e}}\) or \(X > \overline A_{\mathrm{e}}\), the result is one of the bounds, which differ from \(A_{\mathrm{e}}\), and if \(X\) lies between the bounds, the result is \(X\). So \(A_{\mathrm{e}}(B_{\mathrm{e}}/\lambda)^\eta = A_{\mathrm{e}}\), hence \(B_{\mathrm{e}} = \lambda\) (\(A_{\mathrm{e}} > 0\)).
  2. Let \(A_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\min}\). The lower bound is \(\max\{A_{\min}, (1 - \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\min}\} = A_{\min}\). The result is \(A_{\min}\) exactly when \(A_{\min}(B_{\mathrm{e}}/\lambda)^\eta \le A_{\min}\), that is, \(B_{\mathrm{e}} \le \lambda\).
  3. The conditions are reversible (steps 1 and 2 are equivalences), which is the second claim. The conditions are those of theorem 29.37 with the lower bound \(A_{\min}\), the problem is convex (theorem 29.34), so by theorem 29.30 a fixed point is a global minimum.

Source: The proof is ours. CHK section 9.1.2 (PDF p. 191) states that at convergence the specific strain energy is constant in every element, which is the first condition of the lemma.

A fixed point is therefore an optimum if all bars have \(A_{\mathrm{e}} \ge A_{\min} > 0\). The lemma does not say whether the loop reaches a fixed point: convergence is not proved, but in our tests the loop always converged. The lower bound is truly necessary.

Example 29.56 A bar with zero area never returns (palica z ničelno ploščino se ne vrne)

Take the same truss with three bars and the force at \(30^\circ\) and a starting design in which the middle bar is removed: \((106{,}07;\ 0;\ 106{,}07)\) mm², volume \(300\) cm³. The matrix \(\mat K\) is nonsingular, since bars \(1\) and \(3\) lie at \(\pm 45^\circ\). If the lower bound is \(A_{\min} = 0\), the right-hand side of (29.46) at \(A_2 = 0\) is \(0\cdot(B_2/\lambda)^\eta = 0\) for every \(B_2\), so the middle bar stays zero forever. The loop converges to the design \((167{,}30;\ 0;\ 44{,}83)\) mm² with compliance \(5{,}1020\) J, not to \(3{,}1735\) J. The condition of the lemma is violated there: \(E\varepsilon_2^2 = 68{,}03\) kJ/m³ is larger than \(\lambda = 17{,}007\) kJ/m³, the middle bar should receive material but cannot. With \(A_{\min} = 10^{-4}\) mm² (and a move limit) the same start converges to \((155{,}29;\ 80{,}38;\ 0)\) mm² and \(3{,}1735\) J, because the result of expression (29.46) at \(A_2 = A_{\min}\) can grow up to \((1 + \delta_{\mathrm{p}})A_{\min}\) and onward.

Programs therefore always use a positive lower bound, even for bars that should vanish. A consequence is that at \(A_{\min} = 1\) mm² the compliance is \(0{,}5\) % above the optimum, so we choose the lower bound small.

CHK (PDF pp. 186-191) shows that OC is a special case of sequential convex approximations: it linearizes the compliance in the intervening variable \(y_{\mathrm{e}} = A_{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha}\) with \(\alpha > 0\), at \(\alpha = 1\) this is CONLIN (PDF p. 186), and the damping is \(\eta = 1/(1 + \alpha)\) (PDF p. 191). Our \(\eta = 1/2\) is therefore CONLIN. We state this, we do not prove it.

Open questions. We tested the loops SLP and OC on small trusses with two and three bars, their convergence is not proved, and it is not proved for larger problems. How OC performs when the variable is the area of an element on a plate and the bars number in the thousands is shown by section 29.9, where the same rule solves a problem of topology.
