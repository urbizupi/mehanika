Chapter 18 Plates

18.9 Finite elements of plates

For the beam, chapter 12 gave an element with two nodes, the Hermite functions and the Timoshenko element, in which the shear term locked the mesh until we took it in one Gauss point. The plate of chapter 18 has the same story in two dimensions: the field \(w(x, y)\) has to be replaced by a piecewise polynomial field on a mesh of rectangles, and the matrices assembled as for the beam. Two questions are new. The first: beam elements meet at a point, but plate elements meet along an edge, so we have to check what must hold along the edge. The second: the Mindlin element has three unknowns in a node and a shear term that locks in two directions at once. We compute both answers on the plates of sections 18.5 and 18.8.

From history

The framework method (okvirna metoda), which replaced a body by a mesh of bars, was published for plane elasticity and the bending of plates in 1941. COOK (PDF p. 24) says of it that it can be seen as a forerunner of finite element analysis and not as its early form: it was not used for bodies of arbitrary shape, and instead of dividing the body into smaller pieces it replaced the body by elements of a different kind.

Let us return to plate elements as we know them today. For the Kirchhoff plate (definition 18.2) the energy contains second derivatives of the field. Let us see what this means for an element.

The two plates of the chapter are the Kirchhoff plate of section 18.3 and the Mindlin plate of section 18.8, where the idealization is different: the normal stays straight, but is not perpendicular to the middle plane. The running example is the steel plate of example B of section 18.5: a square of side \(a = 0{,}5\) m, simply supported on all edges (hard support), \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\nu = 0{,}3\), a uniform pressure \(q = 10\) kPa in the direction of \(w\) and the shear correction factor \(k = 5/6\) (definition 5.86). We change the thickness through the ratio \(a/h\). Because of the two planes of symmetry we compute only a quarter of the plate, which we divide along a side into \(n_{\mathrm{e}}\) equal rectangular elements, so into \(n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) elements.

18.9.1 The Kirchhoff element needs a continuous slope

For the Hermite element of the beam the value and the slope were given in a node (definition 12.1), so \(v\) and \(v'\) were continuous on the whole mesh. For the plate let us look at the simplest possibility, that only the deflection \(w\) is continuous and the normal slope jumps across the edge of the elements. Let us draw one section, perpendicular to the edge (figure 18.26).

On the left we draw the axes \(x\) and \(w\) and the field that runs along two sloping segments meeting in a point on the edge. The slope on the left is \(-J/2\), on the right \(J/2\), so the jump of the slope is \(J\). We cannot afford such a field, because the second derivative \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), which enters the energy (definition 18.14), does not exist at the point on the edge. Let us show with a number what this means for the energy: we smooth the jump. On the right we take a strip of width \(\ell\) around the edge, outside it we keep the field, and in the strip the slope passes uniformly from \(-J/2\) to \(J/2\). The smoothed field has \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = J/\ell\) in the strip, the height difference at the centre is \(J\ell/8\), which is also the largest deviation from the field with the jump, and the curvature is the larger, the narrower the strip.

Lemma 18.58 A Kirchhoff element needs a continuous slope (Kirchhoffov element zahteva zvezen nagib)

Let \(J > 0\) and \(\ell > 0\). Let \(\ell\) be the width of the strip \(|x| \le \ell/2\) along the edge \(x = 0\) and \(w\) a field that depends only on \(x\), twice continuously differentiable on the strip, with slopes \(w' = -J/2\) at \(x = -\ell/2\) and \(w' = J/2\) at \(x = \ell/2\). Then:

  1. The energy of the plate (definition 18.14) on the strip, per unit length of the edge, is \(\tfrac12 D\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} (w'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x \ge D J^2/(2\ell)\), with equality for the field \(w = J\ell/8 + J x^2/(2\ell)\). When \(\ell \to 0\) the energy goes to infinity.
  2. The bicubic Hermite rectangle (its field is described in step 4 of the proof) has \(w\) and the normal slope continuous along the edge: the values of \(w\) and \(\partial w/\partial n\) on the edge are determined only by the numbers in the two nodes of this edge.
  3. A rectangle with the same cubic course of the normal slope along the edge, but with two values instead of four (the values of \(\partial w/\partial n\) in the two nodes), does not have a continuous normal slope.
Proof

We have to show first the lower bound of the energy of the field with a jump, then for two families of rectangles whether \(w\) and \(\partial w/\partial n\) agree across the edge.

  1. The field depends only on \(x\), so \(w_{\mathrm{yy}} = w_{\mathrm{xy}} = 0\). The energy density \(\tfrac12 D[(\nabla^2 w)^2 - 2(1-\nu)(w_{\mathrm{xx}}w_{\mathrm{yy}} - w_{\mathrm{xy}}^2)]\) is \(\tfrac12 D\,(w'')^2\), and the energy per unit length of the edge is its integral over the strip.
  2. By the fundamental theorem (theorem 3.40) \(\int_{-\ell/2}^{\ell/2} w''\,\mathrm{d}x = w'(\ell/2) - w'(-\ell/2) = J\).
  3. The function \((w'' - J/\ell)^2\) is nonnegative, so its integral is nonnegative (lemma 3.39). Expanding: \(0 \le \int (w'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x - \tfrac{2J}{\ell}\int w''\,\mathrm{d}x + \tfrac{J^2}{\ell^2}\,\ell = \int (w'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x - \tfrac{J^2}{\ell}\), where we used step 2. This is \(\int (w'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x \ge J^2/\ell\), so point 1. For \(w = J\ell/8 + Jx^2/(2\ell)\) we have \(w'' = J/\ell\), \(w' = Jx/\ell\) (slope \(\pm J/2\) at the ends) and the integral is exactly \(\ell\,(J/\ell)^2 = J^2/\ell\).
  4. For point 2 take a rectangle \([0, l_{\mathrm{x}}] \times [0, l_{\mathrm{y}}]\) and the Hermite functions \(H_1, \dots, H_4\) in \(x\) on \([0, l_{\mathrm{x}}]\) and in \(y\) on \([0, l_{\mathrm{y}}]\) (definition 12.1). The field is a sum of products \(H_{\mathrm{a}}(x)\,H_{\mathrm{c}}(y)\) with the numbers in the four nodes: \(w\) with \(H_1(x)H_1(y)\) in the node \((0, 0)\), \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) with \(H_2(x)H_1(y)\), \(w_{\mathrm{y}}\) with \(H_1(x)H_2(y)\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) with \(H_2(x)H_2(y)\), and in the other nodes by replacing \(H_1, H_2\) in \(x\) by \(H_3, H_4\) at \(x = l_{\mathrm{x}}\) and in \(y\) at \(y = l_{\mathrm{y}}\). In all this is 16 numbers.
  5. On the edge \(x = 0\) we have by definition \(H_1(0) = 1\), \(H_2(0) = H_3(0) = H_4(0) = 0\) and \(H_2'(0) = 1\), \(H_1'(0) = H_3'(0) = H_4'(0) = 0\). Only the nodes \(1 = (0, 0)\) and \(4 = (0, l_{\mathrm{y}})\) remain, and \(w(0, y) = w_1 H_1(y) + (w_{\mathrm{y}})_1 H_2(y) + w_4 H_3(y) + (w_{\mathrm{y}})_4 H_4(y)\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}}(0, y) = (w_{\mathrm{x}})_1 H_1(y) + (w_{\mathrm{xy}})_1 H_2(y) + (w_{\mathrm{x}})_4 H_3(y) + (w_{\mathrm{xy}})_4 H_4(y)\). Both functions are determined by the eight numbers of the two nodes of the edge. The neighbouring element has these nodes and these numbers in common, so on the common edge \(w\) and the normal slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) agree. The same holds for the other edges.
  6. For point 3: with 12 degrees of freedom (without \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\) in the nodes) \(w_{\mathrm{x}}(0, y)\) is a cubic polynomial in \(y\), but has only two free numbers at the nodes, the values at \(y = 0\) and \(y = l_{\mathrm{y}}\). Two cubic polynomials with equal values at both end points differ by \(y\,(l_{\mathrm{y}} - y)\,(\alpha + \beta y)\), which for every pair \((\alpha, \beta) \ne (0, 0)\) is a function that is not zero. For this element \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) depend also on numbers in the nodes off the edge (on the edge \(x = 0\) on \(w\) and \(w_{\mathrm{y}}\) in the nodes \(2\) and \(3\), SymPy). Two neighbouring elements that share the nodes of the edge have these numbers different, so they can have different \(\alpha, \beta\) and the normal slope jumps across the edge. By point 1 a field assembled in this way does not have a finite energy of the Kirchhoff plate.

Source: EP (p. 568-569, section 8.5.3) separates conforming elements (skladni), in which \(w\) and both slopes are continuous, from nonconforming ones (neskladni) and says that the conforming rectangle has \(w\), \(\partial w/\partial x\), \(\partial w/\partial y\) and \(\partial^2 w/\partial x\partial y\) in a node, 16 degrees of freedom, the nonconforming one three in a node, 12 degrees of freedom, and that the normal slope along the edge has there only two values for a cubic course. ONATE2 (PDF p. 278-280, section 5.4.1) describes the same nonconforming element with twelve terms of the polynomial (MZC). The estimate by the Cauchy-Schwarz procedure and the comparison of two cubics are ours.

Point 3 is a case that does not pass the test of a continuous slope: the nonconforming element has a polynomial of the right degree, but one number is missing in a node, so the normal slope on the edge is not determined by the numbers of the two nodes of the edge alone. The nonconformity does not rule the element out: ONATE2 (PDF p. 285-286) says that it passes the convergence test we mention at the end of the section, and so it converges as the mesh of rectangles is refined, while for arbitrary quadrilaterals there is no such guarantee. The conforming element (point 2) pays dearly for it: in a node, besides the deflection and both slopes, there is the mixed derivative \(w_{\mathrm{xy}}\), the twist of the middle plane, which tells by how much the slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) changes per unit length in the direction \(y\). This element is tied to rectangles and cannot simply be transferred to a triangle. So EP and ONATE2 name several elements that differ in the way they solve this (ONATE2, PDF p. 277-302 for rectangles and triangles, 380-385 for discrete Kirchhoff elements, DKT). We do not treat these elements here.

The Mindlin plate avoids this difficulty. Its energy contains only first derivatives of \(w\) and of both rotations \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}}\) (section 18.8), so for a finite energy it suffices that \(w\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}}\) are continuous functions with piecewise continuous first derivatives, and the slope may jump across the edge of the elements. The way to this element is therefore shorter, but it has its own price, which we compute in the third section.

18.9.2 The Mindlin rectangle

Let us draw the element (figure 18.27). We take a rectangle with sides \(l_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(l_{\mathrm{y}}\) from the middle plane of the plate and place nodes \(1, 2, 3, 4\) in its four corners counterclockwise. Each corner brings three numbers: the deflection \(w_{\mathrm{i}}\) and the rotations \(\theta_{\mathrm{xi}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{yi}}\) (in the figure for node \(3\) as arrows in the directions \(x\) and \(y\)). In all this is \(12\) numbers, the column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\). Because we look for an arbitrary quotient of these numbers over the element, we introduce the natural coordinates \(s\) and \(t\) with the origin at the centre, which run along the sides from \(-1\) to \(1\), \(s = 2(x - x_{\mathrm{c}})/l_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(t = 2(y - y_{\mathrm{c}})/l_{\mathrm{y}}\).

Definition 18.59 The bilinear Mindlin rectangle (bilinearni Mindlinov pravokotnik)

Let \(R_{\mathrm{e}} = [x_{\mathrm{c}} - l_{\mathrm{x}}/2,\ x_{\mathrm{c}} + l_{\mathrm{x}}/2] \times [y_{\mathrm{c}} - l_{\mathrm{y}}/2,\ y_{\mathrm{c}} + l_{\mathrm{y}}/2]\) be a rectangle in the middle plane of the plate and \(s = 2(x - x_{\mathrm{c}})/l_{\mathrm{x}}\), \(t = 2(y - y_{\mathrm{c}})/l_{\mathrm{y}}\). The shape functions (definition 7.2) are products of the linear shape functions of chapter 7 in \(s\) and in \(t\),

\[N_1 = \tfrac14(1-s)(1-t) , \quad N_2 = \tfrac14(1+s)(1-t) , \quad N_3 = \tfrac14(1+s)(1+t) , \quad N_4 = \tfrac14(1-s)(1+t) . \tag{18.71}\]

The bilinear Mindlin rectangle is an element with the column of \(12\) numbers \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = (w_1, \theta_{\mathrm{x}1}, \theta_{\mathrm{y}1}, \dots, w_4, \theta_{\mathrm{x}4}, \theta_{\mathrm{y}4})\), which forms from them three fields on \(R_{\mathrm{e}}\) with the same interpolation,

\[w_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^4 N_{\mathrm{i}}\,w_{\mathrm{i}} , \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{xh}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^4 N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\theta_{\mathrm{xi}} , \qquad \theta_{\mathrm{yh}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^4 N_{\mathrm{i}}\,\theta_{\mathrm{yi}} . \tag{18.72}\]

Every function \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) equals \(1\) in node \(i\) and \(0\) in the other three, so \(w_{\mathrm{i}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{xi}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{yi}}\) are the values of the fields in the node. The fields are continuous across the edges of the elements, because there are two nodes on an edge and a bilinear polynomial is on the edge a linear function, determined by the values at both end points. Their derivatives jump across the edge.

Source: ONATE2 (PDF p. 343, section 6.5.1): the element Q4 has standard bilinear shape functions. EP (PDF p. 578, figure 8.6.2a): the four-node \(C^0\) rectangle for plates. The notation with \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) and the \(12\) numbers in the order \((w, \theta_{\mathrm{x}}, \theta_{\mathrm{y}})\) by nodes is ours.

What do we get from these fields? The bending energy by theorem 18.52 is \(\tfrac12\int \vek\kappa^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\,\vek\kappa\,\mathrm{d}A\) with the bending curvatures \(\vek\kappa = (\theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}},\ \theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}},\ \theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}} + \theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{x}})\) and \(\mat D_{\mathrm{b}} = D\begin{pmatrix} 1 & \nu & 0 \\ \nu & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & (1-\nu)/2 \end{pmatrix}\), and the shear energy is \(\tfrac12\,kGh\int |\vek\gamma|^2\,\mathrm{d}A\) with the shear strains \(\vek\gamma = (w_{,\mathrm{x}} - \theta_{\mathrm{x}},\ w_{,\mathrm{y}} - \theta_{\mathrm{y}})\). From this follows the matrix of the element.

Lemma 18.60 The stiffness matrix of the Mindlin rectangle (togostna matrika Mindlinovega pravokotnika)

Let the element of definition 18.59 have constant \(D\), \(\nu\), \(G\), \(h\) and \(k\). Let \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\) be a \(3 \times 12\) matrix and \(\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\) a \(2 \times 12\) matrix that have for each node \(i\) three columns

\[\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^{(i)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}} \\ 0 & N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}} & N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}} \end{pmatrix} , \qquad \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}^{(i)} = \begin{pmatrix} N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}} & -N_{\mathrm{i}} & 0 \\ N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}} & 0 & -N_{\mathrm{i}} \end{pmatrix} , \tag{18.73}\]

where \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}} = (2/l_{\mathrm{x}})\,\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial s\) and \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}} = (2/l_{\mathrm{y}})\,\partial N_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial t\). Then:

  1. \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) on \(R_{\mathrm{e}}\), and the energy of the element is \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with
    \[\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} = \int_{\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\,\mathrm{d}A = \frac{l_{\mathrm{x}} l_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1} \mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t , \qquad \mat k_{\mathrm{s}} = kGh\int_{\mathrm{R}_\mathrm{e}} \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\,\mathrm{d}A = kGh\,\frac{l_{\mathrm{x}} l_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1} \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t . \tag{18.74}\]
  2. The matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) are symmetric and positive semidefinite.
  3. The column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) of the field \(w = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 y\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}} = a_1\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}} = a_2\) (a rigid motion, three numbers \(a_0, a_1, a_2\)) has \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\).
Proof

We have to show that the curvature and the shear strain are linear in \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), that the energy is a quadratic form and that a rigid motion has zero energy.

  1. The fields (18.72) are linear in \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), so are their derivatives, and with the derivatives in \(x\) and \(y\) from the chain rule \(\partial/\partial x = (2/l_{\mathrm{x}})\,\partial/\partial s\), \(\partial/\partial y = (2/l_{\mathrm{y}})\,\partial/\partial t\) we have \(\theta_{\mathrm{xh},\mathrm{x}} = \sum N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}\theta_{\mathrm{xi}}\) (the second columns of each node in the first row of the matrix \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\)), \(\theta_{\mathrm{yh},\mathrm{y}} = \sum N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}}\theta_{\mathrm{yi}}\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{xh},\mathrm{y}} + \theta_{\mathrm{yh},\mathrm{x}} = \sum (N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}}\theta_{\mathrm{xi}} + N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}\theta_{\mathrm{yi}})\), and \(w_{\mathrm{h},\mathrm{x}} - \theta_{\mathrm{xh}} = \sum (N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}w_{\mathrm{i}} - N_{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{\mathrm{xi}})\), \(w_{\mathrm{h},\mathrm{y}} - \theta_{\mathrm{yh}} = \sum (N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}}w_{\mathrm{i}} - N_{\mathrm{i}}\theta_{\mathrm{yi}})\). This is \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  2. We insert into the energy: \(\tfrac12\int \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mathrm{d}A + \tfrac12\,kGh\int \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\,\mathrm{d}A\). The column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) does not depend on \(x\) and \(y\) and goes in front of the integral, which gives the forms (18.74). The last equality follows from the rectangle integral: by theorem 4.9 and the linear change of variable \(x = x_{\mathrm{c}} + l_{\mathrm{x}} s/2\), \(y = y_{\mathrm{c}} + l_{\mathrm{y}} t/2\) in each single integral, the integral over \(R_{\mathrm{e}}\) becomes an integral over \([-1, 1]^2\) multiplied by \((l_{\mathrm{x}}/2)(l_{\mathrm{y}}/2)\).
  3. The matrices are symmetric, because each integrand is symmetric. The quadratic form \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d = \int \vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\,\mathrm{d}A \ge 0\), because \(\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\) is positive definite for \(0 \le \nu < 1\) (a sum of squares \(D[(\kappa_1 + \nu\kappa_2)^2 + (1-\nu^2)\,\kappa_2^2 + \tfrac{1-\nu}2\,\kappa_3^2]\), and this is nonnegative), \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\vek d = kGh\int |\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}|^2\,\mathrm{d}A \ge 0\).
  4. For a rigid motion \(\theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} = \theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} = \theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}} + \theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{x}} = 0\) and \(w_{,\mathrm{x}} - \theta_{\mathrm{x}} = a_1 - a_1 = 0\), \(w_{,\mathrm{y}} - \theta_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), so \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\) and \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\). The field is bilinear in \(s, t\) (in fact linear), so the element represents it exactly.

Source: ONATE2 (PDF p. 332-339, section 6.3) gives the bending and shear stiffness matrices of the element with \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\), EP (PDF p. 578) says that the shear terms differ from the bending ones. The proof by the quadratic form is ours.

The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) is therefore 12 × 12, symmetric and positive semidefinite. Its kernel contains the three rigid motions of point 3. For the whole plate the number of zero eigenvalues will depend on how we integrate, and this is the subject of the following sections. First we have to say how the integral (18.74) is computed at all.

18.9.3 Gauss quadrature on a rectangle

For the beam the Gauss rule (definition 7.24) computed an integral over \([-1, 1]\). For a rectangle in the coordinates \(s, t\) two choices offer themselves, shown in figure 18.27: four points \(s, t = \pm 1/\sqrt3\), which we get as a product of two two-point rules, and one point at the centre, where \(s = t = 0\). Each such rule is a product of the rules of chapter 7, which means that all Gauss points have the weight equal to the product of the weights.

Lemma 18.61 Gauss quadrature on a rectangle (Gaussovo pravilo na pravokotniku)

Let \(g(s, t) = \sum_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{j}} c_{\mathrm{ij}}\,s^i t^j\) be a polynomial in \(s\) and \(t\). Then:

  1. If \(i \le 3\) and \(j \le 3\) in all terms, then \(\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1} g\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t = \sum_{\mathrm{a}=1}^{2}\sum_{\mathrm{b}=1}^{2} g(\xi_{\mathrm{a}}, \xi_{\mathrm{b}})\) with \(\xi_{1,2} = \mp 1/\sqrt3\) (weight \(1\) in each point).
  2. If \(i \le 1\) and \(j \le 1\) in all terms, then \(\int_{-1}^{1}\!\int_{-1}^{1} g\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t = 4\,g(0, 0)\).
  3. The integrands of the matrices (18.74), that is all entries of the matrices \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}\), are polynomials in \(s, t\) of degree at most \(2\) in each variable. The four-point rule computes them exactly. The one-point rule does not compute them exactly in general, it is exact only for the bilinear terms \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}N_{\mathrm{j},\mathrm{y}}\) of \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\).
Proof

We have to show that the exactness of the rule on \([-1, 1]\) implies the exactness of the product, then count the degrees.

  1. By theorem 4.9 \(\int\!\!\int g = \int_{-1}^{1}\big(\int_{-1}^{1} g(s, t)\,\mathrm{d}t\big)\mathrm{d}s\). For each \(s\) the function \(g(s, \cdot)\) is a polynomial of degree at most \(3\) in \(t\), so by lemma 7.25 (\(q = 2\), degree \(2q - 1 = 3\)) the inner integral equals \(\sum_{\mathrm{b}} g(s, \xi_{\mathrm{b}})\). This sum is a polynomial in \(s\) of degree at most \(3\), so by the same lemma again \(\int_{-1}^{1}\sum_{\mathrm{b}} g(s, \xi_{\mathrm{b}})\,\mathrm{d}s = \sum_{\mathrm{a}}\sum_{\mathrm{b}} g(\xi_{\mathrm{a}}, \xi_{\mathrm{b}})\).
  2. The same for \(q = 1\) (\(2q - 1 = 1\), the point \(0\) with weight \(2\)): the inner integral is \(2g(s, 0)\), the outer one \(2\cdot 2\,g(0, 0)\).
  3. The functions \(N_{\mathrm{i}}\) are bilinear, so \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}\) depends only on \(t\), and linearly, and \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{y}}\) only on \(s\), also linearly. A product of two such functions has degree at most \(2\) in both variables, for \(N_{\mathrm{i}}N_{\mathrm{j}}\) the degree is \(2\) in \(s\) and \(2\) in \(t\), for \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}N_{\mathrm{j},\mathrm{x}}\) it is \(2\) in \(t\), and nowhere more. By point 1 the four-point rule is exact. The one-point rule is not exact for \(N_{\mathrm{i}}N_{\mathrm{j}}\): for \(N_1^2 = \tfrac{1}{16}(1 - s)^2(1 - t)^2\) the integral over \([-1, 1]^2\) equals \(\tfrac{1}{16}\cdot\tfrac83\cdot\tfrac83 = \tfrac49\), while the rule gives \(4\,N_1(0, 0)^2 = 4\cdot\tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac14\) (SymPy). The term \(N_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{x}}N_{\mathrm{j},\mathrm{y}}\) in \(\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}^\T\mat D_{\mathrm{b}}\mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\) is bilinear in \(s, t\), so the one-point rule is exact for it.

Source: EP (PDF p. 579, section 8.6.4): the Gauss-Legendre rule on the square \([-1, 1]^2\) in natural coordinates, EP (PDF p. 582) separates full (\(N \times N\)), selective and reduced (\((N - 1) \times (N - 1)\)) integration, ONATE2 (PDF p. 343): for the rectangle it requires full \(2 \times 2\) integration. The proof from lemma 7.25 and theorem 4.9 is ours.

So full integration (polno integriranje) on rectangles is completely exact: the matrix (18.74) does not differ from its exact value, and \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) equals the one with the integral over the whole area. The other choice is the interesting one. The shear term computed in one point is not exact, although the integrand has only degree \(2\), and therefore no longer has the same kernel. This is called selective integration (izbirno integriranje): the bending term \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) in four points, the shear term \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) at the centre with weight \(4\). The connection with the one Gauss point of the beam (lemma 12.37) we will now test on the plate.

18.9.4 Shear locking in two directions

In chapter 12 the shear term of the Timoshenko element acted for a slender beam as a penalty (lemma 12.35): the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) has a large penalty term and the system forces the shear strain in the Gauss points towards zero. For the plate the shear strain \(\vek\gamma\) is a vector with two components, so each Gauss point has two constraints instead of one. First let us compare the number of constraints with the number of unknowns. A quarter of the plate has \(n_{\mathrm{e}}\) elements on each side, so \((n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1)^2\) nodes and \(3(n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1)^2\) numbers. The supports remove \(w\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}}\) on the edge \(x = 0\) and \(w\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}}\) on the edge \(y = 0\) (hard support), on the symmetry side \(x = a/2\) we have \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), and on \(y = a/2\) we have \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\). The prescribed deflections number \(2n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1\) (the edges \(x = 0\) and \(y = 0\) share a node), the rotations \(\theta_{\mathrm{y}}\) number \((n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1) + (n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1) - 1 = 2n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1\) (the edges \(x = 0\) and \(y = a/2\) share a node) and so do the rotations \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}}\). In all \(3(2n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1)\) numbers are prescribed and the free ones are

\[3(n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1)^2 - 3(2n_{\mathrm{e}} + 1) = 3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \qquad \text{unknowns.} \tag{18.75}\]
Lemma 18.62 Shear locking of the plate is a penalty in two directions (strižno zaklepanje plošče je kazen v dveh smereh)

Let the mesh be \(n_{\mathrm{e}} \times n_{\mathrm{e}}\) rectangular elements on a quarter of the square plate of this section, with the supports and symmetries of the paragraph above. Let \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)} = kGh\sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,\tfrac{l_{\mathrm{x}}l_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\,\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})^\T\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\) be the shear matrix computed with a rule of \(q\) points (\(q = 4\) full, \(q = 1\) selective), and \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) the matrix that has for every element and each of its Gauss points \(g\) two rows, one for each of the two rows of the matrix \(\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}}\,l_{\mathrm{x}}l_{\mathrm{y}}/4}\;\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\), multiplied by the selection matrix of the element (definition 7.11, without the columns of prescribed unknowns). Then:

  1. The reduced system \((\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat K_{\mathrm{s}})\,\vek d = \vek f\) is a penalty system (definition 12.27), \((\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + kGh\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek d = \vek f\) with the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) in the place of \(\mat K\), the constraint \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) and the penalty number \(\alpha = kGh\). The constraint \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) means that both components of \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) are zero in every Gauss point of every element.
  2. The matrix \(\mat C_4\) has \(8\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) rows and \(\mat C_1\) has \(2\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\), and there are \(3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) unknowns. For \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\) the matrix \(\mat C_4\) has exactly \(3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) pivots (full column rank), \(\mat C_1\) has \(2\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) pivots (full row rank) and the kernel of \(\mat C_1\) has \(n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) free numbers (NumPy).
  3. For full integration (\(q = 4\)) the matrix \(\mat C_4^\T\mat C_4\) is positive definite, its smallest eigenvalue \(\nu_4 > 0\), and for the solution \(|\vek d| \le |\vek f|/(kGh\,\nu_4)\). When the plate becomes thinner (\(h \to 0\) at an unchanged load \(\vek f\)), this bound grows only as \(1/h\), while the Kirchhoff deflection grows as \(1/h^3\), so the ratio of the displacement to the deflection goes to zero at least as \(h^2\).
Proof

We have to show that the shear matrix is a product of a column and a row and that this form is preserved by assembly, and then estimate the solution in the case when the constraints are too many.

  1. As in lemma 12.35: \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}}) = \mat B_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is a vector of two numbers, so \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = kGh\sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,\tfrac{l_{\mathrm{x}}l_{\mathrm{y}}}{4}\,|\mat B_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}|^2 = kGh\,|\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}|^2\) with the rows \(\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\) of all points of the element. The difference of the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)}\) and \(kGh\,\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\) is symmetric with a zero quadratic form, so zero (lemma 7.16). In assembly each row contributes a product \(\vek c^\T\vek c\) with its selected column, as in step 2 of lemma 12.35. The right-hand side \(\vek f\) contains only the loads and does not depend on \(kGh\).
  2. The number of unknowns by equation (18.75) is \(3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\). The constraints for \(q = 4\) are \(4 \cdot 2 \cdot n_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = 8\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\), for \(q = 1\) they are \(1 \cdot 2 \cdot n_{\mathrm{e}}^2 = 2\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\). We computed the number of pivots of the matrix with NumPy for the meshes \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\) (table below). This is not a proof for every mesh.
  3. If \(\mat C_4\) has a pivot in every column, then \(\mat C_4\vek d = \vek 0\) implies \(\vek d = \vek 0\). The symmetric matrix \(\mat C_4^\T\mat C_4\) has the quadratic form \(|\mat C_4\vek d|^2 > 0\) for \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) and is positive definite, its smallest eigenvalue \(\nu_4\) is positive and \(\vek d^\T\mat C_4^\T\mat C_4\vek d \ge \nu_4|\vek d|^2\) (theorem 10.26 with \(\mat M = \mat I\)).
  4. Let \(\vek d\) be the solution. We multiply the system by \(\vek d^\T\): \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d + kGh\,|\mat C_4\vek d|^2 = \vek f^\T\vek d\). Because \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d \ge 0\), the left side is at least \(kGh\,\nu_4|\vek d|^2\), and the right side at most \(|\vek f|\,|\vek d|\) (Cauchy-Schwarz, lemma 8.23). So \(kGh\,\nu_4|\vek d|^2 \le |\vek f|\,|\vek d|\), which gives the estimate. The bound is proportional to \(1/h\), the Kirchhoff deflection to \(1/D\), that is to \(1/h^3\) (equation (18.6)), so their ratio is proportional to \(h^2\).

Source: ONATE2 (PDF p. 339-340, section 6.4.1): the plate of Reissner and Mindlin locks like the Timoshenko beam when \(t \to 0\), so the solution is not different from \(\vek 0\) only if \(\mat K_{\mathrm{s}}\) is singular, and this can be achieved by reduced integration. EP (PDF p. 578): selective integration of the shear terms alleviates locking. The notation with the penalty matrix, the counting of the constraints and the estimate \(|\vek d| \le |\vek f|/(kGh\,\nu_4)\) are ours.

The estimate of point 3 is still somewhat crude, but it tells the essence. The system has more constraints than unknowns (\(8\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2 > 3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) on a quarter of the plate), so the requirement \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\) in all Gauss points allows only the displacement zero. As a technique it cannot reach the Kirchhoff solution, which has \(\vek\gamma = \vek 0\) and is very far from zero. Let us also count how strong the penalty is. The ratio of the shear and bending stiffness, measured by the penalty number against \(D/a^2\), is

\[\frac{kGh\,a^2}{D} = 12(1 - \nu^2)\,k\,\frac GE\,\Big(\frac ah\Big)^2 = 6(1 - \nu)\,k\,\Big(\frac ah\Big)^2 = 3{,}5\,\Big(\frac ah\Big)^2 , \tag{18.76}\]

since \(G/E = 1/(2(1 + \nu))\) and \(12(1 - \nu^2)/(2(1 + \nu)) = 6(1 - \nu)\). At \(a/h = 10\) this is \(350\), at \(100\) it is \(3{,}5 \cdot 10^4\) and at \(1000\) it is \(3{,}5 \cdot 10^6\).

Let us also look at the meshes we computed (table). The columns give the number of constraints and the actual number of pivots, computed with NumPy.

For \(q = 4\) the number of pivots always equals the number of unknowns, for \(q = 1\) it equals the number of constraints, so the kernel is not empty. This is no accident, but we do not prove it: for a general mesh we would have to show that the constraints are independent, which goes beyond this section. Let us draw what locking did to the numbers (figure 18.28).

On the axes \(a/h\) runs from \(3\) to \(3000\) and the ratio \(w_{\mathrm{c}}/w_{\mathrm{M}}\) from \(10^{-6}\) to \(1\). The solid curves are all four meshes with full integration. At \(a/h = 3\) all are close to \(1\), which corresponds to the small penalty in equation (18.76) and to a thick plate, in which shear really matters. As the plate gets thinner the curves fall: for \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 2\) the ratio is \(0{,}0110\) at \(a/h = 100\) and \(0{,}0001\) at \(a/h = 1000\), so it decreases by about a factor of \(100\) when the ratio \(a/h\) grows tenfold. This is exactly the \((h/a)^2\) from the estimate \(|\vek d| \le |\vek f|/(kGh\,\nu_4)\): the bound of the displacement grows only as \(1/h\), the exact value as \(1/h^3\), so the ratio falls as \(h^2\). The dashed curve of selective integration at \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 4\) is horizontal at \(0{,}995\) from \(a/h = 30\) on.

18.9.5 Selective integration removes locking

Let us complete the picture with the computed ratios \(w_{\mathrm{c}}/w_{\mathrm{M}}\):

\(a/h\) integration \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 2\) \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 4\) \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 8\) \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 16\)

10 selective 0,9846 0,9964 0,9991 0,9998

With selective integration the ratios are almost equal at \(a/h = 100\) and \(1000\): the shear stiffness is ten times higher, and the solution does not change. This is stated by the following theorem.

Theorem 18.63 Selective integration does not lock (izbirno integriranje ne zaklene)

Let the plate of this section have the mesh \(n_{\mathrm{e}} \times n_{\mathrm{e}}\) and the shear constraints \(\mat C_1\) of lemma 18.62 (\(q = 1\)). We divide the system by \(D\) and measure lengths in units of \(a\). So \(\mat K = \mat K_{\mathrm{b}}/D\) and the constraints \(\mat C_1\) do not depend on \(h\), the penalty number is \(\alpha = 3{,}5\,(a/h)^2\) from equation (18.76). Let the following conditions hold:

  • (P1) the matrix \(\mat C_1\) has \(2n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) pivots (full row rank),
  • (P2) \(\vek d^\T\mat K\,\vek d > 0\) for every \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat C_1\vek d = \vek 0\).

Then:

  1. There is exactly one solution \(\vek d^*\) of the minimum energy problem with the constraints \(\mat C_1\vek d = \vek 0\) and exactly one column \(\vek\lambda\) (theorem 12.26), which does not depend on \(a/h\).
  2. For \(\alpha \ge 1\) we have \(|\vek d_\alpha - \vek d^*| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}\), where \(\mu > 0\) is the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat K + \mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\) (theorem 12.28). So the ratio \(w_{\mathrm{c}}/w_{\mathrm{K}}\) has a finite limit as \(a/h \to \infty\), which depends only on the mesh.
  3. The conditions (P1) and (P2) hold for \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\) (NumPy: the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat K + \mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\) is \(0{,}2914\), \(0{,}1678\), \(0{,}06788\), \(0{,}02177\), \(0{,}006169\)).
Proof

We have to show that the hypotheses of theorem 12.26 and theorem 12.28 are met and that after scaling the system does not depend on \(h\) except through \(\alpha\).

  1. By lemma 18.62 the system is \((\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + kGh\,\mat C_1^\T\mat C_1)\vek d = \vek f\). We divide by \(D\): \((\mat K + \alpha\,\mat C_1^\T\mat C_1)\,\vek d = \vek f/D\) with \(\alpha = kGh/D\), in units of \(a\) (\(a = 1\)) this is \(\alpha = 3{,}5\,(a/h)^2\) by equation (18.76). The matrix \(\mat K\) and the constraints \(\mat C_1\) depend only on the mesh and \(\nu\), not on \(h\). The right-hand side \(\vek f/D\) grows as \(1/h^3\), so we denote \(\vek d = (qa^4/D)\,\vek\delta\), which gives the same system for \(\vek\delta\) with a right-hand side \(\vek f_1\) independent of \(h\).
  2. Condition (P1) is the pivot condition of definition 12.25. Condition (P2) is the requirement of theorem 12.26 that the energy is positive on the kernel of the constraints, and is equivalent to the positive definiteness of the matrix \(\mat K + \mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\): if \(\vek d^\T(\mat K + \mat C_1^\T\mat C_1)\vek d = 0\), both nonnegative terms are zero, so \(\mat C_1\vek d = \vek 0\) and \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = 0\), by (P2) \(\vek d = \vek 0\). This is exactly what we checked with NumPy, and the numbers are in point 3.
  3. Theorem 12.26 gives point 1, and theorem 12.28 gives the estimate of point 2 (point 3 of that theorem). Both hold for every \(\alpha\), so also for \(3{,}5\,(a/h)^2\).

Source: ONATE2 (PDF p. 340 and 343-344): the shear stiffness is for selective integration singular because of the smaller quadrature and so does not lock, the stress resultants are computed in \(2 \times 2\) points, and the shear forces are optimal at the centre. The proof is ours.

The theorem has three things to think over. The first: at \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 4\) the ratio is \(0{,}99486\) at \(a/h = 100\) and \(0{,}99485\) at \(a/h = 300, 1000, 3000\). To five places this is already the limit of point 2. It is \(0{,}5\ \%\) below the exact value, so it is an error of discretization and not of locking. The second: the error falls with a finer mesh by a factor of \(4\) at each halving of the side of the element (\(0{,}0230\), \(0{,}0052\), \(0{,}0013\), \(0{,}0003\) at \(n_{\mathrm{e}} = 2, 4, 8, 16\)), which corresponds to the square of the size of the element, but we did not prove this. The third: the theorem says only that a limit exists, not that it is good. We saw this in the table, but it is not a consequence of the theorem.

The question remains whether the selective element is really safe.

18.9.6 Near miss: the two spurious modes of one element

Hypothesis (P2) is the one that selective integration can spoil. Let us look at a single element without supports, as figure 18.29 shows. With full integration the free element has exactly three zero eigenvalues (NumPy), that is the three rigid motions of lemma 18.60, as it must be. With selective integration it has five.

Lemma 18.64 The two spurious modes of the selective element (lažna načina izbirnega elementa)

Let a free square element have side \(l = 0{,}1\) m, \(h = 0{,}01\) m and its centre at the origin, other quantities as in this section. Let

\[(\mathrm a)\ \ w = x\,y ,\ \ \theta_{\mathrm{x}} = \theta_{\mathrm{y}} = 0 , \qquad (\mathrm b)\ \ w = 0 ,\ \ \theta_{\mathrm{x}} = y ,\ \ \theta_{\mathrm{y}} = -x \tag{18.77}\]

be fields on the element, both bilinear in \(s, t\), and \(\vek d_{\mathrm{a}}\), \(\vek d_{\mathrm{b}}\) their columns of nodal values (in SI units). Then:

  1. \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\) for both fields, so \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d = 0\).
  2. \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = (y, x)\) for (a) and \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = (-y, x)\) for (b), both equal to \(\vek 0\) at the centre. With selective integration therefore \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d = 0\), with full integration \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(4)}\vek d = kGh\,l^4/6 = 1{,}047 \cdot 10^4\) (SI).
  3. With selective integration \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) has five zero eigenvalues: three rigid motions and the modes (a) and (b). With full integration it has three.
Proof

We have to show that the fields (a) and (b) have zero curvature, that their shear strain at the centre is zero, and that where it is not, full integration sees the energy.

  1. For (a) \(\theta_{\mathrm{x}} = \theta_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\), so \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\). For (b) \(\theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{x}} = 0\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{x},\mathrm{y}} + \theta_{\mathrm{y},\mathrm{x}} = 1 - 1 = 0\). Both fields are in the space of the element (\(xy\) and \(y\), \(-x\) are bilinear), so the element represents them exactly and \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \mat B_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d = \vek 0\) (lemma 18.60, point 1).
  2. For (a) \(\vek\gamma = \nabla w - \vek\theta = (y, x)\), for (b) \(\vek\gamma = \vek 0 - (y, -x) = (-y, x)\). At the centre \(x = y = 0\) in both cases \(\vek\gamma = \vek 0\). The one-point rule takes \(\vek\gamma(0, 0)\), so \(\vek d^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d = kGh\cdot 4\,\tfrac{l^2}{4}\,|\vek\gamma(0, 0)|^2 = 0\).
  3. With full integration the shear energy is exactly \(kGh\int |\vek\gamma|^2\,\mathrm{d}A = kGh\int (x^2 + y^2)\,\mathrm{d}A = kGh\cdot 2\,l\cdot l^3/12 = kGh\,l^4/6\). For \(l = 0{,}1\), \(G = 196/2{,}6\) GPa, \(k = 5/6\) and \(h = 0{,}01\) we have \(kGh = 6{,}28 \cdot 10^8\) N/m and \(kGh\,l^4/6 = 1{,}047\cdot 10^4\) (NumPy: \(1{,}0470\cdot 10^4\)).
  4. With selective integration the kernel of the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) contains all vectors with \(\vek\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\) and \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(0, 0) = \vek 0\). By steps 1 and 2 (a) and (b) are in it, and are independent of the rigid motions (these have \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \vek 0\) everywhere). The number five is the NumPy result for this element, not a proof that there is no sixth mode. With full integration the number is exactly three (NumPy), and both modes have positive energy by step 3.

Source: ONATE2 (PDF p. 343-344, section 6.5.1): reduced integration of the element Q4 gives seven zero eigenvalues, and selective integration reduces the number of spurious mechanisms to two, of which one cannot spread through the mesh and the other can cause trouble, for example for a plate supported only at the four corners. The explicit fields (a), (b) and the energy calculation are ours.

Let us look at mode (a) in the figure: the deflection \(w = xy\) is a saddle, in two opposite corners \(w > 0\) and in the other two \(w < 0\), the rotations are zero. The shear strain \(\vek\gamma = \nabla w = (y, x)\) is different from zero in the four Gauss points, at the centre it is zero, and its average over the element is zero. Mode (b) is a vortex: the rotations \(\vek\theta = (y, -x)\) circle the centre, \(w\) is zero. This mode has zero curvature, because a rotation as a rigid motion does not bend, and the shear strain is exactly opposite to \(\vek\theta\) and again is not zero anywhere except at the centre. Both fields are therefore the ones that the one-point rule does not see: the shear strain of a bilinear field has the average over the element exactly equal to the value at the centre (point 2 of lemma 18.61, applied to the bilinear field \(\vek\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\), NumPy also checks this on a random bilinear field), so fields with a zero average and a nonzero deviation remain hidden.

What does this mean for theorem 18.63? Hypothesis (P2) fails at the single element, because \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) has five zero eigenvalues and not three. On the quarter of the plate of this section the supports (and the symmetry sides) are such that (P2) holds for all our meshes: the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix \(\mat K + \mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\) is positive, and the spurious modes do not spread through them. Hypothesis (P2) must not be forgotten, however: for a plate supported only at a corner or at other too sparse points, a spurious mode can spoil it again, which ONATE2 also mentions for a plate supported only at its four corners (PDF p. 343-344). The spurious modes can be stabilized, the four of the one-point rule for all terms and the two of selective integration, which gives a safe element (ONATE2, PDF p. 343-344, with a reference to Belytschko and co-workers), but this goes beyond our aim.

Finally let us mention that we restricted ourselves to rectangles. For general quadrilaterals we need a mapping to \([-1, 1]^2\) and its Jacobian determinant (chapter 15), and for a test of whether a finite element converges at all, the patch test, which for Kirchhoff plates is described by ONATE2 (section 5.9, PDF p. 316).

18.9.7 What we have obtained

The energy of the Kirchhoff plate requires a continuous normal slope (lemma 18.58), so the conforming rectangle has 16 numbers, and for the nonconforming one the normal slope jumps across the edge. The Mindlin rectangle needs only the continuity of the fields and has \(12\) numbers (definition 18.59). Its matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) (lemma 18.60) is computed exactly with four Gauss points (lemma 18.61), but this choice locks for a thin plate, because it has \(8\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) constraints for \(3\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) unknowns (lemma 18.62). The shear term in one point has \(2\,n_{\mathrm{e}}^2\) constraints and does not lock (theorem 18.63), but a single element has two spurious modes, which we must not overlook when the supports are too few (lemma 18.64). The difference between the errors in the table and in the figure tells what we achieved: the selective element on a quarter of the plate with 16 × 16 elements is for all \(a/h\) within \(0{,}03\ \%\) of the exact Mindlin solution, and the full one reaches at \(a/h = 100\) only \(42\ \%\) of it.
