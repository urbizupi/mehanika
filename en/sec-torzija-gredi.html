Chapter 23 Reciprocating engines: dynamics, balancing and vibration isolation

23.9 Torsional vibration of the shaft line and critical speeds (torzijsko nihanje gredi in kritični vrtljaji)

The sections so far treated the crankshaft as one rigid body that turns through the angle \(\theta = \Omega t\). Such a model gives the torque and the size of the flywheel (section 23.5), but it cannot say what happens because the shaft is not rigid. The torque of the cylinders does not appear everywhere at once. A cylinder presses it into its own crank pin, and the flywheel and the generator feel it only through the shaft, which twists as it passes it on. Chapter 13 promised that we would use the twist of the shaft and its stiffness for several disks. This section keeps the promise in three steps. First we build a model of shafts and disks with at least two degrees of freedom and show that it always has one rigid-body mode with zero frequency. Then we show how an engine torque of order \(n\) excites a single mode, and finally we use this to compute the speeds at which the response of the model is unbounded.

23.9.1 Chain of disks (veriga diskov)

Take a drive line of the kind a piston engine set has: the engine, a flywheel on the shaft, and then a generator. Draw it as in figure 23.31. We start with the common axis about which everything turns. On it we put three rigid disks, disk \(J_1\) for the engine with its crankshaft, disk \(J_2\) for the flywheel and disk \(J_3\) for the generator. We call them disks because from each body we need only one number, its moment of inertia about the axis (definition 8.80). The shape and size of a disk are therefore drawn only roughly. For the disks we compute with, \(J_1 = 0{,}05\), \(J_2 = 0{,}5\) and \(J_3 = 0{,}3\) kg m\(^2\). These three numbers are chosen for the example, they are not data of any engine, and the flywheel \(J_2\) is not the one that section 23.5 computed for \(\delta_{\mathrm{s}} = 0{,}02\).

Between the disks we put shafts. Each is a circular bar to which chapter 13 assigns a torsional stiffness \(k_1\) and \(k_2\), \(k_{\mathrm{i}} = G I_{\mathrm{p}}/L_{\mathrm{i}}\) by lemma 13.17, where the lengths \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) are measured between the centres of the disks. The steel has \(G = 75{,}385\) GPa and density \(\rho = 7800\) kg/m\(^3\), the diameter is \(d = 40\) mm and \(I_{\mathrm{p}} = \pi d^4/32 = 2{,}5133\cdot 10^{-7}\) m\(^4\) (example 13.23), so \(k_1 = 94\,731{,}6\) and \(k_2 = 37\,892{,}6\) N m/rad. The shafts have no mass. This is an idealization, because a disk has much more inertia than a shaft: the moment of inertia of the second shaft about the axis is \(\rho I_{\mathrm{p}} L_2 = 0{,}00098\) kg m\(^2\) against \(0{,}3\) kg m\(^2\) of the generator, that is \(0{,}33\) %. The error of such an idealization is already measured by corollary 13.22 (for the shaft with a disk of example 13.23 \(0{,}65\) %), and below we shall see that for FPGL it is less than \(0{,}5\) %.

Each disk has its own twist \(\varphi_1\), \(\varphi_2\) and \(\varphi_3\), the angle between the present and the reference position of a line on the disk, measured about the axis in the same direction. These are three coordinates: three rigid disks on one axis have three degrees of freedom by definition 8.45. The twist \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) may be many revolutions, because the line turns. Only the difference of the twists of neighbouring disks has to be small, that is the twist of the shaft \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\), since lemma 13.17 holds for a linearly elastic shaft. In the vibration of order \(3\) that we compute below, the amplitude of the twist of the second shaft is \(2{,}35\) mrad (\(0{,}13^\circ\)). The amplitude of the torque in it is \(M = 88{,}86\) N m, so the amplitude of the shear stress at its surface is \(M d/(2 I_{\mathrm{p}}) = 7{,}07\) MPa. The mean engine torque \(84{,}61\) N m (section 23.5) also goes to the generator through this shaft and twists it steadily by a further \(84{,}61/k_2 = 2{,}23\) mrad, with a stress of \(6{,}73\) MPa. Even together, \(4{,}58\) mrad and \(13{,}8\) MPa, the shaft is still very far from the limit of linearity. The chain has its name for a reason: the disks are linked one after another by shafts like the links of a chain, and each link carries only a torque between two neighbours.

Definition 23.56 Chain of disks (veriga diskov)

A chain of disks (veriga diskov) is a system of \(N \ge 2\) rigid disks with moments of inertia \(J_1, \dots, J_{\mathrm{N}} > 0\) about a common axis, linked in order by \(N - 1\) massless shafts, where shaft \(i\) between disks \(i\) and \(i + 1\) has torsional stiffness \(k_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\) (lemma 13.17). The state is given by the column of twists \(\vek\varphi = (\varphi_1, \dots, \varphi_{\mathrm{N}})\) of the disks about the axis, measured in the same direction. An external torque \(Q_{\mathrm{k}}(t)\) about the axis acts on disk \(k\). The chain is free if there is no other support about the axis.

Source: FPGL §9.4 (PDF pp. 405 to 406): disks with inertia \(I_{\mathrm{p},\mathrm{i}}\), shafts as torsional springs \(k_{\mathrm{i}}\), twist of a disk \(\theta_{\mathrm{i}}\). The name and the symbols are ours: FPGL writes \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) and \(\theta\), we write \(J\) and \(\varphi\), because \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) is here the second moment of area of the shaft section (lemma 13.17).

The symbol \(J_{\mathrm{k}}\) here means the moment of inertia of a disk about its axis. It is neither \(J(\theta)\), the moment of inertia of the crank mechanism of section 23.4, nor \(J_{\mathrm{G}}\) about the centre of mass from chapter 8, and \(k_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the torsional stiffness of a shaft in N m/rad, not the stiffness of a spring of chapter 9. We write eigenvalues as \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\), because in this chapter \(\lambda\) already stands for the ratio \(a/l\).

Do we get the equation of motion of the chain from our theorems? We get it from Lagrange's equations, as for the chains of masses on springs in chapter 10.

Lemma 23.57 Equation of motion of the chain of disks (gibalna enačba verige diskov)

Let a chain of disks be given by definition 23.56, \(\mat M = \mathrm{diag}(J_1, \dots, J_{\mathrm{N}})\) and \(\mat K\) the tridiagonal matrix of order \(N\) with entries \(K_{\mathrm{kk}} = k_{\mathrm{k}-1} + k_{\mathrm{k}}\) (where \(k_0 = k_{\mathrm{N}} = 0\)), \(K_{\mathrm{k},\mathrm{k}+1} = K_{\mathrm{k}+1,\mathrm{k}} = -k_{\mathrm{k}}\) and zero elsewhere. Then:

  1. A motion \(\vek\varphi(t)\) of the chain solves the equation
    \[\mat M\ddot{\vek\varphi} + \mat K\vek\varphi = \vek Q(t) . \tag{23.46}\]
  2. The matrix \(\mat M\) is symmetric positive definite, the matrix \(\mat K\) symmetric positive semidefinite and \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{N-1}k_{\mathrm{i}}\,(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})^2\).
  3. \(\mat K\vek\varphi = \vek 0\) if and only if all twists are equal, \(\varphi_1 = \dots = \varphi_{\mathrm{N}}\). The kernel of \(\mat K\) is therefore the line spanned by the column \(\vek s = (1, \dots, 1)\).
Proof

We have to show the equation from the kinetic and potential energy, and then the properties of the matrices from the form of the potential.

  1. Disk \(k\) turns about a fixed axis with angular velocity \(\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) and has its centre of mass on the axis, so by theorem 8.82 its kinetic energy is \(\tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{k}}\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}^2\). The kinetic energy of the chain is \(T = \tfrac12\sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}^2 = \tfrac12\dot{\vek\varphi}^\T\mat M\dot{\vek\varphi}\), of the form of theorem 8.86 with a constant matrix \(\mat M\).
  2. For a twist of its ends \(\delta_{\mathrm{i}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\), shaft \(i\) is a linear torsional spring with torque \(k_{\mathrm{i}}\delta_{\mathrm{i}}\) (lemma 13.17). The torque grows linearly with the twist from \(0\) to \(k_{\mathrm{i}}\delta_{\mathrm{i}}\), so its work over the twist \(\delta_{\mathrm{i}}\) is the average value \(\tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{i}}\delta_{\mathrm{i}}\) times \(\delta_{\mathrm{i}}\) (the area of the triangle under the torque-twist line). This is the potential energy of the shaft. The sum over the shafts is \(V = \tfrac12\sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}\,(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})^2\).
  3. Expanding the square gives \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})^2 = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1}^2 - 2\sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} + \sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}^2 = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} (k_{\mathrm{k}-1} + k_{\mathrm{k}})\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}^2 - 2\sum_{\mathrm{i}} k_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1}\), because \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}^2\) appears at shaft \(k - 1\) (as \(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1}^2\)) and at shaft \(k\) (as \(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}}^2\)). This is \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi\) with the stated matrix, so \(V = \tfrac12\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi\), the matrix is symmetric by construction, and \(\partial V/\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{j}} = (\mat K\vek\varphi)_{\mathrm{j}}\).
  4. The work of the external torques in a virtual twist \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) is \(Q_{\mathrm{k}}\,\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\), so the \(Q_{\mathrm{k}}\) are generalized forces (lemma 8.51). By theorem 8.90, for \(T\) and \(V\) above, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(J_{\mathrm{k}}\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}) + (\mat K\vek\varphi)_{\mathrm{k}} = Q_{\mathrm{k}}\), which is equation (23.46).
  5. The matrix \(\mat M\) has \(J_{\mathrm{k}} > 0\) on its diagonal, so it is symmetric positive definite. From step 3, \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi = 2V \ge 0\) because \(k_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\), so \(\mat K\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15).
  6. The sum \(\sum k_{\mathrm{i}}(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} - \varphi_{\mathrm{i}})^2\) is zero if and only if every term is zero, that is \(\varphi_{\mathrm{i}+1} = \varphi_{\mathrm{i}}\) for every \(i\) because \(k_{\mathrm{i}} > 0\). By lemma 7.16, \(\mat K\vek\varphi = \vek 0\) if and only if \(\vek\varphi^\T\mat K\vek\varphi = 0\), which is point 3.

Source: FPGL §9.4 (PDF p. 406): the equations are as equation (9.2), with the matrices (9.5) for four disks. The proof is not from the source.

The lemma says what the rigid motion of the chain is. All disks turn through the same angle (mode 1 in the figure) and no shaft twists, so there is no potential energy. This is the turn of the whole line as one body, as section 23.5 used it. Because \(\mat K\) is semidefinite with a one-dimensional kernel, everything we proved about semidefinite matrices in chapter 10 can be used.

Corollary 23.58 A free chain of disks has exactly one rigid-body mode (prosta veriga diskov ima en togi način)

Let a chain of disks be free by definition 23.56 and let \((\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}})\), \(r = 1, \dots, N\), be the eigenpairs of the pair \((\mat K, \mat M)\) by theorem 10.13, \(\omega_1 \le \dots \le \omega_{\mathrm{N}}\), with \(\mat M\)-orthonormal modes. Then:

  1. Exactly one eigenvalue is zero, \(\omega_1 = 0\), and \(\omega_2 > 0\). The rigid-body mode is \(\vek\varphi_1 = \vek s/\sqrt{J_\mathrm{c}}\) with \(J_\mathrm{c} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\), in which all disks turn through the same angle.
  2. Every elastic mode (\(r \ge 2\)) satisfies \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}} = 0\). The weighted mean twist \(\bar\varphi = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}/J_\mathrm{c}\), with the moments of inertia as weights, is therefore zero in an elastic mode.
  3. For any torques \(Q_{\mathrm{k}}(t)\), \(J_\mathrm{c}\,\ddot{\bar\varphi} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} Q_{\mathrm{k}}\): the chain as a whole turns like one disk with inertia \(J_\mathrm{c}\) under the sum of the torques.
Proof

We have to show that the kernel is exactly one-dimensional, and then we use theorem 10.23.

  1. By lemma 23.57 (point 3) the set \(\mathcal N\) of columns with \(\mat K\vek w = \vek 0\) is the line \(\{c\,\vek s\}\), so it is one-dimensional. By theorem 10.23 (point 2) the number of zero eigenvalues is \(z = 1\), so \(\omega_1 = 0\) and \(\omega_2 > 0\) (point 1 of the same theorem).
  2. The mode \(\vek\varphi_1\) lies in \(\mathcal N\), so \(\vek\varphi_1 = c\,\vek s\), and \(\vek\varphi_1^\T\mat M\vek\varphi_1 = c^2 J_\mathrm{c} = 1\) gives \(c = 1/\sqrt{J_\mathrm{c}}\) (up to sign).
  3. By theorem 10.23 (point 3), \(\vek s^\T\mat M\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}} = 0\) for \(r \ge 2\). The modal coordinate \(\eta_1 = \vek\varphi_1^\T\mat M\vek\varphi = \sqrt{J_\mathrm{c}}\,\bar\varphi\) solves, by theorem 10.20, the equation \(\ddot\eta_1 = \vek\varphi_1^\T\vek Q\) (from \(\omega_1 = 0\)), that is \(J_\mathrm{c}\,\ddot{\bar\varphi} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} Q_{\mathrm{k}}\).

Source: FPGL (PDF p. 406): "the torsional system invariably has one" rigid-body mode. The proof is not from the source.

Point 3 is exactly the model of section 23.5, now as a consequence. When this model is good, the theorem below tells: for an excitation with frequency \(\omega\) far below \(\omega_2\), the denominator \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \omega^2\) of the elastic modes is almost \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\), which is the static response.

FPGL Example 9.4.1 (PDF p. 407) has four equal disks on a tube with outer diameter \(80\) mm and inner diameter \(40\) mm, centres \(0{,}3\) m apart, \(G = 80\) GPa and \(\rho = 7850\) kg/m\(^3\), disks of outer diameter \(400\) mm and thickness \(50\) mm. Example 23.60 Engine, flywheel and generator on two shafts (motor, vztrajnik in generator na dveh gredeh)

The chain of the table above has, by corollary 23.58, one zero frequency and two positive ones. We check point 2 (mode 2 in the figure): \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} J_{\mathrm{k}}\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}}\) for the second mode is \(0{,}05\cdot(-0{,}6016) + 0{,}5\cdot(-0{,}5398) + 0{,}3\cdot 1 = -0{,}03008 - 0{,}2699 + 0{,}3 = 0\) (to \(10^{-16}\)). In the second mode disk \(J_3\) turns the opposite way to the engine and the flywheel together, and the weights balance exactly (the angular momentum of the whole stays zero).

Why is the frequency of the second mode exactly \(70{,}19\) Hz? The twist of the second shaft in this mode is \(1 - (-0{,}5398) = 1{,}5398\) and of the first only \(0{,}0618\): almost all the twist is in the long, softer shaft. The engine and the flywheel therefore move almost together, like one disk \(J_1 + J_2 = 0{,}55\) kg m\(^2\) against the generator. By the same computation as for the railway cars in chapter 10, such a system of two disks has \(\omega^2 = k_2(J_1 + J_2 + J_3)/((J_1 + J_2)\,J_3) = 195\,204{,}5\) 1/s\(^2\), so \(\omega = 441{,}82\) 1/s and \(f = 70{,}318\) Hz, which is \(0{,}18\) % above the exact value. The node of the second mode lies on the second shaft: in a massless shaft the twist is linear between the disks (lemma 13.17, \(\varphi(x) = M x/(G I_{\mathrm{p}})\) at constant torque \(M\)), so the zero is at \(0{,}5398/(0{,}5398 + 1) = 0{,}3506\) of the length, \(175{,}3\) mm from the flywheel. The figure shows the node as an open circle, and the third mode has two.

A near miss: the same chain, but with the generator locked (\(\varphi_3 = 0\), as with a brake or a seized rotor). Two disks remain, \(\mat K_\mathrm{l} = \begin{pmatrix} k_1 & -k_1 \\ -k_1 & k_1 + k_2 \end{pmatrix}\) with determinant \(3{,}59\cdot 10^9\) (N m/rad)\(^2\). The frequencies are \(41{,}704\) and \(230{,}155\) Hz, both positive, so \(\mat K_\mathrm{l}\) is positive definite and there is no rigid-body mode. The assumption of freedom failed, so the zero frequency fails too. The first frequency also drops from \(70{,}19\) to \(41{,}70\) Hz. The reason: in the free chain the second shaft is twisted between \(J_1 + J_2\) and the light generator, so the reduced moment of inertia is \(0{,}55\cdot 0{,}3/0{,}85 = 0{,}194\) kg m\(^2\), while now the shaft is held at the generator as if in a wall and the whole \(0{,}55\) kg m\(^2\) vibrates on it. Since \(\omega^2\) is inversely proportional to the moment of inertia, the ratio of the frequencies is \(\sqrt{0{,}55/0{,}194} = 1{,}683\), which agrees with the ratio of the exact frequencies \(70{,}19/41{,}70 = 1{,}683\) to three digits.

What is proved in the example and what is only tested? Corollary 23.58 is proved for every free chain. The exact frequencies \(70{,}190\) and \(230{,}181\) Hz are computed, not proved in closed form, and the two-disk estimate is an approximation with a measured error of \(0{,}18\) %.

23.9.2 Torque of order \(n\) and the modal force (moment reda \(n\) in modalna sila)

The engine drives the line with a torque that is not constant, and presses it into the engine disk. In a four-stroke engine it repeats after two turns, that is after the angle \(4\pi\) (section 23.5). Over this period the torque is expanded in a Fourier series (lemma 9.97) with period \(T_{\mathrm{p}} = 4\pi/\Omega\), so with terms \(c_{\mathrm{m}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\Omega t/2}\). The term with \(m = 2n\) has frequency \(n\Omega\) and is called order \(n\) (red \(n\)): it repeats \(n\) times in one turn of the crankshaft, so \(n\) says how many times per turn. In a four-stroke engine \(n\) is a multiple of \(\tfrac12\), \(n = \tfrac12, 1, \tfrac32, \dots\) If \(g(\theta)\) is the torque that a cylinder gives at the angle \(\theta\) of its crank, and \(T_{\mathrm{n}} = 2c_{2\mathrm{n}}\) is the complex amplitude of order \(n\) (lemma 9.97, \(2c_{2\mathrm{n}} = a_{2\mathrm{n}} - \mathrm{i}b_{2\mathrm{n}}\)), its term of order \(n\) is \(\mathrm{Re}\big(T_{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\theta}\big)\) with amplitude \(|T_{\mathrm{n}}|\).

Cylinder \(k\) fires with a lag \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) (firing phase, zamik vžiga), that is by the crank angle by which its cycle is delayed against the cylinder with \(\psi = 0\). Its torque on disk \(k\) (in figure 23.31 this is, for the engine, disk \(J_1\)) is therefore \(g(\Omega t - \psi_{\mathrm{k}})\) and order \(n\) is \(\mathrm{Re}\big(T_{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\psi_{\mathrm{k}}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}n\Omega t}\big)\). The torque of order \(n\) thus has the same frequency \(\omega = n\Omega\) on every disk and a different phase. For even firing \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) is fixed in section 23.7. The question is how much each factor \(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\psi_{\mathrm{k}}}\) contributes to a given mode. It is like pushing a swing: a push in time with its own swing drives it, while two children who push it at the same moment from opposite sides cancel.

Theorem 23.61 Response of the chain of disks to a torque of order n (odziv verige diskov na moment reda n)

Let a chain of disks be free by definition 23.56 with eigenpairs \((\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2, \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}})\) by corollary 23.58 (\(\omega_1 = 0\)), without damping, and let \(\vek Q(t) = \mathrm{Re}\big(\vek q\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\) with \(\omega = n\Omega > 0\) and a complex column \(\vek q\), for instance \(q_{\mathrm{k}} = T_{\mathrm{n}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\psi_{\mathrm{k}}}\) on the disks with cylinders and \(q_{\mathrm{k}} = 0\) elsewhere. The modal force of order \(n\) of mode \(r\) is

\[F_{\mathrm{r}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{N}\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}}\,q_{\mathrm{k}} . \tag{23.47}\]

Then:

  1. If \(\omega \ne \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) for all \(r\), equation (23.46) has the solution \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{p}}(t) = \mathrm{Re}\big(\vek x\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\) with
    \[\vek x = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{N}\frac{F_{\mathrm{r}}}{\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \omega^2}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} , \tag{23.48}\]
    and every other solution differs from it by a free motion. In the rigid-body mode \(r = 1\) the term is \(-F_1\vek\varphi_1/\omega^2\), where \(F_1 = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}/\sqrt{J_\mathrm{c}}\).
  2. If \(\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) for some \(r \ge 2\) and \(F_{\mathrm{r}} \ne 0\), the equation has no bounded solution: the modal coordinate \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) contains a term \(\mathrm{Re}\big(F_{\mathrm{r}}\,t\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}/(2\mathrm{i}\omega)\big)\) with amplitude \(|F_{\mathrm{r}}|\,t/(2\omega)\), which grows with \(t\) without bound.
  3. If \(\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) and \(F_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\), mode \(r\) is not excited and the solution (23.48) (without the term \(r\)) holds as in point 1.
Proof

We have to show that the equation decouples into modes and that each modal equation under a harmonic force offers a solution in closed form. Here \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is real, so \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\vek Q = \mathrm{Re}\big(F_{\mathrm{r}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\).

  1. By theorem 10.20, equation (23.46) is equivalent to the equations \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(F_{\mathrm{r}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\) for the modal coordinates \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\vek\varphi\) (definition 10.19).
  2. For \(\omega \ne \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) we try \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(A\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\). By theorem 9.29 (point 3), \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(-\omega^2A\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\), so \((\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \omega^2)A = F_{\mathrm{r}}\) and \(A = F_{\mathrm{r}}/(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \omega^2)\). For \(r = 1\) (\(\omega_1 = 0\)), \(A = -F_1/\omega^2\).
  3. Because \(\vek\varphi = \mat\Phi\boldsymbol\eta = \sum_{\mathrm{r}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) (definition 10.19), we get (23.48). The difference of two solutions solves the equation without force (lemma 9.6, point 2), that is a free motion. This settles the first point.
  4. For \(\omega = \omega_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\) we try \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(B\,t\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\). By theorem 9.29 (point 3) and the product rule, \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(B\,(2\mathrm{i}\omega - \omega^2 t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\), so \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \omega^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \mathrm{Re}\big(2\mathrm{i}\omega B\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\big)\) and \(B = F_{\mathrm{r}}/(2\mathrm{i}\omega)\). Every other solution differs from this one by a free vibration of constant amplitude, while \(|B|\,t\) grows, so it is not bounded. This is the second point.
  5. For \(F_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) the equation for \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}}\) is homogeneous, its solutions are free vibrations, and the other modes have the same form by step 2. This is the third point.

Source: The proof is not from the source.

Point 1 says what each mode does with the excitation (figure 23.31, the modes below). The modal force \(F_{\mathrm{r}}\) is the scalar product of the mode and the excitation, so it measures how much the excitation presses into the shape of the mode, and on each disk it counts with the weight that the mode has there. The denominator \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2 - \omega^2\) is the stiffness of the mode \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) (for an \(\mat M\)-orthonormal mode \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^\T\mat M\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\)), reduced by the inertia term \(\omega^2\) at the frequency of the excitation, so the response grows as \(\omega\) approaches \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\). For the rigid-body mode the denominator is \(-\omega^2\): the response of order \(n\) in the rigid-body mode is a twist with amplitude \(|\sum_{\mathrm{k}} q_{\mathrm{k}}|/(J_\mathrm{c}\,\omega^2)\) on every disk. This is the oscillation of the twist of the line as a whole with inertia \(J_\mathrm{c}\), as for a flywheel. For order \(3\) of our example (\(T_3 = 31{,}9\) N m on the engine disk, a value from section 23.5, which is our model of engine E, at \(1500\) 1/min) it is \(31{,}9/(0{,}85\cdot 471{,}24^2) = 0{,}169\) mrad.

What if the excitation is exactly at the frequency of a mode and yet nothing happens? The third point of the theorem shows this when the line has several disks with cylinders. Take two equal disks (figure 23.32), \(J = 0{,}5\) kg m\(^2\), joined by a shaft \(k = 2\cdot 10^4\) N m/rad, with a cylinder on each, and look at the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}}\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\psi_{\mathrm{k}}}\) for the mode in which the disks twist in opposite senses.

First the left side, order \(n = 1\). The factor \(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\psi_{\mathrm{k}}}\) is \(1\) for \(\psi_1 = 0\) and also \(1\) for \(\psi_2 = 2\pi\), so the exciting torques on the two disks are in phase. The mode in which the disks twist oppositely is \(\vek\varphi_2 = (1, -1)/\sqrt{2J}\) (the sign is arbitrary), here \((1, -1)\) kg\(^{-1/2}\) m\(^{-1}\). The term of the first disk is \(+1\) (blue arrow), of the second \(-1\) (orange), the sum is zero (open circle): the mode does not feel a torque that is pushed at the same moment from both sides, like two children on a swing. Then the right side, order \(n = \tfrac12\): the second factor is \(\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\pi} = -1\), the term of the second disk is \((-1)\cdot(-1) = +1\) and the sum is \(2\) (blackwhite arrow). In a formula, \(F_2 = T_{\mathrm{n}}\,(1 - \mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}n})/\sqrt{2J}\) and \(|F_2| = 2\,|T_{\mathrm{n}}|\,|\sin\pi n|/\sqrt{2J}\), which is zero for whole \(n\) and \(2|T_{\mathrm{n}}|/\sqrt{2J}\) for odd \(2n\).

Example 23.62 Two disks, two cylinders: whole orders do not excite the vibration (dva diska, dva valja: celi redi ne vzbujajo nihanja)

Two equal disks (figure 23.32), \(J = 0{,}5\) kg m\(^2\), \(k = 2\cdot 10^4\) N m/rad, have by lemma 23.57 \(\omega_1 = 0\) and \(\omega_2 = \sqrt{2k/J} = 282{,}84\) 1/s (\(f_2 = 45{,}016\) Hz), mode \(\vek\varphi_2 = (1, -1)/\sqrt{2J} = (1, -1)\). The cylinders fire with the lag \(\psi_2 - \psi_1 = 2\pi\), as two cylinders of a four-stroke engine do after \(4\pi/2\) (section 23.7). Computed (num-09.py): \(|F_2|/|T_{\mathrm{n}}|\) is \(2{,}000\) at \(n = \tfrac12, \tfrac32, \tfrac52\) and \(0{,}000\) at \(n = 1, 2, 3\), while the rigid-body mode has the opposite, \(2{,}000\) at whole and \(0\) at half orders. Point 3 of the theorem says that whole orders do not excite the second mode even when \(n\Omega = \omega_2\) exactly. This happens at \(2701\) (\(n = 1\)), \(1350\) (\(n = 2\)) and \(900\) 1/min (\(n = 3\)): there is no resonance in them. Half orders do excite it, at \(5402\) (\(n = \tfrac12\)), \(1801\) (\(n = \tfrac32\)) and \(1080\) 1/min (\(n = \tfrac52\)), at \(60\,f_2/n\) (definition 23.63 below).

A near miss (figure 23.32, left) of the claim "every crossing of an order with a frequency is a resonance": the crossing at \(2701\) 1/min is exact in the model without damping, yet the modal force is zero, so the solution stays bounded (point 3). Whole orders go instead into the rigid-body mode, that is into the oscillation of the speed of the whole line as one disk (corollary 23.58), which the flywheel limits. Cylinders without lag (\(\psi_2 = 0\)) would give \(F_2 = T_{\mathrm{n}}(1 - 1)/\sqrt{2J} = 0\) at every order: the opposite mode would never be excited. This example is an invented line with two equal disks, not data of any engine.

A sum of unit vectors that close into a polygon appears also in the balancing of several cylinders (section 23.7, where the vectors are the crank phase angles). Here the vectors are weighted with the values of the mode \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{k}}\). For the rigid-body mode, where all \(\varphi_{1,\mathrm{k}}\) are equal, the sum is the same as in balancing.

23.9.3 Critical speeds of order \(n\) (kritični vrtljaji reda \(n\))

Now we can look for the speeds at which the response of the model is unbounded. The line turns with speed \(\Omega\), order \(n\) excites with frequency \(n\Omega\), so the condition \(n\Omega = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) is met at a definite \(\Omega\).

Definition 23.63 Critical speeds of order n (kritični vrtljaji reda n)

Let a chain of disks be given by definition 23.56 with an elastic mode \(r \ge 2\) (frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}} > 0\), \(f_{\mathrm{r}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}/(2\pi)\)) and a torque of order \(n\) by theorem 23.61 with modal force \(F_{\mathrm{r}} \ne 0\). The critical speed of order \(n\) of mode \(r\) is the angular speed of the crankshaft \(\Omega_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{n}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}/n\), in revolutions per minute \(N_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{n}} = 60\,f_{\mathrm{r}}/n\).

Source: The name and the notation are ours. The reason for the name: at this speed the amplitude in the model grows without bound by point 2 of theorem 23.61, so the speed is critical for the design.

The critical speeds of our example are shown in figure 23.33. We first draw the axes: the speed \(N\) in 1/min horizontally, the frequency \(f\) in Hz vertically. Order \(n\) excites with frequency \(f = nN/60\), which is a straight line through the origin, since there is no excitation at rest. The natural frequency of the first elastic mode, \(f_2 = 70{,}19\) Hz (index \(2\) because \(r = 1\) has frequency zero), we draw as a horizontal line, because it does not depend on the speed. Each crossing of the line of order \(n\) with it is a critical speed. The vertical dashed line is the running speed \(1500\) 1/min.

For our example \(N_{\mathrm{r},\mathrm{n}} = 60\cdot 70{,}190/n\) (in 1/min), which are the crossings in the figure:

Example 23.64 Running speed 1500 1/min and orders 2,5 and 3 (delovna hitrost 1500 1/min in redi 2,5 in 3)

The running speed \(1500\) 1/min is \(25\) Hz, which is \(2{,}8\) times less than \(f_2 = 70{,}19\) Hz, so it would seem that the line is stiff and that the running speed is far from all critical speeds. Yet orders \(2{,}5\) and \(3\) lie near: \(N_{\mathrm{r},3} = 1404\) is \(6{,}4\) % below the running speed and \(N_{\mathrm{r},2{,}5} = 1685\) is \(12{,}3\) % above it (figure 23.33, the distances are marked). At the running speed orders \(2{,}5\) and \(3\) excite with angular frequencies \(392{,}70\) and \(471{,}24\) 1/s, closer to \(\omega_2 = 441{,}02\) 1/s than any other order.

In figure 23.33 the orders \(2{,}5\) and \(3\) are blackwhite. The torque on the engine disk has, by section 23.5 (our model of engine E, not data), amplitudes \(T_{2{,}5} = 49{,}9\) and \(T_3 = 31{,}9\) N m. By theorem 23.61 (NumPy, num-09.py) the modal force is \(F_2 = T_{\mathrm{n}}\varphi_{2,1}\), where \(\varphi_{2,1}\) is the first element of the \(\mat M\)-normalized mode. The ratio \(\omega_2^2/|\omega_2^2 - \omega^2|\) is \(4{,}83\) at order \(2{,}5\) and \(7{,}05\) at order \(3\): the modal part of the response is that many times larger than for slow, static excitation. The twists of the disks at order \(3\) have amplitudes \(0{,}764\), \(1{,}011\) and \(1{,}334\) mrad.

The torque in the second shaft (figure 23.31, the second shaft) is \(k_2\,(\varphi_3 - \varphi_2)\) with amplitude \(88{,}86\) N m, which is \(2{,}79\) times the exciting torque \(31{,}9\) N m and comparable to the mean engine torque \(84{,}61\) N m (section 23.5). If the line were rigid, the second shaft would carry \(J_3 T_3/J_\mathrm{c} = 11{,}26\) N m, so the dynamic torque is \(7{,}9\) times larger. At order \(2{,}5\) the torque of the second shaft is \(91{,}80\) N m (\(1{,}84\,T_{\mathrm{n}}\)) against \(17{,}61\) N m for a rigid line. A shaft that is strong enough for the mean torque can be loaded by the vibration with several times the exciting torque, because the frequencies came close.

A near miss (figure 23.33, order \(2\) and its crossing): "critical speeds are only where an order and a frequency coincide". For order \(2\) (amplitude \(50{,}7\) N m) the ratio is only \(2{,}03\) and the torque of the second shaft is \(38{,}13\) N m, \(2{,}1\) times more than for a rigid line, although \(N_{\mathrm{r},2} = 2106\) 1/min is \(40\) % above the running speed. The factor \(\omega_2^2/|\omega_2^2 - \omega^2|\) grows as the order approaches the critical speed, so the critical speed is not a sharp boundary.

Damping is left out of the theorem. If an elastic mode has a damping ratio \(\zeta\) (modal damping, theorem 10.49), the dynamic factor at frequency ratio \(1\) is by theorem 9.54 (point 3) exactly \(1/(2\zeta)\): for \(\zeta = 0{,}01\) this is \(50\), for \(0{,}02\) it is \(25\). The ratio \(\zeta\) is an assumption here, not data. Damping limits the height of the peak at the line \(f_2\) but does not remove the peak.

Two omissions are named. The first is the variable moment of inertia of the crank mechanism \(J(\theta)\) (section 23.4): within the \(0{,}05\) kg m\(^2\) of disk \(J_1\) it varies from \(0{,}002094\) to \(0{,}007024\) kg m\(^2\), that is by \(\pm 0{,}0025\) kg m\(^2\) (\(\pm 4{,}9\) %), and this moves \(f_2\) by less than \(0{,}1\) % (by the formula for two disks \(0{,}08\) %, by the exact computation with three disks \(0{,}096\) %, NumPy). Its effect as a parametric excitation is a separate phenomenon that we do not treat here. The second is damping, which the theorem and the example take only as \(\zeta\).

What is proved here and what is not? Lemma 23.57, corollary 23.58 and theorem 23.61 are proved for a chain of disks without damping and without shaft mass. FPGL Example 9.4.1 and the computation of the chain engine, flywheel, generator are checked. We do not prove how well the model with disk \(J_1\) replaces a real engine, nor how accurate the chosen \(T_{\mathrm{n}}\) is. We do not treat the mass of the shaft, the variable \(J(\theta)\) or damping. The structure of the excitation for several cylinders with even firing is completed by section 23.7, other shaft sections to which lemma 13.17 does not apply by chapter 16, and the extension to many disks by chapter 21.
