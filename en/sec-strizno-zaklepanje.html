Chapter 12 Beam and frame finite elements

12.8 Shear locking and reduced integration (strižno zaklepanje in nepopolno integriranje)

The Timoshenko beam element of the previous section works on the cantilever \(L = 80\) mm, but not on the cantilever \(L = 1\) m: one element gives \(0{,}00497\) of the true tip deflection, sixteen elements give \(0{,}56\) (table in section 12.7.4). The same element, the same steel, the same bar, only the slenderness differs. Chapter 5 named this phenomenon shear locking (strižno zaklepanje), and chapter 6 met locking (zaklepanje) on the hanging bar (figure 6.17). Why does it happen, and why is it cured merely by integrating the shear part of the matrix with one Gauss point instead of two? At first sight this is strange, since we know reduced integration (nepopolno integriranje) from chapter 7 as a mistake that produces a spurious zero-energy mode (lažni način brez energije) (equation (7.31)).

The answer has five parts. First we show that the system of Timoshenko elements is a penalty system of section 12.6, in which the penalty number \(kGA\) keeps the shear strain close to zero. Then we count how many independent fields these constraints leave: none with two Gauss points, as many as there are elements with one. A theorem on the limit tells what happens as \(kGA \to \infty\). Next we prove that the one-point element is the exact element of a beam whose shear flexibility is slightly smaller than that of the true one. Finally we collect why reduced integration is useful here and what it does not guarantee.

12.8.1 Locking is a penalty (zaklepanje je kazen)

The element matrix is the sum of a bending part and a shear part (lemma 12.32). The shear part is the integral of the square of the shear strain \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\), multiplied by \(kGA\), and the computer evaluates it with a Gauss rule (definition 7.24): a sum over \(q\) points, in each the square of the value of \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\), multiplied by the weight. A sum of squares is exactly the form of the penalty of definition 12.27: the squared length of a column \(\mat C\,\vek d\) whose entries are the values of \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) at the Gauss points, multiplied by the square root of the corresponding weight, and this square is multiplied by \(\alpha = kGA\). Let us write this precisely. We denote the number of Gauss points by \(q \in \{1, 2\}\) (as in lemma 7.25). A distributed load enters this section only through the right-hand side \(\vek f\), so we do not need the letter \(q\) for it.

The Gauss rule is written on \([-1, 1]\) with points \(\xi_{\mathrm{g}}\) and weights \(w_{\mathrm{g}}\), and the element is mapped by \(x = x_{\mathrm{a}} + h\,(1 + \xi)/2\), so \(x'(\xi) = h/2\) and by the substitution rule (equation (7.28)) the integral over \(x\) equals \(\tfrac h2\) times the integral over \(\xi\) (step 5 of the proof of lemma 12.32). In terms of \(\xi\) the row \(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}\) of equation (12.36) is \(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi) = \big(-\tfrac1h,\ -\tfrac{1 - \xi}{2},\ \tfrac1h,\ -\tfrac{1 + \xi}{2}\big)\), and \(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the shear strain of the element at the point \(\xi_{\mathrm{g}}\).

Lemma 12.35 The Timoshenko system is a penalty system (Timošenkov sistem je kazenski sistem)

Let a beam have constant \(EI\) and \(kGA\), a mesh of \(n\) Timoshenko beam elements (definition 12.31) with lengths \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\), and supports with zero prescribed values. Let the element matrix be \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)}\), where \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) is taken from equation (12.37) (one point integrates it exactly) and \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)} = kGA \sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,\tfrac{h_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}\,\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})^\T\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\) is the shear part by the Gauss rule with \(q\) points. Let the system be assembled from these matrices by equation (12.39) with the same columns \(\vek f_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\vek P\) as in theorem 12.33. Let \(\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\) be the matrix with \(q\) rows \(\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}} h_{\mathrm{e}}/2}\;\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\), one for each Gauss point \(g\), and \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) the matrix which has, for every element \(e\) and every Gauss point \(g\) of it, the row \(\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}} h_{\mathrm{e}}/2}\;\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) (selection matrix \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\) of definition 7.11, without the columns of the prescribed unknowns). Then:

  1. \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)} = kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\). The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(2)}\) is the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) of equation (12.37), and \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) is the matrix of equation (12.38).
  2. The reduced system is \((\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek d = \vek f\) with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\), without the columns of the prescribed unknowns, and with a right-hand side \(\vek f\) that does not depend on \(kGA\). The system has the form of the penalty system (12.28) with the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) in place of \(\mat K\), the matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) in place of \(\mat C\), \(\vek g = \vek 0\) and the penalty number \(\alpha = kGA\).
  3. \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) means that \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) at every Gauss point of every element.
Proof

We have to show that the shear part of the element is a sum of products of a column and a row, and that assembly keeps this form.

  1. By step 1 of the proof of lemma 12.32, \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{g}}) = \mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), and this is a number, so \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = kGA \sum_{\mathrm{g}} w_{\mathrm{g}}\,\tfrac{h_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}\,(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{g}})\vek d_{\mathrm{e}})^2 = kGA\,|\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}|^2 = \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T(kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}})\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\). The difference of the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(q)}\) and \(kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\) is symmetric and has a zero quadratic form for every \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), so it is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15). By lemma 7.16 it gives zero when multiplied by any \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\), so it is the zero matrix. For \(q = 2\), \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(2)} = \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\) (point 2 of lemma 12.32), for \(q = 1\), \(\xi_1 = 0\), \(w_1 = 2\) and this is \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) (step 7 of its proof).
  2. By equation (12.39) the matrix of the system is \(\mat K = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\). The rows of \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) that belong to element \(e\) are the rows of the matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\). Since \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}} = \sum \vek c^\T\vek c\) is the sum of the products of the column \(\vek c^\T\) and the row \(\vek c\) over all rows \(\vek c\) of \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\), the contribution of the rows of element \(e\) is \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{e}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\). By step 1, therefore, \(\mat K = \mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\). The right-hand side \(\vek f\) contains only the consistent forces of the load and the forces at the ends (section 12.7.3), so it does not depend on \(kGA\). The reduced system is this system without the rows and columns of the prescribed unknowns (theorem 7.18), and the prescribed values are zero.
  3. Point 3 follows from step 1: row \((e, g)\) of \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\), multiplied by \(\vek d\), is \(\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}} h_{\mathrm{e}}/2}\,\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{g}})\), and the weight is positive.

Source: COOK §13.4 (PDF pp. 509-510, read): "The foregoing development explains shear locking as an instance of penalty constraint" and "as elements become more and more slender, [K_s] acts as a penalty matrix that forces [...] {D} ≈ {0}". The notation with the rows of \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) and the proof are not from the source.

Let us try it on the cantilever of section 12.7.4, \(L = 1\) m with one element. After the clamp the unknowns \(v_2\) and \(\theta_2\) remain, \(h = 1\) m. With two points \(\xi = \mp 1/\sqrt3\), \(\psi_2^e = (1 + \xi)/2\) equals \(0{,}2113\) and \(0{,}7887\), and the rows of \(\mat C_2\) (columns \(v_2\), \(\theta_2\)) are \(\sqrt{h/2}\,(1/h,\ -\psi_2^e)\):

\[\mat C_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 0{,}7071 & -0{,}1494 \\ 0{,}7071 & -0{,}5577 \end{pmatrix} , \qquad \mat C_2^\T\mat C_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -\tfrac12 \\ -\tfrac12 & \tfrac13 \end{pmatrix} .\]

Multiplied by \(kGA\) this is exactly the shear part of the block \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) of section 12.7.3, \(kGA\,(1/h,\ -1/2,\ h/3)\), and the bending part \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} = \mathrm{diag}(0,\ EI/h)\) is the rest of it (NumPy). With one point \(\mat C_1 = (1,\ -\tfrac12)\) (the row \(\sqrt h\,(1/h,\ -\psi_2^e(0))\) with \(\psi_2^e(0) = \tfrac12\)), and \(\mat C_1^\T\mat C_1\) has the entry \((2,2)\) equal to \(\tfrac14\) instead of \(\tfrac13\), as in equation (12.38).

The penalty forces of definition 12.27 have a physical meaning here. Entry \((e, g)\) of the column \(\vek\lambda_\alpha = -kGA\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d\) is \(-\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}} h_{\mathrm{e}}/2}\;kGA\,\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{g}})\), so, up to the positive factor \(\sqrt{w_{\mathrm{g}} h_{\mathrm{e}}/2}\), the negative of the shear force \(V_{\mathrm{h}} = kGA\,\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) of the solution at the Gauss point. As \(kGA\) grows, \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) at the Gauss points tends to zero, while the shear force stays finite, because equilibrium requires it. The multipliers of the constraints \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) at the Gauss points are therefore shear forces. A theorem later in this section will say this precisely, and the table after it with numbers.

12.8.2 Two Gauss points leave nothing (dve Gaussovi točki ne pustita nič)

The constraints \(\mat C_2\,\vek d = \vek 0\) require the shear strain to be zero at two points of every element. The shear strain is linear on the element (section 12.7.2), and a linear function with two zeros is zero everywhere.

Lemma 12.36 Two Gauss points: the constraints allow only rigid motions (dve Gaussovi točki: vezi dovolijo le toga gibanja)

Let \(\mat C_2\) be the matrix of lemma 12.35 with two Gauss points, and let the supports exclude the rigid motions in the sense of point 2 of lemma 12.30. Then \(\mat C_2\,\vek d = \vek 0\) implies \(\vek d = \vek 0\). For a cantilever with \(n\) elements \(\mat C_2\) is a square matrix of order \(2n\) and has \(2n\) pivots (definition 8.20).

Proof

We have to show that on each element the constraints give a rigid motion, and then that the motions of neighbouring elements agree and form a rigid motion of the whole beam.

  1. By point 3 of lemma 12.35, \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{g}}) = 0\) at both points \(x_{\mathrm{g}} = x_{\mathrm{a}} + h\,(1 \mp 1/\sqrt3)/2\) of element \(e\), which are different. The function \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = v_{\mathrm{h}}' - \theta_{\mathrm{h}}\) is linear on the element (its derivative is \(-\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\), a constant), so it is zero everywhere if it is zero at two different points.
  2. From \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{h}} = v_{\mathrm{h}}'\). The slope \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'\) is constant on the element, so \(\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\) is constant as well, \(\theta_1 = \theta_2 =: b_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \((v_2 - v_1)/h = b_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  3. Two neighbouring elements share a node with the same \(\theta\) (selection matrices, definition 7.11), so \(b_{\mathrm{e}}\) is the same value \(b\) everywhere. The function \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a straight line with slope \(b\) on each element and continuous, so it is one straight line \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = a + b\,x\). The pair \((v_{\mathrm{h}}, \theta_{\mathrm{h}}) = (a + b\,x,\ b)\) is a rigid motion (section 12.7.1) with zero prescribed values, so by the assumption on the supports \(a = b = 0\) and \(\vek d = \vek 0\).
  4. For a cantilever with \(n\) elements there are \(2n\) free unknowns (\(v_{\mathrm{N}}, \theta_{\mathrm{N}}\) for \(N = 1, \dots, n\)) and \(2n\) rows (two per element), so \(\mat C_2\) is square. The clamp excludes the rigid motions, so by steps 1 to 3, \(\mat C_2\,\vek d = \vek 0\) implies \(\vek d = \vek 0\), and by lemma 8.21 \(\mat C_2\) has \(2n\) pivots.

Source: The proof is not from the source. COOK (13.3-7) (PDF p. 509, read): if \([\mat C]^\T[\mat C]\) is nonsingular, then as \(\alpha\) grows the solution tends to zero, "the mesh locks", otherwise the number of independent solutions is "equal to the order of [C]T[C] minus its rank".

For the cantilever we check this with numbers as well: with \(n = 1, 2, 4\) elements the matrix \(\mat C_2\) has \(2, 4, 8\) pivots and as many free unknowns, so the zero column is the only solution of \(\mat C_2\,\vek d = \vek 0\) (NumPy). The shear term \(kGA\,\mat C_2^\T\mat C_2\) therefore penalizes every field except zero. What happens to bending we see in the next section, in the same figure with one point and with two.

12.8.3 One Gauss point allows bending (ena Gaussova točka dopusti upogib)

With one point, at the middle of the element, the shear strain is no longer forced to zero along the whole element, but only at the middle. A linear function with one zero can be inclined, and the element can bend.

Lemma 12.37 One Gauss point: one constraint per element (ena Gaussova točka: ena vez na element)

Let \(\mat C_1\) be the matrix of lemma 12.35 with one Gauss point, at which \(\xi_1 = 0\). Then:

  1. \(\mat C_1\,\vek d = \vek 0\) means for every element with unknowns \((v_1, \theta_1, v_2, \theta_2)\) and length \(h\)
    \[\frac{v_2 - v_1}{h} = \frac{\theta_1 + \theta_2}{2} , \tag{12.41}\]
    the slope of the axis equals the mean tilt of the cross-section.
  2. For a cantilever with \(n\) elements of lengths \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\) (free unknowns \(v_{\mathrm{N}}, \theta_{\mathrm{N}}\), \(N = 1, \dots, n\), numbered so that the deflections \(v_1, \dots, v_{\mathrm{n}}\) come first, and \(v_0 = \theta_0 = 0\)) the matrix \(\mat C_1\) has \(n\) pivots (definition 8.20). The solutions of \(\mat C_1\,\vek d = \vek 0\) are exactly the columns in which the tilts \(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{\mathrm{n}}\) are arbitrary and \(v_{\mathrm{N}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e} = 1}^{N} h_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\). This sum is the integral \(\int_0^{x_{\mathrm{N}}}\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\) of the tilt field.
  3. The one-point shear energy of the element is \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,kGA\int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), where \(\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac1h\int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\) is the mean shear strain (povprečna strižna deformacija) of the element.
Proof

We have to show the constraint at the middle, then the pivots and the solution node by node, and finally the value of a linear function at the middle.

  1. At the middle \(\psi_1^e = \psi_2^e = \tfrac12\), so by equation (12.36) \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2) = \mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(0)\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = (v_2 - v_1)/h - (\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2\). By point 3 of lemma 12.35 this expression is zero.
  2. By step 1 the row of element \(e\) is the row \(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(0)\) multiplied by \(\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{e}}}\): at the unknown \(v_{\mathrm{e}}\) it has the entry \(\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{e}}}/h_{\mathrm{e}}\), at \(v_{\mathrm{e}-1}\) the entry \(-\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{e}}}/h_{\mathrm{e}}\) (absent for \(e = 1\), because \(v_0\) is prescribed), and at \(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1}, \theta_{\mathrm{e}}\) the entries \(-\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{e}}}/2\). The columns \(v_1, \dots, v_{\mathrm{n}}\) come first, and they hold a lower bidiagonal matrix of order \(n\) with diagonal entries \(\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{e}}}/h_{\mathrm{e}} \ne 0\). In step \(k\) the elimination (definition 8.20) finds the pivot \(\sqrt{h_{\mathrm{k}}}/h_{\mathrm{k}}\) in column \(v_{\mathrm{k}}\) without a row exchange. Below it only row \(k + 1\) has a nonzero number, and subtracting a multiple of row \(k\) from it does not change its entry in column \(v_{\mathrm{k}+1}\), because row \(k\) has a zero there. So each of the \(n\) rows has a pivot. Solution: for arbitrary \(\theta_{\mathrm{N}}\), row \(e\) taken in the order \(e = 1, 2, \dots\) gives \(v_{\mathrm{e}} = v_{\mathrm{e}-1} + h_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\), and there are no other solutions, because row \(e\) determines \(v_{\mathrm{e}}\) uniquely. The field \(\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\) is linear on the element, so its integral over the element is \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\,(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\) (the area of a trapezoid), and the sum over the elements is \(\int_0^{x_{\mathrm{N}}}\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  3. A linear function \(a + b\,s\) has on \([0, h]\) the mean \(a + b\,h/2\), which is its value at the middle. Hence \(\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}} = \gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2)\), and by step 7 of the proof of lemma 12.32 \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,kGA\,h\,\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2)^2 = \tfrac12\,kGA\int\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).

Source: ONATE2 (2.50) and PDF pp. 87-88 (read): the rule by which each integration point introduces \(s\) independent relationships (\(s\) is the number of strains, here one shear strain), and the "singularity of K_s" as a condition for the solution of slender beams. ONATE2 (2.44), (2.45) (PDF p. 86, read): the substitute transverse shear strain matrix, sampled at the middle of the element, which is our row \(\mat b_{\mathrm{s}}(0)\). EP (p. 562): in the slender limit one constraint per element remains. The proof is not from the source.

Let us count on the cantilever. With two points there are \(2n\) constraints and \(2n\) unknowns, only zero remains (lemma 12.36). With one point there are \(n\) constraints and \(2n\) unknowns, so \(n\) numbers, the tilts, remain free. With \(n = 1, 2, 4\) elements the matrix \(\mat C_1\) has \(1, 2, 4\) pivots, and as many free numbers remain (NumPy). With \(n = 2\) elements of length \(h = 0{,}5\) m the two rows (columns \(v_1, \theta_1, v_2, \theta_2\), before multiplication by \(\sqrt h\)) are \((2,\ -\tfrac12,\ 0,\ 0)\) and \((-2,\ -\tfrac12,\ 2,\ -\tfrac12)\), and the solution is \(v_1 = \theta_1/4\) and \(v_2 = \theta_1/2 + \theta_2/4\) for arbitrary \(\theta_1, \theta_2\). The tilt of the cross-section can grow and the deflection follows its integral, which is exactly the relation \(v' = \theta\) (without shear strain) of the Euler-Bernoulli theory, satisfied on average on each element.

Figure 12.24 shows the same element field under both rules. The field is bending: the tilt \(\theta_{\mathrm{h}}\) grows from \(0\) to \(0{,}4\) and the slope of the axis is \(v_{\mathrm{h}}' = 0{,}2\), so it equals the mean tilt, as equation (12.41) requires. The shear strain \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = v_{\mathrm{h}}' - \theta_{\mathrm{h}}\) is linear, zero at the middle, \(\pm 0{,}2\) at the ends. The one-point rule sees it only at the middle, where it is zero, and the element is not penalized. The two-point rule sees it at \(0{,}2113\,h\) and \(0{,}7887\,h\), where it is \(\pm 0{,}1155 = \pm 0{,}2887\,\theta_2\). The penalty is therefore proportional to \(kGA \cdot 0{,}1155^2\) and is large, because in a slender element \(kGA\) is much larger than \(EI/h^2\) (section 12.7.2).

With the constraints \(\mat C_1\,\vek d = \vek 0\) we also ask whether some spurious zero-energy mode has remained, as with the quadratic bar with one point in chapter 7 (equation (7.31)). The energy of the element is now the sum of the bending energy and the one-point shear energy, and both are nonnegative.

Lemma 12.38 The one-point element has no spurious mode (element z eno točko nima lažnega načina)

Let a Timoshenko beam element have length \(h\) and matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) (equations (12.37) and (12.38)) with \(EI > 0\) and \(kGA > 0\). Then the solutions of \((\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\) are exactly the linear combinations of the rigid motions \((1, 0, 1, 0)\) and \((-h, 1, 0, 1)\). If all elements of a mesh have such matrices with positive \(EI_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(kGA_{\mathrm{e}}\), which may differ from element to element, and the supports exclude the rigid motions in the sense of point 2 of lemma 12.30, then \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite.

Proof

We have to show that zero energy means rigid motions, first on the element, then on the mesh.

  1. The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) is symmetric and \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)})\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = EI\,h\,\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2 + kGA\,h\,\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2)^2 \ge 0\) (point 1 of lemma 12.32, step 7 of its proof). It is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15), so by lemma 7.16 \((\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)})\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\) holds exactly when this energy is zero.
  2. The energy is zero exactly when \(\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = (\theta_2 - \theta_1)/h = 0\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2) = 0\). The first gives \(\theta_1 = \theta_2 =: b\), the second by equation (12.41) \(v_2 - v_1 = h\,b\). Hence \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = v_2\,(1, 0, 1, 0) + b\,(-h, 1, 0, 1)\): the deflection \(v_1 = v_2 - h\,b\) is the value of the straight line \(v_2 + b\,(x - x_{\mathrm{b}})\) at \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\). These are rigid motions, a translation and a rotation about node \(2\). Conversely, every combination \(v_2\,(1, 0, 1, 0) + b\,(-h, 1, 0, 1)\) has \(\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) and by equation (12.41) \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2) = 0\), hence zero energy.
  3. On the mesh, by point 2 of theorem 7.13, \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d\), a sum of nonnegative terms. If it is zero, every term is zero, so by step 2 every element is a rigid motion with \(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} = \theta_{\mathrm{e}} = b_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(v_{\mathrm{e}} - v_{\mathrm{e}-1} = h_{\mathrm{e}}\,b_{\mathrm{e}}\). Neighbouring elements share \(\theta\), so \(b_{\mathrm{e}} = b\) everywhere. The field \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is continuous and a straight line with slope \(b\) on each element, so \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = a + b\,x\), and the pair \((a + b\,x,\ b)\) is a rigid motion. With zero prescribed values, by the assumption on the supports, \(\vek d = \vek 0\). The block \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite without zero energy, hence positive definite (as in step 5 of the proof of theorem 12.33).

Source: The proof is not from the source. ONATE2 PDF p. 88 (read): with one point for \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{s}}\) the rule (2.50) "guarantees the non-singularity of K".

What did the lemma show? The bending energy of the element contains a single combination of the unknowns, the difference of the tilts \(\theta_2 - \theta_1\). The one-point shear energy also contains one, the shear strain at the middle \((v_2 - v_1)/h - (\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2\). Zero energy requires both combinations to be zero. These are two constraints on four unknowns, and since the second contains a deflection and the first does not, neither is a consequence of the other. Two numbers remain free, exactly as many as there are rigid motions, so there is no spurious mode. With the quadratic bar with one point it was different (equation (7.31)). The Euler-Bernoulli element with one Gauss point also has a spurious mode (section 12.1). Its one-point energy contains only the curvature at the middle, \(v_{\mathrm{h}}''(x_{\mathrm{a}} + h/2) = (\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)/h\), so a single constraint on four unknowns. Three numbers remain free, but there are only two rigid motions: the third independent column with zero energy is \((0, 1, 0, 1)\), the field with curvature \((12\xi - 6)/h\), which is zero at the middle but not at the ends. In the Timoshenko element the shear part adds a second, independent constraint to the bending one. With every reduced integration we must therefore check by counting the constraints that it leaves no spurious mode (ONATE2 PDF p. 88: "It is very important to check always that the global stiffness matrix K is not singular").

12.8.4 What happens in a slender beam (kaj se zgodi pri vitkem nosilcu)

The slenderness of the beam enters through the quotient \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} = 12\,EI/(kGA\,h^2)\) of section 12.7.4. For constant \(EI\) and \(h\), \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} \to 0\) is the same as \(kGA \to \infty\), so the shear stiffness is a penalty number that grows. Theorem 12.28 tells what a system with such a number does. We apply it to the cantilever, where the matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) has a pivot in every row (lemmas 12.36 and 12.37). A mesh with more rows of shear constraints than free unknowns (for example a cantilever with an additional support at the free end, with two points) lies outside the theorem, and the theorem says nothing about it.

Theorem 12.39 The thin limit of the Timoshenko system (tanka limita Timošenkovega sistema)

Let a cantilever with constant \(EI\) and \(kGA\) be clamped at \(x = 0\), loaded as in theorem 12.33, with \(n\) Timoshenko beam elements. Let \(q \in \{1, 2\}\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) and \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) from lemma 12.35, and \(\vek d_\alpha\) the solution of the system

\[(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + \alpha\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}})\,\vek d = \vek f \tag{12.42}\]

with the penalty number \(\alpha\) in place of \(kGA\) (the same \(\vek f\) for every \(\alpha\)). Let the free unknowns \(v_{\mathrm{N}}, \theta_{\mathrm{N}}\), \(N = 1, \dots, n\), be numbered so that the deflections come first. Then:

  1. The matrices \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\), \(\vek f\) and the constraints \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) satisfy the hypotheses of theorem 12.26. Hence the function \(\tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d - \vek f^\T\vek d\) has on the set \(\{\vek d : \mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\}\) exactly one minimum, at the column \(\vek d^*\), and there is exactly one column of multipliers \(\vek\lambda\) with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d^* - \vek f = \mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\vek\lambda\).
  2. For \(q = 2\), \(\vek d^* = \vek 0\). For \(q = 1\), \(\vek d^*\) is one of the columns of point 2 of lemma 12.37, which are determined by arbitrary tilts \(\theta_1, \dots, \theta_{\mathrm{n}}\).
  3. Theorem 12.28 holds for \(\mat K = \mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\), \(\mat C = \mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\), \(\vek g = \vek 0\). Hence \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) is positive definite with smallest eigenvalue \(\mu > 0\), and for every \(\alpha > 0\) the solution \(\vek d_\alpha\) is unique, \(|\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\vek d_\alpha| \le |\vek\lambda|/\alpha\) and \((\vek d_\alpha - \vek d^*)^\T(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}} + \alpha\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}^\T\mat C_{\mathrm{q}})(\vek d_\alpha - \vek d^*) \le |\vek\lambda|^2/\alpha\). For \(\alpha \ge 1\) moreover \(|\vek d_\alpha - \vek d^*| \le |\vek\lambda|/\sqrt{\mu\,\alpha}\), and the penalty forces \(\vek\lambda_\alpha = -\alpha\,\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\vek d_\alpha\) tend to \(\vek\lambda\). Hence \(\vek d_\alpha \to \vek d^*\) as \(\alpha \to \infty\).
Proof

We have to show that the hypotheses of theorem 12.26 are satisfied. Then points 1 and 3 are statements of theorems 12.26 and 12.28, and point 2 follows from the constraint set.

  1. By point 1 of lemma 12.32 and point 2 of theorem 7.13, \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} EI\,h_{\mathrm{e}}\,\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}^2 \ge 0\), and the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) is symmetric, because the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) are symmetric (lemma 1.101). So \(\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\) is positive semidefinite (definition 7.15) of order \(2n\).
  2. The matrix \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\) has \(2n\) columns, \(m = 2n\) rows for \(q = 2\) and \(m = n\) rows for \(q = 1\), so \(m \le 2n\). For \(q = 2\) it has \(2n\) pivots by lemma 12.36, for \(q = 1\) it has \(n\) pivots by point 2 of lemma 12.37. The constraints \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) are therefore linear constraints with \(m\) rows on \(2n\) unknowns (definition 12.25).
  3. Let \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\) and \(\vek d^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{b}}\vek d = 0\). For \(q = 2\), \(\vek d = \vek 0\) already by lemma 12.36. For \(q = 1\), by step 1 \(\kappa_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) on every element, so \(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} = \theta_{\mathrm{e}}\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{h}} = b\) is constant. Equation (12.41) gives \((v_{\mathrm{e}} - v_{\mathrm{e}-1})/h_{\mathrm{e}} = b\), so \((v_{\mathrm{h}}, \theta_{\mathrm{h}}) = (a + b\,x,\ b)\) is a rigid motion (section 12.7.1) with zero prescribed values, and the clamp gives \(a = b = 0\), so \(\vek d = \vek 0\). The bending energy is therefore positive for every \(\vek d \ne \vek 0\) with \(\mat C_{\mathrm{q}}\,\vek d = \vek 0\).
  4. By steps 1 to 3 the hypotheses of theorem 12.26 are satisfied with the column \(\vek f\) of \(2n\) numbers, so point 1 follows from its points 1 and 2. Theorem 12.28 has the same hypotheses, so it holds with \(\alpha\) as the penalty number, and point 3 follows from its points 1 to 4.
  5. The column \(\vek d^*\) satisfies the constraints. For \(q = 2\) the only such column is \(\vek 0\) by lemma 12.36, for \(q = 1\) these are the columns of point 2 of lemma 12.37. This is point 2.

12.8.5 The exact element and the one-point element (natančen element in element z eno točko)

With one point the cantilever does not lock, but it is not exact either. For a very slender cantilever the tip deflection with one element is \(0{,}750\) of the true one, with two \(0{,}938\) (ONATE2 Table 2.1, PDF p. 84, and the table below). What does reduced integration bring and what does it lack? We get the answer by comparing the element with one that is exact: a piece of Timoshenko beam whose field solves the beam equations. Such a piece without load has fields of low degree (deflection of degree three, tilt of degree two), so we compute its matrix by hand.

Lemma 12.40 The exact Timoshenko beam element (natančen element Timošenkovega nosilca)

Let an element \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) of length \(h\) have constant \(EI\) and \(kGA\) and no load. For every column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = (v_1, \theta_1, v_2, \theta_2)^\T\) there is exactly one pair \((v, \theta)\) of twice continuously differentiable functions on \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) that solves equations (5.78) with \(q = 0\) and has the values \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) at the nodes. The forces of the surroundings on the element at the nodes, \((-V(x_{\mathrm{a}}), -M(x_{\mathrm{a}}), V(x_{\mathrm{b}}), M(x_{\mathrm{b}}))\) by the convention of chapter 5, are \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T}}\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with

\[\begin{aligned} \mat K_{\mathrm{T}} &= \frac{EI}{(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^3}\begin{pmatrix} 12 & 6h & -12 & 6h \\ 6h & (4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2 & -6h & (2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2 \\ -12 & -6h & 12 & -6h \\ 6h & (2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2 & -6h & (4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2 \end{pmatrix} , \\ &\qquad \beta_{\mathrm{h}} = \frac{12\,EI}{kGA\,h^2} . \end{aligned} \tag{12.43}\]
Proof

We have to show the form of every solution by integrating the equations, then the four constants from the values at the ends, and finally the forces at the ends. Let \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}} \in [0, h]\). We write the functions in the variable \(s\), so that for example \(M(0) = M(x_{\mathrm{a}})\) and \(M(h) = M(x_{\mathrm{b}})\).

  1. With \(V = kGA\,(v' - \theta)\) and \(M = EI\,\theta'\) equations (5.78) with \(q = 0\) are equivalent to \(V' = 0\) and \(M' = -V\) (point 1 of theorem 5.88). From \(V' = 0\), \(V\) is constant (corollary 1.85), so \(M' = -V\) is constant and \(M\) is linear (theorem 5.45 with \(n = 1\)).
  2. From \(\theta' = M/EI\), \(\theta = c_0 + c_1 s + c_2 s^2\) (theorem 5.45: a known derivative determines the function up to a constant), \(M = EI\,(c_1 + 2c_2 s)\) and \(V = -M' = -2EI\,c_2\).
  3. From \(v' = \theta + V/kGA\), \(v = c_3 + c_0 s + c_1 s^2/2 + c_2\,(s^3/3 - 2EI\,s/kGA)\) (the same theorem).
  4. The conditions \(v(0) = v_1\), \(\theta(0) = \theta_1\) give \(c_3 = v_1\), \(c_0 = \theta_1\). The condition \(\theta(h) = \theta_2\) gives \(c_1 = (\theta_2 - \theta_1)/h - c_2 h\). The condition \(v(h) = v_2\) gives after substitution \(h\,(\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2 + c_2\,(-h^3/2 + h^3/3 - 2EI\,h/kGA) = v_2 - v_1\). The coefficient of \(c_2\) is \(-\tfrac{h^3}{6}\,(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}}) \ne 0\), so
    \[c_2 = -\frac{6\,\big[(v_2 - v_1) - h\,(\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2\big]}{(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^3}\]
    and there is a single set of all \(c_{\mathrm{i}}\). The pair with these constants satisfies both equations and the values at the ends, and every solution has this form by steps 1 to 3.
  5. Forces at the ends: \(V = -2EI\,c_2 = 12\,EI\,\big[(v_2 - v_1) - h\,(\theta_1 + \theta_2)/2\big]/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^3)\), \(M(0) = EI\,c_1 = EI\,\big[(\theta_2 - \theta_1)/h - c_2 h\big]\), \(M(h) = EI\,\big[(\theta_2 - \theta_1)/h + c_2 h\big]\). We substitute \(c_2\). The row \(-V\) gives \(\tfrac{EI}{(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3}\,(12,\ 6h,\ -12,\ 6h)\). The row \(-M(0) = EI\,(\theta_1 - \theta_2)/h + EI\,c_2 h\) gives \(\tfrac{EI}{(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3}\,\big((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^2(\theta_1 - \theta_2) - 6h\,(v_2 - v_1) + 3h^2(\theta_1 + \theta_2)\big)\), that is \((6h,\ (4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^2,\ -6h,\ (2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^2)\). The row \(V\) is the first one with the opposite sign. The row \(M(h)\) gives in the same way \((6h,\ (2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^2,\ -6h,\ (4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^2)\). SymPy, which also integrates the equations, confirms the matrix.

Source: ONATE2 §2.9 (PDF pp. 112-113): "a 2-noded Timoshenko beam element that yields nodally exact results", the field is cubic and quadratic, the forces come from integrating the equations. EP (8.4.30) (p. 564): "exact nodal values for any distribution of the transverse load q(x), provided that the bending stiffness [...] and shear stiffness [...] are element-wise constant". The notation and the signs are derived by convention D1, not copied.

As \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} \to 0\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T}}\) tends to the matrix of the Euler-Bernoulli element (lemma 12.4). For \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} > 0\) shear changes the factor in front of the matrix and the entries \((4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\) and \((2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\). Now we can compare \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T}}\) with the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) (equations (12.37) and (12.38)). Both have the same structure: the first and the third row are multiples of the row \((1,\ h/2,\ -1,\ h/2)\) with opposite signs, the entries \((2,2)\) and \((4,4)\) are equal, the sum of the entries \((2,2)\) and \((2,4)\) is \(h^2/2\) times the entry \((1,1)\), and their difference is \(2EI/h\) for both matrices (SymPy). They therefore differ in a single number, the entry \((1,1)\): \(kGA/h\) for \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) and \(12\,EI/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^3)\) for \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T}}\). If we choose the shear stiffness in \(\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) so that these two numbers are equal, the matrices are equal.

Theorem 12.41 The one-point element is the exact element of a beam with smaller shear flexibility (element z eno točko je natančen element nosilca z manjšo strižno podajnostjo)

Let a beam have constant \(EI\) and \(kGA\), and let \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} = 12\,EI/(kGA\,h^2)\) for an element of length \(h\). The substitute shear stiffness (nadomestna strižna togost) of the element is \(kGA^*\) with

\[\frac{1}{kGA^*} = \frac{1}{kGA} + \frac{h^2}{12\,EI} . \tag{12.44}\]

Then:

  1. \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\), computed with \(kGA^*\) in place of \(kGA\), equals the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T}}\) of equation (12.43) (with the true \(kGA\) in \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}\)).
  2. Let a beam of length \(L\) have a mesh \(0 = x_0 < \dots < x_{\mathrm{n}} = L\) with element lengths \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\), supports only at the ends, with zero prescribed values, which exclude the rigid motions in the sense of point 2 of lemma 12.30, and a loading only by forces \(F_{\mathrm{N}}\) (positive upwards) and couples \(C_{\mathrm{N}}\) (positive counterclockwise) at the nodes \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\). The exact solution of the Timoshenko beam for this loading is a continuous pair \((v, \theta)\) which is twice continuously differentiable on every closed element, solves equations (5.78) with \(q = 0\) on it, has the prescribed values, satisfies \(V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) - V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) = -F_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) - M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) = -C_{\mathrm{N}}\) at every interior node (lemma 5.12), and at the ends \(-V(0) = F_0\), \(-M(0) = C_0\), \(V(L) = F_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(M(L) = C_{\mathrm{n}}\) where the support does not prescribe the corresponding value. Then the exact solution exists and is unique, the system of the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) with the substitute stiffnesses \(kGA_{\mathrm{e}}^*\) of the elements has exactly one solution \(\vek d\), and \(\vek d\) are the values of the deflection and the tilt of the exact solution at the nodes.
  3. Let a cantilever with the true \(kGA\) be clamped at \(x = 0\), let a force \(P\) in the \(y\) direction act at \(x = L\), and let it have \(n\) equal elements with matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) (without the substitute stiffness). Then the tip tilt \(\theta_{\mathrm{n}} = P\,L^2/(2EI)\) is exact and the tip deflection is
    \[v_{\mathrm{n}} = \Big(1 - \frac{1}{4n^2}\Big)\frac{P\,L^3}{3\,EI} + \frac{P\,L}{kGA} . \tag{12.45}\]
    The exact deflection \(P\,L^3/(3EI) + P\,L/kGA\) minus \(v_{\mathrm{n}}\) is \(P\,L^3/(12\,EI\,n^2)\) and does not depend on \(kGA\).
Proof

We have to show the equality of the matrices, then by a construction from exact elements that the solution of the system is the exact solution, and finally compute the cantilever from the equations of the system.

1. The matrix.

  1. We write \(\alpha' = kGA^*\). By equation (12.44) and since \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}\,h^2/(12EI) = 1/kGA\), we have \(1/\alpha' = (1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2/(12\,EI)\), so \(\alpha' = 12\,EI/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,h^2)\).
  2. We read the entries of \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) from equations (12.37) and (12.38) with \(\alpha'\) in place of \(kGA\). The entry \((1,1)\) is \(\alpha'/h = 12\,EI/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3)\), the entry \((1,2)\) is \(\alpha'/2 = 6\,EI\,h/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3)\), the entry \((2,2)\) is \(EI/h + \alpha' h/4 = \tfrac{EI}{h}\,\big(1 + \tfrac{3}{1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}}}\big) = (4 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,EI\,h^2/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3)\) and the entry \((2,4)\) is \(-EI/h + \alpha' h/4 = \tfrac{EI}{h}\,\big({-1} + \tfrac{3}{1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}}}\big) = (2 - \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\,EI\,h^2/((1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})h^3)\). The remaining entries follow from the form of the matrices (12.37) and (12.38): the entry \((1,3)\) is \(-(1,1)\), \((2,3)\) is \(-(1,2)\), \((3,3)\) is \((1,1)\), \((3,4)\) is \(-(1,2)\), \((1,4)\) is \((1,2)\) and \((4,4)\) is \((2,2)\), and the matrix is symmetric. Comparison with equation (12.43) gives point 1.

2. Exactness at the nodes.

  1. By point 1 the matrix of element \(e\) is the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{e}}\) of equation (12.43) with length \(h_{\mathrm{e}}\). By lemma 12.38, applied with the positive stiffnesses \(kGA_{\mathrm{e}}^*\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is positive definite, so the system has exactly one solution \(\vek d\) (theorem 6.29).
  2. Let \((v, \theta)\) and \((\tilde v, \tilde\theta)\) be exact solutions, \(u_0 = (v_0, \theta_0) = (v - \tilde v,\ \theta - \tilde\theta)\) their difference and \(V_0 = kGA\,(v_0' - \theta_0)\), \(M_0 = EI\,\theta_0'\). The pair \(u_0\) is continuous, twice continuously differentiable on every element and has zero prescribed values, so it is a test pair (section 12.7.1). On every element \(V_0' = 0\) and \(M_0' + V_0 = 0\), because both solutions satisfy equations (5.78) with \(q = 0\) (point 1 of theorem 5.88).
  3. We split the integral \(B_{\mathrm{T}}(u_0, u_0) = \int_0^L \big(M_0\,\theta_0' + V_0\,(v_0' - \theta_0)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\) over the elements (lemma 6.10, point 1). On an element \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) integration by parts (lemma 6.13) gives \(\int M_0\,\theta_0' = \big[M_0\,\theta_0\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} - \int M_0'\,\theta_0\) and \(\int V_0\,v_0' = \big[V_0\,v_0\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} - \int V_0'\,v_0\), and by step 4 the integral over the element equals \(\big[V_0\,v_0 + M_0\,\theta_0\big]_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\), with the values of \(V_0\), \(M_0\) from inside the element. At an interior node \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) the terms \(\big(V_0(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) - V_0(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+)\big)\,v_0(x_{\mathrm{N}}) + \big(M_0(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) - M_0(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+)\big)\,\theta_0(x_{\mathrm{N}})\) collect, and they are zero because both solutions have the same jumps \(-F_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(-C_{\mathrm{N}}\) at \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\). At an end of the beam each of the products \(V_0\,v_0\) and \(M_0\,\theta_0\) is zero, because either the support prescribes the value (the difference of the fields is zero) or both solutions satisfy the same condition on the force (the difference of the forces is zero). Hence \(B_{\mathrm{T}}(u_0, u_0) = 0\) and by point 2 of lemma 12.30 \(u_0 = 0\). There is therefore at most one exact solution.
  4. Let \(\vek d\) be the solution of the system. On every element \(e\) we take the pair of lemma 12.40 with the values \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d\) at the nodes. The assembled pair is continuous, because neighbouring elements have the same two values at the shared node, and it has zero prescribed values. The elements carry no load, so the right-hand side of the system is only the column of the given nodal forces and couples, and by point 1 of theorem 7.13 row \(I\) of the system says that the sum of the element forces \((\mat K_{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{e}}\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d)_{\mathrm{i}}\) at the node equals the given load (as in step 4 of the proof of theorem 12.13). By lemma 12.40 the element forces are \(\mat K_{\mathrm{T},\mathrm{e}}\,\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d = (-V(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), -M(x_{\mathrm{a}}^+), V(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-), M(x_{\mathrm{b}}^-))\) for the internal forces of the pair on the element. At an interior node \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) the rows \(v_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(\theta_{\mathrm{N}}\) therefore give \(V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) - V(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) = F_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^-) - M(x_{\mathrm{N}}^+) = C_{\mathrm{N}}\), and at the end nodes the conditions on \(V\) and \(M\) where the support does not prescribe the value. The assembled pair is therefore the exact solution, by step 5 the only one, and \(\vek d\) are its values at the nodes. This is point 2.

3. The cantilever with one point.

  1. By point 2 of theorem 7.13, point 1 of lemma 12.32 and point 3 of lemma 12.37, \(\tfrac12\,\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \sum_{\mathrm{e}}\tfrac12\,\big(EI\,h\,\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}^2 + kGA\,h\,\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2\big)\) with \(\kappa_{\mathrm{e}} = (\theta_{\mathrm{e}} - \theta_{\mathrm{e}-1})/h\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{e}} = (v_{\mathrm{e}} - v_{\mathrm{e}-1})/h - (\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\) (the shear strain at the middle of the element, equation (12.41)). The matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric, so row \(I\) of \(\mat K\vek d\) is the derivative of this energy with respect to \(d_{\mathrm{I}}\). The only load is \(P\) in row \(v_{\mathrm{n}}\). The unknown \(v_{\mathrm{N}}\) appears in \(\gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\) with the coefficient \(1/h\) and in \(\gamma_{\mathrm{N}+1}\) with \(-1/h\), so row \(v_{\mathrm{N}}\) is the equation \(kGA\,(\gamma_{\mathrm{N}} - \gamma_{\mathrm{N}+1}) = 0\) for \(N < n\) and \(kGA\,\gamma_{\mathrm{n}} = P\) for \(N = n\). All elements therefore have \(\gamma_{\mathrm{e}} = P/kGA\).
  2. The unknown \(\theta_{\mathrm{N}}\) (\(N < n\)) appears in \(\kappa_{\mathrm{N}}\) with the coefficient \(1/h\), in \(\kappa_{\mathrm{N}+1}\) with \(-1/h\) and in \(\gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\), \(\gamma_{\mathrm{N}+1}\) with \(-\tfrac12\), so row \(\theta_{\mathrm{N}}\) is the equation \(EI\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{N}} - \kappa_{\mathrm{N}+1}) - kGA\,h\,P/kGA = 0\), hence \(EI\,(\kappa_{\mathrm{N}} - \kappa_{\mathrm{N}+1}) = P\,h\). For \(N = n\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{n}}\) appears only in \(\kappa_{\mathrm{n}}\) and \(\gamma_{\mathrm{n}}\), the row is \(EI\,\kappa_{\mathrm{n}} - \tfrac12\,kGA\,h\,P/kGA = 0\) and \(EI\,\kappa_{\mathrm{n}} = P\,h/2\). By induction from \(e = n\) downwards, \(EI\,\kappa_{\mathrm{e}} = P\,h\,(n - e + \tfrac12)\).
  3. Since \(\theta_0 = 0\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{n}} = h\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\), and by the sum \(\sum_{\mathrm{e}=1}^{n}(n - e + \tfrac12) = n^2/2\), \(\theta_{\mathrm{n}} = P\,h^2 n^2/(2EI) = P\,L^2/(2EI)\). This is the exact tip tilt of the Timoshenko cantilever (section 5.13.4, with the force \(P\) in place of \(-F\)).
  4. From the definition of \(\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{e}} - v_{\mathrm{e}-1} = h\,P/kGA + h\,(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\), so \(v_{\mathrm{n}} = P\,L/kGA + S\) with \(S = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} h\,(\theta_{\mathrm{e}-1} + \theta_{\mathrm{e}})/2\). As in step 7 of the proof of lemma 12.34, \(S = h^2\sum_{\mathrm{j}}\kappa_{\mathrm{j}}\,(n - j + \tfrac12) = \dfrac{P\,h^3}{EI}\sum_{\mathrm{m}=0}^{n-1}(m + \tfrac12)^2\), and \(\sum_{\mathrm{m}=0}^{n-1}(m + \tfrac12)^2 = \tfrac{n\,(4n^2 - 1)}{12}\), so with \(h = L/n\), \(S = \tfrac{P\,L^3}{3EI}\,\big(1 - \tfrac{1}{4n^2}\big)\). This is equation (12.45). The exact tip deflection is \(P\,L^3/(3EI) + P\,L/kGA\) (section 5.13.4), and the difference to \(v_{\mathrm{n}}\) is \(\tfrac{P\,L^3}{3EI}\cdot\tfrac{1}{4n^2} = \tfrac{P\,L^3}{12\,EI\,n^2}\).

What, then, does it mean that two points lock? A decomposition of the shear energy shows the cause.

Lemma 12.42 Two-point shear energy equals one-point energy plus extra bending stiffness (strižna energija z dvema točkama je energija z eno točko in dodatna upogibna togost)

Let an element of length \(h\) have constant \(EI\) and \(kGA\), let \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI]\) be the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) with stiffness \(EI\), and \(r_{\mathrm{h}} = \beta_{\mathrm{h}}/(1 + \beta_{\mathrm{h}})\) from equation (12.40). Then

\[\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI] + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}} = \mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\Big[EI + \frac{kGA\,h^2}{12}\Big] + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)} = \mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\Big[\frac{EI}{r_{\mathrm{h}}}\Big] + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)} . \tag{12.46}\]
Proof

We have to show that the two-point shear energy splits into the energy of the mean shear strain and the energy of the deviation from the mean. Let \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}}\).

  1. The shear strain is linear on the element with derivative \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}' = -\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\) (step 1 of the proof of lemma 12.34), so \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}} = \bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}} + \kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\,(h/2 - s)\) with the mean \(\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\) (point 3 of lemma 12.37).
  2. The integral is \(\int_0^h\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}^2\,\mathrm{d}s = h\,\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2 + 2\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\int_0^h (h/2 - s)\,\mathrm{d}s + \kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2\int_0^h (h/2 - s)^2\,\mathrm{d}s = h\,\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2 + \tfrac{h^3}{12}\,\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2\), since the middle integral is zero.
  3. Hence \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \tfrac12\,kGA\,h\,\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}^2 + \tfrac12\,\tfrac{kGA\,h^2}{12}\,h\,\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2\). The first term is \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) (point 3 of lemma 12.37), the second is the bending energy \(\tfrac12\,EI'\,h\,\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2 = \tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI']\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(EI' = kGA\,h^2/12\). Symmetric matrices with the same quadratic form are equal (as in step 1 of the proof of lemma 12.35), the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}\) is proportional to the stiffness, so \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI] + \mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI'] = \mat k_{\mathrm{b}}[EI + EI']\), and \(EI + kGA\,h^2/12 = EI\,(1 + 1/\beta_{\mathrm{h}}) = EI/r_{\mathrm{h}}\).

Source: The proof is not from the source. COOK (PDF p. 510) mentions another explanation of locking by "parasitic shear" (Section 3.6).

Two points therefore give the same as one point for a beam with bending stiffness \(EI/r_{\mathrm{h}}\) instead of \(EI\). At \(L = 1\) m with one element \(1/r_{\mathrm{h}} = 201{,}3\) (bending stiffness \(4{,}209 \cdot 10^6\) N m² instead of \(20\,907\) N m²), with \(16\) elements \(1{,}78\). By point 3 of theorem 12.41 with \(EI/r_{\mathrm{h}}\) the tip deflection is \(P\,L/kGA + r_{\mathrm{h}}\,(1 - 1/(4n^2))\,P\,L^3/(3EI)\), which is exactly point 3 of lemma 12.34, obtained along another route (NumPy confirms it to all digits for \(n = 1\) to \(16\) for both cantilevers). The extra term \(\tfrac12\,(kGA\,h^2/12)\,h\,\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}^2\) is the energy of the deviation \(\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\,(h/2 - s)\) of the shear strain from its mean. An element that bends (\(\kappa_{\mathrm{h}} \ne 0\)) cannot avoid this energy, because \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) is linear with derivative \(-\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\). This is parasitic shear (parazitski strig), by which COOK (PDF p. 510) explains locking. One point sees only the mean \(\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\), so it does not count this energy.

12.8.6 Why reduced integration works here (zakaj nepopolno integriranje tu deluje)

We have collected three reasons, each proved. The first is the count of constraints: with two points there are as many constraints as unknowns and zero remains (lemma 12.36), with one point there are \(n\) constraints against \(2n\) unknowns and \(n\) fields remain (lemma 12.37), which is the singularity condition that ONATE2 states (PDF p. 87). The second is the mean shear strain: one point penalizes only the mean \(\bar\gamma_{\mathrm{e}}\) on the element, not the deviation \(\kappa_{\mathrm{h}}\,(h/2 - s)\) which a linear \(\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}\) unavoidably has under bending (lemma 12.42). The third is exactness. Point 1 of theorem 12.41 can be read in the opposite direction: \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\) with the true \(kGA\) is the matrix of equation (12.43) in which the shear flexibility \(1/kGA\) is replaced by \(c = 1/kGA - h^2/(12\,EI)\), so \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}}\) by \(12\,EI\,c/h^2 = \beta_{\mathrm{h}} - 1\) (SymPy). The one-point element is therefore the exact element of a beam with a slightly smaller shear flexibility than the true beam has. For \(\beta_{\mathrm{h}} < 1\), \(c\) is negative, so this is no real beam and the statement holds for the equations of the element, not for the physics. The error of the tip deflection \(P\,L^3/(12\,EI\,n^2)\) decreases with \(h^2\) and does not depend on \(kGA\), so there is no locking.

What does reduced integration not guarantee? The one-point system is not the Ritz method for the true energy \(\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}\) in the family of piecewise linear pairs. The Ritz method in this family is, by theorem 12.33, the system with the matrices \(\mat k_{\mathrm{b}} + \mat k_{\mathrm{s}}\), while the energy \(\tfrac12\,\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{s}}^{(1)}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) is not \(\tfrac12\,kGA\int\gamma_{\mathrm{h}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) by lemma 12.42. The energy estimate and the bound that the deflection under a single force is not too large (point 3 of theorem 12.33) are therefore not proved for it. With the true stiffness the one-point tip deflection at \(n = 2\) equals \(-7{,}4836\) mm, smaller in size than the true one, and with the substitute stiffness \(-7{,}9819\) mm, exactly the true one. The system with the substitute stiffness is not the Ritz method for \(\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}\) either. If it were, then by point 3 of theorem 12.33 and step 8 of its proof \(\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}(u_{\mathrm{h}}) = -\tfrac12\,P\,v_{\mathrm{h}}(L)\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}(u) = -\tfrac12\,P\,v(L)\). An equal tip deflection would give an equal energy, and from \(\Pi_{\mathrm{T}}(u_{\mathrm{h}}) - \Pi_{\mathrm{T}}(u) = \tfrac12\,B_{\mathrm{T}}(u - u_{\mathrm{h}}, u - u_{\mathrm{h}}) = 0\) point 2 of lemma 12.30 would give \(u_{\mathrm{h}} = u\). But the true tilt of the cross-section of the cantilever is a quadratic function (section 5.13.4) and is not piecewise linear. Cea's lemma (theorem 6.39) says something similar from another side. For the bar it bounds the error of the Ritz solution by \(\sqrt{M/m}\) times the error of the best field of the family, where \(M/m\) is the ratio of the largest to the smallest stiffness. For the Timoshenko form we have not proved the theorem. If we measured the ratio of stiffnesses by the quotient of the shear and the bending term in the element matrix, \(kGA\,h^2/EI = 12/\beta_{\mathrm{h}}\) (section 12.7.2), it would grow like \(1/\beta_{\mathrm{h}}\), and the estimate would say nothing for slender beams. This is locking seen from chapter 6, and it is only an observation, not a proof.
