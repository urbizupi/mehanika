The whole chapter on one page (loads more slowly)

Here the motion of a system of bodies, which we wrote with equations in chapter 8, meets the vibration that we treated on models in chapters 9, 10 and 13. From the first we have the equations of motion of a rigid body and Lagrange's equations, from the others the response to forced excitation and the natural frequencies. The chapter applies them to a machine everyone knows: the engine with pistons.

We explain the words of the name in turn. An engine is a machine that moves by itself and so can drive other parts. Reciprocating (batni) means that this work is done by pistons (bati), parts that move forth and back in a cylinder. Dynamics (dinamika) is the description of motion together with the forces that cause it. Balancing (uravnoteženje) is the handling of mass so that the forces which rotating or sliding parts exert on the frame vanish or become smaller. Vibration isolation (izolacija nihanj) means that we separate the frame from the ground with springs, so that the shaking of the machine does not pass on.

Why does this matter? The wheel of a car that is badly balanced shakes the steering wheel at high speed. An engine whose parts move forth and back shakes its frame even when all its rotation is perfect, and so we place it on rubber or spring mounts. The evenness of the shaft's turning decides whether the machine runs smoothly or in jerks.

The chapter promises to do the following, section by section. First we balance a rigid body turning on two bearings. Then we describe the motion of the piston and compute the forces that one cylinder exerts on the frame, then the turning moment on the shaft and how a mass that turns with the shaft evens out its jerks. Next we show what can be balanced with one cylinder and what with several. After that we compute how an engine on mounts passes its shaking on to the ground, and finally at which speeds the shaft twists in resonance.
