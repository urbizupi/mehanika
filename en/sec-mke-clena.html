Chapter 28 Flexible robot links

28.8 The flexible link from finite elements

In the previous sections we described the flexible link with a floating frame and a field \(\bar{\vek u} = \mat\Psi\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) with arbitrary shapes \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) (definition 28.4). The equations of motion contained three quantities: the stiffness matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\), the mass matrix \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) and the column \(\vek b\) that couples the rotation of the joint to the bending (theorem 28.13). For numbers we used the cantilever shapes of chapter 13, which exist only for a uniform straight beam, and the completeness of these shapes is not proved there. How do we get the three quantities for a link for which we have no closed forms? The answer is the same as for the beam in chapter 12: we divide the link into elements, and the shapes \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) are the Hermite node functions. In doing so we keep the promise of chapter 7: an element without a support has a rigid motion, which something must remove. For a link the reference conditions do it (definition 28.5).

The link is a straight beam of length \(L\) with constant bending stiffness \(EI\) and mass per unit length \(\rho A\), Euler-Bernoulli (definition 5.14), with an elastic displacement only perpendicular to the axis and an axis that does not lengthen, as in section 28.2. The last assumption is an idealization. An axial extension would add coordinates, and we leave it out because the axial stiffness \(EA/L\) of our link is larger by the factor \(1{,}1\cdot 10^5\) than the bending stiffness of the tip \(3EI/L^3\) (c28.py). A piece of the link of finite length \(h\), which we do not get as a limit, we call a finite element (končni element), hence the word "finite".

We build the figure as on a board. First we draw the fixed axes \(X\) and \(Y\), in which we measure the position of the hub. At the point \(o\) the link is attached to the hub, this is the origin of the floating frame, and the small bar beside it shows that the displacement and the slope of the link are prescribed there. The floating frame has the axes \(\bar x\) and \(\bar y\), turned by the angle \(\theta\) against \(X\) and \(Y\) (definition 28.3). Because the hub turns the link as a whole, the angle \(\theta\) may be large, while the bending of the link in this frame is small. The link is in the frame \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\) a bent line with the deflection \(v(x)\), measured from the axis \(\bar x\). We divide it into three elements \(1\), \(2\), \(3\), and between them are the nodes \(0\), \(1\), \(2\), \(3\). At the node \(N\) we measure two numbers, the deflection \(v_{\mathrm{N}}\) and the slope \(\varphi_{\mathrm{N}}\), as for the beam in definition 12.3. The slope is the tangent of the angle between the tangent line and the axis \(\bar x\) and has no unit, the deflection has the unit metre.

We collect all numbers in a column \(\vek d = (v_0, \varphi_0, v_1, \varphi_1, \dots, v_{\mathrm{n}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{n}})^\T\) with \(2(n+1)\) numbers, and the field of the link is \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}} d_{\mathrm{I}} H_{\mathrm{I}}\) with the node functions \(H_{\mathrm{I}}\) of lemma 12.8. The column \(\vek d\) has more than the link needs. Already a single element has a field that is a polynomial of degree three, and among the polynomials of degree three are the straight lines. A straight line means that the element is displaced or tilted as a whole without bending, as figure 12.3 shows. Such a motion costs no energy, but for a link the floating frame already describes it. If we had it also among the elastic coordinates, we would describe the same position in several ways. So we first compute which columns \(\vek d\) the straight lines give at all.

Lemma 28.33 Energy-free rigid motions of an element and of the link (toga gibanja elementa in člena brez energije)

Let \([x_{\mathrm{a}}, x_{\mathrm{b}}]\) be a finite beam element of length \(h\) and constant bending stiffness \(EI > 0\) (definition 12.3), and let \(\vek r_1 = (1, 0, 1, 0)^\T\), \(\vek r_2 = (0, 1, h, 1)^\T\). Then:

  1. For a column \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}\) we have \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\) if and only if \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = a\,\vek r_1 + b\,\vek r_2\) with real numbers \(a\), \(b\), that is, if and only if the field of the element \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is the straight line \(a + b\,(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})\). The condition \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\) is the same, so the kernel of the matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) is exactly two-dimensional.
  2. Let the link be divided into \(n\) elements, and let \(\mat K\) be the full matrix of all \(2(n+1)\) unknowns without supports (theorem 12.9). Then \(\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = 0\) if and only if there are real numbers \(a\), \(b\) such that \(d_{2\mathrm{N}} = a + b\,x_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(d_{2\mathrm{N}+1} = b\) for every node \(N\), that is, when \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is one straight line \(a + b\,x\) along the whole link.
Proof

We must show that zero energy means a zero second derivative of the field, that such fields are straight lines, and that by lemma 12.2 a straight line indeed corresponds to the column \(a\,\vek r_1 + b\,\vek r_2\). Item 1 is already lemma 12.5 (items 2 and 3). We repeat the steps because we need steps 1 and 2 for item 2.

  1. By lemma 12.4, \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}} EI\,(v_{\mathrm{h}}'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x\). The field \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = c_0 + c_1 s + c_2 s^2 + c_3 s^3\) with \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}}\) has \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 2c_2 + 6c_3 s\), and the integrand is continuous and nonnegative.
  2. If the integral is zero, the integrand is zero everywhere (lemma 6.10, item 3). Because \(EI > 0\), we have \(2c_2 + 6c_3 s = 0\) for every \(s \in [0, h]\), hence \(c_3 = 0\) (the value at two different \(s\)) and \(c_2 = 0\). The field \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = c_0 + c_1 s\) is a straight line.
  3. By lemma 12.2 (item 2) the straight line \(a + b\,(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})\) corresponds to the column \((a,\ b,\ a + bh,\ b)^\T = a\,\vek r_1 + b\,\vek r_2\). Conversely, by item 1 of the same lemma the polynomial of degree three with the values and slopes of the column \(a\,\vek r_1 + b\,\vek r_2\) is unique, and the straight line \(a + b\,(x - x_{\mathrm{a}})\) has \(v_{\mathrm{h}}'' = 0\), hence zero energy.
  4. The matrix \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\) is symmetric and \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} \ge 0\) (lemma 12.5), so by lemma 7.16 \(\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = \vek 0\) if and only if \(\vek d_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\mat k_{\mathrm{e}}\vek d_{\mathrm{e}} = 0\). The kernel is thus \(\{a\,\vek r_1 + b\,\vek r_2\}\), the columns \(\vek r_1\), \(\vek r_2\) are independent (\(\vek r_1\) has second entry \(0\), \(\vek r_2\) has \(1\)), and the dimension is two. This is item 1.
  5. Item 2. By item 3 of theorem 12.9, \(\tfrac12\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \tfrac12\int_0^L EI\,(v_{\mathrm{h}}'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x\). This integral is a sum over the elements (lemma 6.10, item 1), each term is nonnegative, so at zero each is zero. By steps 1 and 2, \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is a straight line on every element.
  6. The field \(v_{\mathrm{h}}\) is continuously differentiable (lemma 12.8), so the straight lines of neighbouring elements have the same value and the same slope at the common node. A straight line is determined by the value and the slope at one point, so the lines are equal and \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = a + b\,x\) along the whole link. By item 2 of lemma 12.8, \(d_{2\mathrm{N}} = v_{\mathrm{h}}(x_{\mathrm{N}}) = a + b\,x_{\mathrm{N}}\) and \(d_{2\mathrm{N}+1} = v_{\mathrm{h}}'(x_{\mathrm{N}}) = b\).
  7. Conversely: for such \(d_{\mathrm{I}}\), \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}} d_{\mathrm{I}} H_{\mathrm{I}}\) equals \(a + b\,x\) by the same item of the lemma (a straight line is continuously differentiable and on every element a polynomial of degree three), it has a zero second derivative and zero energy.

Source: The proof is not from the source. SHABANA (PDF p. 280-281): the field of an element with nodal coordinates and slope describes an arbitrary rigid displacement in both axis directions, but nodal coordinates with small rotations do not describe a rigid rotation arbitrarily (chapter 7 of SHABANA). So the rotation is carried by the reference frame (SHABANA, PDF p. 286), here the frame \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\).

The lemma showed that the element and the whole link each have exactly two energy-free rigid motions: a displacement \(a\) perpendicular to the axis and a tilt \(b\). For an element the tilt is a line that rises by \(bh\) over the length \(h\), as the right half of figure 12.3 shows. The tilt \(b\) is a tangent of an angle, while a rigid rotation keeps the length of a rod, so the line \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = b\,x\) rotates only approximately. A point of the axis at distance \(x\) from the pivot comes to the distance \(x\sqrt{1 + b^2}\), so the relative error of the length is \(\sqrt{1 + b^2} - 1\). For \(b = 0{,}01\) this is \(5{,}0\cdot 10^{-5}\), for \(b = 0{,}0354\) (the slope of the end of our cantilever under a force of \(10\) N, \(FL^2/(2EI)\)) \(6{,}3\cdot 10^{-4}\), for \(b = 0{,}1\) already \(5{,}0\cdot 10^{-3}\) and for \(b = 0{,}3\) as much as \(4{,}4\cdot 10^{-2}\) (num-08.out). The field of the elements therefore describes a rotation only to first order in \(b\). For the elastic coordinates the slope is small and this suffices, while the finite rotation of the link is carried by the floating frame with the angle \(\theta\). This is the way in which SHABANA removed the rotation from the element coordinates.

Now we choose what to remove. The reference conditions for the frame at the joint (definition 28.5) prescribe the displacement and the slope of the link at \(x = 0\), \(v(0) = 0\) and \(v'(0) = 0\), that is \(d_0 = d_1 = 0\). Two conditions remove the two rigid motions of lemma 28.33: the line \(a + b\,x\) with value zero and slope zero at \(x = 0\) is \(a = b = 0\), the zero line. SHABANA requires at least as many conditions as rigid modes (PDF p. 287), and for a link there are two modes and two conditions. The elastic coordinates are the remaining \(2n\) numbers \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = (d_2, \dots, d_{2\mathrm{n}+1})^\T = (v_1, \varphi_1, \dots, v_{\mathrm{n}}, \varphi_{\mathrm{n}})^\T\) and the field is \(\bar{\vek u}(x) = (0,\ v_{\mathrm{h}}(x))^\T = \mat\Psi(x)\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) with the rows of \(\mat\Psi\) being the node functions \(H_{\mathrm{I}}\), \(I \ge 2\). Figure 28.21 shows exactly this: at node \(0\) there are no unknowns, at nodes \(1\) to \(3\) there are two each.

We need three quantities: the bending energy \(U = \tfrac12\int_0^L EI\,(v_{\mathrm{h}}'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) (lemma 5.76), the mass matrix \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} = \int_0^L \rho A\,\mat\psi^\T\mat\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\) and the coupling column \(\vek b = \int_0^L \rho A\,x\,\mat\psi^\T\,\mathrm{d}x\) (section 28.4), where \(\mat\psi = (H_2, \dots, H_{2\mathrm{n}+1})\) is the row of functions. The first and the second we know from chapter 12, the third is new. For one element with start \(x_{\mathrm{a}}\) and length \(h\), after integrating the polynomials (SymPy),

\[\vek b_{\mathrm{e}} = \rho A\,h\,\Big(\tfrac{3h + 10\,x_{\mathrm{a}}}{20},\ \tfrac{h\,(2h + 5\,x_{\mathrm{a}})}{60},\ \tfrac{7h + 10\,x_{\mathrm{a}}}{20},\ -\tfrac{h\,(3h + 5\,x_{\mathrm{a}})}{60}\Big)^\T , \tag{28.44}\]

that is \(\vek b_{\mathrm{e}} = \big(\int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\rho A\,x\,H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}x\big)_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{4}\) with units kg m for the deflection and kg m\(^2\) for the slope.

Theorem 28.34 Flexible link from elements (podajni člen iz elementov)

Let the link be a straight beam of length \(L\) with constant \(EI > 0\) and \(\rho A > 0\), divided into \(n\) elements with nodes \(0 = x_0 < \dots < x_{\mathrm{n}} = L\) (definition 12.3), with node functions \(H_0, \dots, H_{2\mathrm{n}+1}\) (lemma 12.8), full matrices \(\mat K\), \(\mat M\) of order \(2(n+1)\) (theorem 12.9 and equation (12.50)) and the full column \(\vek b = (b_0, \dots, b_{2\mathrm{n}+1})^\T\), \(b_{\mathrm{I}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\,\mathrm{d}x\). Let the reference conditions be \(d_0 = d_1 = 0\) (frame attached at the joint, tangent to the link), the elastic coordinates \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = (d_2, \dots, d_{2\mathrm{n}+1})^\T\), \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) and \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) the submatrices of rows and columns \(2, \dots, 2n+1\) of the matrices \(\mat K\) and \(\mat M\), \(\vek b_{\mathrm{d}} = (b_2, \dots, b_{2\mathrm{n}+1})^\T\) and \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x^2\,\mathrm{d}x\). Then:

  1. The elastic energy is \(U = \tfrac12\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) and the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is symmetric and positive definite.
  2. \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,\mat\psi^\T\mat\psi\,\mathrm{d}x\) and \(\vek b_{\mathrm{d}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x\,\mat\psi^\T\mathrm{d}x\) are sums over the elements, \(\vek b = \sum_{\mathrm{e}} \mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek b_{\mathrm{e}}\) with \(\vek b_{\mathrm{e}}\) from equation (28.44) and selection matrices \(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}\).
  3. For every \(n\), \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}=0}^{2n+1} b_{\mathrm{I}}\,(\vek d_{\mathrm{x}})_{\mathrm{I}}\) with \(\vek d_{\mathrm{x}} = (0, 1, x_1, 1, \dots, x_{\mathrm{n}}, 1)^\T\), with no mesh error.
  4. The symmetric matrix \(\begin{pmatrix} J_{\mathrm{link}} & \vek b_{\mathrm{d}}^\T \\ \vek b_{\mathrm{d}} & \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{pmatrix}\) of order \(2n + 1\) is positive definite, and so is \(\begin{pmatrix} J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} + \vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} & \vek b_{\mathrm{d}}^\T \\ \vek b_{\mathrm{d}} & \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{pmatrix}\) for every \(J_{\mathrm{h}} \ge 0\) and every \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\).
Proof

We must show that the energy and the matrices follow from the assembly, that the column \(\vek b\) splits over the elements, that the line \(x\) belongs to the field of the elements, and that the kinetic energy at zero velocity of the field is zero only at zero velocity of the joint and of the elastic coordinates.

  1. By item 3 of theorem 12.9, \(\tfrac12\vek d^\T\mat K\vek d = \tfrac12\int_0^L EI\,(v_{\mathrm{h}}'')^2\,\mathrm{d}x = U\) (lemma 5.76). With \(d_0 = d_1 = 0\) only the block of rows and columns \(2, \dots, 2n+1\) remains in the quadratic form, so \(U = \tfrac12\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\). The matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric, and so is its block.
  2. The support at node \(0\) prescribes the displacement and the slope. The only line \(a + b\,x\) with value \(0\) and slope \(0\) at \(x = 0\) is \(a = b = 0\), the zero line. By corollary 12.11 (item 1) the block \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is positive definite. This is item 1.
  3. By lemma 12.43 and equation (12.50), for \(\vek d\) with \(v_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}} d_{\mathrm{I}} H_{\mathrm{I}}\) the value is \(\vek d^\T\mat M\vek d = \int_0^L\rho A\,v_{\mathrm{h}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), so \(M_{\mathrm{IJ}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,H_{\mathrm{I}}H_{\mathrm{J}}\,\mathrm{d}x\) (by bilinearity and symmetry). For \(I, J \ge 2\) these are the entries of the block \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\).
  4. The integral \(b_{\mathrm{I}}\) is a sum over the elements (lemma 6.10, item 1). By item 1 of lemma 12.8, on the element \(e\) the function \(H_{\mathrm{I}}\) equals \(H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\) for \(I = g_{\mathrm{ei}}\) and is zero elsewhere, so \(b_{\mathrm{I}} = \sum_{\mathrm{e}}(\mat Z_{\mathrm{e}}^\T\vek b_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{I}}\) with \((\vek b_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{i}} = \int_{\mathrm{x}_\mathrm{a}}^{x_{\mathrm{b}}}\rho A\,x\,H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}x\) (lemma 7.12).
  5. With \(s = x - x_{\mathrm{a}}\) we have \(x = x_{\mathrm{a}} + s\) and \((\vek b_{\mathrm{e}})_{\mathrm{i}} = \rho A\,\big(x_{\mathrm{a}}\int_0^h H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}s + \int_0^h s\,H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}s\big)\). Integrals of polynomials (SymPy): \(\int H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}s = (h/2,\ h^2/12,\ h/2,\ -h^2/12)\) and \(\int s\,H_{\mathrm{i}}^e\,\mathrm{d}s = (3h^2/20,\ h^3/30,\ 7h^2/20,\ -h^3/20)\). The sum gives equation (28.44). This is item 2.
  6. Item 3. The line \(x\) is continuously differentiable and on every element a polynomial of degree three, so by item 2 of lemma 12.8 \(x = \sum_{\mathrm{I}} (\vek d_{\mathrm{x}})_{\mathrm{I}}\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\) with the values \(x_{\mathrm{N}}\) and the slope \(1\) at node \(N\). By linearity of the integral, \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x\cdot x\,\mathrm{d}x = \sum_{\mathrm{I}}(\vek d_{\mathrm{x}})_{\mathrm{I}}\int_0^L\rho A\,x\,H_{\mathrm{I}}\,\mathrm{d}x = \sum_{\mathrm{I}}(\vek d_{\mathrm{x}})_{\mathrm{I}}\,b_{\mathrm{I}}\). There is no mesh approximation in this.
  7. Item 4. Let \((\dot\theta, \dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}})\) be a vector at which the quadratic form of the matrix is zero. The form is \(\int_0^L\rho A\,(x\,\dot\theta + \dot v_{\mathrm{h}})^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) with \(\dot v_{\mathrm{h}} = \sum_{\mathrm{I}\ge2}\dot q_{\mathrm{I}} H_{\mathrm{I}}\), because squaring gives \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\dot\theta^2 + 2\dot\theta\,\vek b_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} + \dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}\) by steps 3 to 5 and the definition of \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\). The integrand is continuous and nonnegative and \(\rho A > 0\), so by lemma 6.10 (item 3) \(x\,\dot\theta + \dot v_{\mathrm{h}}(x) = 0\) for every \(x \in [0, L]\).
  8. The derivative at \(x = 0\): \(\dot\theta + \dot v_{\mathrm{h}}'(0) = 0\). The reference condition \(d_1 = 0\) means \(\dot v_{\mathrm{h}}'(0) = 0\), so \(\dot\theta = 0\). Then \(\dot v_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), and by item 2 of lemma 12.8 all \(\dot q_{\mathrm{I}} = 0\). The matrix is positive definite. The diagonal addition \(J_{\mathrm{h}} + \vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^\T\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} \ge 0\) (item 3 for \(\vek d = (0, 0, \vek q_{\mathrm{d}})\)) does not decrease the quadratic form.

Source: The proof is not from the source. SHABANA (PDF pp. 288-289, 298-300) assembles the mass matrix of a body from element matrices and separates the rigid and the elastic motion, and here it is derived directly for the joint and the transverse field with the lemmas of chapter 12.

The proof showed that \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) and \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) are the matrices that we already have from chapter 12 for the cantilever, and that only the row \(\vek b\) is new, which we assemble from elements with equation (28.44). All three quantities are integrals over the undeformed link and do not depend on \(\theta\) or \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\), so we compute them once. Item 3 says something that the modal model of the next section cannot do: we get \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\) exactly, because the line \(x\) belongs to the field of the elements, while for the modal model the sum of squares of the couplings will be smaller than \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\).

Example 28.35 Steel strip with one element and with several (jekleni trak iz enega elementa in iz več)

The link is a steel strip of length \(L = 1\) m with cross-section \(40 \times 6\) mm, the thickness \(6\) mm lying in the plane of motion. We take \(E = 196\) GPa, \(\rho = 7800\) kg/m\(^3\), so \(EI = 141{,}12\) N m\(^2\), \(\rho A = 1{,}872\) kg/m, \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = \rho A L^3/3 = 0{,}624\) kg m\(^2\), and a hub with \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0{,}2\) kg m\(^2\).

With one element (\(n = 1\), \(h = L\), \(x_{\mathrm{a}} = 0\)), in the coordinates \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = (v_1, \varphi_1)\), by lemma 12.4 and lemma 12.43,

\[\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} = \frac{EI}{L^3}\begin{pmatrix}12 & -6L\\ -6L & 4L^2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}1693{,}44 & -846{,}72\\ -846{,}72 & 564{,}48\end{pmatrix} , \quad \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} = \frac{\rho A L}{420}\begin{pmatrix}156 & -22L\\ -22L & 4L^2\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}0{,}69531 & -0{,}09806\\ -0{,}09806 & 0{,}01783\end{pmatrix} ,\]

and by equation (28.44) \(\vek b_{\mathrm{d}} = (7\rho A L^2/20,\ -3\rho A L^3/60)^\T = (0{,}6552,\ -0{,}0936)^\T\). The node \(0\) contributes \(b_0 = 0{,}2808\) and \(b_1 = 0{,}0624\). We check item 3 of theorem 28.34: \(\vek d_{\mathrm{x}} = (0, 1, 1, 1)^\T\) and \(b_1\cdot 1 + b_2\cdot 1 + b_3\cdot 1 = 0{,}0624 + 0{,}6552 - 0{,}0936 = 0{,}6240\) kg m\(^2\), exactly \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\). The mass of the whole element, \(\vek d = (1, 0, 1, 0)^\T\), is \(\vek d^\T\mat M\vek d = 1{,}872\) kg, exactly \(\rho A L\) (lemma 12.43).

In the figure the axes show the number of elements \(n\) and the relative error \(\varepsilon = \omega_{\mathrm{h}}/\omega - 1\), where \(\omega_{\mathrm{h}}\) is the frequency from the elements and \(\omega\) the exact one. Line \(1\) is the first frequency of the locked link, \(0{,}48\) % at one element and \(2{,}1\cdot 10^{-6}\) at eight. For the second and higher frequencies the error is larger, because a higher mode gets one half-wave fewer elements. For the free joint the results are likewise above the exact ones, but we have no proof for the pair with the coupling \(\vek b\), because corollary 13.56 speaks of a beam with a locked joint and not of a pair with a coupling: this is an observation from the numbers. After every doubling of the number of elements from \(n = 4\) onwards the errors fall by a factor from \(13\) to \(16\) (num-08.out), which is an observation, not a theorem.

Now we show why both reference conditions are needed. If we prescribe only the displacement \(v(0) = 0\) and leave the slope at the joint free (one condition), the field of the elements still contains the rigid tilt \(x\). The same bent position of the link would then be written in two ways.

We draw the same blackwhite curve twice. On the left the frame \(\bar x\), \(\bar y\) is directed along the tangent of the link at the joint, which is the angle \(\theta_1\) against the horizontal, and the slope at the joint is zero, as the choice of theorem 28.34 prescribes. On the right the curve stays the same, but the frame is turned by a larger angle \(\theta_2\), for example to the direction of the chord from the joint to the end. The curve now has a slope at the joint, the angle \(\beta\) against the new axis \(\bar x\), and the two descriptions give the same position if \(\theta\) and the elastic slope change against each other. A simple consequence is the following lemma.

Lemma 28.36 One reference condition is not enough (en referenčni pogoj ne zadošča)

Let the link be as in theorem 28.34, but with only the displacement \(d_0 = 0\) prescribed, and the slope \(d_1\) remains an elastic coordinate: \(\vek q'_{\mathrm{d}} = (d_1, \dots, d_{2\mathrm{n}+1})^\T\) with submatrices \(\mat K'\), \(\mat M'\) and the column \(\vek b'\). Let \(J_{\mathrm{h}} > 0\) be the moment of inertia of the hub. Then:

  1. The column \(\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}} = (1, x_1, 1, \dots, x_{\mathrm{n}}, 1)^\T\) has \(\vek d'^\T_{\mathrm{x}}\mat K'\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), so \(\mat K'\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}} = \vek 0\). The pair of matrices for \((\theta, \vek q'_{\mathrm{d}})\) thus has two zero frequencies instead of the one for a link with both conditions (the rigid rotation of the link with the hub).
  2. In the undeformed position \(\vek q'_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0\) the velocity vector \((\dot\theta, \dot{\vek q}'_{\mathrm{d}}) = (1, -\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}})\) has kinetic energy \(\tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{h}}\), that is only the energy of the hub. At \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) the mass matrix is singular in this position.
Proof

We must show that the line \(x\) belongs to the chosen field, that it has no energy, and that its kinetic energy, when we cancel it with the rotation of the frame, is only the energy of the hub.

  1. By lemma 28.33 (item 2) the line \(x\) has the column \((0, 1, x_1, 1, \dots, x_{\mathrm{n}}, 1)^\T\). At \(x = 0\) it has the value zero, so it fulfils the prescribed \(d_0 = 0\), and its column without the first entry is \(\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}}\). Its energy is zero (item 2 of the same lemma), so \(\mat K'\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}} = \vek 0\) by lemma 7.16. There are no other columns in the kernel: by item 2 of the same lemma only the line \(a + b\,x\) has zero energy, the condition \(d_0 = 0\) gives \(a = 0\), so the kernel of the matrix \(\mat K'\) is the set of columns \(b\,\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}}\).
  2. The mass matrix of \((\theta, \vek q'_{\mathrm{d}})\) is positive definite for \(J_{\mathrm{h}} > 0\) (a zero quadratic form gives from \(J_{\mathrm{h}}\dot\theta^2\) first \(\dot\theta = 0\), then from \(\int\rho A\,\dot v_{\mathrm{h}}^2 = 0\) the field \(\dot v_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\), and by lemma 12.8 all \(\dot q'_{\mathrm{I}} = 0\)), so the eigenproblem \(\mat K\vek x = \omega^2\mat M\vek x\) with \(\mat K = \mathrm{diag}(0, \mat K')\) counts the zero frequencies as the dimension of the kernel. The kernel of the matrix \(\mat K\) consists of the columns \((t,\ b\,\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}})\) with arbitrary real \(t\) and \(b\), so it is two-dimensional and there are exactly two zero frequencies.
  3. In the position \(\vek q'_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0\) the term \(\vek q'^\T_{\mathrm{d}}\mat M'\vek q'_{\mathrm{d}}\) in the upper left entry of the mass matrix (item 4 of theorem 28.34) is zero, so the kinetic energy is, as in step 7 of the same theorem, \(\tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{h}}\dot\theta^2 + \tfrac12\int_0^L\rho A\,(x\,\dot\theta + \dot v_{\mathrm{h}})^2\,\mathrm{d}x\). At \(\dot\theta = 1\) and \(\dot v_{\mathrm{h}} = -x\) (the column \(-\vek d'_{\mathrm{x}}\)) the integrand is zero, \(\tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{h}}\) remains, and at \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) zero.

Source: The proof is not from the source. SHABANA (PDF p. 287): the number of reference conditions must be greater than or equal to the number of rigid modes in the field, a link has two rigid modes and one condition is too few.

The lemma says that one condition does not merely leave the matter undetermined, but that the model predicts something else. We check it on the link of example 28.35 with \(n = 4\) elements and \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0{,}2\) kg m\(^2\) (num-08.out). With both conditions the lowest frequencies are \(0\), \(59{,}149\) and \(199{,}211\) rad/s. With one condition they are \(0\), \(0\) and \(133{,}946\). The second zero is false, and the third value equals the first nonzero frequency at \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) (the table of the example). The reason is the false rigid rotation: the link can turn against the hub without energy, as if it were attached by a hinge, so the hub does not influence the frequencies of the link. In figure 28.23 this is the exchange of the rotation of the frame with the slope at the joint. At \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) and in the undeformed position the smallest eigenvalue of the mass matrix with one condition is below \(10^{-15}\) kg m\(^2\) in magnitude, which is a zero due to rounding, while with both conditions it is \(2{,}0\cdot 10^{-5}\) kg m\(^2\) against the largest \(1{,}02\) kg m\(^2\). The second value is positive, as item 4 of theorem 28.34 demands, and the first is zero. The choice of both conditions at the joint follows from the fact that the hub holds the position and the tangent of the link. For other links the choice follows from other joints, and SHABANA (PDF p. 286) for the connecting rod (ojnica), hinged at both ends, chooses the conditions of a simply supported beam.

With theorem 28.34 we have, for a link with any number of elements, all three quantities that the equations of motion of section 28.6 need. What remains is to reduce the elastic coordinates: the number \(2n\) is large for a fine mesh, but we are interested only in the lowest modes. This is done by the next section on modal reduction. How we connect the joints of several links and what else a whole manipulator needs is the subject of chapter 27.
