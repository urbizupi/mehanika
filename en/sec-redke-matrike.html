Chapter 7 Basics of FEM for bars and trusses

7.8 Mathematics: sparse and banded matrices (redke in pasovne matrike)

The matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) of the bridge from section 7.7 has order \(21\), so \(21 \cdot 21 = 441\) entries, of which only \(93\) are nonzero, that is \(21\,\%\). Almost four fifths, exactly \(348\) entries or \(78{,}9\,\%\), are zeros, and the nonzero entries lie near the main diagonal (figure 7.30). This raises two questions. May we leave the zeros out of the computation, and how much do we save? And does the elimination of chapter 6 (theorem 6.29) keep the zeros, or fill them with nonzero numbers? This section is mathematical: it proves statements about matrices, and for the bridge each one is tested with numbers.

7.8.1 Sparse and banded matrix (redka in pasovna matrika)

A matrix is sparse (redka matrika) if most of its entries are zero (COOK, PDF p. 51, "Sparsity": "A matrix is called sparse if it contains many zeros"). For practical structures with hundreds or thousands of elements, more than \(99\,\%\) of the entries of the stiffness matrix may be zero by the same source, so programs use special storage formats and algorithms. Why the matrix of the bridge is sparse too, corollary 7.14 tells us, which holds for every structure made of elements, so also for the truss of section 7.5: the entry \(K_{\mathrm{ij}}\) is zero if the unknowns \(i\) and \(j\) are not at nodes of the same element. A bar connects only the nodes at its two ends, and at most five bars meet at any node of the bridge. So no row of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) has more than \(7\) nonzero entries (by counting), and this would stay so for a bridge with more bays, because the number of bars at a node would not grow. The number of nonzero entries would therefore grow in proportion to the order \(n\) of the matrix, and the number of all entries as the square \(n^2\).

Where in a row are the nonzero entries? For the hanging bar of chapter 6 we numbered the nodes of the bar in order and only three diagonals were nonzero, as the matrix (6.15) shows. For the bridge the answer depends on how we numbered the unknowns, and that numbering is our choice. How far from the diagonal the nonzero entries reach, we capture with one number.

Definition 7.40 Banded matrix (pasovna matrika)

Let \(\mat K\) be a square matrix of order \(n\) with entries \(K_{\mathrm{ij}}\) and let \(b\) be an integer between \(0\) and \(n - 1\). The matrix \(\mat K\) is banded (pasovna) with half-bandwidth (polovična širina pasu) \(b\) if

\[K_{\mathrm{ij}} = 0 \quad \text{for every pair } (i, j) \text{ with } |i - j| > b . \tag{7.39}\]

The nonzero entries therefore lie in the band between the diagonals \(j = i - b\) and \(j = i + b\). The half-bandwidth of a matrix is the smallest such number \(b\).

Source: COOK, PDF p. 58, "semibandwidth": for row \(i\) it is the number of columns from the diagonal to the rightmost nonzero entry, and for the whole matrix the source takes the root-mean-square average over the rows. We take the largest distance \(|i - j|\) that carries a nonzero entry, so that the band certainly contains all nonzero entries. The source counts the diagonal in a row, we do not.

For the matrix (6.15) we have \(b = 1\). A banded matrix with \(b = 0\) is diagonal. If \(b = n - 1\), the condition (7.39) demands nothing and every matrix is banded, but the band is worth nothing.

Let us look at what spoils the band. The matrix (6.15) is not banded with \(b = 0\), because \(K_{12} = -EA/h \ne 0\) at \(|1 - 2| = 1 > 0\), and so \(b = 1\) really is the smallest such number. To the hanging bar we add one more bar with the same axial stiffness \(EA/h\), which connects the unknowns \(1\) and \(3\) (it skips the unknown \(2\)). This bar contributes \(+EA/h\) at the places \((1, 1)\) and \((3, 3)\) and \(-EA/h\) at the places \((1, 3)\) and \((3, 1)\), and the matrix becomes

\[\frac{EA}{h}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -1 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & 3 & -1 \\ 0 & 0 & -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} .\]

The condition (7.39) for \(b = 1\) fails at the pair \((1, 3)\), because \(|1 - 3| = 2 > 1\) and \(K_{13} = -EA/h \ne 0\). But \(b = 2\) holds, since all the nonzero entries are at distance at most \(2\) from the diagonal. A single connection across one node has therefore increased \(b\) from \(1\) to \(2\), because the condition demands zeros for every pair, not only for most.

For a truss we number by nodes, as in the paragraph on unknowns in section 7.5: we number the nodes \(1, \dots, N\), every node has \(r\) unknowns (\(r = 1\) for the bar, \(r = 2\) for the plane truss and \(r = 3\) for the space truss), and the unknowns of node \(p\) get the numbers \(r(p - 1) + 1, \dots, rp\). For the plane truss these are \(2p - 1\) and \(2p\). The band of the matrix is then decided by one datum, namely how far apart the numbers of the nodes of one element are.

Lemma 7.41 Band of the structure matrix (pas matrike konstrukcije)

Let a mesh have \(N\) nodes numbered \(1, \dots, N\), and let every node have \(r\) unknowns, numbered by nodes (node \(p\) has the unknowns \(r(p - 1) + 1, \dots, rp\)). Let \(m\) be the largest difference \(|p - q|\) of the numbers of two nodes that belong to the same element. Then the structure matrix \(\mat K\) has half-bandwidth

\[b \le r\,(m + 1) - 1 . \tag{7.40}\]

The matrix of free unknowns \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\), obtained by deleting the rows and columns of the prescribed unknowns (theorem 7.18), has half-bandwidth at most as large.

Proof

We must show that \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} = 0\) for \(|i - j| > r(m + 1) - 1\). We assume \(i \le j\), which loses no generality because of the symmetry of the matrix 7.14.

  1. Let \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} \ne 0\). By corollary 7.14 the unknowns \(i\) and \(j\) stand in the same row of the connectivity table, that is in the same element, so they are at nodes \(p\) and \(q\) of this element (\(p = q\) is allowed).
  2. Since \(i \le j\), we have \(p \le q\), because the unknowns are numbered by nodes. By the definition of \(m\), \(q - p \le m\).
  3. For the unknown \(i\) of node \(p\) we have \(i \ge r(p - 1) + 1\), and for the unknown \(j\) of node \(q\) we have \(j \le rq\).
  4. Therefore \(j - i \le rq - r(p - 1) - 1 = r(q - p + 1) - 1 \le r(m + 1) - 1\). This is (7.40).
  5. For \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) we delete the rows and columns of some unknowns, and the remaining ones keep their order. The new number of an unknown is the old one decreased by the number of deleted unknowns before it, so the difference of the numbers of two remaining unknowns does not grow. The same bound therefore holds for \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. COOK, PDF p. 51 ("Sparsity") and EP, p. 541 for the condition that \(K_{\mathrm{IJ}}\) is zero if the nodes are not in the same element.

Let us test it on the hanging bar: \(r = 1\), nodes in order and \(m = 1\) give \(b \le 1 \cdot 2 - 1 = 1\), the tridiagonal matrix (6.15). Now let us look at two numberings of the bridge (figure 7.30), with the node names from section 7.7.1 (\(b_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the bottom, \(t_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the top chord of the bridge). A chord (pas mostu) is the row of bars along the bottom or top edge of the bridge, the band of a matrix (pas matrike) the places next to the diagonal of definition 7.40, and the two meanings must not be confused, since Slovenian uses the same word for both. In the first we go along the span: \(b_0, b_1, t_1, b_2, t_2, \dots, b_5, t_5, b_6\) get the numbers \(1\) to \(12\). In the second we number the nodes of the bottom chord first, \(b_0, \dots, b_6\) with \(1\) to \(7\), and then those of the top chord, \(t_1, \dots, t_5\) with \(8\) to \(12\), and we call this the numbering by rows.

  1. Along the span, the farthest apart are the bar \(t_4 b_3\) between the numbers \(9\) and \(6\) and the bar \(t_5 b_4\) between \(11\) and \(8\), so \(m = 3\) and \(b \le 2 \cdot 4 - 1 = 7\). The computation gives \(b = 7\) for \(\mat K\) and for \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\), so the bound is reached.
  2. By rows, the farthest apart are the bar \(b_0 t_1\) between \(1\) and \(8\) and the bar \(t_4 b_3\) between \(11\) and \(4\), so \(m = 7\) and \(b \le 2 \cdot 8 - 1 = 15\). The matrix \(\mat K\) has \(b = 15\) indeed, but the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) has \(b = 14\), because between the unknown \(u\) of node \(b_3\) and the unknown \(v\) of node \(t_4\) the unknown \(v\) of node \(b_6\) (a support) is deleted, so the numbers come closer by \(1\).

The cause of the difference is simple. A bar of the bridge reaches at most across one bay, and in the numbering along the span every bay adds only two numbers (\(b_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(t_{\mathrm{i}}\)), so the two nodes of one bar have numbers at most \(3\) apart. In the numbering by rows the vertical bar \(b_{\mathrm{i}} t_{\mathrm{i}}\) has the numbers \(i + 1\) and \(i + 7\), always \(6\) apart, because the bottom chord has \(7\) nodes. The bars \(b_0 t_1\), \(t_4 b_3\) and \(t_5 b_4\) are \(7\) apart. A longer bridge would have the same \(b\) in the first numbering, but in the second \(b\) would grow with the length of the bridge. The number of nonzero entries is the same in both cases, \(93\), since the matrices are the same, only the unknowns are in another order.

7.8.2 Elimination keeps the band (eliminacija ohrani pas)

Zeros and band count only if the elimination does not destroy them. Let us repeat one step of elimination from the proof of theorem 6.29. We write the matrix as \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix} K_{11} & \vek g^\T \\ \vek g & \mat K' \end{smallmatrix}\right]\), where \(\vek g\) is the column of entries \(g_{\mathrm{i}} = K_{\mathrm{i}1}\) for \(i = 2, \dots, n\) (in chapter 6 this column is called \(\vek b\), here we write \(\vek g\) so that the letter \(b\) remains the half-bandwidth). After the step the matrix \(\mat K'' = \mat K' - \vek g\,\vek g^\T/K_{11}\) remains, with entries

\[K''_{\mathrm{ij}} = K_{\mathrm{ij}} - \frac{g_{\mathrm{i}}\,g_{\mathrm{j}}}{K_{11}} \qquad (i, j = 2, \dots, n) . \tag{7.41}\]

The entry \((i, j)\) therefore changes only if \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(g_{\mathrm{j}}\) are both nonzero. If \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} = 0\) but \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(g_{\mathrm{j}}\) are nonzero, the entry becomes nonzero. This is called fill (zapolnitev): a zero that the elimination turns into a nonzero number (COOK, PDF p. 57: "a direct (noniterative) equation solver creates fills").

For the hanging bar \(\vek g\) has only one nonzero entry, so the elimination changes only one diagonal entry and there is no fill. For the bridge with the numbering along the span (the unknowns \(1, 2, \dots, 6\) are \(u\) and \(v\) of the nodes \(b_1\), \(t_1\) and \(b_2\), the unknown \(7\) is \(u\) of node \(t_2\)) it is so in the first two steps. The third step has the pivot \(K_{33}\), which is the unknown \(u\) of node \(t_1\). Its column has nonzero entries in the rows \(5\), \(6\) and \(7\), because \(t_1\) is connected to node \(b_2\) by an oblique bar (it links \(u\) of node \(t_1\) with \(u\) and \(v\) of node \(b_2\)) and to node \(t_2\) by a horizontal bar (it links \(u\) with \(u\)). The entry \(K_{43}\), which links \(u\) and \(v\) of node \(t_1\), is zero, because the contributions of the oblique bars \(b_0 t_1\) and \(t_1 b_2\) cancel in it: the bars have the same \(EA/L\) and the product of direction cosines \(cs = +\tfrac12\) and \(-\tfrac12\). By equation (7.41) all entries with three rows and columns \(5, 6, 7\) change, also the entries \((7, 5)\) and \((7, 6)\), which were zero, since the nodes \(b_2\) and \(t_2\) are connected only by a vertical bar, and that links only \(v\) with \(v\). The elimination of the unknown \(u\) of node \(t_1\) expresses it by the unknowns of its neighbours and so links them to each other. Whether the band endures this, the next lemma tells.

Lemma 7.42 Elimination keeps the band (eliminacija ohrani pas)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\) with half-bandwidth \(b\). Let \(\mat K^{(1)} = \mat K\) and let \(\mat K^{(k+1)}\) be the matrix that remains after the \(k\)-th step of elimination without row exchanges (the matrix \(\mat K''\) of equation (7.41) for \(\mat K^{(k)}\)), of order \(n - k\). Then every matrix \(\mat K^{(k)}\) is symmetric, positive definite and has half-bandwidth at most \(b\). Entries outside the band therefore stay zero throughout the elimination, and fill occurs only inside the band.

Proof

We prove it by induction on \(k\) (theorem 1.44). The statement \(P(k)\): the matrix \(\mat K^{(k)}\) is symmetric, positive definite and \(K^{(k)}_{\mathrm{ij}} = 0\) for \(|i - j| > b\).

  1. Start. \(P(1)\) holds by assumption.
  2. Induction step. Let \(P(k)\) hold for some \(k < n\) and let \(\mat K^{(k)}\) be written as above with \(K_{11}\), \(\vek g\) and \(\mat K'\). By theorem 6.29, steps 5 and 6 of the proof, \(\mat K^{(k+1)} = \mat K^{(k)\prime\prime}\) is symmetric and positive definite.
  3. The entry \(g_{\mathrm{i}} = K_{\mathrm{i}1}\) is zero for \(i > b + 1\), because \(|i - 1| = i - 1 > b\) and \(K_{\mathrm{i}1} = 0\) by \(P(k)\).
  4. Let \(|i - j| > b\) with \(i, j \ge 2\). If \(i\) and \(j\) were both at most \(b + 1\), they would lie in \(2, \dots, b + 1\) and the difference would be \(|i - j| \le b - 1\). Hence at least one of the numbers \(i\), \(j\) is greater than \(b + 1\), and by step 3 at least one of \(g_{\mathrm{i}}\), \(g_{\mathrm{j}}\) is zero.
  5. By equation (7.41), then, \(K''_{\mathrm{ij}} = K_{\mathrm{ij}} - g_{\mathrm{i}} g_{\mathrm{j}}/K_{11} = 0 - 0 = 0\), since \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} = 0\) by \(P(k)\). The matrix \(\mat K^{(k+1)}\) is therefore banded with half-bandwidth at most \(b\) (the difference of two row numbers does not change, because the rows \(2, \dots, n\) get the numbers \(1, \dots, n - 1\), all decreased by \(1\)). This is \(P(k + 1)\).
  6. By the principle of induction \(P(k)\) holds for every \(k = 1, \dots, n\) (the matrix \(\mat K^{(n)}\) has order \(1\)).

Source: The proof is not from a source. COOK, PDF p. 58: for a matrix with a small profile there is little fill in the elimination, "Then nonzero entries in the structure matrix are tightly clustered along its diagonal, so elimination produces few fills".

The lemma says that there is no fill outside the band. How much of it there is inside the band it does not say, so we count on the bridge. The lower triangle of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) has \(57\) nonzero entries in both numberings (\(21\) on the diagonal and \(36\) below it). After the elimination, the lower triangle of multipliers, which we will call \(\mat L\) in the next section, has \(83\) nonzero entries in the numbering along the span, so the fill is \(83 - 57 = 26\) entries. In the numbering by rows it has \(121\) and the fill is \(121 - 57 = 64\) (figure 7.31). A wider band leaves more room for fill: below the diagonal and including it, the band holds \(140\) entries for \(b = 7\) and \(210\) for \(b = 14\), of \(231\) in the lower triangle altogether.

How much work do we save? We count work as the number of multiplications and divisions. For counting we need the sum \(1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + m^2\), which section 3.5 wrote without proof in the history box on Cavalieri. We prove it now, by induction.

Lemma 7.43 Sum of the squares of natural numbers (vsota kvadratov naravnih števil)

For every natural number \(m\)

\[\begin{aligned} 1 + 2 + \dots + m &= \frac{m\,(m + 1)}{2} , \\ 1^2 + 2^2 + \dots + m^2 &= \frac{m\,(m + 1)(2m + 1)}{6} . \end{aligned} \tag{7.42}\]
Proof

We prove it by induction on \(m\) (theorem 1.44) for both equalities together.

  1. Start, \(m = 1\). The left side of both is \(1\), the right sides are \(1 \cdot 2/2 = 1\) and \(1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3/6 = 1\).
  2. Induction step. Let the equalities hold for \(m\). To the first sum we add \(m + 1\), to the second \((m + 1)^2\).
  3. The first sum becomes \(\frac{m(m + 1)}{2} + (m + 1) = \frac{(m + 1)(m + 2)}{2}\). This is the first equality for \(m + 1\).
  4. The second sum becomes \(\frac{m(m + 1)(2m + 1)}{6} + (m + 1)^2 = \frac{(m + 1)\,\big(m(2m + 1) + 6(m + 1)\big)}{6} = \frac{(m + 1)(2m^2 + 7m + 6)}{6}\).
  5. Since \((m + 2)(2m + 3) = 2m^2 + 7m + 6\), this is \(\frac{(m + 1)(m + 2)(2m + 3)}{6}\), which is the second equality for \(m + 1\), because \(2(m + 1) + 1 = 2m + 3\).
  6. By the principle of induction the equalities hold for every \(m\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. The equality is stated without proof in section 3.5.

The proof showed that the formula holds for every \(m\), because it holds for \(m = 1\) and passes from \(m\) to \(m + 1\).

Lemma 7.44 Cost of elimination (cena eliminacije)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\). Elimination without row exchanges that uses the symmetry and corrects only the lower triangle in every step performs, for a full matrix,

\[\frac{(n - 1)\,n\,(n + 1)}{6} \approx \frac{n^3}{6} \tag{7.43}\]

multiplications in the corrections of entries and \(n(n - 1)/2\) divisions in the computation of the multipliers. If \(\mat K\) has half-bandwidth \(b < n\) and in every step we skip the rows that have a zero in the pivot column, the number of multiplications is at most

\[\frac{b\,(b + 1)\,(3n - 2b - 1)}{6} \approx \frac{n\,b^2}{2} \quad (n \gg b \gg 1) , \tag{7.44}\]

and the number of divisions at most \(b\,(2n - b - 1)/2 \approx n\,b\). The bounds are reached if in every step all \(b\) entries below the pivot are nonzero.

Proof

We must show that the number of operations is as stated. We count step by step. Let \(k = 1, \dots, n - 1\) be the step in which the matrix \(\mat K^{(k)}\) has order \(n - k + 1\) (notation of lemma 7.42).

  1. In step \(k\) there are \(s = n - k\) rows below the pivot. The multiplier of row \(i\) is \(g_{\mathrm{i}}/K_{11}\), which is one division per row, \(s\) divisions in all.
  2. The correction \(K_{\mathrm{ij}} - g_{\mathrm{i}} g_{\mathrm{j}}/K_{11} = K_{\mathrm{ij}} - \ell_{\mathrm{i}} g_{\mathrm{j}}\) with the already computed multiplier \(\ell_{\mathrm{i}} = g_{\mathrm{i}}/K_{11}\) is one multiplication. Since the matrix is symmetric (step 5 of the proof of theorem 6.29), we correct only the entries \((i, j)\) with \(i \ge j\) among the \(s\) rows, that is \(s(s + 1)/2\) entries and as many multiplications.
  3. For a full matrix the sum over the steps, as \(s = n - k\) goes from \(1\) to \(n - 1\), by lemma 7.43 with \(m = n - 1\) is
    \[\begin{aligned} \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n-1} \frac{s(s + 1)}{2} &= \frac12\left(\frac{(n - 1)\,n\,(2n - 1)}{6} + \frac{(n - 1)\,n}{2}\right) \\ &= \frac{(n - 1)\,n\,\big((2n - 1) + 3\big)}{12} = \frac{(n - 1)\,n\,(n + 1)}{6} . \end{aligned}\]
    The number of divisions is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n-1} s = n(n - 1)/2\). This is (7.43).
  4. For a banded matrix, by lemma 7.42 all entries outside the band of every matrix \(\mat K^{(k)}\) are zero, so in step \(k\) only \(t_{\mathrm{k}} = \min(b, n - k)\) entries of the column \(\vek g\) are nonzero (step 3 of the proof of the lemma). Multipliers and corrections are needed only for these rows, so at most \(t_{\mathrm{k}}\) divisions and \(t_{\mathrm{k}}(t_{\mathrm{k}} + 1)/2\) multiplications (exactly as many if all these entries are really nonzero).
  5. For \(k \le n - b\) we have \(t_{\mathrm{k}} = b\), and for \(k > n - b\) the number \(t_{\mathrm{k}} = n - k\) goes from \(b - 1\) to \(1\). The sum of these upper bounds is
    \[\begin{aligned} (n - b)\,\frac{b(b + 1)}{2} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{b-1}\frac{s(s + 1)}{2} &= (n - b)\,\frac{b(b + 1)}{2} + \frac{(b - 1)\,b\,(b + 1)}{6} \\ &= \frac{b(b + 1)}{6}\,\big(3(n - b) + (b - 1)\big) , \end{aligned}\]
    where we computed the second sum as in step 3 with \(b - 1\) instead of \(n - 1\). The bracket is \(3n - 2b - 1\), which is (7.44). The number of divisions is \((n - b)\,b + b(b - 1)/2 = b\,(2n - b - 1)/2\).
  6. For \(n \gg b \gg 1\) we have \(b + 1 \approx b\) and \(3n - 2b - 1 \approx 3n\), so \(b \cdot b \cdot 3n/6 = n b^2/2\).

Source: The counting is not from a source. The same proportionality \(n^3\) for a full matrix is given by KREYSZIG, PDF p. 876 (crop p876-b2): \(f(n) = \tfrac12 (n - 1) n + \tfrac23 (n^2 - 1) n \approx \tfrac23 n^3\), where the source counts divisions, multiplications and subtractions for a nonsymmetric matrix, so \(\tfrac13 n^3\) multiplications. COOK, PDF p. 58: for a matrix with \(n \gg b\) the computational effort is "approximately proportional to \(nb^2\)".

The lemma and its bound for \(n \gg b\) say that the cost grows only linearly with the order \(n\) if the band stays equally wide, and for a full matrix as the third power. For the bridge with \(n = 21\) unknowns:

  1. Full matrix: by equation (7.43), \(20 \cdot 21 \cdot 22/6 = 1540\) multiplications and \(210\) divisions, \(1750\) operations in all.
  2. Band \(b = 7\) (along the span), all entries of the band: by equation (7.44), \(7 \cdot 8 \cdot (63 - 15)/6 = 448\) multiplications and \(7 \cdot (42 - 8)/2 = 119\) divisions, at most \(567\) in all. If in every step we count only the entries that are really nonzero in that step (including the fill created earlier), the number of operations is \(148 + 62 = 210\).
  3. Band \(b = 14\) (by rows), all entries of the band give at most \(1190\) multiplications, which is almost as many as for the full matrix, and \(189\) divisions, \(1379\) in all. Skipping zeros, it is \(380 + 100 = 480\) operations.

The approximation \(n b^2/2\) would give \(21 \cdot 196/2 = 2058\) for \(b = 14\), which is more than the full cost \(1540\). This is no contradiction: the approximation \(n b^2/2\) holds for \(n \gg b\), and for the bridge \(b = 14\) is only a little less than \(n = 21\), and the exact equation (7.44) gives \(1190 < 1540\). The order \(21\) is too small for the band to save much, so let us look at a larger system. A mesh of \(50 \times 100\) nodes with two unknowns per node has \(n = 10^4\) unknowns. If we number the nodes by rows of length \(50\) and every element also connects diagonally neighbouring nodes, then \(m = 51\) and by equation (7.40) \(b \le 2 \cdot 52 - 1 = 103\), which for an estimate we round to \(b = 100\) (an example for an estimate, not the computation of some structure, with \(b = 103\) it would be \(5{,}3 \cdot 10^7\)). For \(n = 10^4\) and \(b = 100\) such a system needs \(n^3/6 = 1{,}7 \cdot 10^{11}\) multiplications for a full matrix and \(n b^2/2 = 5 \cdot 10^7\) for a banded one, which is \(3300\) times fewer. For \(n = 10^6\) and \(b = 10^3\) the ratio is \(3{,}3 \cdot 10^5\) (\(1{,}7 \cdot 10^{17}\) against \(5 \cdot 10^{11}\)). If we assume that one operation takes \(10^{-9}\) s (an assumption, not a measurement, but it agrees with the table in KREYSZIG, PDF p. 877, where for \(\tfrac23 n^3\) operations the time is \(0{,}7\) s at \(n = 1000\) and \(11\) min at \(n = 10\,000\)), the first computation takes about \(170\) s and the second \(0{,}05\) s. Figure 7.32 shows all this on one graph.

The band also saves memory. Since the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) is symmetric, it is enough to store the diagonal and the entries below it or above it (ONATE1, appendix B, paragraph after equation (B.9), PDF p. 448: "It is sufficient to store only the coefficients above (or below) the principal diagonal and the diagonal coefficients"), which nearly halves the memory and the work. Profile storage (profile) by COOK, PDF p. 57 saves still more: for every row we store the entries from the first nonzero one to the diagonal, also the zeros in between, because the elimination fills them ("Zeros between the skyline and the diagonal are retained because a direct (noniterative) equation solver creates fills"). For the bridge, below the diagonal and with it there are \(231\) entries, in the band \(140\) in the numbering along the span and \(210\) in the numbering by rows, and in the profile \(88\) and \(140\). The triangle \(\mat L\) has \(83\) and \(121\) entries (above) and all of them lie in the profile, which tells us that the fill does not escape the profile (checked on this example, we do not prove the general statement).

7.8.3 Renumbering the unknowns (preštevilčenje neznank)

The numbering of the unknowns is our choice, but the band, the fill and the cost depend on it. May we renumber the unknowns arbitrarily without changing the solution? Let us look at the smallest case first. For the truss of two bars from section 7.5.2, which meet at the point \(C\), with the unknowns of this point in the order \(x\), then \(y\), we have \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}} = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 7{,}4088 & -1{,}8816 \\ -1{,}8816 & 1{,}4112 \end{smallmatrix}\right] \cdot 10^6\) N/m. If we swap them, the matrix becomes \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1{,}4112 & -1{,}8816 \\ -1{,}8816 & 7{,}4088 \end{smallmatrix}\right] \cdot 10^6\) N/m, and the right-hand side and the solution are swapped. We will do the same with a matrix of any order, so we write the notion in general.

Definition 7.45 Permutation matrix (permutacijska matrika)

Let \(\pi\) be a rearrangement of the numbers \(1, \dots, n\), that is a sequence \(\pi(1), \dots, \pi(n)\) in which every number from \(1\) to \(n\) appears exactly once. The permutation matrix (permutacijska matrika) \(\mat P\) of order \(n\) of the rearrangement \(\pi\) is the matrix with entries

\[P_{\mathrm{ij}} = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } j = \pi(i) , \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} . \end{cases}\]

In every row and every column there is therefore exactly one one, the rest are zeros. For a column \(\vek d\), \((\mat P\,\vek d)_{\mathrm{i}} = d_{\pi(\mathrm{i})}\): in the column \(\mat P\,\vek d\) the unknown with the new number \(i\) has the old number \(\pi(i)\).

Source: The definition is ours. The notion in this form is not from the sources of the book. COOK, PDF p. 57 mentions that programs renumber the unknowns themselves ("renumbering option") and convert the results back to the input numbering.

Lemma 7.46 Renumbering does not change the solution (preštevilčenje ne spremeni rešitve)

Let \(\mat P\) be the permutation matrix of a rearrangement \(\pi\) of order \(n\), \(\mat K\) a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\) and \(\vek f\) a column. Then:

  1. \(\mat P\,\mat P^\T = \mat I\) (the identity matrix, definition 1.105).
  2. The matrix \(\tilde{\mat K} = \mat P\,\mat K\,\mat P^\T\) has entries \(\tilde K_{\mathrm{ij}} = K_{\pi(\mathrm{i})\,\pi(\mathrm{j})}\), is symmetric and positive definite.
  3. If \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\), then \(\tilde{\mat K}\,(\mat P\,\vek d) = \mat P\,\vek f\). The system \(\tilde{\mat K}\,\tilde{\vek d} = \mat P\,\vek f\) has exactly one solution \(\tilde{\vek d} = \mat P\,\vek d\).
Proof

We must show the three points of the lemma, each by the definition of the permutation matrix and the rules for the product and the transpose (lemmas 1.99 and 1.101).

  1. The entry \((\mat P\,\mat P^\T)_{\mathrm{ij}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} P_{\mathrm{ik}}\,P_{\mathrm{jk}}\). The term \(k\) is nonzero only for \(k = \pi(i)\) and at the same time \(k = \pi(j)\). Since in a rearrangement every number occurs only once, this holds only for \(i = j\), and then the sum is \(1\). So \(\mat P\,\mat P^\T = \mat I\). Likewise \((\mat P^\T\mat P)_{\mathrm{ij}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k}} P_{\mathrm{ki}}\,P_{\mathrm{kj}}\) is nonzero only for \(i = \pi(k) = j\), and for every \(i\) there is exactly one such \(k\), so also \(\mat P^\T\mat P = \mat I\).
  2. The entry \((\mat P\,\mat K\,\mat P^\T)_{\mathrm{ij}} = \sum_{\mathrm{k},\mathrm{l}} P_{\mathrm{ik}}\,K_{\mathrm{kl}}\,P_{\mathrm{jl}}\) is nonzero only for \(k = \pi(i)\) and \(l = \pi(j)\), and equals \(K_{\pi(\mathrm{i})\,\pi(\mathrm{j})}\). Since \(\mat K\) is symmetric, \(K_{\pi(\mathrm{j})\,\pi(\mathrm{i})} = K_{\pi(\mathrm{i})\,\pi(\mathrm{j})}\), so \(\tilde{\mat K}\) is symmetric.
  3. Let \(\vek x \ne \vek 0\) and \(\vek y = \mat P^\T\vek x\). By point 1, \(\mat P\,\vek y = \mat P\,\mat P^\T\vek x = \vek x \ne \vek 0\), so \(\vek y \ne \vek 0\). By the rule for the transposed product, \(\vek x^\T\tilde{\mat K}\,\vek x = (\mat P^\T\vek x)^\T\mat K\,(\mat P^\T\vek x) = \vek y^\T\mat K\,\vek y > 0\), because \(\mat K\) is positive definite. The matrix \(\tilde{\mat K}\) is positive definite.
  4. If \(\mat K\,\vek d = \vek f\), we multiply from the left by \(\mat P\) and insert \(\mat I = \mat P^\T\mat P\) (point 1) in front of \(\vek d\): \(\mat P\,\mat K\,\mat P^\T\mat P\,\vek d = \mat P\,\vek f\). This is \(\tilde{\mat K}\,(\mat P\,\vek d) = \mat P\,\vek f\).
  5. By point 3 and theorem 6.29 the system \(\tilde{\mat K}\,\tilde{\vek d} = \mat P\,\vek f\) has exactly one solution. By point 4, \(\mat P\,\vek d\) is a solution, so it is this one.

Source: The proof is not from a source.

For the truss of two bars, \(\mat P = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right]\) (\(\pi = (2, 1)\)), and the matrix \(\tilde{\mat K}\) has the pivots \(1{,}4112 \cdot 10^6\) and \(7{,}4088 - 1{,}8816^2/1{,}4112 = 4{,}9\) (\(\cdot 10^6\) N/m), and with the original numbering \(7{,}4088 \cdot 10^6\) and \(0{,}9333 \cdot 10^6\) N/m. The pivots are different, but their product is \(6{,}9149\) in both cases (for a matrix of order \(2\) it is \(ad - b^2\), which does not change under the swap), and the solution is the same, only in another order. For the bridge \(\mat P\) has order \(21\) with the rearrangement \(\pi = (1, 2, 12, 13, 3, 4, 14, 15, 5, 6, 16, 17, \dots)\) (the number in the numbering along the span, the corresponding number in the numbering by rows): the unknown \(u\) of node \(t_1\) is third along the span and twelfth by rows. The computation confirms \(\mat P\,\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}^{\text{(rows)}}\,\mat P^\T = \mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}^{\text{(span)}}\) with exactly equal entries and \(\mat P\,\vek d^{\text{(rows)}} = \vek d^{\text{(span)}}\), with the largest displacement \(7{,}305\) mm (the displacement \(v\) of node \(b_3\) from section 7.7). Renumbering changes only the band, the fill and the cost (\(7\) against \(14\), \(26\) against \(64\), \(210\) against \(480\) operations), but not the solution. Programs renumber the unknowns themselves (COOK, PDF p. 57, "renumbering option"), and the book does not treat the procedures for it.

The sequence \(\pi = (2, 1, 2)\) of order \(3\) is not a permutation, because \(2\) appears twice and \(3\) never. It differs from the permutation \(\pi = (2, 1, 3)\) only in the last term. The formula from the definition nevertheless gives the matrix \(\mat P = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \end{smallmatrix}\right]\), which has two ones in column \(2\) and none in column \(3\). The matrix is not a permutation matrix, because the condition "exactly one one in each column" fails. A consequence is that item 1 of lemma 7.46 does not hold: the entry \((1, 3)\) of the matrix \(\mat P\,\mat P^\T\) is \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} P_{1\mathrm{k}}\,P_{3\mathrm{k}} = P_{12}\,P_{32} = 1\), not \(0\), and \(\mat P\,\mat P^\T = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\right] \ne \mat I\). The column \(\mat P\,\vek d = (d_2, d_1, d_2)\) writes \(d_2\) into two rows and loses \(d_3\), so we can no longer recover \(\vek d\) from it, and such a renumbering is not invertible.

For what follows we need one more form of the same elimination. For a structure we often have to solve several load cases with the same matrix, for the bridge for example a load on half of the span or wind. Must we eliminate anew every time? No, if we remember the multipliers and pivots of the elimination.

Lemma 7.47 Triangular and diagonal factorization (razcep na trikotni in diagonalni faktor)

Let \(\mat K\) be a symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\). Let \(\mat L\) be the lower triangular matrix with ones on the diagonal in which column \(k\) below the diagonal is the column of multipliers \(\vek g/K_{11}\) of the \(k\)-th step of elimination, and \(\mat D\) the diagonal matrix of the pivots of the \(k\)-th steps from theorem 6.29. Then

\[\mat K = \mat L\,\mat D\,\mat L^\T . \tag{7.45}\]
Proof

We prove it by induction on the order \(n\) (theorem 1.44). The statement \(P(n)\): for every symmetric positive definite matrix of order \(n\) the equality (7.45) holds.

  1. Start, \(n = 1\): \(\mat K = [K_{11}]\), \(\mat L = [1]\), \(\mat D = [K_{11}]\).
  2. Induction step. Let \(P(n - 1)\) hold. We write \(\mat K = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} K_{11} & \vek g^\T \\ \vek g & \mat K' \end{smallmatrix}\right]\) and \(\vek\ell = \vek g/K_{11}\). The matrix \(\mat K'' = \mat K' - \vek g\,\vek g^\T/K_{11}\) is symmetric positive definite of order \(n - 1\) (theorem 6.29, steps 5 and 6). By \(P(n - 1)\), \(\mat K'' = \mat L''\mat D''\mat L''^\T\) for the factorization determined by the multipliers and pivots of \(\mat K''\).
  3. The first step of elimination of the matrix \(\mat K\) gives the multipliers \(\vek\ell\) and the pivot \(K_{11}\), and the further steps are the steps of elimination of the matrix \(\mat K''\) (step 4 of the proof of theorem 6.29). Therefore \(\mat L = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \vek 0^\T \\ \vek\ell & \mat L'' \end{smallmatrix}\right]\) and \(\mat D = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} K_{11} & \vek 0^\T \\ \vek 0 & \mat D'' \end{smallmatrix}\right]\), where \(\mat L''\) and \(\mat D''\) are the multipliers and pivots of the matrix \(\mat K''\).
  4. Block multiplication gives \(\mat L\,\mat D = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} K_{11} & \vek 0^\T \\ K_{11}\vek\ell & \mat L''\mat D'' \end{smallmatrix}\right]\) and
    \[\mat L\,\mat D\,\mat L^\T = \begin{bmatrix} K_{11} & K_{11}\,\vek\ell^\T \\ K_{11}\,\vek\ell & K_{11}\,\vek\ell\,\vek\ell^\T + \mat L''\mat D''\mat L''^\T \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} K_{11} & \vek g^\T \\ \vek g & \vek g\,\vek g^\T/K_{11} + \mat K'' \end{bmatrix} = \mat K ,\]
    because \(K_{11}\vek\ell = \vek g\), \(K_{11}\vek\ell\,\vek\ell^\T = \vek g\,\vek g^\T/K_{11}\) and \(\vek g\,\vek g^\T/K_{11} + \mat K'' = \mat K'\).
  5. By the principle of induction \(P(n)\) holds for every \(n\).

Source: The factorization is named by KREYSZIG §20.2, PDF p. 878 to 881, and ONATE1, appendix B, equations (B.2), (B.3) and (B.7), (B.8), PDF p. 446 to 447 ("forward elimination", "back substitution"). The source has the factorization \(\mat L\mat U\), KREYSZIG on PDF p. 881 for a symmetric positive definite matrix the Cholesky form \(\mat A = \mat L\mat L^\T\). The proof in steps is not from the sources.

By lemma 7.42 the matrix \(\mat L\) has the same band as \(\mat K\) (the column \(\vek\ell\) has at most \(b\) nonzero entries), and the numbers \(83\) and \(121\) of nonzero entries in figure 7.31 are the entries of the matrix \(\mat L\) below the diagonal together with it. We solve the system \(\mat K\,\vek x = \vek f\) in three steps: \(\mat L\,\vek y = \vek f\) from top to bottom (forward, forward elimination), \(\mat D\,\vek z = \vek y\) with divisions, and \(\mat L^\T\vek x = \vek z\) from bottom to top (backward, back substitution). In row \(i\) the first step takes \(i - 1\) multiplications, \(n(n - 1)/2\) in all, the same for the third (KREYSZIG, PDF p. 877: for the unknown \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) there are \(n - i\) multiplications), so about \(n^2\) for a full matrix and \(2 n b\) for a banded one. For \(n = 10^4\) and \(b = 100\) this is \(10^8\) multiplications for a full and \(2 \cdot 10^6\) for a banded matrix, while the factorization cost \(1{,}7 \cdot 10^{11}\) and \(5 \cdot 10^7\) respectively. We do the factorization once, and every further load case costs only this much (ONATE1, PDF p. 448: "several solutions for different right-hand sides \(f\) can be obtained ... This process is often called a resolution since it is not necessary to recompute the \(L\) and \(U\) arrays").

For the truss of two bars with \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) from section 7.5.2, \(\ell_{21} = -1{,}8816/7{,}4088 = -0{,}2540\) and \(\mat D = \mathrm{diag}(7{,}4088,\ 0{,}9333) \cdot 10^6\) N/m. For the load \(10\) kN downward at the point \(C\), \(\vek f = (0, -10\,000)\) N, \(\vek y = (0, -10\,000)\) N, \(\vek z = (0, -10{,}714)\) mm, \(\vek x = (-2{,}721,\ -10{,}714)\) mm, as in section 7.5.2. For the second case, \(10\) kN horizontally in the direction \(+x\), we use the same \(\mat L\) and \(\mat D\): \(\vek y = (10\,000,\ 2540)\) N, \(\vek z = (1{,}350,\ 2{,}721)\) mm, \(\vek x = (2{,}041,\ 2{,}721)\) mm. The second entry of the column \(\vek x\), the displacement \(2{,}721\) mm in the direction \(y\) due to the horizontal force, is equal in magnitude to the displacement \(-2{,}721\) mm in the direction \(x\) due to an equally large vertical force (the sign differs because the vertical force points downward). So the reciprocal theorem 4.140 predicts: the displacement in the direction \(x\) due to a force in the direction \(y\) is equal to the displacement in the direction \(y\) due to a force in the direction \(x\) (the symmetry of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}^{-1}\) says the same). With the same factored matrix we have thus solved both load cases, the second without a new elimination.

The solution that the computer gives is not exactly accurate. ONATE1, §3.13.4, PDF p. 137 lists three kinds of errors in the solution of the global system by a direct method. The first is ill-conditioning of the system (slaba pogojenost): small changes in \(\mat K\) or \(\vek f\) cause large changes of the solution. The second is truncation (okrajšanje): the computer stores only the first \(d\) digits of every number and so loses part of the data. The third is round-off (zaokrožitev): during the computation the computer adjusts the last digit of every number. By the source truncation is more important than round-off. In chapter 6 we measured the conditioning by the condition number \(\kappa(\mat K)\) (definition 6.31): the relative error of the solution can be up to \(\kappa\) times larger than the relative error of the right-hand side. For the truss of two bars the eigenvalues of the matrix \(\mat K_{\mathrm{ff}}\) are \(0{,}870 \cdot 10^6\) and \(7{,}950 \cdot 10^6\) N/m, so \(\kappa = 9{,}14\): we lose less than one digit of about sixteen. For the bridge \(\kappa = 395\), equal in both numberings, because the eigenvalues do not change under renumbering: if \(\mat K\,\vek v = \lambda\,\vek v\), then \(\mat P\,\mat K\,\mat P^\T(\mat P\,\vek v) = \mat P\,\mat K\,\vek v = \lambda\,\mat P\,\vek v\). The hat functions of the bar from chapter 6 gave \(\kappa = 29\), \(113\) and \(438\) for \(4\), \(8\) and \(16\) elements and \(\kappa \approx 1{,}7\,n^2\). If the pattern continues, for \(10^4\) elements \(\kappa\) would be about \(1{,}7 \cdot 10^8\) and we would lose about eight of sixteen digits. The source (ONATE1, PDF p. 137) names as the main cause of ill-conditioning an element of large stiffness connected to elements of much smaller stiffness. The smallest example is two bars in a row, the first with stiffness \(k_1\) fixed to the wall, the second with \(k_2\) at the end of the first. The matrix of free unknowns is \(\left[\begin{smallmatrix} k_1 + k_2 & -k_2 \\ -k_2 & k_2 \end{smallmatrix}\right]\), and for \(k_1/k_2 = 10^6\) we have \(\kappa \approx 10^6\), for \(k_1/k_2 = 10^3\) we have \(\kappa \approx 10^3\). A stiffness ratio of \(10^6\) between neighbouring elements thus costs six digits of sixteen.
