Chapter 16 Torsion

16.10 Connections

The course abbreviations are the same as in section 1.19.

Earlier chapters took three things as a model, because we did not yet have torsion: chapter 12 took the equation \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}'\) (equation (12.16)) with the number \(J\) as data, chapter 13 took \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G I_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) (equation (13.14)) for the round shaft, and chapter 9 took the stiffness \(k_{\mathrm{t}}\) of a shaft under a disc. This chapter derived all three for a shaft in uniform torsion (section 16.1), and for the vibrating round shaft we check equation (13.14) below. Now we put them on one shaft and test them on numbers: what happens to the vibration of a shaft when the circle is replaced by the \(20 \times 40\) mm strip of chapter 12. The question is concrete: by how much does the first natural frequency change, which part of the shaft model must we replace and which may we keep.

Take the shaft of section 13.3. It is fixed in a wall at \(x = 0\), has length \(L = 1\) m and a free end at \(x = L\), and is made of steel (\(G = 75{,}385\) GPa, \(\rho = 7800\) kg/m\(^3\)). In the figure, in the first mode of vibration given by theorem 13.18 (b), each cross section is turned by \(\varphi(x) = \varphi_0 \sin(\pi x/2L)\), and as the curve below the shaft shows, the rotation grows from zero at the wall to its largest value at the free end. The line on a section is the rotation we see, so the rotation \(\varphi_0\) in the figure is greatly enlarged.

Chapter 13 found for the round shaft the speed \(c_{\mathrm{T}} = \sqrt{G/\rho} = 3108{,}8\) m/s and the frequency \(f_1 = c_{\mathrm{T}}/(4L) = 777{,}2\) Hz (theorem 13.18). What changes for the strip? The proof of theorem 13.16 has two steps that we must repeat, and each has its reason. The first is the angular momentum of a piece of the shaft. The section still turns about the axis as a rigid body (besides that it warps, see below), so per unit length it has angular momentum \(\rho I_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{t}}\) with the same polar second moment \(I_{\mathrm{p}} = 133\,333\) mm\(^4\) of the \(20 \times 40\) mm section, because angular momentum depends only on how the mass is spread around the axis. The second is the moment with which the neighbouring part of the shaft acts on the piece. That is no longer \(G I_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\). By theorem 16.10 it is \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) with the torsion constant \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) of theorem 16.30, and \(J = 0{,}549\,I_{\mathrm{p}}\) by lemma 16.12. The law of angular momentum for a piece \([a, b]\) gives, with the same steps 3 to 6 as in theorem 13.16,

\[\rho I_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi_{\mathrm{tt}} = G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xx}} , \qquad c = \sqrt{\frac{G J}{\rho I_{\mathrm{p}}}} = c_{\mathrm{T}}\sqrt{\frac{J}{I_{\mathrm{p}}}} = 0{,}7408\,c_{\mathrm{T}} . \tag{16.61}\]

Hence \(c = 2303{,}1\) m/s and \(f_1 = c/(4L) = 575{,}8\) Hz. In the figure the arrow from the circle to the strip shows this: the frequency is multiplied by \(\sqrt{J/I_{\mathrm{p}}}\). Below each section stand its \(J\) and its frequency. The circle is the fixed term of the comparison: for it \(J = I_{\mathrm{p}}\), because a circle does not warp (corollary 16.13).

What would we get if for the strip we stayed with the model of chapter 13, that is replaced \(J\) by \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\)? This is the near miss, which fails the test. Equation (16.61) would have \(c = c_{\mathrm{T}}\) and \(f_1 = 777{,}2\) Hz, that is \(35{,}0\) % too much. The reason is the same as for the frame in chapter 12, where \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) in place of \(J\) gave a displacement from the twisting of the column of \(4{,}97\) mm instead of \(9{,}06\) mm (\(45\) % too little) and a total displacement of point \(C\) of \(11{,}35\) mm instead of \(15{,}44\) mm (\(26\) % too little): the strip is softer in torsion than \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) says, because the section warps, while the mass stays. In vibration \(\omega^2\) is proportional to the ratio of stiffness to inertia, and only the stiffness falls, to \(0{,}549\) of its former value.

Equation (16.61) neglects two things and both must be named. First: theorem 16.10 gives \(M_{\mathrm{x}} = G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) for a twist per unit length that is constant along the shaft (uniform torsion, enakomerna torzija, definition 16.1). In vibration \(\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) varies with \(x\), in the first mode it falls from its largest value at the wall to zero at the free end, where the rotation curve is horizontal, so for the strip the model is an approximation. We do not estimate the size of this approximation here. For the round shaft there is none. The displacement field \(\vek u = \varphi(x, t)\,(0, -z, y)\) has \(\operatorname{div}\vek u = 0\) and \(\nabla^2\vek u = \varphi_{\mathrm{xx}}\,(0, -z, y)\), because \(-z\varphi\) and \(y\varphi\) are linear in \(y\) and \(z\). By theorem 13.24 without body force, where the Lamé constants are \(\lambda\) and \(\mu = G\), this is a motion of the body exactly when \(G\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xx}} = \rho\,\varphi_{\mathrm{tt}}\), which is equation (13.15). The lateral surface is free: the only nonzero stresses are \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xy}} = -G z\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(\sigma_{\mathrm{xz}} = G y\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\), so on the lateral surface with normal \(\vek n = (0, y, z)/R\) the traction is \(t_{\mathrm{x}} = G\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\,(-z y + y z)/R = 0\) and \(t_{\mathrm{y}} = t_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\). The model of chapter 13 is therefore exact for the circle also in vibration. For the strip the same computation fails. The warping \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\psi\) with \(\nabla^2\psi = 0\) gives in the direction \(x\) the left side \((\lambda + 2G)\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xxx}}\,\psi\), while equation (16.61) gives the right side \(\rho\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xtt}}\,\psi = G\,(J/I_{\mathrm{p}})\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xxx}}\,\psi\), and since for steel (\(\nu = 0{,}3\)) \(\lambda + 2G = 3{,}5\,G\) while \(G J/I_{\mathrm{p}}\) is only \(0{,}549\,G\), they do not agree. Second: the warping of the section moves during vibration. With the warping function (funkcija zvitja) \(\psi\) (definition 16.7) the axial displacement is \(u_{\mathrm{x}} = \varphi_{\mathrm{x}}\,\psi\), and its velocity has kinetic energy per unit length \(\tfrac12\rho\,\varphi_{\mathrm{xt}}^2\int \psi^2\,\mathrm{d}A\), which the equation did not have. How large is it? Since \(\psi\) for the strip is the series of section 16.5, we computed the integral: \(\int\psi^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 1{,}30\cdot10^{6}\) mm\(^4\) and \(\frac{1}{I_{\mathrm{p}}}\int\psi^2\,\mathrm{d}A = 9{,}75\) mm\(^2\) (num-10.out, as a check the same \(\psi\) gave the corner displacement \(0{,}832\) \(\mu\)m at \(50\) N m, as section 16.5 states). With the first mode \(\varphi = \varphi_0\sin(\pi x/2L)\) the ratio of the kinetic energies of warping and of rotation is

\[r = \frac{\int\psi^2\,\mathrm{d}A}{I_{\mathrm{p}}}\,\Big(\frac{\pi}{2L}\Big)^2 = 9{,}75\ \mathrm{mm}^2\cdot\Big(\frac{\pi}{2000\ \mathrm{mm}}\Big)^2 = 2{,}4\cdot10^{-5} ,\]

because by theorem 4.9 the kinetic energy splits into an integral over the section and an integral over \(x\), and the integrals over \(x\) are \(\int_0^L \varphi_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x = (\pi/2L)^2\,\varphi_0^2 L/2\) and \(\int_0^L \varphi^2\,\mathrm{d}x = \varphi_0^2 L/2\). The Rayleigh quotient (Rayleighov količnik) of the shaft (definition 13.52, with numerator \(\int_0^L G J\,\varphi_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\) and denominator \(\int_0^L \rho I_{\mathrm{p}}\,\varphi^2\,\mathrm{d}x\)) with the same mode has, with the added energy, a denominator larger by the factor \(1 + r\). Hence \(\omega^2\) is lowered by the factor \(1/(1 + r)\) and the frequency by about \(r/2 = 0{,}0012\) %. For a shaft of length \(L = 1\) m the neglect of this energy is therefore justified. The ratio \(r\) grows with \(1/L^2\) and reaches \(0{,}38\) % at \(L = 80\) mm, where the frequency is lower by about \(0{,}19\) %, but there the length is only twice the longer side of the section (\(40\) mm), so uniform torsion is a poor model near the ends (section 11.11). The neglect therefore depends on the shape of the section and on the length, it does not hold for every shaft.

Example 16.60 Disc on the strip instead of the round shaft (disk na traku namesto na okrogli gredi)

A shaft fixed at \(x = 0\) carries at \(x = L = 1\) m a disc with moment of inertia \(J_{\mathrm{d}} = 0{,}05\) kg m\(^2\), as in example 13.23, but its section is \(20 \times 40\) mm. As in the proof of theorem 13.21 the boundary condition at the disc, which the shaft turns with a moment, is \(G J\,U'(L) = J_{\mathrm{d}}\,\omega^2\,U(L)\), so \(U'(L) = \lambda\,J_{\mathrm{d}}/(\rho I_{\mathrm{p}})\,U(L)\) with \(\lambda = \omega^2/c^2\) from equation (16.61). Equation (13.20) therefore holds unchanged with \(\mu = \rho I_{\mathrm{p}} L/J_{\mathrm{d}}\) and \(f_{\mathrm{r}} = c\,s_{\mathrm{r}}/(2\pi L)\), where now \(c = 2303{,}1\) m/s.

  1. The stiffness of the shaft is \(k_{\mathrm{t}} = G J/L = 5516{,}5\) N m/rad (by lemma 13.17 with \(J\) in place of \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\)), \(3{,}43\) times less than \(18\,946{,}3\) N m/rad for the circle (\(18\,946{,}3/5516{,}5 = 3{,}434\)).
  2. The one degree of freedom model gives \(\omega_{\mathrm{1PS}} = \sqrt{k_{\mathrm{t}}/J_{\mathrm{d}}} = 332{,}16\) 1/s, so \(f = 52{,}87\) Hz (for the circle \(97{,}97\) Hz).
  3. The inertia of the strip is \(\rho I_{\mathrm{p}} L = 7800\cdot1{,}3333\cdot10^{-7}\cdot 1 = 1{,}040\cdot10^{-3}\) kg m\(^2\), hence \(\mu = 0{,}02080\). The first root of \(s\tan s = \mu\) is \(s_1 = 0{,}14372\) (bisection in num-10.py), so \(f_1 = c\,s_1/(2\pi L) = 52{,}68\) Hz.
  4. By corollary 13.22 (with \(c\) in place of \(c_{\mathrm{T}}\)) the one degree of freedom model lies above the exact value: \(52{,}87/52{,}68 - 1 = 0{,}35\) %. For the circle the error was \(0{,}65\) %, because \(\mu\) was larger (\(0{,}0392\)). A smaller moment of inertia of the shaft relative to the disc, that is a smaller \(\mu\), means a better one degree of freedom model.

Source: The example and its numbers are ours (num-10.out). The value of \(J\) is from theorem 16.30, the equations are from chapter 13.

  • Space frame (AK, MK, chapter 12): equation (12.16), which the element took as an assumption, is proved by theorem 16.10. The number \(J = 73\,178\) mm\(^4\) for the strip, which the element took from a source, is the sum of the series of theorem 16.30. The ratio \(J/I_{\mathrm{p}} = 0{,}549\) is the reason why the frame of chapter 12 (figure 12.16) with \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) in place of \(J\) underestimates the displacement from twisting by \(45\) % and the total displacement of point \(C\) by \(26\) %. The assumption that the centroid is the shear centre (strižno središče) holds for the strip by lemma 16.40, because it has two axes of symmetry. For a channel it is not true: the shear centre lies \(12{,}5\) mm from the centroid (section 16.7), and a force of \(500\) N through the centroid twists the channel in uniform torsion, far from a clamp, by \(22{,}3\) \(^\circ\)/m (corollary 16.41), so the element must place such sections on an axis through the shear centre or add an offset.
  • Vibration of shafts and the disc (VD, DSK, chapters 9 and 13): the stiffness \(k_{\mathrm{t}} = G I_{\mathrm{p}}/L\) of lemma 13.17 is now proved by theorem 16.3 and corollary 16.4. Example 16.60 shows what changes for a non-circular section: \(J\) in place of \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) in the stiffness, \(I_{\mathrm{p}}\) stays in the inertia. Limit (figure 16.37): equation (16.61) is an approximation for a non-circular section. The kinetic energy of warping is estimated by \(r\), nonuniform torsion and warping restrained at the wall we do not estimate. For the circle the equation is exact.
  • Hollow shafts and rotors (DSK, chapters 24 and 23): for a hollow round shaft \(J = I_{\mathrm{p}} = \pi(R^4 - R_{\mathrm{i}}^4)/2\) (corollary 16.46). A \(40/32\) mm tube has \(J = 148\,384\) mm\(^4\) and at equal mass is \(4{,}56\) times stiffer than a solid shaft of diameter \(24\) mm (\(J = 32\,572\) mm\(^4\)). In the crankshaft of an engine the journal piece is round and \(G I_{\mathrm{p}}/\ell\) holds for it. For crank webs and cheeks, which are not round and are also loaded in bending, this chapter has no result and we state them as not covered.
  • Restrained warping (zadržan zvitek) and nonuniform torsion (AK, chapter 12): everything we derived means that the section can warp freely at the end. When a support or a joint holds it, the twist per unit length varies along the shaft and axial stresses appear in the section as well. COOK (PDF p. 42 to 43) describes an I beam five times as long as it is deep, in which complete restraint of warping at one end reduces the rotation at the other by about half and introduces axial normal stresses much larger than the torsional shear stress. The element of chapter 12 assumes free warping (section 12.5.1) and so has no extra degrees of freedom. Restrained warping needs them (the rate of twist at a node, a \(14 \times 14\) matrix), and programs often do not offer them (COOK, PDF p. 43). ONATE2 (section 4.10, PDF p. 227) says the same for thin open sections. The proof of this is not in the book: the derivation is in the sources named.
  • Multi-cell sections (več celic) and variable diameter (MK): in a section with several cells each cell must carry its own shear flow and equilibrium fixes the relations between them (ONATE2 section 4.3.5.1, PDF p. 206), and for a round shaft of variable diameter the field \(u_{\mathrm{r}} = u_{\mathrm{z}} = 0\), \(u_\theta = u_\theta(r, z)\) is again a solution of all the field equations and the stress function is constant on the boundary (SADD section 9.7, PDF p. 270 to 271). We derive neither.
  • Heat conduction and membranes (MK, AK, chapters 14, 18 and 22): the equation \(\nabla^2\Phi = -2G\alpha\) with zero on the boundary (theorem 16.16) is the equation of steady heat conduction with a uniform source and zero temperature on the boundary, so the uniqueness of the solution and the element matrix of chapter 14 hold for it, and section 16.9 uses them for torsion. The reason is the same operator, not the same phenomenon. The membrane of section 16.4, a thin film without bending stiffness that carries a pressure \(p\) with tension \(N\) per unit length (corollary 16.26), has the same equation. The word membrane in shells (chapter 22) means something else, a shell that carries the load by axial forces in its middle surface only, and plates (chapter 18) have a bending stiffness that the film does not.
  • Limits of the chapter: the existence of the functions \(\psi\) and \(\Phi\) for a general section and Saint-Venant's principle at the ends are stated from sources. Open sections made of several strips (junctions), multi-cell sections, restrained warping and nonuniform torsion, variable diameter and plastic torsion are named or left out. The example of this section neglects the kinetic energy of warping, which \(r = 2{,}4\cdot10^{-5}\) justifies only for \(L = 1\) m, and does not estimate nonuniform torsion.
