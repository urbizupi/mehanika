Chapter 28 Flexible robot links

28.9 Modal reduction of the link (modalna redukcija člena)

In section 28.8 we built the link of a steel strip (\(L = 1\) m, \(EI = 141{,}12\) N m², \(\rho A = 1{,}872\) kg/m) from 20 Hermite elements. After the reference conditions of section 28.2 there remain \(n_{\mathrm{d}} = 40\) elastic coordinates \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\), and together with the hub angle \(\theta\) there are 41 unknowns. Linearized at rest (corollary 28.22, with the matrix \(\mat M_0\) there) the equation of motion is

\[\begin{bmatrix} J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} & \vek b^{\mathsf T} \\ \vek b & \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \ddot\theta \\ \ddot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 & \vek 0^{\mathsf T} \\ \vek 0 & \mat K_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \theta \\ \vek q_{\mathrm{d}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \tau \\ \vek 0 \end{bmatrix} , \tag{28.45}\]

where \(J_{\mathrm{h}}\) is the moment of inertia of the hub, \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = 0{,}624\) kg m² the moment of inertia of the link about the joint, \(\vek b\) a column of 40 numbers that ties the rotation of the hub to the elastic coordinates (in section 28.8 \(\vek b_{\mathrm{d}}\)), \(\tau\) the motor torque (lemma 28.19), \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) the mass matrix and \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) the stiffness matrix of the link with the joint clamped (theorem 28.34). Now we ask whether we really need all 41 numbers. The highest natural frequency of the clamped link is \(2{,}08 \cdot 10^5\) rad/s for these 20 elements and the first is \(30{,}5\) rad/s, so the ratio is about \(6800\). The mesh describes the high frequencies worse (chapter 12: with a consistent mass all of them lie above the true ones), and each of them is one more unknown in every step of the computation.

The answer from chapter 10 is the Ritz one: we write the wanted shape as a combination of a few chosen shapes and compute only their coefficients instead of 40 coordinates (definition 10.58). What remains is the question which shapes to take. For a link the answer is natural. We have already computed the link as a cantilever in chapter 13, and the eigenvectors of the pair of matrices are those that decoupling (theorem 10.28) separates from each other. So we take the natural modes of the link with the joint clamped, that is under the same reference conditions under which \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) was assembled. SHABANA calls this step the modal transformation (SHABANA, section 6.7, PDF pp. 309-312) and writes about the choice that the shapes should be chosen so that they describe the displaced shape well (PDF p. 311). The chosen shapes are the blue modes of the figure, the fourth is dropped.

The words have an origin. Mode (način, Latin modus, "manner") is here the manner in which the link vibrates, and the modal coordinates say how much of each mode the present shape contains. Reduction (redukcija, Latin reducere, "to bring back, to lessen") means that we lessen the number of coordinates and return to a smaller model. Each mode is an arc with a different number of nodes.

We build figure 28.64 from left to right. First the clamp: the link is fixed in the hub and the axis of the floating frame points along the axis of the link at the joint, which is the reference condition of definition 28.5 (\(v = v' = 0\) at \(x = 0\)). From this line we draw the length of the link \(L\) as the distance between the end marks. The first mode is an arc without a node that moves farthest at the tip. The second mode has one node, the third two and the fourth three, as the computed shapes in the figure show. Beside each mode stand its frequency and the numbers that we introduce below. We mark the frequencies with the joint clamped with a prime, \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'\), because we shall need \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}\) without a prime for the free joint. They grow quickly: \(\omega_2'/\omega_1' = 6{,}27\) and \(\omega_3'/\omega_1' = 17{,}5\), because the frequency of a cantilever is proportional to the square of the root of equation (13.55) (chapter 13).

Let us write what we replace. In equation (28.45) the pair \((\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}, \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}})\) is the one of chapter 10: by theorem 28.34 \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is symmetric and positive definite (the two reference conditions of definition 28.5 remove both zero-energy fields of the element, \(a + b x\)) and \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is symmetric and positive definite. SHABANA says of his matrix in words that it is positive definite because the reference conditions determine the displacement field uniquely (PDF p. 310). The two matrices are the mass and stiffness blocks of the figure.

Definition 28.37 Modal coordinates of the link (modalne koordinate člena)

Let \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) and \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) be symmetric positive definite matrices of order \(n_{\mathrm{d}}\) of the elastic coordinates of the link under the reference conditions (definition 28.5), \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\) and \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) (\(r = 1, \dots, n_{\mathrm{d}}\), \(\omega_1' \le \omega_2' \le \dots\)) their eigenpairs by theorem 10.21, \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\)-orthonormal, and \(\mat\Phi\) their modal matrix (definition 10.23). For \(1 \le m \le n_{\mathrm{d}}\) let \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\) be the matrix with \(n_{\mathrm{d}}\) rows and the first \(m\) columns of \(\mat\Phi\). The modal coordinates of the link (modal coordinates of the link) are the numbers \(\eta_1, \dots, \eta_{\mathrm{m}}\) in the column \(\boldsymbol\eta\) that satisfy (the small model has \(m\) of them)

\[\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\,\boldsymbol\eta = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{m}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} . \tag{28.46}\]

For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) we have \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) (definition 10.27), and for \(m < n_{\mathrm{d}}\) equation (28.46) is the assumption that the elastic shape lies in the space of the first \(m\) modes. The coupling of mode \(r\) (coupling of mode \(r\)) is the number

\[b_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\vek b \tag{28.47}\]

(the column \(\vek b\) of theorem 28.13 has the elements \(\int_0^L \rho A\,x\,\psi_{\mathrm{k}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) for the shape functions \(\psi_{\mathrm{k}}\) of the mesh). When the shape functions are already the modes of a cantilever (theorem 13.52), normalized with \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) as above, \(\mat\Phi\) is the identity matrix.

Source: SHABANA (6.167)-(6.172) PDF pp. 310-311: "The nm mode shapes should be selected such that a good approximation for the displaced shape can be obtained." The notation and the name "coupling" are ours.

Why "coupling"? In the theorem below every mode will satisfy \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -b_{\mathrm{r}}\ddot\theta\): the number \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\) is the coefficient with which the acceleration of the hub drives mode \(r\), so it couples the rotation of the hub and the vibration. The sign of a mode is arbitrary (\(-\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) is also an eigencolumn), so we choose it so that the displacement of the tip is positive. With this choice the couplings are \(b_1 = 0{,}778\), \(b_2 = -0{,}124\), \(b_3 = 0{,}044\) and \(b_4 = -0{,}023\) kg\(^{1/2}\) m (beside the modes in the figure). The unit is not kg m, because the modes are normalized by \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = 1\) and have the unit kg\(^{-1/2}\).

Before we state the theorem, let us draw it with matrices. The full model is on the left, the reduced one on the right.

In figure 28.65 the mass matrix of equation (28.45) stands at top left, with \(1 + n_{\mathrm{d}}\) rows and columns. The angle block is the number \(J = J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}}\), which would be everything if the link were rigid. The column and row of coupling are beside it, because the rotation of the hub drives the elastic coordinates. The block \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is the mass of the elastic coordinates themselves. At bottom left is the stiffness matrix: the angle block is \(0\), because the link does not deform when the hub turns and so has no elastic energy, and the block \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) carries all the elasticity. The arrow replaces \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\) by \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\boldsymbol\eta\) and multiplies from the left by \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\). On the right is the result with \(1 + m\) rows and columns: the elastic blocks have become small, and because the modes are \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\)-orthonormal, the mass block is the identity matrix \(\mat I_{\mathrm{m}}\) and the stiffness block a diagonal matrix with \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\). The row of coupling is now \(\vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}} = (b_1, \dots, b_{\mathrm{m}})\), \(m\) numbers instead of \(n_{\mathrm{d}}\). The angle block has not changed.

What the shrinking does to the frequencies, figure 28.66 shows on the numbers of link A without a hub from example 28.40.

In figure 28.66 the axis \(\omega\) runs in rad/s. The grey verticals are the frequencies of the full model \(0\), \(133{,}9\), \(433{,}8\), \(905{,}2\), \(1548\) and \(2363\) rad/s. Each row is one reduced model: with one mode it has two frequencies, with two three, with three four and with four five. The first point of every row lies at zero, because the rigid rotation stays rigid. All other blue points lie on their grey vertical or to the right of it, never to the left. The orange segment is the difference at the last frequency of the row, which lies far to the right: \(178{,}3\) against \(133{,}9\), \(642{,}8\) against \(433{,}8\), \(1413\) against \(905{,}2\) and \(2491\) against \(1548\) rad/s. At the lower frequencies the difference is less than \(5\) rad/s, for example \(134{,}0\) against \(133{,}9\) rad/s for \(m = 2\), so the figure cannot show it. The model with all modes has its points exactly on the verticals.

Theorem 28.38 Reduced model of the link (reducirani model člena)

Let the equation of motion (28.45) hold for a link on a hub with \(J_{\mathrm{h}} \ge 0\), let \(\mat M_0 = \begin{bmatrix} J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} & \vek b^{\mathsf T} \\ \vek b & \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{bmatrix}\) be symmetric positive definite (corollary 28.22), \(\mat K = \operatorname{diag}(0, \mat K_{\mathrm{dd}})\), and \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\), \(\boldsymbol\eta\), \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\) as in definition 28.37. Let \(\omega_1 \le \dots \le \omega_{\mathrm{n}_\mathrm{d} + 1}\) denote the frequencies of the full model with the joint free (the roots of the eigenvalues of the pair \((\mat K, \mat M_0)\)). If in (28.45) we insert \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\boldsymbol\eta\) and multiply from the left by \(\operatorname{diag}(1, \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T})\), we get the small matrices

\[\begin{bmatrix} J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} & b_1 & \dots & b_{\mathrm{m}} \\ b_1 & 1 & & \\ \vdots & & \ddots & \\ b_{\mathrm{m}} & & & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} \ddot\theta \\ \ddot\eta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \ddot\eta_{\mathrm{m}} \end{bmatrix} + \operatorname{diag}\big(0,\ \omega_1'^2, \dots, \omega_{\mathrm{m}}'^2\big)\begin{bmatrix} \theta \\ \eta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \eta_{\mathrm{m}} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \tau \\ 0 \\ \vdots \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} , \tag{28.48}\]

that is \((J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}})\,\ddot\theta + \sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \tau\) and \(\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} + \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -b_{\mathrm{r}}\,\ddot\theta\) for \(r = 1, \dots, m\). The matrices of this equation are symmetric, and the mass matrix is positive definite. Its frequencies \(0 = \tilde\omega_1 \le \tilde\omega_2 \le \dots \le \tilde\omega_{\mathrm{m}+1}\) with the joint free satisfy:

  1. \(\tilde\omega_1 = \omega_1 = 0\): the reduced model describes the rigid rotation of the hub exactly. The corner block of the stiffness matrix is zero. In figure 28.66 this is the first point of every row.
  2. \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{k}} \ge \omega_{\mathrm{k}}\) for \(k = 1, \dots, m + 1\). Shrinking from \(1 + n_{\mathrm{d}}\) to \(1 + m\) adds stiffness. In figure 28.66 the difference is the orange segment, which goes from the grey vertical \(\omega_{\mathrm{k}}\) to the right.
  3. For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) we have \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{k}} = \omega_{\mathrm{k}}\) for every \(k\), the reduced model is only written in other coordinates. For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) the small matrix is as large as the full one. In figure 28.66 the bottom row has its points exactly on the grey verticals.
Proof

We must show that the reduced model is a Ritz approximation of the full one, that its matrices are those that equation (28.48) gives, and that theorem 10.59 then gives points 2 and 3. The goal is the map of the full matrices to the small ones.

  1. Let \(\tilde{\mat\Psi} = \operatorname{diag}(1, \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}})\), a matrix with \(n_{\mathrm{d}} + 1\) rows and \(m + 1\) columns. Its columns are independent: if \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}\,\vek c = \vek 0\) and \(\vek c = (c_0, \vek c_1)\), then \(c_0 = 0\) and \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek c_1 = \vek 0\). Since \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat I_{\mathrm{m}}\) (theorem 10.21), it follows that \(\vek c_1 = \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek c_1 = \vek 0\). The columns map \(m + 1\) numbers to \(n_{\mathrm{d}} + 1\).
  2. The substitution \(\vek x = \tilde{\mat\Psi}\,\vek y\) with \(\vek x = (\theta, \vek q_{\mathrm{d}})\) and \(\vek y = (\theta, \boldsymbol\eta)\) is equation (28.46). The matrices \(\mat M_0\) and \(\mat K\) are constant, so \(\dot{\vek x} = \tilde{\mat\Psi}\,\dot{\vek y}\), the kinetic energy is \(\tfrac12\dot{\vek x}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_0\dot{\vek x} = \tfrac12\dot{\vek y}^{\mathsf T}(\tilde{\mat\Psi}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_0\tilde{\mat\Psi})\dot{\vek y}\) and the elastic energy is \(\tfrac12\vek x^{\mathsf T}\mat K\vek x = \tfrac12\vek y^{\mathsf T}(\tilde{\mat\Psi}^{\mathsf T}\mat K\tilde{\mat\Psi})\vek y\). The generalized force at the coordinates \(\vek y\) is by definition 8.51 equal to \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}^{\mathsf T}(\tau, \vek 0) = (\tau, \vek 0)\), because the first column of \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}\) is a unit vector. Lagrange's equations (theorem 8.99) give \(\tilde{\mat M}\ddot{\vek y} + \tilde{\mat K}\vek y = (\tau, \vek 0)\) with \(\tilde{\mat M} = \tilde{\mat\Psi}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_0\tilde{\mat\Psi}\) and \(\tilde{\mat K} = \tilde{\mat\Psi}^{\mathsf T}\mat K\tilde{\mat\Psi}\). The substitution is the arrow of the figure.
  3. We multiply the blocks in turn: \(\tilde{\mat M}\) has \(J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}}\) in the corner, \(\vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}} = (b_1, \dots, b_{\mathrm{m}})\) in the first row, \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\vek b\) in the first column and \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat I_{\mathrm{m}}\) in the elastic block. The result is the corner block, the coupling row and the identity matrix.
  4. In \(\tilde{\mat K}\) the first row and the first column are all zeros. From \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}} = \omega_{\mathrm{s}}'^2\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\) by multiplying with \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\) it follows that \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}} = \omega_{\mathrm{s}}'^2\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\,\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{s}}\), which is \(\omega_{\mathrm{s}}'^2\) for \(r = s\) and zero otherwise. With steps 2 and 3 equation (28.48) is proved. These are the diagonal and the zero corner of the figure.
  5. By lemma 10.57 with the independent columns of step 1, \(\tilde{\mat K}\) is symmetric and \(\tilde{\mat M}\) is symmetric positive definite. Both matrices are small and symmetric.
  6. Point 1. Because \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\) is positive definite, \(\vek x^{\mathsf T}\mat K\vek x \ge 0\) for every \(\vek x\), and \(\vek y^{\mathsf T}\tilde{\mat K}\vek y \ge 0\) for every \(\vek y\). By theorem 10.21 \(\lambda_1\) and \(\tilde\lambda_1\) are the smallest values of these expressions at \(\vek x^{\mathsf T}\mat M_0\vek x = 1\), so they are not negative. The first column of the identity matrix, \(\vek e_1\), gives \(\mat K\vek e_1 = \vek 0\) and \(\tilde{\mat K}\vek e_1 = \vek 0\), so \(\vek e_1/\sqrt{J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}}}\) is a column with \(\vek x^{\mathsf T}\mat K\vek x = 0\) and \(\vek x^{\mathsf T}\mat M_0\vek x = 1\). Hence \(\lambda_1 = \tilde\lambda_1 = 0\). The zero is the corner block of the stiffness matrix and the first point of every row.
  7. Point 2. The matrix \(\mat K\) is symmetric, \(\mat M_0\) symmetric positive definite and \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}\) has independent columns (step 1). By theorem 10.59 \(\tilde\lambda_{\mathrm{k}} \ge \lambda_{\mathrm{k}}\) for \(k \le m + 1\), and the root is an increasing function. The inequality \(\tilde\omega_{\mathrm{k}} \ge \omega_{\mathrm{k}}\) is the orange segment from the grey vertical to the blue point, which never points to the left.
  8. Point 3. For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) the matrix \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}\) is square with independent columns. By point 2 of theorem 10.59 the eigenvalues of the two problems are equal. For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) the full and small matrices have the same size, and the points of the bottom row lie on the grey verticals.

Source: SHABANA (6.169)-(6.173) PDF p. 311, which multiplies the full equations of the link by \(\bar{\mat B}_{\mathrm{m}}^i\) and obtains a coupled system of reference and modal coordinates. The Ritz explanation, points 1 to 3 and the proof in eight steps are not from the source.

What the theorem says, as it would be said at the board. The reduced model is the full model in which we have restricted the elastic shape to the first \(m\) modes. So the link in it is somewhat too stiff and the error of the frequencies has one sign (theorem 10.59). The rigid rotation (frequency zero) is not spoiled by the reduction, because the first column of the matrix \(\tilde{\mat\Psi}\) is the unit vector \(\vek e_1\) and the rotation of the hub at \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0\) lies in the space of the kept shapes. The shrinking is the arrow that keeps the corner block.

We get the frequencies of the reduced model from equation (28.48) with an ansatz in which all columns vary as \(\cos\tilde\omega t\). For \(\lambda = \tilde\omega^2 \ne 0\) and \(J = J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}}\) the computation is short. We draw the result as the error against the number of modes.

  1. Row \(r\) of equation (28.48) at \(\tau = 0\) is \((\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2 - \lambda)\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} - \lambda\,b_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta = 0\), so \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \lambda\,b_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta/(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2 - \lambda)\). Each row is one mode with its own \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\) and \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'\).
  2. The first row is \(-\lambda J\,\theta - \lambda\sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\). We insert step 1 and divide by \(-\lambda\theta\) (\(\theta \ne 0\), otherwise all \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = 0\) by step 1). The sum runs over the kept modes.
  3. The result is the frequency equation, whose roots are the frequencies with the errors of the figure,
    \[J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{m}\frac{b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\,\lambda}{\lambda - \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2} . \tag{28.49}\]

For the full model (\(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\)) the same equation holds with all terms. For \(m = 1\) it can be solved by hand: \(J(\lambda - \omega_1'^2) = b_1^2\lambda\), which is equation (28.36) of section 28.6,

\[\tilde\omega_2^2 = \frac{\omega_1'^2}{1 - b_1^2/(J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}})} . \tag{28.50}\]

In the denominator there is a number smaller than \(1\), so \(\tilde\omega_2 > \omega_1'\), in agreement with the interlacing \(\omega_1' \le \omega_2\) of theorem 28.25 (locking the joint is one constraint). The cause lies in the hub: when the joint is free, the rotation of the hub takes over part of the motion (the share \(b_1^2/J\)), so the effective mass of the mode is smaller and the frequency higher. For \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0{,}2\) kg m² we have \(\tilde\omega_2 = 59{,}31\) rad/s (section 28.6, example 28.24). For \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 0\) we have \(b_1^2/J_{\mathrm{link}} = 0{,}9707\), the denominator is only \(0{,}0293\) and \(\tilde\omega_2 = 178{,}3\) rad/s. For \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 2\) we have \(b_1^2/(J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}}) = 0{,}231\), the denominator \(0{,}769\) and \(\tilde\omega_2 = 34{,}81\) rad/s.

The number \(b_1^2/J_{\mathrm{link}} = 0{,}9707\) is the ratio from figure 28.64: the first mode carries 97,07 % of the inertia of the link. Why a sum of such shares cannot exceed \(100\ \%\) is told by the next lemma. By point 4 of theorem 28.34 the mass matrix of the link alone is \(\mat M_{\mathrm{link}} = \begin{bmatrix} J_{\mathrm{link}} & \vek b^{\mathsf T} \\ \vek b & \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}} \end{bmatrix}\) (without the hub), positive definite, which is the only property that the lemma needs. For all the modes together the same inequality is already given by equation (28.23) of lemma 28.15, here we write it for any number of kept modes.

We read figure 28.67 as vectors in a space with the mass inner product. The field \(x\) is an arrow whose squared length is \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\). The mode axes are perpendicular unit axes \(\vek\varphi_1, \vek\varphi_2\). We project the field onto them and get the projection with components \(b_1, b_2\), whose squared length is \(\sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) and which is shorter than the field. The remainder is perpendicular to the modes, its squared length is \(J_{\mathrm{link}} - \sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\). It points towards the other modes, which we did not keep.

Lemma 28.39 Sum of the coupling shares (vsota deležev sklopitve)

Let \(\mat M_{\mathrm{link}}\) (the mass matrix of the link without the hub, positive definite by point 4 of theorem 28.34), \(\vek b\), \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\), \(\mat\Phi\) and \(b_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\vek b\) be as in theorem 28.38. For every \(m = 1, \dots, n_{\mathrm{d}}\) (the length of the projection)

\[\sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{m} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2 < J_{\mathrm{link}} , \tag{28.51}\]

and the sum grows with \(m\). For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) the sum is \(\vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}^{-1}\vek b\).

Proof

We must show that the kinetic energy of the link at a chosen velocity is positive, and compute it as a difference. The energy is the squared length of the field \(x\).

  1. Choose the velocities \(\dot\theta = 1\) and \(\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} = -\sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{m}} b_{\mathrm{r}}\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}} = -\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek\beta\) with \(\vek\beta = (b_1, \dots, b_{\mathrm{m}})\), so \(\dot{\vek x} = (1, -\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek\beta)\). This vector is not zero (its first component is \(1\)). The vector is the field \(x\), which is not zero.
  2. Twice the kinetic energy of the link at \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek 0\) is \(2T = \dot{\vek x}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{link}}\dot{\vek x}\) and by theorem 28.13 (lemma 28.15) has three parts: \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\dot\theta^2 = J_{\mathrm{link}}\), the mixed term \(2\,\dot\theta\,\vek b^{\mathsf T}\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} = -2\,\vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek\beta = -2\sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) and \(\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\dot{\vek q}_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek\beta^{\mathsf T}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\vek\beta = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\). The mixed term is the projection of the field on the modes.
  3. Together \(2T = J_{\mathrm{link}} - 2\sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2 + \sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2 = J_{\mathrm{link}} - \sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{m}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\). What remains is the squared length of the remainder.
  4. Because \(\mat M_{\mathrm{link}}\) is positive definite, \(2T > 0\) for a nonzero \(\dot{\vek x}\) (step 1). This is inequality (28.51). The sum grows because the terms \(b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) are nonnegative. The remainder is not zero, and the projection grows with every mode.
  5. For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) we have \(\mat\Phi\mat\Phi^{\mathsf T} = \mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}^{-1}\) (lemma 10.24), so \(\sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2 = \vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat\Phi\mat\Phi^{\mathsf T}\vek b = \vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}^{-1}\vek b\). For \(m = n_{\mathrm{d}}\) the modes span the whole space.

Source: The inequality is Bessel's inequality for the \(\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\) scalar product, in which \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\) is the scalar product of the rigid rotation with \(\vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\). The proof is not from the source.

The lemma also has a geometric meaning. A rotation of the link about the joint by a small angle \(\delta\) moves the point \(x\) in the transverse direction by about \(x\,\delta\), so by \(x\) per unit angle. This is the field \(x\) along the link, and \(b_{\mathrm{r}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\,\mathrm{d}x\) is the scalar product of this field with mode \(r\) (with the mass as weight). The sum of squares is the square of the length of the projection of the field \(x\) onto the kept modes, and this cannot be longer than the length of the field itself, \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\). The difference \(J_{\mathrm{link}} - \sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\) is the square of the length of the part of the rigid rotation that the kept modes cannot show. For link A: after one, two, three, four and five modes there is missing \(2{,}93\), \(0{,}46\), \(0{,}144\), \(0{,}062\) and \(0{,}032\ \%\), and all the modes of the mesh of 20 elements together give \(\vek b^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}^{-1}\vek b = 0{,}6239988\) kg m² against \(J_{\mathrm{link}} = 0{,}6240000\) kg m². The field \(x\) has slope \(1\) at \(x = 0\) and does not satisfy \(v'(0) = 0\), so it is not exactly in the space of the mesh, but it is very near: the deficit is \(2 \cdot 10^{-6}\) relative. For a continuous link with the functions \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) of chapter 13 the same proof with integrals instead of sums gives \(\sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{m}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2 < J_{\mathrm{link}}\), and equality as \(m \to \infty\) is the completeness of the cantilever modes. The book does not prove this completeness, it cites it from sources (the remark to theorem 13.72). In figure 28.67 these are the field, the projection and the remainder.

The frequency test is light, but it does not tell us everything. Under load the reduced model can mislead, and we show this as a test of the theorem that fails if we choose the modes by frequency alone. The root moment misleads next to a good tip deflection.

The task is static: the tip of the link carries a load \(F = 10\) N, the joint is locked. The static result can be compared with a known one: by theorem 5.57 (equation (5.57)) the tip deflects by \(FL^3/(3EI) = 23{,}62\) mm. How does the reduced model get this result? The static equation is \(\mat K_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \vek Q\) with \(\vek Q = F\,\vek e_{\mathrm{tip}}\) (the unit vector at the tip coordinate). After inserting \(\vek q_{\mathrm{d}} = \mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}\boldsymbol\eta\) and multiplying by \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\) as in step 2 of theorem 28.38 we have \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \vek\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\vek Q = F\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)\), so (the figure shows the share of each mode)

\[u_{\mathrm{m}} = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{m}\frac{F\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)^2}{\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2} . \tag{28.52}\]

Each term of the sum is nonnegative, so \(u_{\mathrm{m}}\) grows with \(m\) and is always below the true value (the reduced model is too stiff, as we predicted). The work of the load at the tip is \(F\,u_{\mathrm{m}}\), so this is the same phenomenon as in theorem 28.38. The numbers for link A, in mm: \(22{,}93\), \(0{,}584\), \(0{,}0745\), \(0{,}0194\), \(0{,}0071\), \(0{,}0032\) for \(r = 1, \dots, 6\), the sum of all 40 terms \(23{,}62\). With one mode the deflection is too small by \(2{,}93\ \%\), with two by \(0{,}46\ \%\), with three by \(0{,}14\ \%\). The terms fall as \(1/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\), because \(|\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)| = 1{,}4618\) kg\(^{-1/2}\) is the same for the first four modes (the ratio of the second term to the first is \(0{,}0255 = (30{,}53/191{,}3)^2\)). The fast fall is good news, but it holds for the deflection, not for everything that interests us.

We read figure 28.69 from the top. The upper bars are the contributions \(u_{\mathrm{r}}\) of modes \(r = 1, \dots, 6\) to the tip deflection: the first is \(22.93\) mm, the next fall quickly. The lower bars are the contributions to the root moment, in N m: they alternate in sign and fall slowly. The dashed lines show the sums \(23.62\) mm and \(10.000\) N m.

On figure 28.70 the clamp is at \(x = 0\), where displacement and slope are zero. The dashed grey line is the link before loading. The force \(F\) pulls the tip down, so the link bends into the orange curve: its tip lies \(23.62\) mm below the grey line. The blue curve is the deflection that a single mode gives. Its tip lies \(22.93\) mm below the grey line, so it is \(0.69\) mm higher, which is the \(2.93\ \%\). The shapes are almost the same because the first mode resembles the deflection of a cantilever under a tip force, which is why the error is small. All remaining modes together contribute only this difference.

For a robot link the stress at the clamp matters besides the deflection. The stress in the most loaded fibre is proportional to the bending moment \(EI\,v''(0)\) (chapter 5). How much moment at the root does one mode give? The lemma answers. The moment is largest at the clamp.

We build figure 28.71 from left to right. On the left is the clamp at \(x = 0\). The piece is the part of the link from the section \(x\) to the free end \(L\). Along it lies the inertial load \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\rho A\varphi(s)\) that the motion in the mode demands. From the section to the point \(s\) we measure the arm \(s - x\). In the section \(x\) the rest of the link holds the piece with the moment \(EI\varphi''(x)\).

Lemma 28.41 Root moment of a mode (moment načina ob korenu)

Let \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}\) be a mode of a cantilever of length \(L\) with frequency \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'\) and \(b_{\mathrm{r}} = \int_0^L\rho A\,x\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\) its coupling. Then the bending moment of the mode at the root is

\[EI\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}''(0) = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\,b_{\mathrm{r}} . \tag{28.53}\]
Proof

We must show the balance of the piece \([x, L]\) of the link vibrating in the mode and take it at \(x = 0\). The piece is the cut-off part with its load.

  1. By theorem 13.40 \(EI\,\varphi'''' = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\rho A\,\varphi\) on \([0, L]\) (equation (13.47)), and the free end at \(x = L\) means \(\varphi''(L) = \varphi'''(L) = 0\) (definition 13.39). The free end is the right edge of the piece, where moment and shear force are zero.
  2. The integral \(\int_{\mathrm{x}}^L EI\,\varphi''''(s)\,\mathrm{d}s = EI\,\varphi'''(L) - EI\,\varphi'''(x) = -EI\,\varphi'''(x)\). One more integral with a weight: \(\int_{\mathrm{x}}^L (s - x)\,EI\,\varphi''''(s)\,\mathrm{d}s = \big[(s - x)\,EI\,\varphi'''(s)\big]_{\mathrm{x}}^L - \int_{\mathrm{x}}^L EI\,\varphi'''(s)\,\mathrm{d}s = 0 - EI\,\big(\varphi''(L) - \varphi''(x)\big) = EI\,\varphi''(x)\), by integration by parts (lemma 6.13) and step 1. The weight is the arm from the section to the load.
  3. By step 1 the left part in step 2 equals \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\int_{\mathrm{x}}^L(s - x)\,\rho A\,\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\). This is the balance of moments of the piece \([x, L]\): the moment of the inertial load \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\rho A\,\varphi\) about the point \(x\) is carried by the bending moment in the section \(x\). The load with its arm balances the moment in the section.
  4. At \(x = 0\) we have \(\int_0^L s\,\rho A\,\varphi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s = b_{\mathrm{r}}\), so (28.53). At \(x = 0\) the section is at the clamp.

Source: The proof is not from the source.

The lemma says what we wanted. With the statics fulfilled, mode \(r\) at a tip force has the root moment \(EI\,\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}''(0)\,\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = \omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2 b_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot F\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2 = F\,b_{\mathrm{r}}\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)\). The weight \(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\) has dropped out, so the terms do not fall as \(1/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\) but as the couplings \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\), and these fall slowly (\(0{,}778\), \(0{,}124\), \(0{,}044\), \(0{,}023\), \(0{,}014\)). Because \(\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}(L)\) is positive by choice, the terms have the signs of the couplings \(+, -, +, -\), as the computed values give (the reason for the alternation is not proved in the book). The numbers for \(F = 10\) N, in N m: \(11{,}377\), \(-1{,}815\), \(0{,}648\), \(-0{,}331\), \(0{,}200\), \(-0{,}134\), the sum of all \(10{,}000 = FL\), as it must be by the balance of the link (the piece \([0, L]\) carries the moment \(FL\)). The moment contributions fall slowly and give the sum.

In figure 28.72 the vertical axis shows the ratio to the true value, the horizontal one the number of modes \(m\). The blue line shows the ratio of the tip deflection \(u_{\mathrm{m}}/u_{40}\): \(0{,}9707\), \(0{,}9954\), \(0{,}9986\), \(0{,}9994\), \(0{,}9997\) for \(m = 1, \dots, 5\), always below one and growing, fast. The orange line shows the root moment at the same force: \(1{,}138\), \(0{,}956\), \(1{,}021\), \(0{,}988\), \(1{,}008\). With one mode the moment is \(13{,}8\ \%\) too high, because the first term (\(11{,}377\) N m) exceeds the true \(10\) N m, and the other terms correct it with alternating signs, so the line oscillates about the true value and approaches it slowly. The stress at the root is proportional to the moment, so after one mode we would predict a stress \(13{,}8\ \%\) too large and a deflection \(2{,}9\ \%\) too small, with the same model and the same force. Yet the first nonzero frequency of the reduced model at \(J_{\mathrm{h}} = 2\) differs from the full one by only \(3 \cdot 10^{-5}\) relative with one mode (example 28.40).

The same case is the acceleration of the hub, which is the load of the robot itself. An angular acceleration \(\ddot\theta = \alpha = 10\) rad/s² loads the link with inertial forces that by equation (28.48) give mode \(r\) the modal force \(-b_{\mathrm{r}}\alpha\). At slow acceleration (statically) we have \(\eta_{\mathrm{r}} = -b_{\mathrm{r}}\alpha/\omega_{\mathrm{r}}'^2\) and the root moment by lemma 28.41 is in magnitude the sum \(\alpha\sum_{\mathrm{r} \le \mathrm{m}} b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\). The true value is \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\alpha = 6{,}24\) N m, because by the balance of the piece \([0, L]\) about the joint the moment of the inertial load \(\rho A\,\alpha\,x\) is \(\alpha\int_0^L\rho A\,x^2\,\mathrm{d}x\). By lemma 28.39 the sum is always below the true value, and the grey dashed line shows the ratio \(\sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2/J_{\mathrm{link}}\): with \(m = 1\) the moment is \(6{,}057\) N m, \(2{,}9\ \%\) too small, with \(m = 3\) it is \(6{,}231\) N m. The tip moves by \(12{,}208\) mm with one mode in this load (true value \(12{,}160\) mm, \(0{,}4\ \%\) too high). So the deflection is too high, the moment too low, and the errors have different signs. The quantity that always grows with \(m\) is the work of the load (a sum of nonnegative terms), and the tip deflection is the work only for the tip force. The grey line lies on the same curve as the blue one: the ratios agree to six digits (\(0{,}970688\) at \(m = 1\)). We found this by computation for a uniform cantilever and do not use it in this section.

Why are two computations of the same moment in the reduced model different? The first, from the bending at the root, is \(\alpha\sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2\). The second, from the equation of motion for \(\theta\) in equation (28.48), is \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\alpha\): at \(\ddot{\eta}_{\mathrm{r}} \approx 0\) we have \(\tau - J_{\mathrm{h}}\ddot\theta = J_{\mathrm{link}}\ddot\theta + \sum_{\mathrm{r}} b_{\mathrm{r}}\ddot\eta_{\mathrm{r}} \approx J_{\mathrm{link}}\alpha\), which is the moment that the link receives from the hub. In the full model they agree (for the mesh of 20 elements up to the deficit \(2 \cdot 10^{-6}\) relative that we found for lemma 28.39), in the reduced one they differ by \((J_{\mathrm{link}} - \sum b_{\mathrm{r}}^2)\,\alpha\), which is the deficit of lemma 28.39. What the kept modes cannot show as bending is passed through the joint all the same, so the stress at the root must not be read from the reduced model without a correction. We see the difference as the remainder perpendicular to the modes.

The conclusion of the example is a rule of choice. The modes are not chosen by frequency alone: we must keep all that the chosen load excites (\(\varphi_{\mathrm{r}}^{\mathsf T}\vek Q\) in statics, \(b_{\mathrm{r}}\) in hub acceleration), and each mode by the measure of the quantity we seek. The tip deflection needs 2 to 3 modes, the stress at the root 5 or more. CK (section 11.4, PDF p. 367) says the same for a general system: a solution by modal displacements may be inexact even under a static load, because the convergence is often slow and many modes are needed. The mode-acceleration method (metoda modalnega pospeška) improves it, because it computes the pseudostatic response from the full stiffness matrix and takes only a correction from the modes. This method and the related method of Craig and Bampton (CK, section 17.6, PDF p. 575) are treated in chapter 21, here we only name them. The deflection approaches the true value quickly, the moment slowly.

Two things remain open. First, the nonlinear term in the equation for \(\theta\): by lemma 28.15 \(m_{\theta\theta} = J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} + \vek q_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\vek q_{\mathrm{d}}\), and in modal coordinates, because of \(\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}}^{\mathsf T}\mat M_{\mathrm{dd}}\mat\Phi_{\mathrm{m}} = \mat I_{\mathrm{m}}\), simply \(J_{\mathrm{h}} + J_{\mathrm{link}} + \boldsymbol\eta^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\eta\). For a tip displaced by \(10\) mm in the first mode we have \(\eta_1 = 0{,}00684\) kg\(^{1/2}\) m and \(\eta_1^2 = 4{,}68 \cdot 10^{-5}\) kg m², which is \(7{,}5 \cdot 10^{-5}\) of \(J_{\mathrm{link}}\), so we dropped it in the linear equation. SHABANA (PDF p. 312) says that the structure of the equations of motion does not change if the inertia integrals are written in the corresponding modal form, which is done once in advance. Second, for rotation with constant speed (section 28.7) the stiffness matrix changes, so the modes computed at rest are no longer eigenmodes but only the shape functions of the Ritz method. The upper bound of theorem 10.59 still holds, because the matrices are again symmetric and the mass matrix positive definite, which is all the theorem needs. The offset is the first mode with a small amplitude.

How far have we come. The model of the link on a hub, which has 41 unknowns, we reduced to \(m + 1\) unknowns with a proved guarantee (the frequencies above the full ones, the rigid rotation exact) and with two tests. The frequency test is light and depends on the hub, the load test is harder, because it requires choosing the modes by the load and by the quantity we seek. Chapter 21 develops the choice of modes further, the damping of the modes is treated in chapter 20. The reduced model is the small matrix instead of the full one.
