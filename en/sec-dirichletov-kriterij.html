Chapter 8 Analytical dynamics (analitična dinamika)

8.12 Stability of motion: Dirichlet's criterion (Dirichletov kriterij)

In section 8.6 we called an equilibrium stable when the potential energy \(V\) has a strict local minimum there (definition 8.64). That is a test for a position in which the system rests. The question we put off there is about motion: if we disturb the system slightly, for example move a pendulum a little aside or give it a small speed, does it stay near the equilibrium for all time or does it move away? We have the equations of motion since section 8.9 and conservation of energy since section 8.11, so we can now put the question precisely and answer it.

An equilibrium is a motion that does not change. Let the system have \(n\) generalized coordinates (posplošene koordinate) \(\vek q = (q_1, \dots, q_{\mathrm{n}})\) on an open set \(U \subset \mathbb R^n\), scleronomic ideal holonomic constraints and kinetic energy \(T = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\) with mass matrix \(\mat M(\vek q)\) (theorem 8.86). The matrix \(\mat M\) is symmetric and positive definite, and it is continuous because its entries are sums of products of first derivatives of the map \(\vek X\) from the definition of generalized coordinates. A motion on an interval \([0, \tau]\) is a twice continuously differentiable function \(\vek q : [0, \tau] \to U\) that satisfies Lagrange's equations (theorem 8.88). The constant function \(\vek q(t) = \vek q_0\) is a motion exactly when the generalized forces at \(\vek q_0\) are zero, since at \(\dot{\vek q} = \vek 0\) also \(\partial T/\partial\vek q = \vek 0\). For conservative forces this is the condition \(\nabla V(\vek q_0) = \vek 0\) (corollary 8.58), and uniqueness of the motion (corollary 8.94, under its smoothness assumptions) says that this is the only motion that starts at \(\vek q_0\) at rest. Such a \(\vek q_0\) we call an equilibrium.

A disturbed start is described by two numbers, the displacement \(\vek q(0) - \vek q_0\) and the initial velocity \(\dot{\vek q}(0)\). If the start is near the equilibrium in both, the motion must also stay near in both: in position and in velocity, and this on every interval on which the motion exists, with the same \(\delta\) whatever the length of the interval. For a pendulum we ask whether the angle does not grow beyond some limit and whether the pendulum does not spin too fast. Hence the definition. Coordinates may have different units (for a rod on two springs one coordinate is a displacement in metres, the other an angle in radians), so \(|\vek q|\) is the Euclidean norm of the numbers we get in the chosen units. A different choice of units changes distances only by constant factors and does not change whether stability holds.

Definition 8.114 Stable and unstable equilibrium of motion (stabilno in nestabilno ravnovesje gibanja)

Let \(\vek q_0 \in U\) be an equilibrium of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates \(\vek q\) on an open set \(U \subset \mathbb R^n\), as described above. The equilibrium \(\vek q_0\) is stable (stabilno), if for every number \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a number \(\delta > 0\) such that for every number \(\tau > 0\) and every motion on \([0, \tau]\) with \(|\vek q(0) - \vek q_0| < \delta\) and \(|\dot{\vek q}(0)| < \delta\)

\[|\vek q(t) - \vek q_0| < \varepsilon \quad \text{and} \quad |\dot{\vek q}(t)| < \varepsilon\]

holds for every \(t \in [0, \tau]\). The equilibrium is unstable (nestabilno) if it is not stable: there is a number \(\varepsilon > 0\) such that for every number \(\delta > 0\) there is a motion on some \([0, \tau]\) with \(|\vek q(0) - \vek q_0| < \delta\) and \(|\dot{\vek q}(0)| < \delta\) that at some moment \(t \in [0, \tau]\) has \(|\vek q(t) - \vek q_0| \ge \varepsilon\) or \(|\dot{\vek q}(t)| \ge \varepsilon\).

Source: The definition is not from a source. GR §2.1.1 (PDF p. 78) defines stability by an energy bound (remark after theorem 8.115), CK §3.3 (PDF pp. 84-86) classifies only linear systems by the roots of the characteristic equation.

The definition speaks of motions on an interval \([0, \tau]\) with arbitrary \(\tau\), because corollary 8.94 gives a motion only on some, possibly very short, interval around the initial time. In this way stability does not depend on how long the motion exists, and theorem 8.115 proves it without a theorem the book does not have. Stability is therefore a statement about all motions that exist, and does not say that they exist for all times. That would be shown by the continuation theorem for solutions, which the book does not prove (section 8.9.1, after corollary 8.94). We need it for instability (remark after theorem 8.117), not for stability.

Where the border between stable and unstable lies we see on pendulum A (a point mass \(m = 1\) kg on a rigid rod of length \(l = 1\) m). At \(\theta = 0\) the potential energy \(V = -m g l \cos\theta\) has a minimum with \(V'' = 9{,}81\) J. If we give the pendulum a slight push there, it oscillates about that position. At \(\theta = \pi\) we have \(V'' = -9{,}81\) J, and if we balance the pendulum there, an infinitely small push topples it. Dirichlet's criterion says that in the first case no motion needs to be computed, since a look at the shape of \(V\) suffices.

The idea of the proof is conservation of energy (theorem 8.106). The total energy \(\mathcal E = T + V\) stays during the motion equal to the initial one, and \(T\) cannot be negative. Hence at every moment \(V \le \mathcal E\). If the initial energy is smaller than the lowest value of \(V\) on a ball around the equilibrium, the motion cannot reach that sphere, because the potential energy there would already be more than the total energy (figure 8.35). The velocity is bounded because \(T\) is bounded by the same energy and the mass matrix is positive definite. The name Dirichlet's criterion is from the course syllabus. It is in none of the books the chapter uses, so we write nothing about its history.

Theorem 8.115 Dirichlet's stability criterion (Dirichletov kriterij stabilnosti)

Let a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates \(\vek q\) on an open set \(U \subset \mathbb R^n\) be as in definition 8.114: ideal holonomic scleronomic constraints, mass matrix \(\mat M(\vek q)\) symmetric, continuous and positive definite. Let the active forces be conservative with potential \(V(\vek q)\), continuously differentiable on \(U\), and possibly also non-conservative forces \(\vek Q^{\mathrm{nc}}\) that are dissipative, \(\vek Q^{\mathrm{nc}} \cdot \dot{\vek q} \le 0\) (section 8.11). If \(V\) has a strict local minimum at the equilibrium \(\vek q_0\) (definition 8.64), then \(\vek q_0\) is a stable equilibrium.

Proof

We must show that for every \(\varepsilon\) there is a \(\delta\) from definition 8.114. We choose it so that the initial energy is smaller than the lowest value of \(V\) on a sphere smaller than \(\varepsilon\), and than the energy at which the velocity would reach \(\varepsilon\).

  1. Instead of \(\vek q\) we write \(\vek p = \vek q - \vek q_0\) and instead of \(V\) the function \(V(\vek q_0 + \vek p) - V(\vek q_0)\), which has value 0 at \(\vek p = \vek 0\) (the potential is determined up to a constant). The energy changes by a constant, and the displacement \(|\vek p| = |\vek q - \vek q_0|\) and the velocity stay. From now on the equilibrium is at \(\vek p = \vek 0\), \(V(\vek 0) = 0\), and we write \(\mat M\) as a function of \(\vek p\).
  2. Let \(\varepsilon > 0\) be given. Since \(V\) has a strict local minimum at \(\vek 0\), there is \(r > 0\) such that the closed ball \(|\vek p| \le r\) lies in \(U\) and \(V(\vek p) > 0\) for \(0 < |\vek p| \le r\). Let \(\rho = \min(\varepsilon, r)\). On the closed ball \(B = \{|\vek p| \le \rho\}\) we therefore have \(V \ge 0\).
  3. The sphere \(S = \{|\vek p| = \rho\}\) is nonempty, bounded and closed (lemma 8.24, item 4, since \(|\vek p|^2\) is a polynomial), \(V\) is continuous (theorem 8.27, item 2), so by theorem 8.26 \(V\) attains its smallest value \(\mu\) on \(S\). It attains it at a point with \(|\vek p| = \rho > 0\), so \(\mu > 0\).
  4. The function \((\vek p, \vek u) \mapsto \vek u^\T \mat M(\vek p)\,\vek u\) is continuous (lemmas 8.25 and 8.24, item 3) on the nonempty, closed (lemma 8.24, item 4) and bounded set \(B \times \{|\vek u| = 1\} \subset \mathbb R^{2n}\) and positive there, because \(\mat M\) is positive definite. By theorem 8.26 it has a smallest value \(m_0 > 0\) and a largest \(m_1\). For \(|\vek p| \le \rho\) and every \(\dot{\vek p}\) we therefore have \(\tfrac12 m_0 |\dot{\vek p}|^2 \le T \le \tfrac12 m_1 |\dot{\vek p}|^2\).
  5. Let \(\bar\mu = \min\bigl(\mu,\ \tfrac12 m_0 \varepsilon^2\bigr)\). Since \(V\) is continuous at \(\vek 0\) with value 0, there is \(\delta_1 \in (0, \rho]\) such that \(V(\vek p) < \bar\mu/2\) for \(|\vek p| < \delta_1\). Let \(\delta_2 = \sqrt{\bar\mu/m_1}\) and \(\delta = \min(\delta_1, \delta_2)\). For a start with \(|\vek p(0)| < \delta\) and \(|\dot{\vek p}(0)| < \delta\) we have by step 4 \(T(0) \le \tfrac12 m_1 \delta^2 < \bar\mu/2\) and \(V(\vek p(0)) < \bar\mu/2\), so \(\mathcal E(0) < \bar\mu \le \mu\).
  6. A motion on \([0, \tau]\) satisfies theorem 8.106: the total energy \(\mathcal E = T + V\) does not increase for dissipative forces and is constant for conservative ones only. Hence \(\mathcal E(t) \le \mathcal E(0)\) for every \(t \in [0, \tau]\).
  7. We show \(|\vek p(t)| < \rho\) for every \(t \in [0, \tau]\). Suppose that for some \(t_1 \in [0, \tau]\) this is not so. The function \(g(t) = |\vek p(t)|\) is continuous, \(g(0) < \delta \le \rho\) and \(g(t_1) \ge \rho\), so by theorem 2.57 there is \(s \in (0, t_1]\) with \(g(s) = \rho\), so \(\vek p(s) \in S\) and \(V(\vek p(s)) \ge \mu\) by step 3. Since \(T \ge 0\) and by step 6, \(V(\vek p(s)) = \mathcal E(s) - T(s) \le \mathcal E(0) < \mu\). This is a contradiction.
  8. Hence \(|\vek q(t) - \vek q_0| = |\vek p(t)| < \rho \le \varepsilon\) for every \(t \in [0, \tau]\). In the ball \(V \ge 0\), so by step 6 \(T(t) = \mathcal E(t) - V(\vek p(t)) \le \mathcal E(0) < \bar\mu \le \tfrac12 m_0 \varepsilon^2\). By step 4, \(\tfrac12 m_0 |\dot{\vek q}|^2 \le T < \tfrac12 m_0 \varepsilon^2\), so \(|\dot{\vek q}(t)| < \varepsilon\).

Source: The proof is not from a source, no such criterion under the name Dirichlet is in the sources of the project (index.md, gap). GR §2.1.1 (PDF p. 78) uses the same idea, conservation of energy \(\mathcal E = T + V\) (2.4) and \(V \ge 0\) (2.5) around the equilibrium.

The proof showed that the motion cannot escape, because to do so it would have to climb the rim of the potential valley, for which it lacks the energy. Step 3 is essential: we need a strict minimum, since at \(\mu = 0\) (for example if \(V\) were 0 along a line through \(\vek q_0\)) the initial energy could not be smaller than \(\mu\). Step 7 uses the intermediate value theorem on the continuous function \(|\vek p(t)|\), so the motion cannot "jump over" the sphere. The requirement \(\vek Q^{\mathrm{nc}} \cdot \dot{\vek q} \le 0\) is met by forces that brake (viscous friction, air resistance): their power only decreases the energy (theorem 8.106). In this case the motion stays near, but does not necessarily return to the equilibrium. How it really settles into the equilibrium is the subject of chapter 9.

GR (PDF p. 78) defines stability as follows: the equilibrium is stable when there is an energy bound \(\mathcal E^*\) such that for every initial energy \(\mathcal E < \mathcal E^*\) at all later moments \(V \le \mathcal E\) holds, with equality only at the equilibrium. That inequality is from step 7 (the energy stays, \(T \ge 0\)). Our definition requires more: that also the displacement and the velocity stay small, and this is proved by steps 3, 7 and 8.

Example 8.116 Stable equilibria on three examples (stabilna ravnovesja na treh primerih)

Let us take three systems from section 8.6 and in each find \(\delta\) for the chosen \(\varepsilon\) following the steps of the proof.

  1. Pendulum A at \(\theta = 0\). With \(m = 1\) kg, \(l = 1\) m we have \(V(\theta) = 9{,}81\,(1 - \cos\theta)\) J (potential shifted to 0) and \(m_0 = m_1 = m\,l^2 = 1\) kg m². We choose \(\varepsilon = 0{,}1\) rad. \(V > 0\) for \(0 < |\theta| < 2\pi\), so \(\rho = \varepsilon\). The smallest value on \(S = \{\pm 0{,}1\}\) is \(\mu = V(0{,}1) = 0{,}04901\) J, and \(\tfrac12 m_0 \varepsilon^2 = 0{,}005\) J is smaller, so \(\bar\mu = 0{,}005\) J. From \(V(\theta) < \bar\mu/2 = 0{,}0025\) J it follows that \(\delta_1 = 0{,}02258\) rad, and from \(\delta_2 = \sqrt{\bar\mu/m_1} = 0{,}0707\) 1/s we see that \(\delta = \delta_1\). A pendulum released with a displacement smaller than \(0{,}0226\) rad (\(1{,}29^\circ\)) and a velocity smaller than \(0{,}0226\) 1/s therefore never exceeds a displacement of \(0{,}1\) rad (\(5{,}7^\circ\)) and a velocity of \(0{,}1\) 1/s. The estimate is very cautious. A computation of motions from five starts on the edge of the set \(\delta\) (\(\vek p(0) = (\delta, 0)\), \((0, \delta)\) and three others) gives the largest displacement \(0{,}0235\) rad and the largest velocity \(0{,}0735\) 1/s.
  2. Inverted rod B at \(P = 1000\) N. With \(k_{\mathrm{r}} = 2000\) N m, \(L = 1\) m and a point mass \(m = 10\) kg at the top we have \(V(\theta) = \tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{r}}\theta^2 + P L(\cos\theta - 1)\) and \(V''(0) = k_{\mathrm{r}} - P L = 1000\) N m > 0, which by section 8.6 is a strict minimum. The mass is \(m L^2 = 10\) kg m². At \(\varepsilon = 0{,}1\) rad we have \(\mu = V(0{,}1) = 5{,}004\) J, \(\tfrac12 m_0\varepsilon^2 = 0{,}05\) J, so \(\bar\mu = 0{,}05\) J. From \(V < 0{,}025\) J it follows that \(\delta_1 = 0{,}00707\) rad (as for the parabola \(\tfrac12 \cdot 1000\,\theta^2\)), \(\delta_2 = \sqrt{0{,}05/10} = 0{,}0707\) 1/s, so \(\delta = 0{,}00707\). At \(P = P_{\mathrm{cr}} = 2000\) N we have \(V''(0) = 0\) and the second derivative says nothing (section 8.6), but \(V(\theta) = 1000\,\theta^2 + 2000\,(\cos\theta - 1) \approx 83{,}3\,\theta^4\) has a strict minimum. Theorem 8.115 is satisfied by this: \(\mu = V(0{,}1) = 0{,}00833\) J is smaller than \(0{,}05\) J, \(\bar\mu = 0{,}00833\) J, \(\delta_1 = 0{,}0841\) rad, \(\delta_2 = 0{,}0289\) 1/s. The theorem also covers the case that the second derivative could not decide.
  3. Toggle mechanism D at \(P = 100\) N, equilibrium \(\theta = 0{,}50523\) rad (\(28{,}95^\circ\)). Here \(V'' = 2239{,}3\) J (section 8.6) and \(m(\theta) = \tfrac13 + 2\sin^2\theta\) kg m². At \(\varepsilon = 0{,}05\) rad on the interval \([\theta_0 - \varepsilon, \theta_0 + \varepsilon]\) we always have \(V'' \ge 1622\) J, so \(V > V(\theta_0)\) everywhere except at \(\theta_0\) (Taylor's theorem 8.60 with remainder). The mass lies between \(m_0 = 0{,}7199\) and \(m_1 = 0{,}8891\) kg m². The smaller of the values of \(V - V(\theta_0)\) at the two ends of the interval is \(\mu = 2{,}538\) J, and \(\tfrac12 m_0\varepsilon^2 = 0{,}0009\) J is smaller, so \(\bar\mu = 0{,}0009\) J. We get \(\delta_1 = 0{,}000634\) rad and \(\delta_2 = 0{,}0318\) 1/s, so \(\delta = 0{,}000634\) rad (\(0{,}036^\circ\)). The same computation holds at the equilibrium \(-30{,}94^\circ\) with \(V'' = 2741\) J. The computed motions from starts on the border \(\delta\) stay below \(0{,}00064\) rad and \(0{,}034\) 1/s, the velocity also below the bound \(\varepsilon = 0{,}05\) 1/s.

In the third example \(\delta\) is small because in the same circle we mix a displacement in radians and a velocity in 1/s. The kinetic energy \(\tfrac12 m_0\varepsilon^2 = 0{,}0009\) J is there much smaller than \(\mu = 2{,}5\) J, so the kinetic energy limits \(\bar\mu\) and with it \(\delta\). This is the price of a simple estimate, it does not affect the stability of the equilibrium.

The criterion says when an equilibrium is stable. But what if it is not a minimum? For pendulum A at \(\theta = \pi\) we have \(V'' < 0\). Let the pendulum start at \(\theta = \pi\) with a small velocity \(v_0\) and let us compute when it moves away by \(\varepsilon = 0{,}1\) rad (numerical integration of the equation of motion):

\(v_0\) (1/s) time to displacement \(0{,}1\) rad (s)
\(10^{-2}\) \(1{,}32\)
\(10^{-4}\) \(2{,}79\)
\(10^{-6}\) \(4{,}26\)

Each time we reduce the initial velocity by a factor of 100, the time grows by the same increment, \(1{,}47\) s. For uniform motion with the initial velocity the times would be \(0{,}1/v_0\), that is 10 s, 1000 s and 100 000 s. The reason for the slower growth is the force that pulls the pendulum away from the top: at displacement \(p\) its component is \(m g \sin p \approx m g\,p\), so the larger the distance, the larger the acceleration away. The velocity therefore grows faster and faster, and even a very small initial push does not hold back the departure for long. The function that describes this growth exactly is met in this book only with damping (chapter 9). Here we show only that the displacement leaves the circle in a finite time, and estimate the time from above.

Theorem 8.117 Instability for one degree of freedom (nestabilnost pri eni prostostni stopnji)

Let a system have one generalized coordinate \(q\) on an open interval \(U\), scleronomic ideal holonomic constraints, kinetic energy \(T = \tfrac12 m(q)\,\dot q^2\) with continuous function \(m > 0\) and only conservative active forces with potential \(V\), twice continuously differentiable on \(U\). Let \(q_0 \in U\) be an equilibrium, \(V'(q_0) = 0\), and \(V''(q_0) < 0\). Then there is \(\varepsilon > 0\) such that for every number \(v_0 > 0\) every motion on \([0, \varepsilon/c]\) with \(q(0) = q_0\) and \(\dot q(0) = v_0\) reaches the displacement \(\varepsilon\), that is \(q(t) = q_0 + \varepsilon\) at some \(t \le \varepsilon/c\), where \(c = v_0\sqrt{m(q_0)/m_1}\) and \(m_1\) is the largest value of the function \(m\) on \([q_0 - \varepsilon, q_0 + \varepsilon]\). The equilibrium \(q_0\) is therefore unstable if for every such \(v_0\) such a motion exists.

Proof

We must show that a motion that starts at \(q_0\) with an arbitrarily small velocity leaves an interval around \(q_0\). Energy shows that the motion cannot stop, since near \(q_0\) always \(V < V(q_0)\).

  1. Since \(V''\) is continuous and \(V''(q_0) < 0\), there is \(\varepsilon > 0\) such that the closed interval \(I = [q_0 - \varepsilon, q_0 + \varepsilon]\) lies in \(U\) and \(V'' < 0\) on \(I\). By Taylor's theorem (theorem 8.60) with \(V'(q_0) = 0\) we have \(V(q) = V(q_0) + \tfrac12 V''(c)(q - q_0)^2\) for some \(c\) between \(q_0\) and \(q\), so \(V(q) < V(q_0)\) for \(q \in I\), \(q \ne q_0\).
  2. Let \(m_1\) be the largest value of the function \(m\) on \(I\) (theorem 1.73, the extreme value theorem).
  3. Let \(q(0) = q_0\), \(\dot q(0) = v_0 > 0\). By theorem 8.106 \(\tfrac12 m(q)\,\dot q^2 + V(q) = V(q_0) + \tfrac12 m(q_0) v_0^2\) at every moment. As long as \(q(t) \in I\), we therefore have \(\tfrac12 m(q)\,\dot q^2 = \tfrac12 m(q_0)\,v_0^2 + \bigl(V(q_0) - V(q)\bigr) \ge \tfrac12 m(q_0)\,v_0^2 > 0\), so \(\dot q \ne 0\).
  4. Let \(q(t) \in I\) for every \(t \in [0, t']\). The function \(\dot q\) is continuous and \(\dot q(0) = v_0 > 0\). If it were negative at some moment of \([0, t']\), by theorem 2.57 it would have a zero, which step 3 excludes. Hence \(\dot q > 0\) on \([0, t']\) and by step 3 also \(\dot q^2 \ge m(q_0)\,v_0^2/m(q) \ge m(q_0)\,v_0^2/m_1 = c^2\), so \(\dot q \ge c\) as long as \(q \in I\).
  5. Let \(t^*\) be the largest time in \([0, \varepsilon/c]\) up to which \(q\) stays in \(I\) (the set of such times is closed, because \(q\) is continuous and \(I\) is closed, and it contains 0). By the mean value theorem (theorem 1.84) \(q(t^*) - q_0 \ge c\,t^*\). If \(t^* < \varepsilon/c\), \(q(t^*)\) is not an interior point of the interval \(I\) (otherwise \(q\) would stay in \(I\) a little longer), and since \(q(t^*) > q_0\), we have \(q(t^*) = q_0 + \varepsilon\). If \(t^* = \varepsilon/c\), then \(q(t^*) - q_0 \ge \varepsilon\) and \(q(t^*) \in I\), so again \(q(t^*) = q_0 + \varepsilon\). In both cases the motion reaches the displacement \(\varepsilon\) at time \(t^* \le \varepsilon/c\), which is the claim. Since \(v_0\) is arbitrarily small, this means instability, if the motion on \([0, \varepsilon/c]\) exists.

Source: The proof is not from a source (index.md, gap). GR §2.1.1 (PDF p. 78) states that a stable equilibrium corresponds to a local minimum of \(V\), but does not prove instability.

The proof showed that at \(V'' < 0\) the energy itself drives the motion away from the equilibrium: where \(V\) is lower than at \(q_0\), \(T\) must be larger than the initial one, and the velocity cannot change sign. The estimate \(\varepsilon/c\) is an upper bound of the time, since it computes as if the velocity did not increase. The theorem proves that every motion that exists up to time \(\varepsilon/c\) reaches the displacement. That such a motion exists is guaranteed by the continuation theorem for solutions, which the book does not prove (section 8.9.1): in \(I\) both \(q\) and \(\dot q\) are bounded (step 3 bounds \(\dot q^2\) by the energy divided by the smallest value of \(m\) on \(I\)), so the theorem would require the motion to be continued until it leaves \(I\). Without it the proved claim stays conditional: if the motion exists, it reaches the displacement.

Example 8.118 Unstable equilibria on three examples (nestabilna ravnovesja na treh primerih)

We apply theorem 8.117 to the same three systems.

  1. Pendulum A at \(\theta = \pi\). With \(p = \theta - \pi\) we have \(V(\pi + p) = m g l \cos p\) and \(V'' = -9{,}81\) J. The mass is constant, \(m_1 = m(q_0) = 1\) kg m², so \(c = v_0\). At \(\varepsilon = 0{,}1\) rad and \(v_0 = 0{,}01\) 1/s the upper bound is \(\varepsilon/c = 10\) s, and numerical integration shows that the displacement is reached already after \(1{,}32\) s (figure 8.36).
  2. Inverted rod B at \(P = 3000\) N, above \(P_{\mathrm{cr}}\). \(V''(0) = k_{\mathrm{r}} - P L = -1000\) N m, \(m = 10\) kg m² constant. At \(\varepsilon = 0{,}1\) rad and \(v_0 = 0{,}01\) 1/s the bound is \(10\) s, the displacement is reached after \(0{,}53\) s, at \(v_0 = 10^{-4}\) 1/s after \(0{,}99\) s (bound 1000 s). The rod at \(P = 1000\) N is stable (example above), at \(P = 3000\) N it is not. The transition is at \(P_{\mathrm{cr}} = k_{\mathrm{r}}/L\).
  3. Toggle mechanism D at \(P = 100\) N, equilibrium \(\theta_0 = 0{,}0375\) rad (\(2{,}15^\circ\)). \(V'' = -1320\) J. At \(\varepsilon = 0{,}05\) rad we have \(V'' \le -1224\) J on the whole interval \(I\) and \(m(\theta)\) lies between \(0{,}3333\) and \(m_1 = 0{,}3486\) kg m². At \(v_0 = 0{,}01\) 1/s we have \(c = v_0\sqrt{m(\theta_0)/m_1} = 0{,}00982\) 1/s and \(\varepsilon/c = 5{,}09\) s, and the displacement is reached after \(0{,}103\) s. This is the equilibrium between two stable ones.

The theorem holds for one degree of freedom because there the motion is on a line and the velocity cannot change sign. With several degrees of freedom the motion develops in a plane or in space, so this step fails. For the linear system \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek 0\) with mass matrix \(\mat M\) and Hessian matrix (Hessejeva matrika) \(\mat K\) (definition 8.61) of the function \(V\) at the equilibrium, which we derive for oscillation about the equilibrium in section 8.13, instability can nevertheless be proved without natural oscillations. Instead of the energy we watch the function \(f(t) = \tfrac12\,\vek q^\T\mat M\,\vek q\), which for positive definite \(\mat M\) is a measure of the size of the displacement (in units of kinetic energy). We show that the second derivative \(f''\) has a positive lower bound if the energy of the system is negative.

Theorem 8.119 Instability of a linear system with several degrees of freedom (nestabilnost linearnega sistema z več prostostnimi stopnjami)

Let \(\mat M\) be a symmetric positive definite \(n \times n\) matrix with constant entries and \(\mat K\) a symmetric \(n \times n\) matrix. If there is a column \(\vek u\) with \(|\vek u| = 1\) and \(\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u < 0\), then for every \(\eta > 0\) every motion of the system \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek 0\) on \([0, \tau]\) with \(\vek q(0) = \eta\,\vek u\) and \(\dot{\vek q}(0) = \vek 0\) has \(|\vek q(t)| \ge \eta\,t\sqrt{\kappa/m_1}\) for \(t \in [0, \tau]\), with \(\kappa = |\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u|\) and \(m_1\) the largest value of \(\vek v^\T\mat M\vek v\) on unit columns \(\vek v\). The equilibrium \(\vek q = \vek 0\) is therefore not stable if such a motion exists up to the time at which the lower bound \(\eta\,t\sqrt{\kappa/m_1}\) exceeds the chosen \(\varepsilon\) (the continuation theorem from section 8.9.1).

Proof

We must show that the displacement grows at least linearly. We show that the second time derivative of the function \(f\) stays positive, since the energy of the system is not positive.

  1. The energy \(\mathcal E = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q} + \tfrac12\,\vek q^\T\mat K\,\vek q\) is constant: by differentiation and the symmetry of \(\mat M\) and \(\mat K\) we have \(\mathcal E' = \dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \dot{\vek q}^\T\mat K\,\vek q = \dot{\vek q}^\T(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q) = 0\).
  2. For \(f = \tfrac12\,\vek q^\T\mat M\,\vek q\), by the symmetry of \(\mat M\), \(f' = \vek q^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\) and \(f'' = \dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q} + \vek q^\T\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q}\). From the equation \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} = -\mat K\vek q\), so \(f'' = \dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q} - \vek q^\T\mat K\vek q\).
  3. From step 1, \(\vek q^\T\mat K\vek q = 2\mathcal E - \dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\), so \(f'' = 2\,\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q} - 2\mathcal E\).
  4. At \(\vek q(0) = \eta\,\vek u\) and \(\dot{\vek q}(0) = \vek 0\) we have \(\mathcal E = \tfrac12\eta^2\,\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u = -\tfrac12\eta^2\kappa < 0\). Since \(\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q} \ge 0\), we have \(f'' \ge -2\mathcal E = \eta^2\kappa\) at every moment.
  5. The function \(f' - \eta^2\kappa\,t\) has derivative \(f'' - \eta^2\kappa \ge 0\), by theorem 1.84 it does not decrease, and at 0 we have \(f'(0) = \eta\,\vek u^\T\mat M\,\vek 0 = 0\). Hence \(f'(t) \ge \eta^2\kappa\,t\). In the same way the function \(f - \tfrac12\eta^2\kappa\,t^2\) does not decrease, so \(f(t) \ge f(0) + \tfrac12\eta^2\kappa\,t^2 \ge \tfrac12\eta^2\kappa\,t^2\) (\(f(0) \ge 0\)).
  6. By theorem 8.26 the function \(\vek v \mapsto \vek v^\T\mat M\vek v\) has a largest value \(m_1\) on the unit columns, a closed and bounded set as in step 1 of the proof of theorem 8.63, so \(f \le \tfrac12 m_1 |\vek q|^2\). From step 5, \(\tfrac12 m_1|\vek q|^2 \ge \tfrac12\eta^2\kappa\,t^2\), so \(|\vek q(t)| \ge \eta\,t\sqrt{\kappa/m_1}\).

Source: The proof is not from a source. CK §3.3 (PDF pp. 84-86) treats only systems with one degree of freedom with zeros of the characteristic equation, from which the same follows: a zero with positive real part means instability.

The proof showed that in a linear system in which in some direction \(\vek u\) the potential energy decreases (\(\vek u^\T\mat K\vek u < 0\)), the displacement grows at least as a linear function of time, however small it was at the start. Example: the linearized rod B at \(P = 3000\) N, \(\mat M = (10)\) kg m², \(\mat K = (-1000)\) N m, \(\vek u = (1)\), \(\eta = 0{,}01\) rad. Here \(\mathcal E = -0{,}05\) J, \(f(0) = 0{,}0005\) J, \(f'' \ge 0{,}1\) J, so \(|q|^2 \ge 2f/M\) gives \(|q(1\ \text{s})| \ge 0{,}1005\) rad. The displacement \(0{,}1\) rad is therefore reached at the latest after \(0{,}995\) s, and the computer solution of the linear equation reaches it after \(0{,}30\) s. The estimate is again an upper bound of the time, since it does not take into account that \(f''\) grows with \(\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\).

What have we shown. A strict minimum of \(V\) at the equilibrium suffices for stability of motion at the equilibrium (theorem 8.115, with the energy in step 6 and the ball in step 7). For one degree of freedom with \(V'' < 0\) every motion that exists up to time \(\varepsilon/c\) reaches the displacement (theorem 8.117), and the same holds for the linearized system with a negative directional second derivative (theorem 8.119). The equilibrium is not stable if these motions exist, which is guaranteed by the continuation theorem for solutions that we do not prove. Both equilibria of pendulum A, all three cases of rod B and all three equilibria of mechanism D at 100 N we decided in this way. What stays open: for a nonlinear system with several degrees of freedom and an indefinite matrix \(\mat K\) we do not prove instability, we did not find such a theorem in the sources of the project (CK §3.3 treats only linear systems), and at \(V'' = 0\) the higher derivatives decide, as for rod B at \(P = P_{\mathrm{cr}}\). Damping, which settles the motion (the motion approaches the equilibrium, CK §3.3 PDF pp. 84-86 for a linear system with one degree of freedom: roots of the characteristic equation in the left half-plane), we leave to chapter 9. Since at a strict minimum of \(V\) the motion stays near the equilibrium, we can replace the equation of motion there by a simpler, linear one, which we do in section 8.13.
