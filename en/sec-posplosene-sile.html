Chapter 8 Analytical dynamics (analitična dinamika)

8.5 Generalized forces and equilibrium

In section 8.1 we wrote the equilibrium of a system of rigid bodies with ideal constraints as zero virtual work of the external forces at every admissible virtual displacement. For the toggle mechanism D we computed the displacements of the points B and C from \(\delta\theta\) separately for each displacement. Section 8.4 showed that every admissible virtual displacement of the system comes from \(n\) arbitrary numbers \(\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\). The virtual work is therefore a linear function of these numbers, \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\). What is \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\), and how do we get it from the forces acting on the system? When is \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\) the derivative of an energy, just as the force on a bar was the derivative of the strain energy with respect to the elongation (theorem 4.3)? And how do we get back the constraint reactions, which the equations \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) do not contain?

Take a system of rigid bodies as described in sections 8.1 and 8.4. Its configuration is given by a column \(\vek x\) of \(N\) numbers (definition 8.41), and the constraints are independent holonomic constraints \(f_{\mathrm{r}}(\vek x) = 0\), \(r = 1, \dots, m\) (definition 8.42) that do not contain time. Independence means that the matrix \(\mat A = \partial\vek f/\partial\vek x\) with \(m\) rows and \(N\) columns has \(m\) pivots in every admissible configuration (section 8.3). Generalized coordinates \(q_1, \dots, q_{\mathrm{n}}\) with \(n = N - m\) give the configuration through a map \(\vek x = \vek X(\vek q)\) (definition 8.45). By lemma 8.47 the admissible virtual displacements are exactly

\[\delta\vek x = \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} \frac{\partial\vek X}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}} \quad \text{for arbitrary numbers } \delta q_{\mathrm{s}} . \tag{8.32}\]

The constraints are ideal (definition 8.2). The external forces here are the applied forces of section 8.1, that is, all forces except the constraint forces. In this section nothing moves: we look for positions in which the system is at rest, and all displacements are only imagined.

In every example we state the model. The running examples of the earlier sections, the pendulum A, the bar C on two springs and the toggle mechanism D, are denoted as there. Their models are ours and are not from a book, and for each we state what the model idealizes.

8.5.1 Generalized force (posplošena sila)

Start with example C. A homogeneous rigid bar of length \(L = 2\) m and mass \(m = 30\) kg lies horizontally on two vertical linear springs. The left spring has stiffness \(k_1 = 20\) kN/m, the right one \(k_2 = 30\) kN/m (stiffness is the force per unit of elongation), and both are unstretched when the bar is horizontal and its centroid \(G\) is at height zero. A guide prevents the horizontal displacement of the centroid. This is a modelling simplification that removes the horizontal displacements, and the guide is therefore the only body that can act on the bar horizontally. The configuration of the bar is given by the horizontal and vertical coordinates of the centroid and the rotation angle \(\varphi\) (in radians, counterclockwise), so \(N = 3\) numbers. The constraint \(x_{\mathrm{G}} = 0\) reduces them to \(n = 2\) generalized coordinates: the upward vertical displacement \(y\) of the centroid and the rotation \(\varphi\).

A vertical force \(F\) acts upward on the bar at the point \(P\) at distance \(d\) from the centroid, measured along the bar (positive to the right) (figure 8.14). How much virtual work does it do at a displacement \(\delta y\) and a rotation \(\delta\varphi\)? In the position \((y, \varphi)\) the point \(P\) is at height \(y_{\mathrm{P}} = y + d\sin\varphi\). By lemma 4.72 a point of a rigid body moves, at a virtual displacement \(\delta\vek a = (0, \delta y)\) of the centroid and a rotation \(\delta\varphi\) about the axis \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\), by \(\delta\vek a + \delta\varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times\vek r\), where \(\vek r = d\,(\cos\varphi, \sin\varphi)\) is the vector from the centroid to \(P\). Its vertical component is \(\delta y + d\cos\varphi\,\delta\varphi\), which is exactly \(\partial y_{\mathrm{P}}/\partial y\,\delta y + \partial y_{\mathrm{P}}/\partial\varphi\,\delta\varphi\). The virtual work of the force is therefore

\[F\,\delta y + F\,d\cos\varphi\,\delta\varphi ,\]

so the coefficient of \(\delta y\) is \(F\) and that of \(\delta\varphi\) is \(F\,d\cos\varphi\). The second coefficient is the \(z\) component of the moment of the force \(F\) about the centroid (definition 2.32): \(\vek r\times\vek F\) with \(\vek F = (0, F)\) has \(z\) component \(d\cos\varphi\,F\), since \(d\cos\varphi\) is the horizontal distance of the point of application from the centroid, that is, the lever arm of the force. The moment appears by itself, although we added no moment to the bar. For \(F = 100\) N at the right end (\(d = 1\) m) and at \(\varphi = 0\) the coefficient of \(\delta y\) is \(100\) N and that of \(\delta\varphi\) is \(100\) N m.

We do the same for every system. Every external force \(\vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\) acting at a point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) of some body, and every external moment, is multiplied by the virtual displacement, and the displacement is expressed through the \(\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\).

Definition 8.50 Generalized force (posplošena sila)

Let a system of rigid bodies be given with ideal independent holonomic constraints and generalized coordinates \(q_1, \dots, q_{\mathrm{n}}\) (definition 8.45). Let external forces \(\vek F_1, \dots, \vek F_{\mathrm{p}}\) act on the system, each at its own point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) of some body, and external moments \(M_1, \dots, M_{\mathrm{l}}\), each on its own planar body. The position \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek q)\) of the point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) and the rotation angle \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}(\vek q)\) of body \(k\) are to be continuously differentiable functions of the generalized coordinates. The generalized force (posplošena sila) belonging to the coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the number

\[Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{p} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{l} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\frac{\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} , \qquad s = 1, \dots, n . \tag{8.33}\]

The moment \(M_{\mathrm{k}}\) is the component along the axis \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\) perpendicular to the plane of motion. In space the rotation of a body \(k\) is a vector, its virtual rotation is \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \vek w_{\mathrm{ks}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\) with columns \(\vek w_{\mathrm{ks}}\), and the second term is \(\sum_{\mathrm{k}} \vek M_{\mathrm{k}}\cdot\vek w_{\mathrm{ks}}\). We collect the generalized forces into a column \(\vek Q\) of \(n\) numbers. Their unit is work divided by the unit of the coordinate: N when \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a length, and N m when it is an angle in radians. The forces \(\vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\) may be functions of the configuration (a spring force), so \(\vek Q\) is a function of the column \(\vek q\).

Source: GR (1.18) (PDF p. 39), SHABANA (3.53) (PDF p. 116). Both treat forces on points. We add the moments and rotations of rigid bodies from lemma 4.72.

Lemma 8.51 Work of generalized forces (delo posplošenih sil)

Let the system be as in definition 8.50. At every admissible virtual displacement (8.32) the virtual work of the external forces and moments equals

\[\sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}} . \tag{8.34}\]
Proof

We must show that the virtual work of the forces and moments is a linear function of the numbers \(\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\) with coefficients \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\). First we show that the virtual displacement of a point and the rotation of a body given by (8.32) really are a rigid displacement, then we insert them into the work.

  1. In the position of body \(k\) with rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = 0\) the point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) of the body has the vector \(\vek\rho_{\mathrm{i}} = (\rho_1, \rho_2)\) from a chosen point \(O_{\mathrm{k}}\) of the body. The configuration of body \(k\) is given by the coordinates \(\vek o_{\mathrm{k}}\) of the point \(O_{\mathrm{k}}\) and the rotation \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) (definition 8.41), so by lemma 8.40 (equation (8.26) with \(\vek r_0 = \vek\rho_{\mathrm{i}}\)) \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek o_{\mathrm{k}} + \mat R(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}})\,\vek\rho_{\mathrm{i}} = \vek o_{\mathrm{k}} + (\rho_1\cos\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} - \rho_2\sin\varphi_{\mathrm{k}},\ \rho_1\sin\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} + \rho_2\cos\varphi_{\mathrm{k}})\). By theorem 8.12, \(\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial\vek o_{\mathrm{k}}\) is the identity matrix and \(\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = (-\rho_1\sin\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} - \rho_2\cos\varphi_{\mathrm{k}},\ \rho_1\cos\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} - \rho_2\sin\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}) = \vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} - \vek o_{\mathrm{k}})\), because \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times(u, v) = (-v, u)\). For a point with mass that need not be turned, \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}\) is its coordinate.
  2. By the chain rule for \(N\) variables (lemma 8.29) the change of the position \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}\) to first order at a displacement \(\delta\vek x\) equals \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}} (\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x_{\mathrm{j}})\,\delta x_{\mathrm{j}} = \delta\vek o_{\mathrm{k}} + \delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} - \vek o_{\mathrm{k}})\). This is the rigid displacement \(\delta\vek a + \delta\boldsymbol\varphi\times\overrightarrow{OP}\) of lemma 4.72 with \(\delta\vek a = \delta\vek o_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi = \delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\), so it is the virtual displacement of the point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) (definition 8.1).
  3. At an admissible displacement (8.32) we have \(\delta x_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} (\partial X_{\mathrm{j}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}})\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\). Inserting into step 2 and using the same lemma (the function \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek X(\vek q))\) is a composition) we get \(\delta\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} (\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}})\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and likewise \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} (\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}})\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\), because \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) is one of the coordinates \(x_{\mathrm{j}}\).
  4. The virtual work is \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\delta\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) (definitions 4.71 and 2.32). We insert step 3 and exchange the order of the finite sums over \(i\), \(k\) and \(s\):
    \[\sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} \Big(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\frac{\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\Big)\delta q_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}} .\]

Source: GR (1.16) and (1.18) (PDF p. 39) for points. Steps 1 and 2 for a rigid body are not from the source.

The lemma was already tested in example C: \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = F\) and \(Q_\varphi = F\,d\cos\varphi\). Now we compute the generalized forces of all three forces acting on the bar C. The weight \(m g\) acts downward at the centroid (\(d = 0\)). Spring \(1\) is at the left end (\(d = -L/2\)) and the height of its end is \(y_1 = y - (L/2)\sin\varphi\). It is unstretched at \(y_1 = 0\), so it acts on the bar upward with the force \(-k_1\,y_1\). The right spring has \(d = +L/2\), end height \(y_2 = y + (L/2)\sin\varphi\) and force \(-k_2\,y_2\). By definition 8.50,

\[\begin{aligned} Q_{\mathrm{y}} &= -m g - k_1\,y_1 - k_2\,y_2 , \\ Q_\varphi &= \tfrac{L}{2}\cos\varphi\,\big(k_1\,y_1 - k_2\,y_2\big) . \end{aligned} \tag{8.35}\]

The second term \(Q_\varphi\) is the sum of the moments of the spring forces about the centroid, and the weight has no moment. Because of \(\cos\varphi\) in \(Q_\varphi\) the generalized force depends on the configuration, not only on the forces.

8.5.2 Equilibrium in generalized coordinates (ravnovesje v posplošenih koordinatah)

Since the numbers \(\delta q_{\mathrm{s}}\) are arbitrary, every coefficient in (8.34) must be zero if the work is to be zero. Is that also enough for equilibrium? In section 8.1 we proved in theorem 8.3 that equilibrium implies zero virtual work of the external forces. The converse does not follow from that theorem: zero work of the external forces at admissible displacements does not say that the constraints can really supply forces that balance every body separately. Section 8.1 postponed the converse to here. Lemma 8.35 helps. Let us first say what it means that a constraint can supply a force.

The load (obremenitev) of a group of forces acting on the bodies of the system is a column \(\vek h\) of \(N\) numbers that has, at every virtual displacement \(\delta\vek x\) (also a non-admissible one, that is, at every rigid displacement of every body separately), the virtual work \(\vek h\cdot\delta\vek x\). Such a column exists for every group of forces, because by theorem 4.73 (equation (4.44)) the virtual work of the forces on a body is a linear function of the numbers \(\delta\vek a\) and \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi\), and these are numbers from \(\delta\vek x\). The coefficients of this linear function are the numbers \(h_{\mathrm{j}}\). The load of the constraint forces is called the load of the constraints. The external forces also have a load: the column \(\vek g(\vek x)\) with numbers \(g_{\mathrm{j}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial x_{\mathrm{j}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}/\partial x_{\mathrm{j}}\) is the generalized force in the coordinates \(\vek x\) (definition 8.50 for a system without constraints, \(m = 0\), \(\vek q = \vek x\)), and by lemma 8.51 the virtual work of the external forces equals \(\vek g\cdot\delta\vek x\). The relation between it and the generalized forces in the coordinates \(\vek q\) is, by the chain rule for \(N\) variables (lemma 8.29, applied to \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek X(\vek q))\) and \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}(\vek X(\vek q))\)),

\[Q_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{j}} g_{\mathrm{j}}\,\frac{\partial X_{\mathrm{j}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} . \tag{8.36}\]

An ideal constraint (definition 8.2) does zero work at every displacement the constraint allows. The admissible displacements are the solutions of the equation \(\mat A\,\delta\vek x = \vek 0\) (equation (8.29)). So the load \(\vek h\) of the constraints is such that \(\vek h\cdot\delta\vek x = 0\) at every such \(\delta\vek x\), and by lemma 8.35 we have \(\vek h = \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\) for exactly one column \(\vek\lambda\) of \(m\) numbers. Conversely, what a constraint can supply is a property of the model of the constraint. For a bilateral constraint (dvostranska vez) the model says that the constraint can supply every load of the form \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\): a hinged support gives every force (its pair of opposite forces on two bodies, or on a body and the ground), a smooth guide that holds a point on a curve from both sides gives every force perpendicular to it. A one-sided contact (a contact that only pushes, or a rope that only pulls) lacks this property: it can supply only one sign of force. Then the condition \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) stays necessary but is no longer sufficient. Friction is not an ideal constraint either, because its force does work during the sliding that the constraint allows.

Theorem 8.52 Equilibrium in generalized coordinates (ravnovesje v posplošenih koordinatah)

Let a system of rigid bodies have a configuration \(\vek x\) in \(\mathbb R^N\) and independent holonomic constraints \(\vek f(\vek x) = \vek 0\) without time, \(n = N - m \ge 1\). The matrix \(\mat A = \partial\vek f/\partial\vek x\) is to have \(m\) pivots in the configuration \(\vek x_0 = \vek X(\vek q_0)\). The constraints are to be ideal and bilateral: every load of the form \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\) is to be the load of some constraint forces. Let external forces and moments act on the system with generalized forces \(\vek Q(\vek q)\) in the coordinates \(\vek q\) and with column \(\vek g(\vek x)\) in the coordinates \(\vek x\). Then:

  1. The system is in equilibrium in the configuration \(\vek x_0\) (there are constraint forces under which every body is in equilibrium under the external forces and the constraint forces) exactly when \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q_0) = 0\) for \(s = 1, \dots, n\).
  2. Then there is exactly one column \(\vek\lambda\) of \(m\) numbers with
    \[\vek g(\vek x_0) + \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = \vek 0 , \tag{8.37}\]
    and the load of the constraint forces is \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\). The number \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\) is thus the intensity of the reaction of constraint \(r\), and its load is \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\partial f_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\vek x\).
Proof

We must show that equilibrium is equivalent to the condition \(\vek g + \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = \vek 0\) and that this is equivalent to \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\). The direction "equilibrium, hence \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\)" needs only the idealness of the constraints, and the converse direction needs bilaterality.

  1. By theorem 4.73, body \(k\) is in equilibrium exactly when the virtual work of all forces on it is zero at every rigid virtual displacement of this body. The virtual work of the external forces and the constraint forces on body \(k\) is \(\sum_{\mathrm{j}}(g_{\mathrm{j}} + h_{\mathrm{j}})\,\delta x_{\mathrm{j}}\) over the coordinates \(x_{\mathrm{j}}\) of this body, where \(\vek h\) is the load of the constraint forces. The coordinates of different bodies are independent, so the system is in equilibrium exactly when \((\vek g + \vek h)\cdot\delta\vek x = 0\) at every \(\delta\vek x\) in \(\mathbb R^N\). With \(\delta\vek x = \vek e_{\mathrm{j}}\) we get \(g_{\mathrm{j}} + h_{\mathrm{j}} = 0\) for every \(j\), and conversely this equality gives zero for every \(\delta\vek x\). So the system is in equilibrium exactly when the constraint forces have the load \(\vek h = -\vek g\).
  2. Let the system be in equilibrium. By the definition of an ideal constraint (definition 8.2) \(\vek h\cdot\delta\vek x = 0\) at every admissible displacement, that is, at every \(\delta\vek x\) with \(\mat A\,\delta\vek x = \vek 0\) (equation (8.29)). By lemma 8.35, \(\vek h = \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\mu\) for exactly one \(\vek\mu\). By step 1, \(\vek g + \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\mu = \vek 0\), so \(\vek\lambda = \vek\mu\) exists and the load of the constraint forces is \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\).
  3. Let \(\vek\lambda\) exist with \(\vek g + \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = \vek 0\). By the assumption on bilateral constraints there are constraint forces with the load \(\vek h = \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = -\vek g\), and by step 1 the system is in equilibrium.
  4. Let the system be in equilibrium, so step 2 holds. At every admissible displacement \(\delta\vek x\) we have \(\vek g\cdot\delta\vek x = -\vek\lambda\cdot\mat A\,\delta\vek x = 0\). The displacement \(\partial\vek X/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is admissible (lemma 8.47 with \(\delta\vek q = \vek e_{\mathrm{s}}\)), so by (8.36), \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek g\cdot\partial\vek X/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\).
  5. Let \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) for all \(s\). By lemma 8.47 every admissible displacement has the form (8.32), and by (8.36) we have \(\vek g\cdot\delta\vek x = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\delta q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\). So \(\vek g\cdot\delta\vek x = 0\) at every \(\delta\vek x\) with \(\mat A\,\delta\vek x = \vek 0\), and by lemma 8.35 (with the column \(\vek g\)) we have \(\vek g = \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\nu\) for exactly one \(\vek\nu\). With \(\vek\lambda = -\vek\nu\) we get \(\vek g + \mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = \vek 0\).
  6. Part 1: equilibrium gives \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) by step 4, and \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) gives equilibrium by steps 5 and 3. Part 2: by steps 2 and 3 the load of the constraint forces is \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\), and \(\vek\lambda\) is unique by the uniqueness in lemma 8.35, since \(\mat A\) has \(m\) pivots.

Source: Direction "equilibrium, hence \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\)": GR (1.17) (PDF p. 39, in statics the inertial term is zero), SHABANA (3.51)-(3.53) (PDF p. 116). The converse direction, the assumption on bilateral constraints and part 2 are not from the source. The form of the reactions as multipliers: GR (1.79) (PDF p. 63).

The proof showed that the conditions \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) contain all of equilibrium, while the reactions drop out of them. The direction "equilibrium, hence \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\)" is theorem 8.3 written in coordinates and needs only ideal constraints. The converse direction, which section 8.1 postponed, needs bilateral constraints as well, and lemma 8.35 helps there. The constraint reactions, when we need them, are \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda\) with \(\vek\lambda\) from (8.37), which we use in the last subsection. Now let us test the theorem on two examples.

Example A: pendulum. A point mass \(m = 1\) kg is at the end of a rigid inextensible massless bar of length \(l = 1\) m that turns about a hinge \(O\) in a vertical plane. We measure the angle \(\theta\) from the vertical downward, and it is the only generalized coordinate (\(N = 2\), \(m = 1\), \(n = 1\)). The position of the mass is \(\vek r = l\,(\sin\theta, -\cos\theta)\), the weight is \(\vek F = (0, -m g)\) with \(g = 9{,}81\) m/s\(^2\), and \(\partial\vek r/\partial\theta = l\,(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\). By definition 8.50,

\[Q_\theta = \vek F\cdot\frac{\partial\vek r}{\partial\theta} = -m g\,l\,\sin\theta .\]

By theorem 8.52, equilibrium requires \(\sin\theta = 0\), so \(\theta = 0\) (the mass is below the hinge) or \(\theta = \pi\) (above it). The mass is thus in equilibrium in both positions. Which of them survives a disturbance is told by section 8.6. The force of the bar does not appear in the equation, because the bar acts perpendicular to the virtual displacement of the mass.

Example C: bar on two springs. The conditions \(Q_{\mathrm{y}} = 0\) and \(Q_\varphi = 0\) from (8.35) are two equations for two unknowns. The angle \(\varphi\) will be small: below we get about \(1{,}2\cdot 10^{-3}\) rad. At such an angle, by lemma 8.19, \(\sin\varphi\) differs from \(\varphi\) by less than \(\varphi^2/6 \approx 2{,}5\cdot10^{-7}\) relatively, and \(\cos\varphi\) differs from \(1\) by less than \(\varphi^2/2 \approx 7{,}5\cdot10^{-7}\). We therefore replace them by \(\varphi\) and \(1\) and get the linear equations

\[\begin{aligned} 50\,000\,y + 10\,000\,\varphi &= -m g = -294{,}3 , \\ 10\,000\,y + 50\,000\,\varphi &= 0 , \end{aligned}\]

in units N, N/m, N m (\(k_1 + k_2 = 50\,000\) N/m, \((k_2 - k_1)\,L/2 = 10\,000\) N, \((k_1 + k_2)\,(L/2)^2 = 50\,000\) N m). The second equation gives \(\varphi = -y/5\), and the first then \(48\,000\,y = -294{,}3\), so \(y = -6{,}13125\) mm and \(\varphi = 1{,}22625\cdot10^{-3}\) rad. The solution of the exact equations (8.35), found by SymPy, agrees with it to six digits. The coordinates of the ends of the bar are \(y_1 = y - (L/2)\varphi = -7{,}3575\) mm and \(y_2 = -4{,}9050\) mm. The springs hold them with the forces \(k_1\cdot 7{,}3575\ \text{mm} = 147{,}15\) N and \(k_2\cdot 4{,}9050\ \text{mm} = 147{,}15\) N. Both equal \(m g/2\), as the statics of a rigid body from chapter 2 demands: the centroid is in the middle, so the moment about it demands equal forces, and their sum must be \(m g\). There is no horizontal external force, so by (8.37) (horizontal component) the reaction of the guide is zero.

8.5.3 Conservative forces and potential energy (konservativne sile in potencialna energija)

For the bar (theorem 4.3) we got the spring force as the derivative of an energy with respect to the elongation. Are the generalized forces in example C derivatives as well? They are: \(Q_{\mathrm{y}}\) and \(Q_\varphi\) from (8.35) are the negative derivatives with respect to \(y\) and \(\varphi\) of the function \(V = \tfrac12 k_1 y_1^2 + \tfrac12 k_2 y_2^2 + m g y\) (checked with SymPy). For such forces the statement of equilibrium is much simpler, so we give them a name.

Definition 8.53 Conservative forces and potential energy (konservativne sile in potencialna energija)

Let \(D\) be an open set of columns \(\vek q\) of \(n\) numbers in which the generalized coordinates of the system lie (definition 8.45). The external forces are conservative on \(D\) if there is a continuously differentiable function \(V\colon D \to \mathbb R\) with input a column \(\vek q\) of \(n\) numbers and output a number, and

\[Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q) = -\frac{\partial V}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}(\vek q) \quad \text{for } s = 1, \dots, n \text{ and every } \vek q \text{ in } D . \tag{8.38}\]

The function \(V\) is the potential energy of the system, in units of work. It is determined up to a constant.

Source: GR (1.20), (1.21) (PDF p. 41). The statement with an open set and continuous differentiability is ours.

The potential is not uniquely determined, since an added constant changes no derivative. Let us test which known forces are conservative. In each case we compute the generalized force (definition 8.50) and find out whether it has a potential.

  1. Weight. A point of mass \(m\) has height \(h(\vek q)\) above a chosen horizontal plane. The force \(-m g\,\vek e_{\mathrm{y}}\) gives \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = -m g\,\partial h/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(V = m g\,h\).
  2. Linear spring. A spring of stiffness \(k\) between the points \(P_1\) and \(P_2\) with positions \(\vek r_1(\vek q)\) and \(\vek r_2(\vek q)\) has length \(\ell = |\vek r_2 - \vek r_1|\) and unstretched length \(\ell_0\). The elongation is \(e = \ell - \ell_0\), and the force acts along the spring at each point towards the other one when \(e > 0\), with magnitude \(k\,e\). Let \(\vek n = (\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)/\ell\). The force on \(P_1\) is \(k\,e\,\vek n\) and on \(P_2\) it is \(-k\,e\,\vek n\). The generalized force is \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = k\,e\,\vek n\cdot\partial(\vek r_1 - \vek r_2)/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = -k\,e\,\vek n\cdot\partial(\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\). The spring has positive length \(\ell > 0\) everywhere. Since \(\ell^2 = (\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)\cdot(\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)\), we have \(2\ell\,\partial\ell/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 2\,(\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)\cdot\partial(\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(\partial\ell/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek n\cdot\partial(\vek r_2 - \vek r_1)/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\). Hence \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = -k\,e\,\partial e/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = -\partial(\tfrac12 k\,e^2)/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(V = \tfrac12 k\,e^2\), as for the bar in theorem 4.3 (there \(k = EA/L\)).
  3. Rotational spring (zasučna vzmet). The spring connects two bodies, the relative angle between them is \(\theta\) (in radians), and it is unstretched at \(\theta = 0\). It acts on one body with the moment \(-k_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta\) and on the other with the opposite one. The virtual work of both moments is \(-k_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta\,\delta\theta\), so \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = -k_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta\,\partial\theta/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(V = \tfrac12 k_{\mathrm{r}}\,\theta^2\). The unit of \(k_{\mathrm{r}}\) is N m per radian.
  4. Constant force (a load that does not change its magnitude and direction). A force \(\vek F\) at the point \(\vek r(\vek q)\) gives \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = \vek F\cdot\partial\vek r/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(V = -\vek F\cdot\vek r\).

A sum of conservative forces is conservative, and its potential is the sum of the potentials. The elastic parts of a structure enter with the strain energy, as in the total potential energy \(\Pi\) of definition 4.89. In this chapter we model them by springs and bars, which by item 2 have the potential \(\tfrac12 k\,e^2\).

Example D: toggle mechanism. The bars \(AB\) and \(BC\) have length \(a = 0{,}5\) m and no mass (the weight of the bars is neglected, which is a modelling simplification). The hinge \(A\) is at the origin, the hinge \(B\) is at the angle \(\theta\) above the horizontal, and the slider \(C\) slides along a horizontal guide through \(A\). A horizontal spring of stiffness \(k = 10\) kN/m connects \(A\) and \(C\) and is unstretched at \(\theta_0 = 30^\circ\). A vertical force \(P\) acts downward at the point \(B\). The coordinate \(\theta\) is the only generalized coordinate. The slider is at \(x_{\mathrm{C}} = 2a\cos\theta\), the height of the point \(B\) is \(a\sin\theta\), and the elongation of the spring is \(e = 2a\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\). By items 1 and 2,

\[V(\theta) = \tfrac12 k\,(2a)^2\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)^2 + P\,a\sin\theta , \qquad Q_\theta = -V'(\theta) . \tag{8.39}\]

We compute the derivative with the derivatives of cosine and sine from theorem 8.12: \(V'(\theta) = -4k\,a^2\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\sin\theta + P\,a\cos\theta\). The condition \(Q_\theta = 0\) gives, after dividing by \(a\cos\theta\), the same as the virtual work in section 8.1: \(P = 4k\,a\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\tan\theta\). At \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad we get \(P = 552{,}54\) N.

For a given load \(P\) we ask at which angles \(\theta\) the mechanism is in equilibrium. These are the zeros of \(V'(\theta)\), which we find with SymPy (figure 8.15). At \(P = 0\) there are three: \(\theta = 0\) and \(\theta = \pm 30^\circ\) (the spring is unstretched at \(+30^\circ\) and at \(-30^\circ\), because cosine is an even function). In equilibrium at \(\pm 30^\circ\) we have \(V = 0\) and \(V\) has its smallest value there (\(V'' = 2500\) J), while at \(\theta = 0\) there is a peak with \(V = 89{,}75\) J (\(V'' = -1340\) J). At \(P = 100\) N there are three equilibrium positions, \(28{,}95^\circ\), \(2{,}15^\circ\) and \(-30{,}94^\circ\), and at \(P = 300\) N they are \(26{,}32^\circ\), \(6{,}73^\circ\) and \(-32{,}59^\circ\). At \(P = 553{,}01\) N the two equilibria at positive \(\theta\) merge at \(17{,}60^\circ\), and at a larger load there are none near \(\theta_0\). Which equilibria survive a disturbance is told by section 8.6.

Conservative forces have one more important property: the work they do in a displacement from one configuration to another does not depend on the path. What is the work along a curve \(\vek q(t)\)? We split \([t_1, t_2]\) by points \(t_1 = \tau_0 < \dots < \tau_{\mathrm{m}} = t_2\). On piece \(i\) the coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) changes by \(\Delta q_{\mathrm{s},\mathrm{i}} = q_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau_{\mathrm{i}}) - q_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau_{\mathrm{i}-1}) = \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{s},\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{i}}\) with a point \(\xi_{\mathrm{s},\mathrm{i}}\) inside the piece (theorem 1.84). If the forces kept the values \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau_{\mathrm{i}})\) on the piece, the work of the step would be \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau_{\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta q_{\mathrm{s},\mathrm{i}}\) by lemma 8.51. The work over the whole motion is therefore approximately \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\tau_{\mathrm{i}})\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(\xi_{\mathrm{s},\mathrm{i}})\,\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{i}}\), which is close to a Riemann sum of \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). That is why the definition takes the integral. For one point on a straight line with \(q = s\) it agrees with definition 4.1, item 2: \(\int Q(q(t))\,\dot q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\) equals \(\int Q(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\) between the same endpoints by the fundamental theorem of calculus (theorem 3.40) and the chain rule.

Definition 8.54 Work and power of generalized forces (delo in moč posplošenih sil)

Let a system be as in definition 8.50, let \(\vek q(t)\), \(t_1 \le t \le t_2\), be a continuously differentiable motion, and let the generalized forces \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\) along it be continuous functions of time. The power (moč) of the generalized forces at time \(t\) is the number

\[P(t) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(t) . \tag{8.40}\]

The work along the motion (delo vzdolž gibanja) is

\[W = \int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2} P(t)\,\mathrm{d}t . \tag{8.41}\]

Source: Power \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\): GR (1.58) (PDF p. 54), SPONG (PDF p. 217, the term \(\dot{\vek q}^{\mathsf T}\vek\tau\) has units of power). Work as the time integral of the power of a force on a point: EP (3.1.5) (PDF p. 98). The definition itself is not from a source.

Lemma 8.55 Power is the derivative of work and the power of forces on points (moč je odvod dela in moč sil na točke)

Let \(P\) be the power from definition 8.54.

  1. The function \(W(t) = \int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t} P(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\) is differentiable on \([t_1, t_2]\) and \(W'(t) = P(t)\).
  2. Let \(\vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{k}}\) be the forces and moments of definition 8.50, \(\vek v_{\mathrm{i}}\) the velocity of the point \(P_{\mathrm{i}}\) and \(\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) the time derivative of the angle of rotation of the plane body \(k\) at time \(t\). Then
    \[P(t) = \sum_{\mathrm{i}=1}^{p} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\vek v_{\mathrm{i}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{l} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} . \tag{8.42}\]
Proof

For item 1 we must show that \(P\) is continuous and apply the fundamental theorem of calculus. For item 2 we write the velocities with the chain rule and insert the definition of the generalized force.

  1. Derivative of the work. The functions \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\) and \(\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\) are continuous, so every sum of products \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is continuous too, hence \(P\). By item 2 of the fundamental theorem of calculus 3.40, \(W\) is differentiable and \(W'(t) = P(t)\).
  2. Power of forces on points. The position \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}(\vek q(t))\) and the angle \(\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}(\vek q(t))\) are composite functions of time, so by the chain rule (lemma 8.29, item 1, componentwise) \(\vek v_{\mathrm{i}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} (\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}})\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} (\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}})\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). We insert them and swap the order of the finite sums: \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\vek v_{\mathrm{i}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\dot\varphi_{\mathrm{k}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \big(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek F_{\mathrm{i}}\cdot\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} + \sum_{\mathrm{k}} M_{\mathrm{k}}\,\partial\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\big)\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). The bracket is \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}\) by equation (8.33), so the sum is \(P(t)\).

Source: Item 2 for points with time-independent constraints: GR (1.18) (PDF p. 39), (1.24) with \(\partial U_{\mathrm{ik}}/\partial t = 0\) (PDF p. 42) and (1.44) (PDF p. 47), where \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal T/\mathrm{d}t = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}\). Item 1 and the moment with the rotation of a rigid body are not from the source. The proof is not from a source.

Item 1 is the precise form of the words "power is work per unit time". Item 2 says that the power of the system is the sum of the powers of the single forces, and each force contributes its dot product with the velocity of the point where it acts.

Lemma 8.56 Work of conservative forces (delo konservativnih sil)

Let the forces be conservative on an open set \(D\) with potential \(V\) (definition 8.53), and let \(\vek q(t)\), \(t_1 \le t \le t_2\), be a continuously differentiable curve in \(D\). Then the work of the forces along it (definition 8.54) equals

\[\int_{\mathrm{t}_1}^{t_2} \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q(t))\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t = V(\vek q(t_1)) - V(\vek q(t_2)) . \tag{8.43}\]

So the work depends only on the end points and is zero on a closed curve.

Proof

We must show that the integrand is the derivative of the function \(-V(\vek q(t))\), and then use the fundamental theorem of calculus.

  1. The function \(g(t) = V(\vek q(t))\) is continuously differentiable on \([t_1, t_2]\) and by the chain rule for \(n\) variables (lemma 8.29) we have \(g'(t) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q(t))\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}(t)\).
  2. By (8.38) the integrand in (8.43) equals \(-g'(t)\), and \(g'\) is continuous.
  3. By the fundamental theorem of calculus 3.40 the integral is \(-(g(t_2) - g(t_1))\), which is the right-hand side of (8.43).

Source: The proof is not from a source. GR (1.21) (PDF p. 41) contains only the relation between force and potential. Chapter 4 announced this property in subsection 4.7.6.

The lemma fulfils the promise of chapter 4 that we would need the path independence of work in this chapter. We use it in section 8.11, where we show that the energy of such a system does not change during motion.

8.5.4 When forces have a potential (kdaj imajo sile potencial)

In the examples we always guessed the potential from the physics. But how do we know that given generalized forces \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q)\) have one, and how do we find it if we cannot guess it? If \(V\) is a twice continuously differentiable function, then \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = -\partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\partial Q_{\mathrm{s}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{r}} = -\partial^2 V/\partial q_{\mathrm{r}}\,\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\). By theorem 8.30 on the interchange of mixed derivatives this equals \(\partial Q_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\). The condition \(\partial Q_{\mathrm{s}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{r}} = \partial Q_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is therefore necessary. Chapter 4 showed that on a star-shaped set it is also sufficient, but for three variables (theorem 4.104). Here we need \(n\) variables, and the proof goes along the same steps.

Corollary 8.57 Potential from generalized forces (potencial iz posplošenih sil)

Let \(D\) be an open set of columns \(\vek q\) of \(n\) numbers, star-shaped with respect to \(\vek q_0\): for every \(\vek q\) in \(D\) the point \(\vek q_0 + t\,(\vek q - \vek q_0)\) is in \(D\) for every \(t\) with \(0 \le t \le 1\). Let \(Q_1, \dots, Q_{\mathrm{n}}\) be continuously differentiable functions on \(D\).

  1. If the forces are conservative on \(D\) with a twice continuously differentiable potential \(V\), then
    \[\frac{\partial Q_{\mathrm{s}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{r}}} = \frac{\partial Q_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} \quad \text{for all } s, r . \tag{8.44}\]
  2. If (8.44) holds on \(D\), the forces are conservative on \(D\) with the potential
    \[V(\vek q) = -\int_0^1 \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d)\,d_{\mathrm{s}}\,\mathrm{d}t , \qquad \vek d = \vek q - \vek q_0 , \tag{8.45}\]
    which is continuously differentiable, \(V(\vek q_0) = 0\).
Proof

Part 1 is theorem 8.30, applied to the pair of variables \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\), \(q_{\mathrm{r}}\) with the others constant, as in the introduction. We prove part 2 as theorem 4.104, with the components \(w_{\mathrm{s}} = -Q_{\mathrm{s}}\) for \(s = 1, \dots, n\) instead of three. The steps repeat the steps of the proof of that theorem, with \(n\) components instead of three.

  1. Let \(\vek q\) be in \(D\), \(\vek d = \vek q - \vek q_0\). Since \(D\) is open and star-shaped with respect to \(\vek q_0\), there is an open interval \(I\) containing \([0, 1]\) on which \(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d\) is in \(D\) (as step 1 there).
  2. The function \(t \mapsto w_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d)\) is differentiable on \(I\) by lemma 8.28 on the derivative along a segment for \(n\) variables, hence continuous. The integrand of (8.45) is a sum of such functions multiplied by the numbers \(d_{\mathrm{s}}\), so it is continuous and integrable (theorem 3.38). At \(\vek q = \vek q_0\) we have \(\vek d = \vek 0\), so \(V(\vek q_0) = 0\).
  3. Choose an index \(l\). Since \(D\) is open, there is \(r > 0\) such that \(\vek q + \sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{l}}\) is in \(D\) for \(|\sigma| < r\). Let \(\vek Q(t, \sigma) = \vek q_0 + t\,(\vek d + \sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{l}})\) (in \(D\), because \(D\) is star-shaped with respect to \(\vek q_0\) and \(\vek q + \sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{l}}\) is in \(D\)) and \(f(t, \sigma) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} w_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek Q(t, \sigma))\,(d_{\mathrm{s}} + \sigma\,\delta_{\mathrm{sl}})\). Then \(V(\vek q + \sigma\,\vek e_{\mathrm{l}}) = \int_0^1 f(t, \sigma)\,\mathrm{d}t\).
  4. By lemma 8.28 and the product rule,
    \[\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma}(t, \sigma) = t\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \frac{\partial w_{\mathrm{s}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}}\big(\vek Q(t, \sigma)\big)\,(d_{\mathrm{s}} + \sigma\,\delta_{\mathrm{sl}}) + w_{\mathrm{l}}\big(\vek Q(t, \sigma)\big) .\]
  5. The functions \(f\) and \(\partial f/\partial\sigma\) are continuous on \([0, 1] \times [-r/2, r/2]\), because \(w_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(\partial w_{\mathrm{s}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}\) are continuous (theorem 8.27, item 2), and \(\vek Q\) is a continuous function of the numbers \(t\) and \(\sigma\).
  6. By lemma 4.19 on the derivative of an integral with respect to a parameter, \(\partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}\,(\vek q) = \int_0^1 \frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma}(t, 0)\,\mathrm{d}t\).
  7. At \(\sigma = 0\) and with (8.44), which replaces \(\partial w_{\mathrm{s}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}\) by \(\partial w_{\mathrm{l}}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\), we have \(\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma}(t, 0) = t\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \frac{\partial w_{\mathrm{l}}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d)\,d_{\mathrm{s}} + w_{\mathrm{l}}(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d)\).
  8. This is the derivative \(h'(t)\) of the function \(h(t) = t\,w_{\mathrm{l}}(\vek q_0 + t\,\vek d)\) (lemma 8.28 and the product rule), which is continuous on \([0, 1]\).
  9. By the fundamental theorem of calculus 3.40, \(\partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}\,(\vek q) = h(1) - h(0) = w_{\mathrm{l}}(\vek q) = -Q_{\mathrm{l}}(\vek q)\).
  10. The partial derivatives \(-Q_{\mathrm{l}}\) are continuous, so \(V\) is continuously differentiable on \(D\). By step 9, \(Q_{\mathrm{l}} = -\partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{l}}\) for all \(l\).

Source: LEBL2 Thm. 9.3.6 (Poincaré lemma, PDF p. 87), for star-shaped sets in \(\mathbb R^n\), with the formula (8.45) and differentiation under the integral. The steps that check the assumptions of differentiation under the integral and of the fundamental theorem we leave as in chapter 4: they are not from the source.

The corollary tells us when to look for a potential. When does it turn out that there is none? An example is a force that turns together with the body, called a follower force (sledilna sila): its direction is tied to the position of the body, so its work along a closed path in the coordinate space is not zero.

Take two rigid massless bars of length \(L = 1\) m. The bar \(OA\) is fixed to the ground at the hinge \(O\), the bar \(AB\) is fixed to the first at the hinge \(A\). The rotations from the horizontal are \(\varphi_1\) and \(\varphi_2\) (in radians, counterclockwise), and the end is \(\vek r_{\mathrm{B}} = L\,(\cos\varphi_1 + \cos\varphi_2,\ \sin\varphi_1 + \sin\varphi_2)\). At the point \(B\) acts a force \(F = 100\) N that always has the direction of the bar \(AB\) from \(A\) towards \(B\), \(\vek F = F\,(\cos\varphi_2, \sin\varphi_2)\) (figure 8.16). This is our model of the thrust of a motor bolted to the end of the bar: the model and the numbers in this example are ours and are not in a source, and we checked the numbers with SymPy. By definition 8.50, with \(\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{B}}/\partial\varphi_1 = L\,(-\sin\varphi_1, \cos\varphi_1)\) and \(\partial\vek r_{\mathrm{B}}/\partial\varphi_2 = L\,(-\sin\varphi_2, \cos\varphi_2)\),

\[Q_1 = F\,L\,\sin(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1) , \qquad Q_2 = 0 . \tag{8.46}\]

We get the first equality from \(-\cos\varphi_2\sin\varphi_1 + \sin\varphi_2\cos\varphi_1 = \sin(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)\) by the addition formula (corollary 8.15 with \(\beta = -\varphi_1\), sine is odd and cosine is even, lemma 8.10, item 4). In the second the terms cancel: a force along the bar \(AB\) does not turn that bar about \(A\). The derivatives are \(\partial Q_1/\partial\varphi_2 = F\,L\cos(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1)\) and \(\partial Q_2/\partial\varphi_1 = 0\). So the condition (8.44) does not hold (except in positions with \(\cos(\varphi_2 - \varphi_1) = 0\)), and by part 1 of corollary 8.57 the follower force has no potential on any open set of coordinates.

We can also compute this. We find the work along a closed path in the plane \((\varphi_1, \varphi_2)\): a square of side \(\alpha = 0{,}5\) rad, which we traverse from \((0, 0)\) to \((\alpha, 0)\), \((\alpha, \alpha)\), \((0, \alpha)\) and back (figure 8.16). On the horizontal side at \(\varphi_2 = 0\) the work is \(\int_0^\alpha Q_1\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_1 = F\,L\int_0^\alpha \sin(-\varphi_1)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_1 = F\,L\,(\cos\alpha - 1)\). On the sides where \(\varphi_2\) changes, \(Q_2 = 0\) and the work is zero. On the horizontal side at \(\varphi_2 = \alpha\) the angle \(\varphi_1\) decreases from \(\alpha\) to \(0\) and the work is \(F\,L\int_\alpha^0 \sin(\alpha - \varphi_1)\,\mathrm{d}\varphi_1 = F\,L\,(\cos\alpha - 1)\). In total the work is

\[2\,F\,L\,(\cos\alpha - 1) = -24{,}48\ \text{J} .\]

Each side is a continuously differentiable curve (for example \(\varphi_1 = t\) at constant \(\varphi_2\)). If the force had a potential on a set containing the square, then by lemma 8.56 the work on each side would be the difference of the potential at its start and end, and the sum over the four sides would cancel telescopically, so the work on a closed path would be zero. The system returns to the same position, but the force has done the negative work \(-24{,}48\) J. So for a follower force we cannot speak of a potential energy, and the condition for the stability of an equilibrium through the energy, which we meet in section 8.6, does not hold for it. We do not treat the stability of such systems in this book, and we did not find a source that treats it.

8.5.5 Equilibrium from the potential energy (ravnovesje iz potencialne energije)

For conservative forces theorem 8.52 takes a simple form.

Corollary 8.58 Equilibrium is a stationary point of the energy (ravnovesje je stacionarna točka energije)

Let the conditions of theorem 8.52 be satisfied and let the forces be conservative on an open set \(D\) with potential \(V\) (definition 8.53). A configuration \(\vek q_0\) in \(D\) is an equilibrium exactly when

\[\frac{\partial V}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}(\vek q_0) = 0 \quad \text{for } s = 1, \dots, n , \tag{8.47}\]

that is, \(\vek q_0\) is a stationary point (stacionarna točka) of the function \(V\), a point at which all its partial derivatives are zero.

Proof

By theorem 8.52, part 1, \(\vek q_0\) is an equilibrium exactly when \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q_0) = 0\) for all \(s\). By (8.38), \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = -\partial V/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}\).

Source: GR (1.21) (PDF p. 41) and EP p. 180 (for an elastic body it says that \(\delta\Pi = \delta(U + V_{\mathrm{E}}) = 0\) is the principle of minimum total potential energy, EP (4.3.5)). The relation to theorem 8.52 is ours.

The corollary is the same condition that we got for an elastic body in theorem 4.91 (there equilibrium is the smallest value of \(\Pi\)) and for a beam in lemma 5.70 (the smallest value of the functional is a stationary point). A stationary point is a more general condition than a smallest value: a peak or a saddle also has zero partial derivatives. So (8.47) requires only that equilibrium is possible, not that the system also keeps it.

Test on the examples. Pendulum A: the height of the mass below the hinge is \(-l\cos\theta\), so \(V = -m g\,l\cos\theta\) and \(V' = m g\,l\sin\theta\), equal to \(-Q_\theta\) above, with the zeros \(\theta = 0\) and \(\pi\). Bar C: the potential is the function from (8.35), its gradient equals \(-(Q_{\mathrm{y}}, Q_\varphi)\) (checked with SymPy), and the zero of the gradient is the solution we found, \(y = -6{,}13125\) mm, \(\varphi = 1{,}22625\cdot10^{-3}\) rad. Mechanism D: the zeros of \(V'(\theta)\) from (8.39) are the positions of figure 8.15, at \(P = 100\) N they are \(28{,}95^\circ\), \(2{,}15^\circ\) and \(-30{,}94^\circ\).

8.5.6 Constraint reactions (reakcije vezi)

The equations \(Q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) do not contain the constraint reactions, because the constraints are ideal and their forces do no work at admissible displacements. Sometimes we need a reaction: the force in a hinge for sizing, the force of the guide on a slider. Theorem 8.52, part 2, tells us how to get it. For conservative forces the condition (8.37) is simple.

Corollary 8.59 Reactions from Lagrange multipliers (reakcije iz Lagrangeevih množiteljev)

Let the conditions of theorem 8.52 be satisfied, and let the external forces have a potential \(V(\vek x)\) in the coordinates \(\vek x\), which still contain the constraints \(\vek f(\vek x) = \vek 0\) (definition 8.53 with \(\vek q = \vek x\), \(n = N\)). A configuration \(\vek x_0\) with \(\vek f(\vek x_0) = \vek 0\) is an equilibrium exactly when there is exactly one column \(\vek\lambda\) of \(m\) numbers such that

\[\frac{\partial V}{\partial\vek x}(\vek x_0) = \sum_{\mathrm{r}=1}^{m} \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\frac{\partial f_{\mathrm{r}}}{\partial\vek x}(\vek x_0) . \tag{8.48}\]

The load of the constraint forces \(r\) is then \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\partial f_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\vek x\).

Proof

By definition 8.53, \(\vek g = -\partial V/\partial\vek x\), and (8.48) is (8.37) from theorem 8.52, part 2, written by components, since \(\mat A^{\mathsf T}\vek\lambda = \sum_{\mathrm{r}} \lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\,\partial f_{\mathrm{r}}/\partial\vek x\) (the rows of the matrix \(\mat A\) are the gradients of \(f_{\mathrm{r}}\)).

Source: GR (1.79)-(1.82) (PDF p. 63). The use of theorem 8.52 is ours. At a local extremum of \(V\) on the set \(\vek f = \vek 0\) theorem 8.36 gives the same equation.

Equation (8.48) is the same condition as in theorem 8.36 on Lagrange multipliers in section 8.3. There we proved it for a local extremum of \(V\) on the set \(\vek f = \vek 0\), and here for an equilibrium, which is a stationary point of \(V\) on this set (corollary 8.58 in the coordinates \(\vek q\)). The multiplier has a physical meaning: it is the intensity of the reaction. There are \(N + m\) unknowns (the columns \(\vek x\) and \(\vek\lambda\)) and just as many equations: \(N\) equations (8.48) and \(m\) constraint equations, as in GR (1.82), where the unknowns are generalized coordinates and the numbers \(\lambda_{\mathrm{r}}\).

Example A in Cartesian coordinates. Instead of the angle we take the coordinates \((x, y)\) of the mass and the constraint \(f = x^2 + y^2 - l^2 = 0\) (the end of the bar is on a circle), so \(N = 2\), \(m = 1\). The potential of the weight is \(V = m g\,y\) and \(\mat A = (2x,\ 2y)\) (it has one pivot, except at \(x = y = 0\), where there is no admissible position). The condition (8.48) is \((0,\ m g) = \lambda\,(2x,\ 2y)\). The first component gives \(x = 0\) or \(\lambda = 0\), the second \(\lambda = m g/(2y) \neq 0\), so \(x = 0\) and by the constraint \(y = \pm l\). At \(y = -l\) (the mass below the hinge) we have \(\lambda = -m g/(2l) = -4{,}905\) N/m and the load of the constraint \(\lambda\,\mat A^{\mathsf T} = (0,\ 2\lambda y) = (0,\ 9{,}81)\) N upward: the bar opposes the weight of the mass, pulling it upward with \(m g = 9{,}81\) N. At \(y = +l\) (above the hinge) we have \(\lambda = +4{,}905\) N/m and the load is again \((0,\ 9{,}81)\) N upward, but at the top the bar does not pull the mass, it pushes it upward. In both positions the mass is indeed in equilibrium, but the constraint reaction differs: the bar is stretched below the hinge and compressed above it, both times with \(9{,}81\) N.

Example D: force of the guide on the slider. On the guide \(y_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\), and the guide pushes the slider only vertically, because it is smooth (section 8.1). To get this force, we do not build the constraint \(y_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\) into the coordinates but keep it as an equation. We take two coordinates: the angle \(\theta\) of the bar \(AB\) above the horizontal and the angle \(\psi\) of the bar \(BC\) below the horizontal, both in \((-\pi/2, \pi/2)\) (at \(30^\circ\) the bars are far from the vertical). The hinge \(A\) and the hinge \(B\) are already built into them, they are generalized coordinates of the system without the guide. So \(\vek B = a\,(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\) and \(\vek C = \vek B + a\,(\cos\psi, -\sin\psi)\), hence \(y_{\mathrm{C}} = a\,(\sin\theta - \sin\psi)\). The constraint is \(f(\theta, \psi) = a\,(\sin\theta - \sin\psi) = 0\), that is, \(y_{\mathrm{C}} = 0\), and the matrix \(\mat A = (a\cos\theta,\ -a\cos\psi)\) has one pivot, because both cosines are positive on this interval (lemma 8.10, items 3 and 4). The potential is

\[V(\theta, \psi) = \tfrac12 k\,\big(a\cos\theta + a\cos\psi - 2a\cos\theta_0\big)^2 + P\,a\sin\theta ,\]

and \(x_{\mathrm{C}} - 2a\cos\theta_0 = a\cos\theta + a\cos\psi - 2a\cos\theta_0 =: \Delta\) is the elongation of the spring (item 2 with \(\vek r_1 = \vek 0\)). We write the force of the guide as a vertical force \(N_{\mathrm{C}}\) (positive upward) on the slider, an unknown external force on the system without the guide. Its generalized force in the coordinates \((\theta, \psi)\) is \(N_{\mathrm{C}}\,\partial y_{\mathrm{C}}/\partial(\theta, \psi) = N_{\mathrm{C}}\,\partial f/\partial(\theta, \psi)\), since \(y_{\mathrm{C}} = f\). Part 1 of theorem 8.52, applied to the system without the guide (two degrees of freedom, the hinges are ideal and bilateral), demands \(-\partial V/\partial\theta + N_{\mathrm{C}}\,\partial f/\partial\theta = 0\) and \(-\partial V/\partial\psi + N_{\mathrm{C}}\,\partial f/\partial\psi = 0\). This is the condition (8.48) with \(\lambda = N_{\mathrm{C}}\), and the constraint gives the third equation:

\[\begin{aligned} -k\,\Delta\,a\sin\theta + P\,a\cos\theta &= N_{\mathrm{C}}\,a\cos\theta , \\ -k\,\Delta\,a\sin\psi &= -N_{\mathrm{C}}\,a\cos\psi , \\ a\,(\sin\theta - \sin\psi) &= 0 . \end{aligned}\]

The third equation gives \(\sin\theta = \sin\psi\). Since the cosines are positive, \(\cos^2 = 1 - \sin^2\) (lemma 8.10, item 1) gives \(\cos\theta = \cos\psi\). By the addition formula (corollary 8.15 with \(\beta = -\psi\) and the oddness of sine and evenness of cosine) \(\cos(\theta - \psi) = \cos\theta\cos\psi + \sin\theta\sin\psi = \cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1\). The difference \(|\theta - \psi|\) is smaller than \(\pi\), and cosine is strictly decreasing on \([0, \pi]\) with \(\cos 0 = 1\) (lemma 8.10, items 2 and 3), so \(\theta - \psi = 0\). The first and second equations then give \(N_{\mathrm{C}}\cos\theta = -k\,\Delta\sin\theta + P\cos\theta\) and \(N_{\mathrm{C}}\cos\theta = k\,\Delta\sin\theta\). Subtracting, \(2k\,\Delta\sin\theta = P\cos\theta\), so \(P = 2k\,\Delta\tan\theta = 4k\,a\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\tan\theta\), which is the same relation as before, and \(N_{\mathrm{C}} = k\,\Delta\tan\theta = P/2\). At \(P = 100\) N we solve the system of three equations with SymPy: \(\theta = \psi = 0{,}50523\) rad (\(28{,}95^\circ\)) and \(N_{\mathrm{C}} = 50{,}000\) N. At \(P = 300\) N we get \(\theta = 26{,}32^\circ\) and \(N_{\mathrm{C}} = 150{,}0\) N. The guide pushes the slider upward with \(50\) N at \(P = 100\) N.

The condition has the form of corollary 8.59, where \(\lambda\) is the intensity of the reaction, and here it is the force of the guide. We check with the statics of chapter 2: the moment of all forces on the mechanism about \(A\) is zero. The load \(P\) is at the horizontal distance \(a\cos\theta\) from \(A\) and the force of the guide at the distance \(2a\cos\theta\), so \(P\,a\cos\theta = N_{\mathrm{C}}\cdot 2a\cos\theta\) and \(N_{\mathrm{C}} = P/2 = 50\) N (the spring force and the force of the hinge \(A\) have lines of action through \(A\) and have no moment about it). The two computations give the same number, although they used different ideas.
