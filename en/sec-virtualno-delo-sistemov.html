Chapter 8 Analytical dynamics (analitična dinamika)

8.1 Virtual work for systems of rigid bodies (virtualno delo za sisteme togih teles)

Theorem 4.73 gives the equilibrium of one rigid body from the work of forces on its rigid virtual displacements. The two-bar toggle mechanism, however, has two bars, a pin, a hinge and a slider. For a given position of the mechanism, can we compute the load that holds it in equilibrium without computing the forces in the pin, the hinge and the guide? In this section we answer: we can, if the constraints are ideal. First we set up the mechanism, then we say which displacements of the system the constraints allow and which constraints do no work on such displacements, finally we prove the theorem and test it.

8.1.1 The toggle mechanism (kolenasti mehanizem)

Mechanism D (figure 8.1) lies in a vertical plane. The axis \(x\) points horizontally to the right, the axis \(y\) vertically up. Bar \(AB\) is fixed by a pin (zatič) at the point \(A\), which is the origin, and bar \(BC\) is joined to bar \(AB\) by a hinge (zgib) at the point \(B\). The end \(C\) of bar \(BC\) is a slider (drsnik) on a horizontal guide (vodilo) through \(A\). Between \(A\) and \(C\) there is a spring. A load \(P\) hangs at the hinge \(B\). This is our model, not an example from a source. Let us list its assumptions and say for each what a real machine would change.

  1. The bars are rigid (definition 1.11), each of length \(a = 0{,}5\) m. We neglect their weight. For steel bars of mass 2 kg each the total weight of both would be \(39{,}2\) N, about 7 % of the load \(552{,}54\) N that we will compute. The weight would act at the centroids of the bars and would join the load as another force with its own virtual work. Later in this chapter we will give the bars a mass.
  2. The pin \(A\) is firmly fixed, the hinge \(B\) and the slider \(C\) have no friction, the guide is smooth. A real pin has friction, which would carry part of the load.
  3. The spring is linear with stiffness \(k = 10\) kN/m. When it is stretched, it pulls its two ends toward itself with the force \(k\,(\text{length} - \text{length without tension})\). It is without tension at \(\theta_0 = 30° = 0{,}5236\) rad, when its length is \(2a\cos\theta_0 = 0{,}8660\) m.
  4. The load \(P\) acts at the hinge \(B\) vertically downward and is constant, the mechanism is at rest. In the computation of virtual work we attribute it to bar \(AB\). The two bars move equally at \(B\), so the work is the same.
  5. The angle \(\theta\) between bar \(AB\) and the horizontal we measure like the rotation \(\delta\varphi\) of a rigid body: as the ratio of the length of a circular arc to the radius, so as a dimensionless number. Why this choice is the right one is told by the section that follows this one. Until then we take the values of \(\cos\theta\) and \(\sin\theta\) from school.

The question is: how large must the load \(P\) be at a given position \(\theta\) for the mechanism to be at rest? With the method of chapter 4 we would cut each bar out of the system and write its equilibrium. Bar \(AB\) has three equilibrium equations, bar \(BC\) three, and the slider is its end \(C\). The unknowns are the pin force at \(A\) with components \(A_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(A_{\mathrm{y}}\), the hinge force at \(B\) with components \(B_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(B_{\mathrm{y}}\), the guide force \(G\) and the load \(P\). That is six unknowns for six equations, and the solution would be a single number, \(P\). Virtual work will give the same number from one equation in which only \(P\) appears. For this we need two things: what a displacement of the system that respects the constraints means, and what the constraints do to the work of their forces.

8.1.2 Constraints and virtual displacements of a system (vezi in virtualni pomiki sistema)

In definition 4.70, chapter 4 said that a virtual displacement respects the constraints (vezi), that is, the conditions that restrict the displacements of points. Now we write these conditions for a system of bodies. The system consists of \(K\) rigid bodies. Each body \(k\) has its own small rigid displacement \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{k}}(P) = \delta\vek a_{\mathrm{k}} + \delta\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\times\overrightarrow{O_{\mathrm{k}}P}\) with respect to a chosen point \(O_{\mathrm{k}}\) of the body (lemma 4.72). In the plane each body has three numbers: the two components of the vector \(\delta\vek a_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\delta\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\), since the vector \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) points along the axis \(z\). The constraints are conditions that these numbers must satisfy. As in section 4.4.2 we respect them up to terms of first order, so these conditions are linear equations in the virtual quantities. Let us list three constraints that we will use.

Definition 8.1 Admissible virtual displacement of a system (dopusten virtualni pomik sistema)

Let a system of rigid bodies \(1, \dots, K\) be in a given position. A virtual displacement of a system (virtualni pomik sistema) is a choice of a small rigid displacement \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{k}}\) for each body, with vectors \(\delta\vek a_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\) (lemma 4.72). It is admissible (dopusten) if it satisfies the conditions of all constraints of the system up to terms of first order:

  1. Hinge of two bodies or pin of a body on the ground (zgib, zatič): the point \(P\) belongs to bodies \(k\) and \(l\). Then \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{k}}(P) = \delta\vek u_{\mathrm{l}}(P)\). If the body \(l\) is the ground, \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{l}}(P) = \vek 0\).
  2. Smooth guide (gladko vodilo): the point \(P\) of body \(k\) stays on a given line or curve. Then \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{k}}(P)\) is along the tangent of the guide.
  3. Rolling without slipping (kotaljenje brez spodrsavanja): the body touches a ground that is at rest, and the point \(Q\) of the body that touches the ground in the given position does not move along the ground. Then \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{k}}(Q) = \vek 0\).

Source: GR §1.2.2 (PDF p. 36-37) for constraints between particles, CK §2.4 (PDF p. 52-58) for the concept of a virtual displacement. The notation for a system of rigid bodies is not from the sources.

A constraint not only restricts displacements, it also acts with a force. A pin holds the point \(A\) at rest, so it acts on it with a force that we do not know in advance. The forces that constraints exert on bodies we call constraint forces (sile vezi). All other forces on bodies, that is the load, the weight and the spring force, we call applied forces (uporabljene sile). We count the spring force among the applied ones because it is determined by the stretch of the spring, which we know for every position.

Why did constraint forces not appear for the lever in chapter 4? The pin acted at a point that did not move. This is not a peculiarity of the lever. Let us see what the three constraints of the definition do to the sum of the work of the forces they exert (figure 8.2).

Hinge. The hinge acts on bar 1 with a force \(\vek F_1\) and on bar 2 with a force \(\vek F_2\). The hinge has no mass, so the law of action and reaction holds (section 2.1): \(\vek F_2 = -\vek F_1\). By item 1 of the definition the virtual displacement of the point \(P\) is the same in both bars, \(\delta\vek u\). The work of the two forces is \(\vek F_1\cdot\delta\vek u + \vek F_2\cdot\delta\vek u = \vek F_1\cdot\delta\vek u - \vek F_1\cdot\delta\vek u = 0\), whatever the force \(\vek F_1\) is. For a pin on the ground \(\delta\vek u = \vek 0\), so the work of the pin force is zero.

Guide. Smooth means that the guide does not act with a force along itself. The guide force \(\vek N\) is therefore perpendicular to the guide, while the virtual displacement is along the guide by item 2, and \(\vek N\cdot\delta\vek u = 0\). In mechanism D the guide is horizontal, the guide force on the slider is \((0, G)\), the displacement of the slider is \((\delta x_{\mathrm{C}}, 0)\), and \(0\cdot\delta x_{\mathrm{C}} + G\cdot 0 = 0\).

Rolling. The force of the ground on the wheel, normal and along the ground, acts at the contact point \(Q\). We draw it as one force \(\vek F_{\mathrm{Q}}\) that ends at \(Q\) from below. By item 3 this point does not move, so the work of the force is zero.

Friction spoils this. If the slider slides on the guide with friction, the guide acts on the slider with an additional force along the guide. In mechanism D a friction force of 10 N on \(C\), directed toward \(+x\) because the slider slides toward \(A\), would on the admissible displacement \(\delta x_{\mathrm{C}} = -0{,}2955\,\delta\theta\) m (computed in the example below) do the work \(10\cdot(-0{,}2955)\,\delta\theta = -2{,}955\,\delta\theta\) N m. This is not zero, so with friction the work of the constraint does not drop out, as it does in the three previous cases.

Definition 8.2 Ideal constraint (idealna vez)

A constraint of a system of rigid bodies is ideal (idealna) if the sum of the virtual works of the forces that the constraint exerts on the bodies is zero for every admissible virtual displacement of the system (definition 8.1).

Source: GR §1.2.1 (PDF p. 35-36) for the rigid constraint between two particles, where the forces cancel because the points have the same displacement in the direction of the constraint. The definition for a system of rigid bodies and the examples of hinge, guide and rolling are not from the sources.

By the reasoning above, the hinge, the pin on the ground, the smooth guide and rolling without slipping are ideal constraints, and friction on the guide is not. In this chapter the constraints will be ideal, except where we say otherwise.

8.1.3 The principle of virtual displacements for a system (načelo virtualnih pomikov za sistem)

We say that a system of rigid bodies is in equilibrium when each body is in equilibrium under all forces on it, applied and constraint forces (definition 2.3).

Theorem 8.3 Principle of virtual displacements for a system of rigid bodies (načelo virtualnih pomikov za sistem togih teles)

Let a system of rigid bodies with ideal constraints be in equilibrium. Then the virtual work of the applied forces is zero for every admissible virtual displacement of the system.

Proof

We must show that the constraint forces cancel in the sum of the work. We write the sum of the work of all forces on all bodies in two ways.

  1. Body \(k\) is in equilibrium under all forces on it, applied and constraint forces. By theorem 4.73 the virtual work of all forces on body \(k\) is zero for all \(\delta\vek a_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(\delta\boldsymbol\varphi_{\mathrm{k}}\). We take them from an admissible virtual displacement of the system.
  2. We add the virtual works over all bodies \(k = 1, \dots, K\). The sum is zero because each term is zero.
  3. The sum is the virtual work of all forces on the system. We split it into the virtual work of the applied forces and the virtual work of the constraint forces, because each force is either an applied force or a constraint force.
  4. By definition 8.2 the virtual work of the constraint forces is zero for every admissible virtual displacement.
  5. From steps 2 and 4 it follows that the virtual work of the applied forces is zero.

Source: GR §1.2.1 (PDF p. 35-36) for a system of particles, EP §4.2.1 (p. 167). The proof is not from the sources.

The proof showed where the advantage comes from. The equilibrium equations of the individual bodies contain the constraint forces. On an admissible displacement their work drops out, and only the work of the forces we know remains. The converse, that zero virtual work implies equilibrium, we do not prove in this section. It requires constraint forces that balance each body, and we will obtain them later in this chapter, when we prove the theorem that tells when a condition between numbers determines one by the other. We state and prove the converse even later, when we describe the position of the system with numbers we choose ourselves. Until then we check the result by virtual work with a computation of equilibrium joint by joint.

8.1.4 Example: the toggle mechanism (zgled: kolenasti mehanizem)

Now let us compute mechanism D of section 8.1.1 at \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad. First we find the admissible virtual displacements. Bar \(AB\) has three numbers, \(\delta\vek a_1\) (two components) and \(\delta\varphi_1\), and bar \(BC\) has \(\delta\vek a_2\) and \(\delta\varphi_2\). Together that is six numbers. The constraints give five conditions: the pin \(A\) two, the hinge \(B\) two, the guide at \(C\) one. One free number remains, so a single number determines the position of the mechanism. We choose \(\delta\theta := \delta\varphi_1\), since the angle \(\theta\) changes by the rotation of bar \(AB\). The point \(A\) is the origin and the chosen point \(O_1\) of bar \(AB\).

  1. The pin \(A\) is at rest (item 1 of definition 8.1), so \(\delta\vek a_1 = \vek 0\) and \(\delta\vek u_1(P) = \delta\theta\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times\overrightarrow{AP}\).
  2. The hinge \(B\) is at \(\vek r_{\mathrm{B}} = (a\cos\theta, a\sin\theta, 0)\). By formula (2.32), \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times\vek r_{\mathrm{B}} = (-a\sin\theta, a\cos\theta, 0)\), so \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} = a\,\delta\theta\,(-\sin\theta, \cos\theta, 0)\) and it is perpendicular to \(AB\). At \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad, \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} = \delta\theta\,(-0{,}1478, 0{,}4777, 0)\) m.
  3. For bar \(BC\) we take the point \(B\) as the chosen point \(O_2\). The hinge \(B\) (item 1) requires \(\delta\vek a_2 = \delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}}\). The end \(C\) is from \(B\) by \(\overrightarrow{BC} = (a\cos\theta, -a\sin\theta, 0)\), and \(\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times\overrightarrow{BC} = (a\sin\theta, a\cos\theta, 0)\). Therefore \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} + \delta\varphi_2\,(a\sin\theta, a\cos\theta, 0) = \big(a\sin\theta\,(\delta\varphi_2 - \delta\theta),\ a\cos\theta\,(\delta\theta + \delta\varphi_2),\ 0\big)\).
  4. The guide is horizontal (item 2), so the component \(y\) of \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) is zero. Since \(\cos\theta \ne 0\), it follows that \(\delta\varphi_2 = -\delta\theta\). Bar \(BC\) turns opposite to bar \(AB\).
  5. We insert \(\delta\varphi_2 = -\delta\theta\) and obtain \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = (-2a\sin\theta\,\delta\theta, 0, 0)\). At \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad, \(\delta x_{\mathrm{C}} = -0{,}2955\,\delta\theta\) m. When \(B\) rises, \(C\) moves toward \(A\), as figure 8.1 shows.

The guide condition from step 4 really rejects wrong displacements. Take at \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad \(\delta\varphi_2 = 0\) instead of \(-\delta\theta\), so bar \(BC\) does not turn but only moves along with \(B\). The displacement \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}}\) is not admissible unless it lies along the guide, that is if it has a component \(y\) that is not zero. The pin \(A\) and the hinge \(B\) are satisfied as before, since steps 1 and 3 are the same, only the guide fails. By step 3, \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} = \delta\theta\,(-0{,}1478, 0{,}4777, 0)\) m, which is the same \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}}\) as in step 2. Its component \(y\) is \(a\cos\theta\,\delta\theta = 0{,}4777\,\delta\theta\) m, different from zero for every \(\delta\theta \ne 0\), so the slider would leave the guide (figure 8.3). The component \(y\) from step 3 is \(a\cos\theta\,(\delta\theta + \delta\varphi_2)\) and is zero only at \(\delta\varphi_2 = -\delta\theta\). So there is exactly one admissible \(\delta\varphi_2\) for a given \(\delta\theta\).

Now let us compute the virtual work of the applied forces. These are the load \((0, -P, 0)\) on bar \(AB\) at \(B\) and the spring force on \(C\). At \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad the spring is stretched, because \(\cos\theta = 0{,}9553\) is larger than \(\cos\theta_0 = 0{,}8660\). Its length is \(2a\cos\theta = 0{,}9553\) m against \(0{,}8660\) m without tension, a stretch of \(0{,}0893\) m. The spring force is \(F_{\mathrm{s}} = k\cdot 0{,}0893\ \text{m} = 893{,}1\) N and it pulls \(C\) toward \(A\), in the direction \(-x\). The other end of the spring is at \(A\), which does not move, so the spring does no work there. We do not know the forces in the pin, the hinge and the guide, and we do not need them.

  1. Load: \((0, -P, 0)\cdot\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} = -P\,a\cos\theta\,\delta\theta\). At \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad this is \(-0{,}4777\,P\,\delta\theta\) m.
  2. Spring: \((-F_{\mathrm{s}}, 0, 0)\cdot\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = F_{\mathrm{s}}\cdot 2a\sin\theta\,\delta\theta = 893{,}1\cdot 0{,}2955\,\delta\theta = 263{,}9\,\delta\theta\) N m.
  3. By theorem 8.3 the sum is zero for every \(\delta\theta\), so \(-0{,}4777\,P + 263{,}9 = 0\). The solution is \(P = 552{,}54\) N (computed with more digits).

In symbols: \(F_{\mathrm{s}} = k\,(2a\cos\theta - 2a\cos\theta_0)\), so \(-P\,a\cos\theta + 2k a\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\cdot 2a\sin\theta = 0\), and after dividing by \(a\cos\theta\),

\[P = 4 k a\,(\cos\theta - \cos\theta_0)\tan\theta ,\]

where \(\tan\theta = \sin\theta/\cos\theta\). For our data, \(4\cdot 10\,000\cdot 0{,}5\cdot 0{,}08931\cdot 0{,}30934 = 552{,}54\) N. Why such a result? The point \(B\) rises by \(0{,}4777\,\delta\theta\), while the point \(C\) moves by only \(0{,}2955\,\delta\theta\), in the ratio \(2\tan\theta = 0{,}6187\). The works of the load and the spring cancel if \(0{,}4777\,P = 0{,}2955\,F_{\mathrm{s}}\), that is \(P = 0{,}6187\,F_{\mathrm{s}}\). The mechanism is a lever that transmits the spring force to the load with the ratio \(2\tan\theta\), which changes with the position.

We check the same result with equilibrium joint by joint (figure 8.4), as in chapter 4. Here we hang the load on the hinge \(B\), which is a point without mass. The bars have no weight and on each only forces at the ends act, so by lemma 4.5 the forces point along the bar. Both bars are compressed. Let \(N_1\) and \(N_2\) be the magnitudes of the compressive forces in bars \(AB\) and \(BC\). Hinge \(B\): the load \((0, -P)\), bar \(AB\) pushes the hinge along \((\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\) with the force \(N_1\), bar \(BC\) along \((-\cos\theta, \sin\theta)\) with the force \(N_2\). The component \(x\) is \(N_1\cos\theta - N_2\cos\theta = 0\), so \(N_1 = N_2 = N\). The component \(y\) is \(2N\sin\theta = P\), so \(N = P/(2\sin\theta)\). Slider \(C\): bar \(BC\) pushes it along \((\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)\) with the force \(N\), the spring pulls it toward \(A\) with the force \(F_{\mathrm{s}}\), the guide acts vertically with the force \(G\). The component \(x\) is \(N\cos\theta = F_{\mathrm{s}}\), so \(P\cos\theta/(2\sin\theta) = F_{\mathrm{s}}\) and \(P = 2F_{\mathrm{s}}\tan\theta = 2\cdot 893{,}1\cdot 0{,}30934 = 552{,}54\) N. This is the same number as by virtual work. The component \(y\) at \(C\) gives in addition \(G = N\sin\theta = P/2 = 276{,}3\) N, and the pin force on bar \(AB\) is \((N\cos\theta, N\sin\theta) = (893{,}1, 276{,}3)\) N, because the pin pushes the compressed bar toward \(B\).

The computation joint by joint required the equilibrium of the hinge and the slider and the forces in the bars. Virtual work gave \(P\) from one equation. To see that the theorem really works, we check it on the numbers. On bar \(AB\) act the pin force \((893{,}1, 276{,}3)\) N at \(A\), the load \((0, -552{,}54)\) N at \(B\) and the hinge force, which by the equilibrium of the bar is \((-893{,}1, 276{,}3)\) N. The work of the pin force is zero, because \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{A}} = \vek 0\). The work of the load is \(-263{,}9\,\delta\theta\), and the work of the hinge force is \((-893{,}1)(-0{,}1478) + 276{,}3\cdot 0{,}4777 = +263{,}9\), the sum is zero, as theorem 4.73 requires for one bar. On bar \(BC\) acts the hinge force \((893{,}1, -276{,}3)\) N at \(B\), opposite to the hinge force on bar \(AB\), with the work \(-263{,}9\,\delta\theta\). At \(C\) act the spring force with the work \(+263{,}9\,\delta\theta\) and the guide force \((0, 276{,}3)\) N with the work \(0\), because \(\delta x_{\mathrm{C}}\) is horizontal. The sum for bar \(BC\) is zero too. The work of the constraint forces, that is the pin force (zero), the two hinge forces (\(+263{,}9\) and \(-263{,}9\)) and the guide force (zero), sums to zero, as the definition of an ideal constraint requires. The work of the forces we know, the load \(-263{,}9\) and the spring \(+263{,}9\), is zero exactly at \(P = 552{,}54\) N.

The guide force can also be obtained by virtual work, without writing the equilibrium of the hinge. We remove the guide in thought and keep its force \((0, G)\) as an applied force on \(C\). The system then has two free numbers and a displacement is admissible in which bar \(AB\) does not move, \(\delta\theta = 0\), and bar \(BC\) rotates about \(B\) by \(\delta\varphi_2\). By step 3, \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{B}} = \vek 0\) and \(\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = \delta\varphi_2\,(a\sin\theta, a\cos\theta, 0)\). The load does no work because \(B\) does not move. The work of the spring is \((-F_{\mathrm{s}}, 0, 0)\cdot\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = -F_{\mathrm{s}}\,a\sin\theta\,\delta\varphi_2\), and the work of the guide force is \((0, G, 0)\cdot\delta\vek u_{\mathrm{C}} = G\,a\cos\theta\,\delta\varphi_2\). The sum is zero for every \(\delta\varphi_2\), so \(G = F_{\mathrm{s}}\tan\theta = 893{,}1\cdot 0{,}30934 = 276{,}3\) N. This is the same force as by the joints. The idea of removing a constraint and keeping its force as a known one is the seed of the unit virtual displacement method of chapter 4. That equilibrium really follows from zero virtual work also in such a computation we prove later in this chapter.

8.1.5 Example: a bar on two springs (zgled: palica na dveh vzmeteh)

One more example, with a single rigid body, as a test of theorem 4.73, on which the system rests. A uniform bar of length \(L = 2\) m and mass \(m = 30\) kg lies horizontally on two vertical springs below it, the left with stiffness \(k_1 = 20\) kN/m and the right with \(k_2 = 30\) kN/m. The horizontal motion of the bar we exclude with a smooth guide, which is an idealization: for a real bar the ends would shift a little sideways when it tilts. The axis \(y\) points up. The springs are without tension when the bar is in the position \(y = 0\), \(\varphi = 0\). The displacement of the centroid \(G\) is \(y\), the rotation of the bar is \(\varphi\) (counterclockwise, as a ratio of arc to radius), and the virtual displacements are \(\delta y\) and \(\delta\varphi\). The model is ours, not from a source.

By lemma 4.72 the ends of the bar, which are \(\mp L/2\) horizontally from the centroid, move vertically by \(y_1 = y - \tfrac{L}{2}\varphi\) and \(y_2 = y + \tfrac{L}{2}\varphi\). For the vector from the centroid to the left end \((-L/2, 0, 0)\) we have \(\varphi\,\vek e_{\mathrm{z}}\times(-L/2, 0, 0) = (0, -\tfrac{L}{2}\varphi, 0)\), and for the right end the opposite. May we neglect that the ends also move horizontally? At \(\varphi = 1{,}2\cdot 10^{-3}\) rad (computed below) the distance between the ends changes by the lemma at most by \(\tfrac12 L\varphi^2 = 1{,}5\cdot 10^{-6}\) m, which is 0,03 % of the vertical displacements of the ends, which are of the order of 5 mm. The springs remain vertical. The applied forces are the weight \((0, -m g)\) at \(G\) with \(m g = 294{,}3\) N and the spring forces upward \(-k_1 y_1\) and \(-k_2 y_2\) at the ends. The other end of each spring is on the ground and is at rest. The guide forces are horizontal and do no work. The virtual work is

\[-m g\,\delta y - k_1 y_1\,\Big(\delta y - \tfrac{L}{2}\delta\varphi\Big) - k_2 y_2\,\Big(\delta y + \tfrac{L}{2}\delta\varphi\Big) = 0\]

for all \(\delta y\) and \(\delta\varphi\). Since they are independent, each of their coefficients must be zero. At \(\delta y\) we get \(m g + k_1 y_1 + k_2 y_2 = 0\), at \(\delta\varphi\) we get \(\tfrac{L}{2}(k_1 y_1 - k_2 y_2) = 0\). So \(k_1 y_1 = k_2 y_2 = -m g/2 = -147{,}15\) N and \(y_1 = -7{,}3575\) mm, \(y_2 = -4{,}905\) mm. The centroid sinks by \(\tfrac12(7{,}3575 + 4{,}905)\) mm \(= 6{,}1313\) mm and the bar turns by \((y_2 - y_1)/L = 1{,}2263\cdot 10^{-3}\) rad. This is also the result of the equilibrium of the bar by definition 2.3: the sum of the forces in the direction \(y\) is \(k_1 y_1 + k_2 y_2 + m g = 0\), and the sum of the moments about the centroid is \(\tfrac{L}{2}(k_1 y_1 - k_2 y_2) = 0\). These two equations are the same as above, which is expected for one body. The softer spring is the left one, and since the forces in both springs are equal, the left one is compressed more: \(7{,}36\) mm against \(4{,}91\) mm.

The section showed the following. For a system of rigid bodies with ideal constraints, the virtual work of the applied forces in equilibrium is zero for every admissible virtual displacement, and the constraint forces do not appear in this equation. We proved it by adding the principle for one body over the bodies and eliminating the constraint forces with the definition of an ideal constraint. For mechanism D we obtained the load \(552{,}54\) N at \(\theta = 0{,}3\) rad from one equation, and the computation joint by joint gave the same number. For the bar on springs we obtained the sinkings of the ends from two equations. For mechanism D one number \(\delta\theta\) described the admissible virtual displacements, and in \(\delta x_{\mathrm{C}} = -2a\sin\theta\,\delta\theta\) we recognized the derivative of the position \(x_{\mathrm{C}} = 2a\cos\theta\) with respect to \(\theta\), multiplied by \(\delta\theta\). That the derivative of \(\cos\theta\) is really \(-\sin\theta\) we have not proved so far, since we used both as in school. We do that in the next section.
