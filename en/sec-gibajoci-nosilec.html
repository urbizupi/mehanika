Chapter 26 Belts as axially moving continua

26.7 The moving beam

The string of sections 26.1 to 26.5 has no bending stiffness. A real band or saw blade has a thickness and bends only if we bend it, like a ruler. This section asks three questions. How does the equation of the moving string change if the band has bending stiffness? How large is the change for the steel band of example A, that is, is the string a good model of the band? And what happens to the critical speed of section 26.4? The last question leads to a kinship: the critical speed of the beam is the critical force of the column of chapter 8, converted into a speed.

26.7.1 Model and equation

We draw the band as before (figure 26.21, top). It is held in two supports, fixed in space at \(x = 0\) and \(x = L\), and slides through them with speed \(v\) towards increasing \(x\), drawn as the velocity arrow. The shape of the band is the graph of the deflection \(w(x, t)\) over the \(x\) axis, with the deflection \(w\) upwards, strongly enlarged for clarity. The particles of the band that lie between \(a + vt\) and \(b + vt\) at time \(t\) we call the piece: it is the same material all the time, moving together along \(x\) with speed \(v\) (section 26.1, lemma 26.1). Below we draw the piece in a separate picture and enter the forces that act on it.

We draw the forces on the piece in the bottom picture one by one. The piece is curved along the band like the band. At each end its neighbour pulls it with the tension force \(T\) along the tangent: the right part in the direction of increasing \(x\), the left part in the opposite direction. Each pull starts at the end of the piece on which it acts. The tension along the tangent gives the vertical component \(T\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\). Because the band has bending stiffness, the neighbour does not act only along the tangent: the part to the right of \(b + vt\) also acts on the piece with the vertical shear force \(V(b + vt, t)\) upwards and the part to the left of \(a + vt\) with \(-V(a + vt, t)\), drawn as the force \(V(a + vt, t)\) downwards, by the law of action and reaction, as for the beam in chapter 13 (lemma 13.29). Finally the right part also acts with a bending moment \(M(b + vt, t)\) counterclockwise and the left part with \(-M(a + vt, t)\), drawn as \(M(a + vt, t)\) clockwise.

A band with bending stiffness is thus a moving string with one more force. We write the model with all idealizations named.

Definition 26.35 Axially moving beam (gibajoči nosilec)

An axially moving beam is a band of length \(L\) between two supports fixed in space at \(x = 0\) and \(x = L\), which moves through them with constant transport speed \(v > 0\) in the direction of increasing \(x\). Its deflection \(w(x, t)\) is a real function of two variables with continuous partial derivatives up to the fourth order. The idealizations of the moving string of definition 26.3 hold (constant mass \(\mu\) per unit length, constant tension \(T\), \(|w_{\mathrm{x}}| \le 0{,}01\), the transport speed \(v\) the same for all particles), except the idealization of zero bending stiffness, which is replaced by two new ones, (i) and (ii):

(i) The band has constant bending stiffness \(EI\) and in every cross-section the moment \(M = EI\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), as the beam in chapter 13 (theorem 13.28). (ii) The rotary inertia of the cross-sections, that is the kinetic energy of the rotation of the cross-sections owing to the turn \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\), is neglected, as for that beam.

The supports are either simple (členkasta), where \(w = 0\) and \(M = EI\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\), or fixed (vpeta), where \(w = 0\) and \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), at both ends and at every time.

Source: Model: WM1990 §2.2 (PDF p. 2), which extends the moving string by bending stiffness, and Mote 1965 (cited there, not in the books). The naming of the idealizations and the bounds are ours.

The idealization (ii) has a measure. We estimate it for the steel band of example A (band \(0{,}9 \times 50\) mm, \(L = 1\) m, stress \(100\) MPa, \(T = 4500\) N, \(\mu = 0{,}3510\) kg/m, the dimensions and speeds are our choice, we have no data of real belts). A cross-section turns by the angle \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) when the band bends. The cross-section moves with the band, so its angular velocity is the time derivative of \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\) following a particle that moves with speed \(v\), that is \(w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) (chain rule as in lemma 26.1), and the transverse velocity of a particle is \(w_{\mathrm{t}} + v\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\). The kinetic energy of rotation per unit length is \(\tfrac12\,\rho I\,(w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v\,w_{\mathrm{xx}})^2\), and the kinetic energy of the transverse motion is \(\tfrac12\,\rho A\,(w_{\mathrm{t}} + v\,w_{\mathrm{x}})^2\). The angular velocity is the derivative of the transverse velocity with respect to \(x\), so for a mode with wave number \(k\) it is of the order \(k\) times the transverse velocity, and the ratio is of the order \((I/A)\,k^2\) with \(I/A = h^2/12\) for a rectangle of thickness \(h\). For \(h = 0{,}9\) mm this is \(6{,}75\cdot 10^{-8}\ \text{m}^2 \cdot 9{,}87\ \text{m}^{-2} = 6{,}7\cdot 10^{-7}\) for the first mode (\(k = \pi/L\)). The estimate is an order of magnitude, not a calculation, and it suffices for accepting the idealization.

Now we derive the equation. The way is that of section 26.1: the momentum of a piece made of the same particles, then the vanishing of the integrand. The band is the same as for the string, and the shear force joins the forces at the ends of the piece.

Theorem 26.36 Equation of motion of the axially moving beam (gibalna enačba gibajočega nosilca)

Let \(w\) be the deflection of an axially moving beam by definition 26.35 without external load, and let the law of momentum hold for every piece of particles: the derivative of the momentum of a piece is the sum of the transverse forces that the two neighbouring parts exert on it. Then for every \(x \in [0, L]\) and every \(t\)

\[\mu\,\big(w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v^2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\big) = T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} - EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} , \tag{26.45}\]

that is

\[\mu\,\big(w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\big) + \big(\mu v^2 - T\big)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} + EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} = 0 . \tag{26.46}\]
Proof

We must show that the piece has the momentum \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b \mu\,g_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) with \(g(s, t) = w(s + vt, t)\), that its derivative equals the difference of the forces at the ends of the piece, and that the equation at a point follows from the integral over the piece.

  1. A particle that is at \(x = s + vt\) at time \(t\) has the deflection \(g(s, t) = w(s + vt, t)\). So for every \(t\) the piece has the particles \(s \in [a, b]\), the variable \(s\) is their label and the limits are constant. By lemma 26.1 the velocity of the particle is \(g_{\mathrm{t}} = w_{\mathrm{t}} + v\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\) and the acceleration \(g_{\mathrm{tt}} = w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v^2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), evaluated at \((s + vt, t)\) (chain rule, lemma 8.29).
  2. The momentum of the piece is \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b \mu\,g_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}s\). The limits are constant and \(g_{\mathrm{tt}}\) is continuous, so its derivative in time is \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b \mu\,g_{\mathrm{tt}}\,\mathrm{d}s\) by lemma 4.19.
  3. The transverse force with which the right part acts on the piece at \(x = b + vt\) is the sum of the component of the tension \(T\,w_{\mathrm{x}}\) (derivation 13.1, the tangent has slope \(w_{\mathrm{x}}\)) and of the shear force \(V = -EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxx}}\) (balance of moments of the piece, lemma 13.29, item 1, step 2, with rotary inertia neglected by idealization (ii)). The left part acts at \(x = a + vt\) with the opposite value (law of action and reaction). The force is \(F = T\,w_{\mathrm{x}} - EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxx}}\), so the sum of the forces on the piece is \(F(b + vt, t) - F(a + vt, t)\).
  4. The law of momentum from steps 2 and 3 is \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b \mu\,g_{\mathrm{tt}}\,\mathrm{d}s = F(b + vt, t) - F(a + vt, t)\). By the fundamental theorem (theorem 3.40, item 1, antiderivative \(F\) in \(x\)) the right-hand side is \(\int_{\mathrm{a}+\mathrm{vt}}^{b+vt} F_{\mathrm{x}}\,\mathrm{d}x\), and by the shift \(x = s + vt\) it is \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b F_{\mathrm{x}}(s + vt, t)\,\mathrm{d}s\) with \(F_{\mathrm{x}} = T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} - EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}}\).
  5. From steps 1 and 4, \(\int_{\mathrm{a}}^b r(s + vt, t)\,\mathrm{d}s = 0\) with \(r = \mu\,(w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v^2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}) - T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} + EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}}\), a continuous function. At time \(t\) every interval \([x_1, x_2] \subset [0, L]\) is a piece with \(a = x_1 - vt\) and \(b = x_2 - vt\). So \(\int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x_2} r(x, t)\,\mathrm{d}x = 0\) for every such interval.
  6. Let \(x_1 \in [0, L)\). By step 5 the function \(x \mapsto \int_{\mathrm{x}_1}^{x} r\,\mathrm{d}s\) is identically zero, by the fundamental theorem (item 2) its derivative is \(r(x, t)\), so \(r(x, t) = 0\). For \(x = L\) we take the intervals \([x_1, L]\) and continuity. Thus equation (26.45) is proved, and equation (26.46) follows by rearranging the terms.

Source: WM1990 (5) (PDF p. 2): \(\rho(U_{\mathrm{TT}} + 2VU_{\mathrm{XT}} + V^2 U_{\mathrm{XX}}) - P U_{\mathrm{XX}} + EI\,U_{\mathrm{XXXX}} = F\), the same equation with \(F = 0\) (\(\rho \to \mu\), \(P \to T\)). The source cites Mote 1965 and does not derive it. The proof with a piece of particles is not from the source, the way is as for theorem 26.4.

We test the equation on two limiting cases. For \(EI = 0\) what remains is \(\mu\,(w_{\mathrm{tt}} + 2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}} + v^2 w_{\mathrm{xx}}) = T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), the equation of the moving string (theorem 26.4). For \(v = 0\) what remains is \(\mu\,w_{\mathrm{tt}} + EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} - T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\), a beam with an axial force. For the band of example A the sine is still a solution: \(w = \sin(\pi x/L)\cos\omega t\) satisfies the equation exactly when \(\mu\,\omega^2 = T\,(\pi/L)^2 + EI\,(\pi/L)^4\), because \(w_{\mathrm{tt}} = -\omega^2 w\), \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -(\pi/L)^2 w\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} = (\pi/L)^4 w\). The first term gives \(T\,(\pi/L)^2 = 44\,413\) N/m², the second \(EI\,(\pi/L)^4 = 58{,}0\) N/m², so \(f_1 = 56{,}651\) Hz. The string has \(f_1 = c/(2L) = 56{,}614\) Hz: the bending stiffness raises the frequency by \(0{,}065\) %, which is half the ratio of the second and the first term, \(\tfrac12\cdot 0{,}1306\ \% = 0{,}0653\ \%\).

For \(v \ne 0\) the sine is no longer a solution. The term \(2v\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\) contains \(-2v\,(\pi/L)\,\omega\cos(\pi x/L)\sin\omega t\), and the cosine occurs in no other term. This is the same reason as for the string (section 26.3): the oscillation is no longer standing. We therefore find the natural oscillations by the Galerkin method with the sines of section 26.5. With \(w = \sum_{\mathrm{j}} q_{\mathrm{j}}(t)\,\sin(j\pi x/L)\) and \(k_{\mathrm{j}} = j\pi/L\) we get \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat G\,\dot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek 0\) with

\[M_{\mathrm{jj}} = \tfrac12\,\mu L , \qquad K_{\mathrm{jj}} = \tfrac12\,L\,\big(EI\,k_{\mathrm{j}}^4 - (\mu v^2 - T)\,k_{\mathrm{j}}^2\big) , \qquad G_{\mathrm{jk}} = \frac{4\mu v\,j\,k}{j^2 - k^2}\ \ (j + k\ \text{odd}) , \tag{26.47}\]

all other entries are zero. The last value follows from \(G_{\mathrm{jk}} = 2\mu v\,k_{\mathrm{k}} \int_0^L \sin k_{\mathrm{j}} x\,\cos k_{\mathrm{k}} x\,\mathrm{d}x\) and \(\sin a\cos b = \tfrac12(\sin(a + b) + \sin(a - b))\), where \(\int_0^L \sin(m\pi x/L)\,\mathrm{d}x = 2L/(m\pi)\) for odd \(m\) and \(0\) for even \(m\). The matrix \(\mat G\) is skew (\(G_{\mathrm{kj}} = -G_{\mathrm{jk}}\)). For example A and \(N = 24\) sines we get the first frequency \(f_1\) as follows (the string by the equation \(f_1 = (c/2L)(1 - v^2/c^2)\)):

\(v\) (m/s) 0 20 40 80
\(f_1\) of the beam (Hz) 56,651 54,891 49,613 28,504
\(f_1\) of the string (Hz) 56,614 54,847 49,548 28,352
difference 0,065 % 0,080 % 0,130 % 0,535 %

The difference grows with the speed. The reason is that the motion reduces the tension that is left for bending: \(T - \mu v^2\) is \(2254\) N out of \(4500\) N at \(80\) m/s, while the bending part \(EI\,k_1^2 = 5{,}9\) N stays the same and so has a larger share. How much exactly is told only by the calculation in the table. At \(v = 80\) m/s the difference is still below \(0{,}6\) %. In this range the band is therefore a string and the model of sections 26.1 to 26.5 suffices. When does the bending stiffness overcome the tension? The term \(EI\,k_{\mathrm{j}}^4\) exceeds \(T\,k_{\mathrm{j}}^2\) when \(j^2 > T L^2/(\pi^2 EI) = 766\), that is from \(j = 28\) on. The wavelength is then \(2L/28 = 71\) mm, which is \(79\) times the thickness of the band.

The critical speed remains. There the bending stiffness changes not only the numbers but the content of the theorem.

26.7.2 Critical speed of the beam

Let us see what equation (26.46) says if the band rests in shape and only the material moves. Let \(w\) not depend on time. The terms \(w_{\mathrm{tt}}\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xt}}\) drop out and what remains is

\[EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + \big(\mu v^2 - T\big)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0 . \tag{26.48}\]

This is the equation of the column of chapter 8, \(EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + P\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\), with the force

\[P = \mu v^2 - T . \tag{26.49}\]

The reason is Newton's law for a particle. A particle that moves along a curve with curvature \(w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) at speed \(v\) has the acceleration \(v^2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) (the term \(v^2 w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) of lemma 26.1), so the band must act on it with the force \(\mu v^2\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\) per unit length. The tension provides \(T\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\). For bending there remains \((T - \mu v^2)\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\), as if the tension were only \(T - \mu v^2\). If this is negative, the band behaves like a column compressed by the force \(\mu v^2 - T\): the acceleration of the particles along the curve eats up the tension and finally exceeds it.

Figure 26.22 shows this for the band of example A. The left graph is the force \(P(v) = \mu v^2 - T\) from \(-T = -4500\) N at \(v = 0\) to \(1900\) N at \(v = 135\) m/s: a parabola that passes from tension (\(P < 0\)) to compression (\(P > 0\)) at the speed of the string \(c = 113{,}23\) m/s. The critical forces of the column are \(\pi^2 EI/L^2 = 5{,}876\) N and \(4\pi^2 EI/L^2 = 23{,}503\) N, which at the scale of \(4500\) N is less than the thickness of a line. So we draw a magnification of the neighbourhood of the point \(P = 0\) and get the right graph: on it the two critical forces are horizontal lines that the parabola crosses at \(113{,}30\) m/s and \(113{,}52\) m/s, which are \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) for simple and for fixed supports.

We name the speed at which this happens and estimate it. For the string we called critical in section 26.4 the speed at which the behaviour changes. For the beam the same holds.

Theorem 26.37 Critical speed of the axially moving beam (kritična hitrost gibajočega nosilca)

Let the axially moving beam be as in definition 26.35 with simple supports (case a) or with fixed supports (case b). Let \(P = \mu v^2 - T\), \(P_{\mathrm{cr}} = \pi^2 EI/L^2\) in case a and \(P_{\mathrm{cr}} = 4\pi^2 EI/L^2\) in case b, and

\[v_{\mathrm{c}} = \sqrt{\frac{T + P_{\mathrm{cr}}}{\mu}} . \tag{26.50}\]

For \(\mathcal Q(\eta) = \int_0^L \big(EI\,\eta''^2 - P\,\eta'^2\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\) it holds:

  1. For every solution \(w\) of equation (26.46), \(\mathcal E^* = \tfrac12\int_0^L \mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x + \tfrac12\,\mathcal Q(w(\cdot, t))\) is constant in time.
  2. For \(v < v_{\mathrm{c}}\), \(\mathcal Q(\eta) > 0\) for every twice continuously differentiable \(\eta \ne 0\) with the support conditions, equation (26.48) with the same conditions has only the solution \(w = 0\), and with \(\theta = 1 - \max(P, 0)/P_{\mathrm{cr}} > 0\) we have \(\theta\,EI\int_0^L w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x \le 2\,\mathcal E^*\) at every time.
  3. For \(v = v_{\mathrm{c}}\), equation (26.48) has a nonzero solution: \(w = \sin(\pi x/L)\) in case a and \(w = 1 - \cos(2\pi x/L)\) in case b, and \(\mathcal Q(w) = 0\) for it.
Proof

We must show the conservation of the functional in time, the positivity of the form \(\mathcal Q\) below the limit by the theorems on the column, and the solution at the limit. Below, \(w\) has continuous derivatives up to the fourth order and satisfies the support conditions at every time.

  1. We multiply equation (26.46) by \(w_{\mathrm{t}}\) and integrate over \([0, L]\). By lemma 4.19, \(\int \mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}} w_{\mathrm{tt}}\,\mathrm{d}x = \tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\tfrac12\int\mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  2. The term with \(2v\) is \(2\mu v\int w_{\mathrm{t}}\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\,\mathrm{d}x = \mu v\,\big[w_{\mathrm{t}}^2\big]_0^L = 0\), because \(w = 0\) at both supports at every time, so also \(w_{\mathrm{t}} = 0\). The term of the gyroscopic force thus does no work, which is a property of the force (lemma 26.26).
  3. By integration by parts (corollary 3.41), \(\int P\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}x = \big[P\,w_{\mathrm{x}} w_{\mathrm{t}}\big]_0^L - P\int w_{\mathrm{x}}\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\,\mathrm{d}x = -\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\tfrac12 P\int w_{\mathrm{x}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\), because \(w_{\mathrm{t}} = 0\) at the supports.
  4. Twice by parts: \(\int EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}}\,w_{\mathrm{t}}\,\mathrm{d}x = EI\big[w_{\mathrm{xxx}}\,w_{\mathrm{t}} - w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{xt}}\big]_0^L + EI\int w_{\mathrm{xx}}\,w_{\mathrm{xxt}}\,\mathrm{d}x\). The first term is zero (\(w_{\mathrm{t}} = 0\)). The second is zero for simple supports (\(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\)) and for fixed supports (\(w_{\mathrm{xt}} = 0\), because \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) at every time). What remains is \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\tfrac12\,EI\int w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  5. The sum of steps 1 to 4 is \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm{d}t}\,\big(\tfrac12\int\mu\,w_{\mathrm{t}}^2 + \tfrac12\int (EI\,w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 - P\,w_{\mathrm{x}}^2)\big) = 0\). This is item 1.
  6. For \(P \le 0\), \(\mathcal Q(\eta) \ge EI\int\eta''^2\), for \(P_{\mathrm{cr}} > P > 0\) by theorem 8.134 (case a) or 8.135 (case b), step 1 of their proofs, \(\mathcal Q(\eta) \ge EI\,(1 - P/P_{\mathrm{cr}})\int\eta''^2\). So \(\mathcal Q(\eta) \ge \theta\,EI\int\eta''^2\). If \(\int\eta''^2 = 0\), then \(\eta''= 0\), \(\eta\) is linear and because of \(\eta(0) = \eta(L) = 0\) equal to zero. Therefore \(\mathcal Q > 0\) for \(\eta \ne 0\). The condition \(P < P_{\mathrm{cr}}\) is \(\mu v^2 < T + P_{\mathrm{cr}}\), that is \(v < v_{\mathrm{c}}\).
  7. The static solution: we multiply equation (26.48) by \(w\) and integrate by parts twice. The terms at the ends are zero, because \(w = 0\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\) or \(w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\), what remains is \(\mathcal Q(w) = 0\), as in the proof of theorem 8.134. For \(v < v_{\mathrm{c}}\) step 6 gives \(w = 0\). Item 2 follows also from step 5: \(\theta\,EI\int w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2 \le \mathcal Q(w) \le 2\,\mathcal E^*\).
  8. At \(v = v_{\mathrm{c}}\) we have \(P = P_{\mathrm{cr}}\). For \(w = \sin(\pi x/L)\), \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = -(\pi/L)^2 w\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} = (\pi/L)^4 w\), so \(EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + P_{\mathrm{cr}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = \big(EI\,(\pi/L)^4 - (\pi^2 EI/L^2)(\pi/L)^2\big)\,w = 0\). The conditions of simple supports hold (\(w = 0\), \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = 0\) at \(0\) and \(L\)). For \(w = 1 - \cos(2\pi x/L)\), \(w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} = -(2\pi/L)^4\cos(2\pi x/L)\) and \(w_{\mathrm{xx}} = (2\pi/L)^2\cos(2\pi x/L)\), so \(EI\,w_{\mathrm{xxxx}} + P_{\mathrm{cr}}\,w_{\mathrm{xx}} = (2\pi/L)^2\cos(2\pi x/L)\,\big(-EI\,(2\pi/L)^2 + 4\pi^2 EI/L^2\big) = 0\), and \(w = w_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) at \(0\) and \(L\). By step 7, therefore, \(\mathcal Q(w) = 0\).

Source: WM1990 (13) (PDF p. 2): \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = \sqrt{\mu^2 + \pi^2}\) for simple and \(\sqrt{\mu^2 + 4\pi^2}\) for fixed supports, in dimensionless notation \(\mu^2 = P L^2/EI\) from WM1990 (6) (our \(T L^2/EI\)). Appendix A (PDF pp. 6 and 7), (A1) and (A2): the static equation and the positive definiteness of \(\langle u, Ku\rangle\). For fixed supports the source gives the static solution only as \(v^2 - \mu^2 = (2n\pi)^2\). In chapter 8 it is shown for fixed ends that the next zero of the determinant is \(kL = 8{,}987\) (\(\tan(kL/2) = kL/2\)): \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) (the first zero) does not change. The functional \(\mathcal E^*\) for the beam is not from the source, the pattern is CHEN2005 (33) (PDF p. 7) for the string.

What we have shown. For \(v < v_{\mathrm{c}}\) the form \(\mathcal Q\) is positive and the functional \(\mathcal E^*\) bounds \(\int w_{\mathrm{xx}}^2\), so the shape of the band cannot grow in time beyond the bound set by the initial state. At \(v = v_{\mathrm{c}}\) there exists an equilibrium shape that we may enlarge by an arbitrary factor: the band can stay bent at unchanged tension and speed. The name critical speed has the same reason as the critical force of the column: below it the straight position is energetically stable (definition 8.64), at it the question is decided whether a bent shape stays in equilibrium at all. For \(EI \to 0\), \(P_{\mathrm{cr}} \to 0\) and \(v_{\mathrm{c}} \to \sqrt{T/\mu} = c\): the critical speed of the string of section 26.4 is the limit of the case without bending stiffness.

Now a number. For the band of example A the ratio \(P_{\mathrm{cr}}/T\) is small: \(5{,}876/4500 = 1/766 = 0{,}001306\) in case a. We denote it by \(\varepsilon\), so that \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = c\,\sqrt{1 + \varepsilon}\). Since \((1 + \varepsilon/2)^2 = 1 + \varepsilon + \varepsilon^2/4\), the difference is \(1 + \varepsilon/2 - \sqrt{1 + \varepsilon} = (\varepsilon^2/4)/\big(1 + \varepsilon/2 + \sqrt{1 + \varepsilon}\big)\) (the difference of the squares divided by the sum). The denominator is larger than \(2\), so the difference is smaller than \(\varepsilon^2/8 = 2{,}1\cdot 10^{-7}\) and \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = c\,(1 + \varepsilon/2)\) holds with a relative error below \(2{,}1\cdot 10^{-7}\). Therefore \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = 113{,}23\,(1 + 0{,}000653) = 113{,}30\) m/s, which is \(0{,}0653\) % above \(c\). For fixed supports \(\varepsilon = 23{,}503/4500 = 0{,}005223\) and \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = 113{,}52\) m/s, \(0{,}2608\) % above \(c\). The difference between \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) and \(c\) is \(0{,}07\) m/s out of \(113\) m/s for this band, so the critical speed of the string is a good approximation. It is different for a band without tension: for \(T = 0\), \(v_{\mathrm{c}} = (\pi/L)\sqrt{EI/\mu} = 4{,}09\) m/s (simple supports) or \(8{,}18\) m/s (fixed). A string without tension has \(c = 0\), so critical speed zero, while the beam has it at a few metres per second. There the bending stiffness is not a correction but decides.

26.7.3 Test: a window of stability above the critical speed

Theorem 26.37 is one-sided: below \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) the band is stable. We try to reverse it. For the column of chapter 8 the opposite holds: above \(P_{\mathrm{cr}}\) the straight position is unstable. So it would be natural for the band to be unstable for every \(v > v_{\mathrm{c}}\). We show that this is not true, and why. The theorem is thereby tested on an example that it does not survive.

We use the dimensionless notation of WM1990: \(L = 1\), \(EI = 1\), \(\mu = 1\) and \(\bar T = T L^2/EI\) (in the source \(\mu^2\), but \(\mu\) is the mass per length for us, so we do not use it) and \(\bar v = v\sqrt{\mu L^2/EI}\) (in the source \(\nu\), which is Poisson's ratio in the book). Let \(\bar T = 100\) and simple supports. By equation (26.47), \(K_{\mathrm{jj}} = \tfrac12\,\big((j\pi)^4 + (100 - \bar v^2)\,(j\pi)^2\big)\), which is negative exactly for \(\bar v^2 > 100 + j^2\pi^2\). The limits are \(\bar v_1 = 10{,}4819\), \(\bar v_2 = 11{,}8101\) and \(\bar v_3 = 13{,}7414\) (SymPy and NumPy, num-07.out). The first is \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) of theorem 26.37.

Figure 26.23 shows what happens to the eigenvalues of the system \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat G\,\dot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek 0\) with \(N = 24\) sines. On the axes are the speed \(\bar v\) from \(10\) to \(13\) and the largest real part of the eigenvalues \(\lambda\). Zero means that all eigenvalues are purely imaginary and the solutions are bounded oscillations, a positive real part means growth like \(e^{\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda\, t}\). The curve is zero up to \(10{,}48\), then rises to \(4{,}77\) at \(11{,}25\) and falls back to zero at \(11{,}81\). Between \(11{,}81\) and \(12{,}18\) it is zero again: all eigenvalues are purely imaginary again. Above \(12{,}177\) the curve rises again and equals \(10{,}3\) at \(13\). The vertical lines mark \(\bar v_1\) and \(\bar v_2\) and the computed end of the window at \(12{,}177\).

The window between \(\bar v_2\) and \(12{,}18\) has an explanation that we prove for the system we use. We look at the first part, above \(\bar v_1\).

Lemma 26.38 Instability for a negative determinant of the stiffness (nestabilnost pri negativni determinanti togosti)

Let \(\mat M\) be a symmetric positive definite \(N \times N\) matrix, \(\mat G\) skew and \(\mat K\) symmetric, all with constant real entries. If \(\det\mat K < 0\), the system \(\mat M\,\ddot{\vek q} + \mat G\,\dot{\vek q} + \mat K\,\vek q = \vek 0\) has a solution whose size grows like \(e^{\sigma t}\) with \(\sigma > 0\).

Proof

We must show that the polynomial \(p(\lambda) = \det(\mat M\lambda^2 + \mat G\lambda + \mat K)\) has a root with nonzero real part, and that this root gives a growing solution.

  1. The matrix \(\mat M\lambda^2 - \mat G\lambda + \mat K\) is the transpose of the matrix \(\mat M\lambda^2 + \mat G\lambda + \mat K\) (\(\mat G^\T = -\mat G\)), and the determinant does not change under transposition. So \(p(-\lambda) = p(\lambda)\) and \(p(\lambda) = q(\lambda^2)\) with a polynomial \(q\) of degree \(N\) with leading coefficient \(\det\mat M > 0\) and \(q(0) = p(0) = \det\mat K\).
  2. We write \(q(s) = \det\mat M\,\prod_{\mathrm{k}=1}^{N}(s - s_{\mathrm{k}})\) with complex roots \(s_{\mathrm{k}}\). Then \(\det\mat K = q(0) = \det\mat M\,\prod_{\mathrm{k}}(-s_{\mathrm{k}})\).
  3. If all \(s_{\mathrm{k}}\) were real and \(\le 0\), every \(-s_{\mathrm{k}} \ge 0\) and \(\det\mat K \ge 0\). Since \(\det\mat K < 0\), there is an \(s_{\mathrm{k}}\) that is not real and \(\le 0\).
  4. Let \(\lambda\) be a square root of such an \(s_{\mathrm{k}}\). If \(\lambda\) were purely imaginary, \(\lambda = \mathrm{i}\omega\), then \(s_{\mathrm{k}} = \lambda^2 = -\omega^2 \le 0\) would be real. This is a contradiction, so \(\lambda\) has a nonzero real part. Since \(-\lambda\) is also a root, we may take \(\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda = \sigma > 0\).
  5. For \(\lambda\) there is \(\vek\varphi \ne \vek 0\) with \((\mat M\lambda^2 + \mat G\lambda + \mat K)\,\vek\varphi = \vek 0\), so \(\vek z(t) = \mathrm{e}^{\lambda t}\vek\varphi\) is a solution, with \(|\vek z(t)| = \mathrm{e}^{\sigma t}|\vek\varphi|\). Its real and imaginary parts are real solutions that together give \(|\vek z|^2 = |\mathrm{Re}\,\vek z|^2 + |\mathrm{Im}\,\vek z|^2\), so at least one grows like \(\mathrm{e}^{\sigma t}\).

Source: The proof is not from the source. The theorem on the eigenvalues of a gyroscopic system with positive definite stiffness (theorem 26.27) is the converse direction, GR §2.11 (PDF pp. 137 to 141).

The lemma says: if \(\mat K\) has an odd number of negative eigenvalues, the system is unstable even if the Coriolis term \(\mat G\) is not zero. Our \(\mat K\) is diagonal, so \(\det\mat K = \prod K_{\mathrm{jj}}\) and the sign is decided by the number of negative entries. Between \(\bar v_1\) and \(\bar v_2\) only \(K_{11}\) is negative (SymPy), so by the lemma the system is unstable for every \(N \ge 1\). This agrees with the figure: at \(\bar v = 10{,}6\) the largest real part is \(2{,}489\), at \(11\) it is \(4{,}508\) and at \(11{,}5\) it is \(4{,}324\) (num-07.out). Above \(\bar v_2\) both \(K_{11}\) and \(K_{22}\) are negative, the determinant is positive again and the lemma says nothing: it proves neither instability nor stability.

The stability in the window is computed, not proved, and we understand it with two sines. Let \(N = 2\) and \(\mat M = m\,\mat I\), \(\mat K = \mathrm{diag}(k_1, k_2)\) with \(k_1, k_2 < 0\), \(\mat G = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & g \\ -g & 0\end{pmatrix}\) (by equation (26.47), \(g = G_{12} = -8\bar v/3\)). The determinant of the lemma is

\[p(\lambda) = (m\lambda^2 + k_1)(m\lambda^2 + k_2) + g^2\lambda^2 = m^2\lambda^4 + \big(m\,(k_1 + k_2) + g^2\big)\lambda^2 + k_1 k_2 .\]

Purely imaginary roots require both roots \(\lambda^2\) to be real and negative. We denote \(b = m(k_1 + k_2) + g^2\). Since \(k_1 k_2 > 0\), the roots have the same sign, their sum is \(-b/m^2\), so both are negative and real exactly when \(b > 0\) and the discriminant \(b^2 - 4m^2 k_1 k_2 \ge 0\), that is \(b \ge 2m\sqrt{k_1 k_2}\). With \(|k_{\mathrm{i}}| = -k_{\mathrm{i}}\) this is \(g^2 - m\,(|k_1| + |k_2|) \ge 2m\sqrt{|k_1|\,|k_2|}\), which is the same as \(g^2 \ge m\,\big(\sqrt{|k_1|} + \sqrt{|k_2|}\big)^2\). At \(\bar v = 12\), \(g = -32\), \(g^2 = 1024\), \(k_1 = -168{,}43\), \(k_2 = -89{,}25\) and \(m = 1/2\), so \(m(\sqrt{|k_1|} + \sqrt{|k_2|})^2 = 251{,}4\). The condition is met with a large margin. The term \(\mat G\,\dot{\vek q}\) does no work and cannot add energy, but it couples two modes that would each grow alone into an oscillation. We call it gyroscopic stabilization (giroskopska stabilizacija), because \(\mat G\,\dot{\vek q}\) has the form of a gyroscopic force (section 26.5).

Two sines do not explain the end of the window. For \(N = 2\) the limit of stability moves to \(13{,}85\), for \(N = 4\) it is \(12{,}181\) and for \(N = 24\) it is \(12{,}177\) (num-07.out): modes 3 and 4, which \(G_{\mathrm{jk}}\) couples with modes 1 and 2 (the sum \(j + k\) is odd), move the end of the window. WM1990 gives the window \(11{,}81 < \bar v < 12{,}18\) (PDF p. 6) and \(\bar v_{\mathrm{c}} = 10{,}48\) (figure 4, PDF p. 5), which agrees with our calculation.

What we have got. Theorem 26.37 is correct and says only that below \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) the band is stable, not that above it it is unstable. Above \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) the band is unstable at least as long as \(\mat K\) has an odd number of negative eigenvalues (lemma 26.38), then the Coriolis term decides. For the column of chapter 8 there is no Coriolis force and theorem 8.119 requires \(\mat G = \mat 0\): here it is not applicable. For the band of example A (\(\bar T = 7559\)) we did not compute windows, the numbers are for \(\bar T = 100\) as in the source. Above \(v_{\mathrm{c}}\) the contribution of the nonlinear terms, which the model does not have, grows with the speed (WM1990 PDF p. 6, Mote 1966, Thurman and Mote 1969): the linear windows are therefore a property of the model, not a prediction.
