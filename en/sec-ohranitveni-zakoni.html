Chapter 8 Analytical dynamics (analitična dinamika)

8.11 Conservation laws (Ohranitveni zakoni)

For the pendulum of section 8.9 we solved the equation of motion \(\ddot\theta + \tfrac{g}{\ell}\sin\theta = 0\) numerically with RK4 (Runge-Kutta of fourth order, metoda RK4) and after every step computed the sum of the kinetic and the potential energy. In 10 s and 20 000 steps the sum changed by \(4{,}6 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J. This change is an error of the computation and not a property of the motion, since RK4 does not conserve energy exactly: with 10 000 steps it is \(6{,}5 \cdot 10^{-12}\) J, with 20 000 steps \(4{,}6 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J and with 40 000 steps \(4{,}0 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J, where it is already limited by the round-off error of the computer. The equation of motion itself conserves energy exactly. Why does the energy stay constant, and which numbers stay constant in other systems described by Lagrange's equations?

The state of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates (posplošene koordinate) at time \(t\) is the \(n\) coordinates \(\vek q\) and the \(n\) velocities \(\dot{\vek q}\). A motion is a curve \(\vek q(t)\) and at each of its points we can compute many numbers from the state. We are interested in those that do not change along any motion. If we know them, we know something about the motion without solving the equations. For the pendulum, for example, the height from which we released it gives the speed at the lowest position.

Definition 8.104 Conserved quantity (ohranjena količina)

Let \(F(t, \vek q, \dot{\vek q})\) be a continuously differentiable function that assigns a number to the state of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates at time \(t\). The function \(F\) is a conserved quantity of the system if for every motion \(\vek q(t)\) that solves the equations of motion of the system on an interval \(I\), the number \(F(t, \vek q(t), \dot{\vek q}(t))\) is the same for all \(t \in I\).

8.11.1 Energy (Energija)

For a system whose constraints do not contain time, the kinetic energy is by theorem 8.86 a quadratic form of the velocities, \(T = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\) with a symmetric mass matrix \(\mat M\). For the pendulum \(T = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2\). Differentiating with respect to the velocity gives \(m\ell^2\dot\theta\), and multiplying by the velocity \(\dot\theta\) gives \(m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 = 2T\). The factor \(\tfrac12\) in \(T\) and the factor 2 from differentiating the square cancel, and multiplying by the velocity restores the power, so \(2T\) remains and not \(T\). The same computation holds for all such systems.

Lemma 8.105 Euler's identity for the kinetic energy (Eulerjeva enakost za kinetično energijo)

Let \(T(\vek q, \dot{\vek q}) = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\), where \(\mat M(\vek q)\) is a symmetric \(n \times n\) matrix of numbers that does not depend on \(\dot{\vek q}\). Then for every state \((\vek q, \dot{\vek q})\)

\[\sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = 2\,T . \tag{8.100}\]
Proof

We must show that the derivative with respect to the velocities, multiplied by the velocities, gives twice the quadratic form.

  1. Write \(T = \tfrac12 \sum_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{ab}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\), where \(M_{\mathrm{ab}}\) does not depend on the velocities.
  2. The derivative \(\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is \(1\) for \(a = s\) and \(0\) otherwise. By the rules of differentiation (lemma 1.81, sum and product) we get \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\big(\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\big) = \delta_{\mathrm{as}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} + \dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{bs}}\), where \(\delta_{\mathrm{as}} = 1\) for \(a = s\) and \(0\) otherwise. For \(a = b = s\) this gives \(2\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\), as it must for \(\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}^2\). Hence \(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \tfrac12 \Big( \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} + \sum_{\mathrm{a}} M_{\mathrm{as}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{a}} \Big)\).
  3. The matrix is symmetric, \(M_{\mathrm{as}} = M_{\mathrm{sa}}\), so the two sums are equal and \(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\).
  4. Multiply by \(\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and sum over \(s\): \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} = \dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M\,\dot{\vek q} = 2T\). This is (8.100).

Source: GR (1.34) (PDF p. 46), which obtains it from Euler's theorem on homogeneous functions (1.35) for degree 2. The proof for a quadratic form is not from the source.

The lemma showed that for a quadratic kinetic energy there is a relation between the derivative and the value that we need for the energy law. We still have to say which forces we allow. Conservative forces with a potential \(V(\vek q)\) (definition 8.53) enter the function \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\). All other generalized forces we collect in \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\) (non-conservative forces, nekonservativne sile), as in theorem 8.90. Their power is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) by definition 8.54, and by lemma 8.55 it is the derivative of their work. Viscous damping, which opposes the velocity, is such a force. Non-conservative forces are dissipative (disipativne) if their power is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \le 0\) in every state, that is, together they never do positive work. GR (PDF p. 52) takes the narrower case of a force parallel to the velocity and opposite to it, which satisfies this condition. We call the sum \(\mathcal{E} = T + V\) the total mechanical energy (celotna mehanska energija).

Theorem 8.106 Conservation of energy of a system (ohranitev energije)

Let a system have \(n\) degrees of freedom, ideal scleronomic holonomic constraints (definition 8.42), conservative forces with a potential \(V(\vek q)\) that does not depend on time, and possibly other generalized forces \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\). Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a twice continuously differentiable motion that solves the equations of theorem 8.90. Then \(\mathcal{E}(t) = T + V\) is continuously differentiable and

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} . \tag{8.101}\]

If all \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\), then \(\mathcal{E}\) is a conserved quantity (definition 8.104). If the forces are dissipative, \(\mathcal{E}\) does not increase with time.

Proof

We must show that \(\mathcal{E}\) changes only by the power of the forces that we did not include in the potential.

  1. Scleronomic means \(\vek X = \vek X(\vek q)\) without time, so \(T = \tfrac12 \dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\) (theorem 8.86) and \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\) does not contain \(t\). The motion solves \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\) (theorem 8.90).
  2. By the chain rule (lemma 8.29, item 1) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). There is no term with \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial t\), because \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain time.
  3. By the product rule (lemma 1.81) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\).
  4. Subtract step 2 from step 3. The terms with \(\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) cancel and by step 1 we are left with \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big( \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \mathcal{L} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \Big( \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\).
  5. \(V\) does not contain the velocities, so \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = \partial T/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and by lemma 8.105 \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = 2T\). The expression in the brackets of step 4 is therefore \(2T - (T - V) = T + V = \mathcal{E}\). This is (8.101).
  6. If all \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\), then \(\mathcal{E}' = 0\) on the whole interval and by corollary 1.85 \(\mathcal{E}(t)\) is the same for all \(t\). If \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \le 0\), then for \(t_1 < t_2\) by theorem 1.84 \(\mathcal{E}(t_2) - \mathcal{E}(t_1) = \mathcal{E}'(\xi)\,(t_2 - t_1) \le 0\) for some \(\xi \in (t_1, t_2)\), so \(\mathcal{E}\) does not increase.

Source: GR (1.42) to (1.46) (PDF pp. 47 to 48, conservation of energy for scleronomic constraints and conservative forces), (1.55) (PDF p. 53, energy for dissipative forces) and (1.58) (PDF p. 54, derivative of the energy with non-conservative forces). GR derives it from Lagrange's equations along the same path, the notation with the function \(\mathcal{L}\) and steps 2 to 4 are ours.

The proof showed that \(\mathcal{E}\) changes only because of forces that do not come from a potential, and that the key is lemma 8.105: for a quadratic kinetic energy the expression \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} - \mathcal{L}\) is exactly \(T + V\). In step 2 we also see where the energy law comes from. If \(\mathcal{L}\) contained time, a term \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial t\) would appear there and in step 4 an extra term \(-\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial t\) would remain.

Example 8.107 Pendulum: speed from the energy and loss by damping (nihalo: hitrost iz energije in izguba pri dušenju)

The pendulum of example A: a point mass \(m = 1\) kg on a rigid light rod of length \(\ell = 1\) m, angle \(\theta\) from the vertical downward, \(g = 9{,}81\) m/s\(^2\). The potential is \(V = -mg\ell\cos\theta\), the kinetic energy \(T = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2\).

  1. Release it from rest at \(\theta = 90^\circ\). Then \(T = 0\) and \(V = -mg\ell\cos 90^\circ = 0\), so \(\mathcal{E} = 0\) J.
  2. At the lowest position \(\theta = 0\) we have \(V = -mg\ell = -9{,}81\) J. Since \(\mathcal{E}\) is constant, \(T = 9{,}81\) J, so \(\tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 = 9{,}81\) J and \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2g/\ell} = 4{,}4294\) 1/s. The speed of the mass is \(\ell\,|\dot\theta| = 4{,}43\) m/s.
  3. At \(\theta = 45^\circ\) we have \(T = \mathcal{E} - V = mg\ell\cos 45^\circ\), so \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2g\cos 45^\circ/\ell} = 3{,}7247\) 1/s. We obtained the speed at the intermediate position without the equation of motion.
  4. Release it from rest at \(10^\circ\) and at \(170^\circ\). Then \(\mathcal{E} = -9{,}661\) J and \(+9{,}661\) J, and the speeds at the lowest position are \(0{,}5460\) 1/s and \(6{,}2403\) 1/s. A higher starting position means more energy and therefore a larger speed at the same position.

Let us add a damping moment \(Q^{\mathrm{nc}} = -c\,\dot\theta\) with \(c = 0{,}2\) N m s at the hinge. The power is \(Q^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot\theta = -c\,\dot\theta^2 \le 0\), the force is dissipative, and by (8.101) \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{d}t = -c\,\dot\theta^2\). The numerical RK4 solution from \(90^\circ\) gives \(\mathcal{E} = -1{,}869\) J after 1 s, \(-3{,}279\) J after 2 s, \(-6{,}016\) J after 5 s and \(-8{,}348\) J after 10 s. The energy therefore decreases and approaches \(-9{,}81\) J, the value for rest at the lowest position. The loss in 10 s is \(\mathcal{E}(0) - \mathcal{E}(10) = 8{,}3477\) J, and the integral \(\int_0^{10} c\,\dot\theta^2\,\mathrm{d}t\) along the solution is also \(8{,}3477\) J, as the time integral of (8.101) requires.

Example 8.108 Toggle mechanism: the velocity-square term (kolenasti mehanizem: člen s kvadratom hitrosti)

Mechanism D (two bars, a slider, a spring and a load \(P = 100\) N at the joint) has \(T = \tfrac12 m(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2\) with \(m(\theta) = \tfrac13 + 2\sin^2\theta\) kg m\(^2\) and the potential \(V(\theta)\) from section 8.5. The equation of motion is \(m(\theta)\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2 + V'(\theta) = 0\) (section 8.9). We check the conservation of \(\mathcal{E}\) and see what happens if we omit the velocity-square term.

  1. By the product rule and the chain rule (lemma 8.29, item 3) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\tfrac12 m(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2\big) = m\,\dot\theta\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^3\), since in differentiation \(m(\theta(t))\) also changes with time. So \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \dot\theta\,\big( m\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^2 + V' \big)\) and the bracket is the equation of motion, hence zero.
  2. Release the mechanism from rest at \(\theta = 0{,}7\) rad. There \(\mathcal{E} = V = 83{,}401\) J. The equilibrium position \(0{,}50523\) rad has \(V = 24{,}609\) J and \(m = 0{,}80185\) kg m\(^2\), so \(T = 83{,}401 - 24{,}609 = 58{,}792\) J and \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2T/m} = 12{,}110\) 1/s.
  3. The other turning point, where \(\dot\theta = 0\) and again \(V(\theta) = 83{,}401\) J, is at \(0{,}14547\) rad. The unstable position at \(2{,}15^\circ = 0{,}0375\) rad lies to the left of it, so the mechanism does not reach it.
  4. RK4 after 3 s with 30 000 steps: \(\mathcal{E}\) changes by \(1{,}7 \cdot 10^{-9}\) J. If we omit the term \(\tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^2\), the computed energy changes by 25,5 J, because \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{d}t\) from step 1 is no longer zero. The velocity-square term is therefore part of the law and must not be dropped.

In the proof of theorem 8.106 we used that the constraints do not contain time. What remains if a constraint moves? For a rheonomic constraint the kinetic energy by theorem 8.86 has three parts, \(T = T_2 + T_1 + T_0\): \(T_2 = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\) is a quadratic form of the velocities, \(T_1 = \vek b\cdot\dot{\vek q}\) is linear and \(T_0\) does not contain the velocities. Lemma 8.105 holds only for \(T_2\): for \(T_1\) we have \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial T_1/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = T_1\) and for \(T_0\) the sum is zero. So the conserved quantity changes too, as the next theorem shows. Its proof repeats steps 1 to 4 of the previous one.

Theorem 8.109 Energy integral for rheonomic constraints (energijski integral pri reonomnih vezeh)

Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a twice continuously differentiable motion of a system with ideal holonomic constraints that solves the equations of theorem 8.90 with \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\). Let \(\mat M\), \(\vek b\), \(T_0\) from theorem 8.86 and \(V\) not contain the time \(t\). Then

\[\mathcal{E}^* = T_2 - T_0 + V \tag{8.102}\]

is a conserved quantity (definition 8.104).

Proof

We must show that \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} - \mathcal{L}\) is constant and equal to \(\mathcal{E}^*\).

  1. \(\mathcal{L} = T - V = T_2 + T_1 + T_0 - V\) does not contain \(t\), so by the chain rule (lemma 8.29, item 1), as in step 2 of the previous proof, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\).
  2. By the product rule (lemma 1.81), as in step 3, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\).
  3. Subtract the first from the second. By theorem 8.90 with \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\) we have \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big( \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \mathcal{L} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \Big( \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} \Big) = 0\).
  4. \(V\) does not contain the velocities, so \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial (T_2 + T_1)/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) (\(T_0\) does not contain the velocities). For \(T_2\) the sum is \(2T_2\) by lemma 8.105, for \(T_1 = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} b_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q)\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) it is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,b_{\mathrm{s}} = T_1\).
  5. So the expression in the brackets of step 3 equals \(2T_2 + T_1 - (T_2 + T_1 + T_0 - V) = T_2 - T_0 + V = \mathcal{E}^*\). By corollary 1.85 \(\mathcal{E}^*(t)\) is the same for all \(t\).

Source: Not from the source in this form: GR (1.46) (PDF p. 48) writes the energy integral for scleronomic constraints, where \(T_1 = T_0 = 0\). For rheonomic constraints GR (PDF p. 54) uses the potential \(V^* = V - T_0\) (by the text of the page), which agrees with \(\mathcal{E}^*\). The proof is ours.

This energy integral (energijski integral) differs from \(T + V\), which now changes. The difference \(\mathcal{E}^* - (T + V) = -2T_0 - T_1\) is zero when the constraints are scleronomic.

Example 8.110 Ring on a rotating rod (obroč na vrteči se palici)

A ring of mass \(m = 0{,}1\) kg slides without friction on a straight rod in a horizontal plane. The rod rotates about one of its points with constant angular velocity \(\Omega = 2\) 1/s, gravity does not act in the plane. This is our model. The coordinate is the distance \(r\) of the ring from the pivot. The position of the ring is \((r\cos\Omega t,\ r\sin\Omega t)\) and the constraint is rheonomic. The velocity of the ring has the components \((\dot r\cos\Omega t - r\Omega\sin\Omega t,\ \dot r\sin\Omega t + r\Omega\cos\Omega t)\), its square is \(\dot r^2 + r^2\Omega^2\). So \(T_2 = \tfrac12 m\,\dot r^2\), \(T_1 = 0\) and \(T_0 = \tfrac12 m\,\Omega^2 r^2\), there is no potential and \(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 + \Omega^2 r^2)\) does not contain \(t\). Lagrange's equation is \(m\,\ddot r = m\,\Omega^2 r\) (SymPy).

  1. By theorem 8.109 \(\mathcal{E}^* = T_2 - T_0 = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 - \Omega^2 r^2)\) is conserved. If we release the ring at \(r = 0{,}5\) m with \(\dot r = 0\), its value is \(-\tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot 4 \cdot 0{,}25 = -0{,}05\) J.
  2. From \(\tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 - \Omega^2 r^2) = -0{,}05\) J we get \(\dot r^2 = \Omega^2 r^2 - 2 \cdot 0{,}05/m\). At \(r = 1\) m this is \(4 - 1 = 3\) m\(^2\)/s\(^2\), so \(\dot r = 1{,}7321\) m/s.
  3. Then \(T + V = T = \tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot (3 + 4) = 0{,}35\) J, and at the start \(\tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot (0 + 4 \cdot 0{,}25) = 0{,}05\) J. The sum \(T + V\) grew by 0,30 J. The rod pushes the ring in the direction of rotation and does work in the actual displacement, because it moves itself. The constraint is nevertheless ideal: in a virtual displacement with frozen time (section 8.4) its force does no work, since it is perpendicular to the rod and the displacement is along it. So there is no generalized constraint force in Lagrange's equation, but in the energy \(T + V\) there is its work.

Finally let us see what the conservation of energy says about the motion of the pendulum. The state of the pendulum is a pair \((\theta, \dot\theta)\) in the plane and a motion is a curve in this plane. Since \(\mathcal{E}\) is constant, every motion lies on a level curve (nivojnica) of the function \(\mathcal{E}(\theta, \dot\theta) = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 - mg\ell\cos\theta\), that is, on the curve with the equation

\[\dot\theta = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2\,(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta)}{m\ell^2}} , \tag{8.103}\]

which is real only where \(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta \ge 0\). For the pendulum the motion is unique for a given state (corollary 8.94), so the level curves do not cross, except at the resting positions, where \(\dot\theta = 0\) and the motion is constant. Figure 8.33 shows the level curves for \(m = 1\) kg and \(\ell = 1\) m.

From equation (8.103) we read three regions. Since \(T \ge 0\), we have \(\mathcal{E} \ge V \ge -mg\ell\) and the value \(-mg\ell = -9{,}81\) J belongs only to rest at the lowest position (the centre of the curves in the figure). For \(-mg\ell < \mathcal{E} < mg\ell\) we have \(\cos\theta \ge -\mathcal{E}/(mg\ell)\) and this is the interval \(|\theta| \le \theta_{\max}\), where \(0 < \theta_{\max} < \pi\) and \(\cos\theta_{\max} = -\mathcal{E}/(mg\ell)\). The speed is zero at the end points and has both signs in between, so the level curve is a closed curve and the pendulum oscillates between \(-\theta_{\max}\) and \(\theta_{\max}\). For \(\mathcal{E} = 0\) we have \(\theta_{\max} = 90^\circ\), which is our release. For \(\mathcal{E} = mg\ell\) the double-angle formula \(1 + \cos\theta = 2\cos^2(\theta/2)\) gives \(\dot\theta = \pm 2\sqrt{g/\ell}\,\cos(\theta/2)\) for \(|\theta| \le \pi\), and at \(\theta = 0\) it is \(2\sqrt{g/\ell} = 6{,}2642\) 1/s. This curve ends at \((\pm\pi, 0)\), which is the state of rest of the unstable equilibrium position (section 8.6). For \(\mathcal{E} > mg\ell\) we have \(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta > 0\) for every \(\theta\), the speed is never zero and keeps its sign, and the pendulum rotates. At \(\mathcal{E} = 15\) J the value \(|\dot\theta|\) is smallest at the top position, \(\sqrt{2\,(15 - 9{,}81)} = 3{,}2218\) 1/s, and largest at the bottom, \(7{,}0441\) 1/s. The figure shows where oscillation turns into rotation: at the energy that is just enough for the pendulum to reach the top position. There the speed decreases towards zero, so the motion near this energy is slow and the oscillation period gets longer: for an amplitude of \(170^\circ\) it is \(4{,}8935\) s, 2,44 times longer than the \(2{,}0061\) s for a small amplitude (section 8.9).

8.11.2 Cyclic coordinates and generalized momentum (Ciklične koordinate in posplošena gibalna količina)

Energy is one conserved quantity, which belongs to an \(\mathcal{L}\) without time. Are there others? Take a free point of mass \(m\) in the plane \((x, y)\) with no forces in the direction of the \(x\) axis. Its equation of motion in the \(x\) direction is \(m\,\ddot x = 0\) and \(m\,\dot x\) is constant. In Lagrange's form \(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) - V(y)\), which does not contain \(x\), and the equation for \(x\) is \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot x} = 0\). The same structure holds for every coordinate that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain.

Definition 8.111 Generalized momentum (posplošena gibalna količina)

Let \(\mathcal{L}(t, \vek q, \dot{\vek q}) = T - V\) be the Lagrangian of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates. The generalized momentum belonging to the coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the function of the state

\[p_{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} , \qquad s = 1, \dots, n . \tag{8.104}\]

Since \(V\) does not contain the velocities, \(p_{\mathrm{s}} = \partial T/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). For scleronomic constraints, by step 3 of the proof of lemma 8.105, \(p_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\). A coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain (that is, \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) in every state) is cyclic (ciklična).

The unit of \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the unit of the generalized force multiplied by a second: kg m/s if \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a length and N m s if \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is an angle. For the free point from the introduction \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = m\,\dot x\), which is the momentum (gibalna količina) in the \(x\) direction. For the pendulum \(p_\theta = m\ell^2\dot\theta\), which is the angular momentum (kotna količina) of the mass about the pivot: the mass has the velocity \(\ell\dot\theta\) perpendicular to the rod, and the distance \(\ell\) multiplies \(m\,\ell\dot\theta\).

Corollary 8.112 A cyclic coordinate gives a conserved quantity (ciklična koordinata da ohranjeno količino)

Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a motion from theorem 8.90 and let \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) be a cyclic coordinate, \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\). Then \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a conserved quantity.

Proof

We must show that the time derivative of \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is zero.

  1. The equation for \(s\) from theorem 8.90 is \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\), which is \(\dot p_{\mathrm{s}}\) by (8.104).
  2. The right-hand side is zero: \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\), because the coordinate is cyclic, and \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\).
  3. So \(\dot p_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) on the whole interval and by corollary 1.85 \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is constant.

The momentum and angular momentum of a free system we obtain as cases of a cyclic coordinate. Take \(N\) points with masses \(m_{\mathrm{i}}\) and positions \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the plane, whose only forces are internal forces from a potential \(V\) that depends only on the distances \(|\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} - \vek r_{\mathrm{j}}|\). If we shift all points by \(a\) in the direction of the \(x\) axis, this is the coordinate \(q_1 = a\), and the remaining coordinates describe the positions of the points relative to this shifted picture, then the distances between the points are the same, so \(V\) does not contain \(a\). Neither does \(T\) contain \(a\), since the velocities do not change if all points are shifted by the same distance. The coordinate \(a\) is therefore cyclic and \(p_{\mathrm{a}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} m_{\mathrm{i}}\,\dot x_{\mathrm{i}}\), because each \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) contains \(a\) with derivative 1. This is the \(x\) component of the total momentum. For a rotation of all points by an angle \(\varphi\) about the origin, \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} = (x_{\mathrm{i}}, y_{\mathrm{i}})\) changes with \(\partial \vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial\varphi = (-y_{\mathrm{i}}, x_{\mathrm{i}})\), the distances and speeds do not change, so \(\varphi\) is cyclic and \(p_\varphi = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} m_{\mathrm{i}}\,(-\dot x_{\mathrm{i}}\,y_{\mathrm{i}} + \dot y_{\mathrm{i}}\,x_{\mathrm{i}})\). By (2.32) this is the \(z\) component of the vector \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek r_{\mathrm{i}} \times m_{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\vek r}_{\mathrm{i}}\), the angular momentum of the system about the origin. The conservation of momentum and angular momentum of a free system is therefore a special case of corollary 8.112: each belongs to one kind of displacement that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not detect. Energy belongs to a displacement in time. The same is shown by step 2 of the proof of theorem 8.106: \(\mathcal{E}\) is conserved because \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain \(t\).

Example 8.113 Free bar in the plane (prosta palica v ravnini)

The uniform bar of example C (\(L = 2\) m, \(m = 30\) kg, \(J_{\mathrm{G}} = mL^2/12 = 10\) kg m\(^2\)) flies freely in a vertical plane, without constraints, under its weight. This is our model. The coordinates are \(x\) and \(y\) of the centre of mass \(G\) (\(y\) axis upward) and the angle \(\varphi\) of the bar. By theorem 8.82 for planar motion \(T = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{G}}\dot\varphi^2\), the potential of the weight is \(V = mg\,y\), so

\[\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{G}}\,\dot\varphi^2 - mg\,y . \tag{8.105}\]

The coordinates \(x\) and \(\varphi\) are cyclic, \(y\) is not.

  1. The generalized momenta by (8.104): \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = m\,\dot x\), \(p_{\mathrm{y}} = m\,\dot y\), \(p_\varphi = J_{\mathrm{G}}\,\dot\varphi\). By corollary 8.112 \(p_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(p_\varphi\) are constant and \(p_{\mathrm{y}}\) is not: \(\dot p_{\mathrm{y}} = -mg\) from the equation for \(y\).
  2. We throw the bar from \(y = 0\) with velocity \((\dot x, \dot y) = (2, 5)\) m/s and angular velocity \(\dot\varphi = 3\) 1/s. Then \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = 30 \cdot 2 = 60\) kg m/s, \(p_\varphi = 10 \cdot 3 = 30\) kg m\(^2\)/s and \(p_{\mathrm{y}} = 30 \cdot 5 = 150\) kg m/s. The energy is \(\mathcal{E} = \tfrac12 \cdot 30 \cdot (4 + 25) + \tfrac12 \cdot 10 \cdot 9 + 0 = 480\) J.
  3. At the highest position \(\dot y = 0\), and \(\dot x = 2\), \(\dot\varphi = 3\) remain by step 1. Then \(\mathcal{E} = 480 = \tfrac12 \cdot 30 \cdot 4 + \tfrac12 \cdot 10 \cdot 9 + mg\,h = 105 + 294{,}3\,h\), so \(h = 1{,}2742\) m. This is the same as \(\dot y_0^2/(2g) = 25/19{,}62 = 1{,}2742\) m, the known result for a vertical throw, which we have now obtained from the energy.
  4. The bar falls back to \(y = 0\) after \(2 \dot y_0/g = 1{,}0194\) s. In this time the centre of mass moves horizontally by \(2 \cdot 1{,}0194 = 2{,}0387\) m and the bar rotates by \(3 \cdot 1{,}0194 = 3{,}0581\) rad. Figure 8.34 shows the centre of mass at equal time intervals: the horizontal distances are equal because \(p_{\mathrm{x}}\) is constant, the angle grows uniformly because \(p_\varphi\) is constant, and the height follows a parabola because \(p_{\mathrm{y}}\) is not constant.

The energy law and cyclic coordinates answer our question: in systems with time-independent constraints and conservative forces \(T + V\) is conserved, and for every coordinate that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain, one more generalized momentum is conserved. We will use the first law in section 8.12 to prove that a motion near a stable equilibrium position stays near it.
