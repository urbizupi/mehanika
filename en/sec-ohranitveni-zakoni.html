Chapter 8 Analytical dynamics (analitična dinamika)

8.11 Conservation laws (Ohranitveni zakoni)

For the pendulum of section 8.9 we solved the equation of motion \(\ddot\theta + \tfrac{g}{\ell}\sin\theta = 0\) numerically with RK4 (Runge-Kutta of fourth order, metoda RK4) and after every step computed the sum of the kinetic and the potential energy. Let us repeat the computation for a release from rest at \(90^\circ\). In 10 s and 20 000 steps the sum changes by at most \(2{,}4 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J. This change is an error of the computation and not a property of the motion, since RK4 does not conserve energy exactly: with 10 000 steps it is \(3{,}9 \cdot 10^{-12}\) J, with 20 000 steps \(2{,}4 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J and with 40 000 steps \(3{,}5 \cdot 10^{-13}\) J, where it is already limited by the round-off error of the computer. The equation of motion itself conserves energy exactly. Why does the energy stay constant, and which numbers stay constant in other systems described by Lagrange's equations?

The state of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates (posplošene koordinate) at time \(t\) is the \(n\) coordinates \(\vek q\) and the \(n\) velocities \(\dot{\vek q}\). For \(n = 1\) we draw the coordinate \(q\) horizontally and the velocity \(\dot q\) vertically, the state is one point of this plane and the motion is the curve that the point traces. At each of its points we can compute many numbers from the state. We are interested in those that do not change along any motion: the states at \(t_1\) and \(t_2\) have the same value \(F = c\), so the motion lies on a level curve. If we know them, we know something about the motion without solving the equations. For the pendulum, for example, the height from which we released it gives the speed at the lowest position. Let us first look at this in a picture, the computations follow.

We hold the mass of the pendulum so that the rod is horizontal, which is the angle \(90^\circ\), and let it go. The rod keeps it at a constant distance from the pivot \(O\), so the mass moves on a circle. When it reaches the angle \(45^\circ\) it is \(0{,}707\) m lower than at the start, and at the lowest position it is \(1\) m lower, the length of the rod. On the way down it gets faster and faster: at \(45^\circ\) it has the speed \(3{,}72\) m/s, at the bottom \(4{,}43\) m/s. Why exactly these values? The weight of the mass is \(mg = 9{,}81\) N and in a descent of \(0{,}707\) m it does the work \(9{,}81 \cdot 0{,}707 = 6{,}94\) J. The mass receives this work as kinetic energy \(T\), and the potential energy \(V\) decreases by the same amount, which the two bars at \(45^\circ\) show. At the start both are zero, at the bottom they are \(9{,}81\) J and \(-9{,}81\) J. The sum of the two bars stays on the line \(\mathcal{E} = 0\). The theorem below will prove this picture for every system without moving constraints and without friction. The right part of the figure shows what friction in the hinge does, and we return to it in example 8.122.

Definition 8.119 Conserved quantity (ohranjena količina)

Let \(F(t, \vek q, \dot{\vek q})\) be a continuously differentiable function that assigns a number to the state of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates at time \(t\). The function \(F\) is a conserved quantity of the system if for every motion \(\vek q(t)\) that solves the equations of motion of the system on an interval \(I\), the number \(F(t, \vek q(t), \dot{\vek q}(t))\) is the same for all \(t \in I\). In figure 8.173 this means that the state at every time lies on the same level curve \(F = c\).

8.11.1 Energy (Energija)

For a system whose constraints do not contain time, the kinetic energy is by theorem 8.97 a quadratic form of the velocities, \(T = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\) with a symmetric mass matrix \(\mat M\). For the pendulum \(T = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2\), in the figure the parabola, and in the chosen state it has the value \(T\). Differentiating with respect to the velocity gives \(m\ell^2\dot\theta\), which is the slope of the tangent, and multiplying by the velocity \(\dot\theta\) gives \(m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 = 2T\), which is twice the height \(T\): over the distance \(\dot\theta\) the tangent rises by \(2T\). The factor \(\tfrac12\) in \(T\) and the factor 2 from differentiating the square cancel, and multiplying by the velocity restores the power, so \(2T\) remains and not \(T\). The same computation holds for all such systems.

Lemma 8.120 Euler's identity for the kinetic energy (Eulerjeva enakost za kinetično energijo)

Let \(T(\vek q, \dot{\vek q}) = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\), where \(\mat M(\vek q)\) is a symmetric \(n \times n\) matrix of numbers that does not depend on \(\dot{\vek q}\). Then for every state \((\vek q, \dot{\vek q})\) (for \(n = 1\) the left side is the slope of the tangent times the velocity, the right side twice the height of the parabola)

\[\sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = 2\,T . \tag{8.132}\]
Proof

We must show that the derivative with respect to the velocities, multiplied by the velocities, gives twice the quadratic form.

  1. Write \(T = \tfrac12 \sum_{\mathrm{a},\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{ab}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\), where \(M_{\mathrm{ab}}\) does not depend on the velocities.
  2. The derivative \(\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is \(\delta_{\mathrm{as}}\), that is \(1\) for \(a = s\) and \(0\) otherwise. By the product rule (lemma 1.82) \(\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\big(\dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\big) = \delta_{\mathrm{as}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} + \dot q_{\mathrm{a}}\,\delta_{\mathrm{bs}}\), hence \(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \tfrac12 \Big( \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} + \sum_{\mathrm{a}} M_{\mathrm{as}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{a}} \Big)\).
  3. The matrix is symmetric, \(M_{\mathrm{as}} = M_{\mathrm{sa}}\), so the two sums are equal and \(\dfrac{\partial T}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\).
  4. Multiply by \(\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and sum over \(s\): \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}} = \dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M\,\dot{\vek q} = 2T\). This is (8.132).

Source: GR (1.34) (PDF p. 46), which obtains it from Euler's theorem on homogeneous functions (1.35) for degree 2. The proof for a quadratic form is not from the source.

The lemma showed that for a quadratic kinetic energy there is a relation between the derivative and the value that we need for the energy law. We still have to say which forces we allow. Conservative forces with a potential \(V(\vek q)\) (definition 8.58) enter the function \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\). All other generalized forces we collect in \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\) (non-conservative forces, nekonservativne sile), as in theorem 8.101. Their power is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) by definition 8.59, and by lemma 8.60 it is the derivative of their work. Viscous damping, which opposes the velocity, is such a force: for the damped pendulum the energy falls, without damping it would stay on the zero line. Non-conservative forces are dissipative (disipativne) if their power is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \le 0\) in every state, that is, together they never do positive work. GR (PDF p. 52) takes the narrower case of a force parallel to the velocity and opposite to it, which satisfies this condition. We call the sum \(\mathcal{E} = T + V\) the total mechanical energy (celotna mehanska energija).

Theorem 8.121 Conservation of energy of a system (ohranitev energije)

Let a system have \(n\) degrees of freedom, ideal scleronomic holonomic constraints (definition 8.43), conservative forces with a potential \(V(\vek q)\) that does not depend on time, and possibly other generalized forces \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\). Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a twice continuously differentiable motion that solves the equations of theorem 8.101. Then \(\mathcal{E}(t) = T + V\) is continuously differentiable and

\[\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}=1}^{n} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} . \tag{8.133}\]

If all \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\), then \(\mathcal{E}\) is a conserved quantity (definition 8.119). If the forces are dissipative, \(\mathcal{E}\) does not increase with time.

Proof

We must show that \(\mathcal{E}\) changes only by the power of the forces that we did not include in the potential.

  1. Scleronomic means \(\vek X = \vek X(\vek q)\) without time, so \(T = \tfrac12 \dot{\vek q}^\T \mat M(\vek q)\,\dot{\vek q}\) (theorem 8.97) and \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\) does not contain \(t\). For the pendulum the pivot is at rest. The motion solves \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\) (theorem 8.101).
  2. By the chain rule (lemma 8.30, item 1) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). There is no term with \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial t\), because \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain time.
  3. By the product rule (lemma 1.82) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\).
  4. Subtract step 2 from step 3. The terms with \(\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) cancel and by step 1 we are left with \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big( \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \mathcal{L} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \Big( \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\).
  5. \(V\) does not contain the velocities, so \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = \partial T/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) and by lemma 8.120 \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = 2T\). The expression in the brackets of step 4 is therefore \(2T - (T - V) = T + V = \mathcal{E}\). This is (8.133).
  6. If all \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\), then \(\mathcal{E}' = 0\) on the whole interval and by corollary 1.86 \(\mathcal{E}(t)\) is the same for all \(t\). If \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \le 0\), then for \(t_1 < t_2\) by theorem 1.85 \(\mathcal{E}(t_2) - \mathcal{E}(t_1) = \mathcal{E}'(\xi)\,(t_2 - t_1) \le 0\) for some \(\xi \in (t_1, t_2)\), so \(\mathcal{E}\) does not increase.

Source: GR (1.42) to (1.46) (PDF pp. 47 to 48, conservation of energy for scleronomic constraints and conservative forces), (1.55) (PDF p. 53, energy for dissipative forces) and (1.58) (PDF p. 54, derivative of the energy with non-conservative forces). GR derives it from Lagrange's equations along the same path, the notation with the function \(\mathcal{L}\) and steps 2 to 4 are ours.

The proof showed that \(\mathcal{E}\) changes only because of forces that do not come from a potential (for the damped pendulum this is the loss), and that the key is lemma 8.120: for a quadratic kinetic energy the expression \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} - \mathcal{L}\) is exactly \(T + V\). This expression is called the Hamiltonian (Hamiltonova funkcija) and denoted by \(H\). For scleronomic constraints and a potential without velocities \(H = T + V\), for rheonomic constraints it is not, as we will see at theorem 8.124. The theorem has three assumptions. Each enters the proof at one place, and for each we say when it holds and what it costs when it does not.

  1. The constraints are scleronomic (step 1). This holds when no support and no guide moves by a schedule given in advance: for the pendulum the pivot \(O\) is at rest. It does not hold when a motor drives a part of the system with a prescribed motion. Then \(T\) is no longer only a quadratic form of the velocities, lemma 8.120 does not give \(2T\) and \(T + V\) changes: for the ring on a rotating rod (example 8.125 below) it grows from \(0{,}05\) J to \(0{,}35\) J.
  2. The potential \(V\) does not contain time (step 2). This holds for weight and for a spring of constant stiffness. It does not hold when a load changes during the motion. Then step 2 gets the term \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial t = -\partial V/\partial t\) and on the right-hand side of equation (8.133) an extra term \(\partial V/\partial t\) remains. In mechanism D the load has the potential \(P\,a\sin\theta\) with \(a = 0{,}5\) m ((8.50)). If the load grew by \(10\) N per second, the curve of the load potential would rise every second, at the position \(\theta = 0{,}505\) rad by the difference, and this term would be \(10 \cdot 0{,}5 \cdot \sin 0{,}505 = 2{,}42\) W.
  3. There are no other forces, \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\) (step 6). This holds when friction and air resistance are small compared with the energy that flows between \(T\) and \(V\). When they are not, the energy decreases by equation (8.133): the pendulum with the damping \(c = 0{,}2\) N m s loses in 10 s \(8{,}35\) J of the \(9{,}81\) J it has above rest at the lowest position.
Example 8.122 Pendulum: speed from the energy and loss by damping (nihalo: hitrost iz energije in izguba pri dušenju)

The pendulum of example A: a point mass \(m = 1\) kg on a rigid light rod of length \(\ell = 1\) m about the pivot, angle \(\theta\) from the vertical downward, \(g = 9{,}81\) m/s\(^2\). The potential is \(V = -mg\ell\cos\theta\), the kinetic energy \(T = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2\).

  1. Release it from rest at \(\theta = 90^\circ\). Then \(T = 0\) and \(V = -mg\ell\cos 90^\circ = 0\), so \(\mathcal{E} = 0\) J.
  2. At the lowest position \(\theta = 0\) we have \(V = -mg\ell = -9{,}81\) J. Since \(\mathcal{E}\) is constant, \(T = 9{,}81\) J, so \(\tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 = 9{,}81\) J and \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2g/\ell} = 4{,}4294\) 1/s. The speed of the mass is \(\ell\,|\dot\theta| = 4{,}43\) m/s.
  3. At \(\theta = 45^\circ\) we have \(T = \mathcal{E} - V = mg\ell\cos 45^\circ\), so \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2g\cos 45^\circ/\ell} = 3{,}7247\) 1/s. We obtained the speed at the intermediate position without the equation of motion.
  4. Release it from rest at \(10^\circ\) and at \(170^\circ\). Then \(\mathcal{E} = -9{,}661\) J and \(+9{,}661\) J, and the speeds at the lowest position are \(0{,}5460\) 1/s and \(6{,}2403\) 1/s. A higher starting position means more energy and therefore a larger speed at the same position.

Let us add a damping moment \(Q^{\mathrm{nc}} = -c\,\dot\theta\) with \(c = 0{,}2\) N m s at the hinge. The power is \(Q^{\mathrm{nc}}\,\dot\theta = -c\,\dot\theta^2 \le 0\), the force is dissipative, and by (8.133) \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{d}t = -c\,\dot\theta^2\). The right part of figure 8.174 shows the energy against time. Without damping it would stay on the zero line. The numerical RK4 solution from \(90^\circ\) gives \(\mathcal{E} = -1{,}869\) J after 1 s, \(-3{,}279\) J after 2 s, \(-6{,}016\) J after 5 s and \(-8{,}348\) J after 10 s. The energy therefore decreases and approaches \(-9{,}81\) J, the value for rest at the lowest position. The curve descends in steps: the loss \(c\,\dot\theta^2\) is largest when the pendulum passes fast through the lowest position, and zero at the turning points, where the pendulum is at rest for an instant. The loss in 10 s is \(\mathcal{E}(0) - \mathcal{E}(10) = 8{,}3477\) J, and the integral \(\int_0^{10} c\,\dot\theta^2\,\mathrm{d}t\) along the solution is also \(8{,}3477\) J, as the time integral of (8.133) requires.

The conservation of energy has a second picture, which tells more about the motion than the bars: we draw the potential as a landscape and the energy as a horizontal line above it. Take the toggle mechanism D (two bars, a slider, a spring and a load \(P = 100\) N at the joint) with the potential \(V(\theta)\) of equation (8.50).

The curve \(V(\theta)\) above the axis of the angle has a valley at \(0{,}505\) rad, which is the stable equilibrium position, and a hill at \(0{,}0375\) rad, the unstable position (section 8.6). We release the mechanism from rest at \(\theta = 0{,}7\) rad. There \(T = 0\), so \(\mathcal{E} = V(0{,}7) = 83{,}4\) J. Since \(\mathcal{E}\) is conserved, we draw a horizontal line at this height. At every position the kinetic energy is \(T = \mathcal{E} - V\), that is the vertical distance from the curve to the line. The kinetic energy is never negative, because \(m(\theta) > 0\) and \(\dot\theta^2 \ge 0\), so the mechanism cannot be at a position where the curve is above the line. It moves between the two points where the line cuts the curve: there \(T = 0\), the mechanism stops and turns back. The hill is \(7{,}3\) J above the line, so the mechanism does not reach it and stays in the valley. To the bars of figure 8.174 this picture adds the range of the motion, which we read off without solving the equation of motion. We check the numbers in the example.

Example 8.123 Toggle mechanism: the velocity-square term (kolenasti mehanizem: člen s kvadratom hitrosti)

Mechanism D has \(T = \tfrac12 m(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2\) with \(m(\theta) = \tfrac13 + 2\sin^2\theta\) kg m\(^2\) and the potential \(V(\theta)\) from section 8.5. The equation of motion is \(m(\theta)\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2 + V'(\theta) = 0\) (section 8.9). We check the conservation of \(\mathcal{E}\) and see what happens if we omit the velocity-square term.

  1. By the product rule and the chain rule (lemma 8.30, item 3) \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\tfrac12 m(\theta)\,\dot\theta^2\big) = m\,\dot\theta\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^3\), since in differentiation \(m(\theta(t))\) also changes with time. So \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \dot\theta\,\big( m\,\ddot\theta + \tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^2 + V' \big)\) and the bracket is the equation of motion, hence zero: the energy line stays horizontal.
  2. Release the mechanism from rest at \(\theta = 0{,}7\) rad. There \(\mathcal{E} = V = 83{,}401\) J. The equilibrium position \(0{,}50523\) rad has \(V = 24{,}609\) J and \(m = 0{,}80185\) kg m\(^2\), so \(T = 83{,}401 - 24{,}609 = 58{,}792\) J and \(|\dot\theta| = \sqrt{2T/m} = 12{,}110\) 1/s.
  3. The other turning point, where \(\dot\theta = 0\) and again \(V(\theta) = 83{,}401\) J, is at \(0{,}14547\) rad. The unstable position at \(2{,}15^\circ = 0{,}0375\) rad has \(V = 90{,}681\) J, which is \(7{,}280\) J more than \(\mathcal{E}\), so the mechanism does not reach it.
  4. RK4 after 3 s with 30 000 steps: \(\mathcal{E}\) changes by \(1{,}7 \cdot 10^{-9}\) J. If we omit the term \(\tfrac12 m'\,\dot\theta^2\), the computed energy drops by up to \(25{,}5\) J in every swing (at \(\theta = 0{,}2848\) rad) and returns to \(83{,}401\) J only when the mechanism is back at its starting position, because \(\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}/\mathrm{d}t\) from step 1 is no longer zero. The velocity-square term is therefore part of the law and must not be dropped.

In the proof of theorem 8.121 we used that the constraints do not contain time. What remains if a constraint moves? Take a ring that slides on a straight rod, while a motor turns the rod in a horizontal plane about the pivot \(O\) with constant angular velocity \(\Omega\). The rod is here at the start and here somewhat later, turned by the angle \(\Omega t\), whatever the ring does. The velocity of the ring therefore has two parts: the part \(\dot r\) along the rod, set by the motion of the ring, and the part \(r\Omega\) across the rod, imposed by the rod. For a rheonomic constraint the kinetic energy by theorem 8.97 has three parts, \(T = T_2 + T_1 + T_0\): \(T_2 = \tfrac12\,\dot{\vek q}^\T\mat M\,\dot{\vek q}\) is a quadratic form of the velocities (for the ring the term with \(\dot r^2\)), \(T_1 = \vek b\cdot\dot{\vek q}\) is linear and \(T_0\) does not contain the velocities (for the ring the term with \(r^2\Omega^2\)). Lemma 8.120 holds only for \(T_2\): for \(T_1\) we have \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial T_1/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = T_1\) and for \(T_0\) the sum is zero. So the conserved quantity changes too, as the next theorem shows. Its proof repeats steps 1 to 4 of the previous one.

Theorem 8.124 Energy integral for rheonomic constraints (energijski integral pri reonomnih vezeh)

Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a twice continuously differentiable motion of a system with ideal holonomic constraints that solves the equations of theorem 8.101 with \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\). Let \(\mat M\), \(\vek b\), \(T_0\) from theorem 8.97 and \(V\) not contain the time \(t\). For the ring on the rod \(T_2\) comes from the part of the velocity along the rod and \(T_0\) from the part across it. Then

\[\mathcal{E}^* = T_2 - T_0 + V \tag{8.134}\]

is a conserved quantity (definition 8.119).

Proof

We must show that \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} - \mathcal{L}\) is constant and equal to \(\mathcal{E}^*\).

  1. \(\mathcal{L} = T - V = T_2 + T_1 + T_0 - V\) does not contain \(t\), so by the chain rule (lemma 8.30, item 1), as in step 2 of the previous proof, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\,\ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\).
  2. By the product rule (lemma 1.82), as in step 3, \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \ddot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} + \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}}\).
  3. Subtract the first from the second. By theorem 8.101 with \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\) we have \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}}\), so \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big( \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \mathcal{L} \Big) = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}} \Big( \dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} - \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} \Big) = 0\).
  4. \(V\) does not contain the velocities, so \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,\partial (T_2 + T_1)/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) (\(T_0\) does not contain the velocities). For \(T_2\) the sum is \(2T_2\) by lemma 8.120, for \(T_1 = \sum_{\mathrm{s}} b_{\mathrm{s}}(\vek q)\,\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\) it is \(\sum_{\mathrm{s}} \dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\,b_{\mathrm{s}} = T_1\).
  5. So the expression in the brackets of step 3 equals \(2T_2 + T_1 - (T_2 + T_1 + T_0 - V) = T_2 - T_0 + V = \mathcal{E}^*\). By corollary 1.86 \(\mathcal{E}^*(t)\) is the same for all \(t\).

Source: Not from the source in this form: GR (1.46) (PDF p. 48) writes the energy integral for scleronomic constraints, where \(T_1 = T_0 = 0\). For rheonomic constraints GR (PDF p. 54) uses the potential \(V^* = V - T_0\) (by the text of the page), which agrees with \(\mathcal{E}^*\). The proof is ours.

This energy integral (energijski integral) differs from \(T + V\), which now changes. The difference \(\mathcal{E}^* - (T + V) = -2T_0 - T_1\) is zero when the constraints are scleronomic. For the ring it is \(-2T_0\), which comes from the transverse part of the velocity imposed by the rod.

Example 8.125 Ring on a rotating rod (obroč na vrteči se palici)

A ring of mass \(m = 0{,}1\) kg slides without friction on a straight rod in a horizontal plane. The rod rotates about one of its points with constant angular velocity \(\Omega = 2\) 1/s, gravity does not act in the plane. This is our model. The coordinate is the distance \(r\) of the ring from the pivot. The position of the ring is \((r\cos\Omega t,\ r\sin\Omega t)\) and the constraint is rheonomic. The velocity of the ring has the components \((\dot r\cos\Omega t - r\Omega\sin\Omega t,\ \dot r\sin\Omega t + r\Omega\cos\Omega t)\), its square is \(\dot r^2 + r^2\Omega^2\), the sum of the squares of the two perpendicular parts of the velocity. So \(T_2 = \tfrac12 m\,\dot r^2\), \(T_1 = 0\) and \(T_0 = \tfrac12 m\,\Omega^2 r^2\), there is no potential and \(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 + \Omega^2 r^2)\) does not contain \(t\). Lagrange's equation is \(m\,\ddot r = m\,\Omega^2 r\) (SymPy).

  1. By theorem 8.124 \(\mathcal{E}^* = T_2 - T_0 = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 - \Omega^2 r^2)\) is conserved. If we release the ring at \(r = 0{,}5\) m with \(\dot r = 0\), its value is \(-\tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot 4 \cdot 0{,}25 = -0{,}05\) J.
  2. From \(\tfrac12 m\,(\dot r^2 - \Omega^2 r^2) = -0{,}05\) J we get \(\dot r^2 = \Omega^2 r^2 - 2 \cdot 0{,}05/m\). At \(r = 1\) m this is \(4 - 1 = 3\) m\(^2\)/s\(^2\), so \(\dot r = 1{,}7321\) m/s. The numerical solution of the equation of motion shows that the ring gets there after \(0{,}658\) s, when the rod has turned by \(\Omega t = 1{,}317\) rad, that is \(75{,}5^\circ\).
  3. Then \(T + V = T = \tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot (3 + 4) = 0{,}35\) J, and at the start \(\tfrac12 \cdot 0{,}1 \cdot (0 + 4 \cdot 0{,}25) = 0{,}05\) J, because there the ring has only the velocity \(1\) m/s across the rod. The sum \(T + V\) grew by 0,30 J. Where from? The rod pushes the ring with the force \(\vek N\) in the direction of rotation. There is no friction, so this force is perpendicular to the rod. But the path of the ring is not along the rod, because the rod turns meanwhile, so the force does work in the actual displacement. The constraint is nevertheless ideal: in a virtual displacement \(\delta r\) with frozen time (section 8.4) the rod is at rest, the displacement is along it and the force perpendicular to it, so there is no work. So there is no generalized constraint force in Lagrange's equation, but in the energy \(T + V\) there is its work.

Finally let us see what the conservation of energy says about the motion of the pendulum. The state of the pendulum is a pair \((\theta, \dot\theta)\) in the plane and a motion is a curve in this plane. Since \(\mathcal{E}\) is constant, every motion lies on a level curve (nivojnica) of the function \(\mathcal{E}(\theta, \dot\theta) = \tfrac12 m\ell^2\dot\theta^2 - mg\ell\cos\theta\), that is, on the curve with the equation

\[\dot\theta = \pm\sqrt{\frac{2\,(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta)}{m\ell^2}} , \tag{8.135}\]

which is real only where \(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta \ge 0\). For the pendulum the motion is unique for a given state (corollary 8.108), so the level curves do not cross, except at the resting positions, where \(\dot\theta = 0\) and the motion is constant. Figure 8.180 shows the level curves for \(m = 1\) kg and \(\ell = 1\) m. The lectures call such a picture of level curves in the plane of states a phase portrait (fazni portret). The level curve \(\mathcal{E} = mg\ell\), which separates oscillation from rotation, is called the separatrix (separatrisa). The pendulum approaches the unstable position \(\theta = \pm\pi\) along it but does not reach it in finite time: in a resting position the motion is constant, and by corollary 8.108 the motion is unique for a given state, so the pendulum would have to have rested there forever. It needs infinite time to reach the unstable position.

From equation (8.135) we read three regions. Since \(T \ge 0\), we have \(\mathcal{E} \ge V \ge -mg\ell\) and the value \(-mg\ell = -9{,}81\) J belongs only to rest at the lowest position (the centre of the curves in the figure). For \(-mg\ell < \mathcal{E} < mg\ell\) we have \(\cos\theta \ge -\mathcal{E}/(mg\ell)\) and for \(|\theta| \le \pi\) this is the interval \(|\theta| \le \theta_{\max}\), where \(0 < \theta_{\max} < \pi\) and \(\cos\theta_{\max} = -\mathcal{E}/(mg\ell)\). The speed is zero at the end points and has both signs in between, so the level curve is a closed curve and the pendulum oscillates between \(-\theta_{\max}\) and \(\theta_{\max}\). For \(\mathcal{E} = 0\) we have \(\theta_{\max} = 90^\circ\), which is our release with the speed \(4{,}4294\) 1/s at the lowest position. For \(\mathcal{E} = mg\ell\) the double-angle formula \(1 + \cos\theta = 2\cos^2(\theta/2)\) gives \(\dot\theta = \pm 2\sqrt{g/\ell}\,\cos(\theta/2)\) for \(|\theta| \le \pi\), and at \(\theta = 0\) it is \(2\sqrt{g/\ell} = 6{,}2642\) 1/s. This curve ends at \((\pm\pi, 0)\), which is the state of rest of the unstable equilibrium position (section 8.6). For \(\mathcal{E} > mg\ell\) we have \(\mathcal{E} + mg\ell\cos\theta > 0\) for every \(\theta\), the speed is never zero and keeps its sign, and the pendulum rotates. At \(\mathcal{E} = 15\) J the value \(|\dot\theta|\) is smallest at the top position, \(\sqrt{2\,(15 - 9{,}81)} = 3{,}2218\) 1/s, and largest at the bottom, \(7{,}0441\) 1/s. The figure shows where oscillation turns into rotation: at the energy that is just enough for the pendulum to reach the top position. There the speed decreases towards zero, so the motion near this energy is slow and the oscillation period gets longer: for an amplitude of \(170^\circ\) it is \(4{,}8935\) s, 2,44 times longer than the \(2{,}0061\) s for a small amplitude (section 8.9).

8.11.2 Cyclic coordinates and generalized momentum (Ciklične koordinate in posplošena gibalna količina)

Energy is one conserved quantity, which belongs to an \(\mathcal{L}\) without time. Are there others? Take a free point of mass \(m\) in the plane \((x, y)\) that weight pulls only in the direction of the \(y\) axis, so with no forces in the direction of the \(x\) axis. Its equation of motion in the \(x\) direction is \(m\,\ddot x = 0\) and \(m\,\dot x\) is constant: the horizontal part of the velocity is the same in all positions. In Lagrange's form \(\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) - V(y)\), which does not contain \(x\), and the equation for \(x\) is \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot x} = 0\). The same structure holds for every coordinate that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain.

Definition 8.126 Generalized momentum (posplošena gibalna količina)

Let \(\mathcal{L}(t, \vek q, \dot{\vek q}) = T - V\) be the Lagrangian of a system with \(n\) generalized coordinates. The generalized momentum belonging to the coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the function of the state

\[p_{\mathrm{s}} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} , \qquad s = 1, \dots, n . \tag{8.136}\]

Since \(V\) does not contain the velocities, \(p_{\mathrm{s}} = \partial T/\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}\). For scleronomic constraints, by step 3 of the proof of lemma 8.120, \(p_{\mathrm{s}} = \sum_{\mathrm{b}} M_{\mathrm{sb}}\,\dot q_{\mathrm{b}}\). A coordinate \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain (that is, \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) in every state) is cyclic (ciklična). For the point in the figure \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = m\,\dot x\) is the horizontal part of the velocity times the mass, and \(x\) is cyclic.

The unit of \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is the unit of the generalized force multiplied by a second: kg m/s if \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a length and N m s if \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) is an angle. For the free point from the introduction \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = m\,\dot x\), which is the momentum (gibalna količina) in the \(x\) direction. For the pendulum \(p_\theta = m\ell^2\dot\theta\), which is the angular momentum (kotna količina) of the mass about the pivot: the mass has the velocity \(\ell\dot\theta\) perpendicular to the rod, and the distance \(\ell\) from the pivot multiplies \(m\,\ell\dot\theta\).

Corollary 8.127 A cyclic coordinate gives a conserved quantity (ciklična koordinata da ohranjeno količino)

Let \(\vek q(t)\) be a motion from theorem 8.101 and let \(q_{\mathrm{s}}\) be a cyclic coordinate, \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\). Then \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is a conserved quantity, as the horizontal part of the velocity is the same in all positions.

Proof

We must show that the time derivative of \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is zero.

  1. The equation for \(s\) from theorem 8.101 is \(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot q_{\mathrm{s}}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial q_{\mathrm{s}}} + Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}}\), which is \(\dot p_{\mathrm{s}}\) by (8.136).
  2. The right-hand side is zero: \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial q_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\), because the coordinate is cyclic, and \(Q_{\mathrm{s}}^{\mathrm{nc}} = 0\).
  3. So \(\dot p_{\mathrm{s}} = 0\) on the whole interval and by corollary 1.86 \(p_{\mathrm{s}}\) is constant.

The momentum and angular momentum of a free system we obtain as cases of a cyclic coordinate. Take \(N\) points with masses \(m_{\mathrm{i}}\) and positions \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}\) in the plane, whose only forces are internal forces from a potential \(V\) that depends only on the distances \(|\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} - \vek r_{\mathrm{j}}|\). If we shift all points by \(a\) in the direction of the \(x\) axis, this is the coordinate \(q_1 = a\), and the remaining coordinates describe the positions of the points relative to this shifted picture, then the distances between the points are the same, so \(V\) does not contain \(a\). Neither does \(T\) contain \(a\), since the velocities do not change if all points are shifted by the same distance. The coordinate \(a\) is therefore cyclic and \(p_{\mathrm{a}} = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} m_{\mathrm{i}}\,\dot x_{\mathrm{i}}\), because each \(x_{\mathrm{i}}\) contains \(a\) with derivative 1. This is the \(x\) component of the total momentum. For a rotation of all points by an angle \(\varphi\) about the origin, \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}} = (x_{\mathrm{i}}, y_{\mathrm{i}})\) changes with the derivative \(\partial \vek r_{\mathrm{i}}/\partial\varphi = (-y_{\mathrm{i}}, x_{\mathrm{i}})\), which is perpendicular to \(\vek r_{\mathrm{i}}\) and of the same length, because the point circles about the origin. The distances and speeds do not change, so \(\varphi\) is cyclic and \(p_\varphi = \sum_{\mathrm{i}} m_{\mathrm{i}}\,(-\dot x_{\mathrm{i}}\,y_{\mathrm{i}} + \dot y_{\mathrm{i}}\,x_{\mathrm{i}})\). By (2.39) this is the \(z\) component of the vector \(\sum_{\mathrm{i}} \vek r_{\mathrm{i}} \times m_{\mathrm{i}}\dot{\vek r}_{\mathrm{i}}\), the angular momentum of the system about the origin. The conservation of momentum and angular momentum of a free system is therefore a special case of corollary 8.127: each belongs to one kind of displacement that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not detect. Energy belongs to a displacement in time. The same is shown by step 2 of the proof of theorem 8.121: \(\mathcal{E}\) is conserved because \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain \(t\).

Example 8.128 Free bar in the plane (prosta palica v ravnini)

The uniform bar of example C (\(L = 2\) m, \(m = 30\) kg, \(J_{\mathrm{G}} = mL^2/12 = 10\) kg m\(^2\)) flies freely in a vertical plane, without constraints, under its weight. This is our model. The coordinates are \(x\) and \(y\) of the centre of mass \(G\) (\(y\) axis upward) and the angle \(\varphi\) of the bar. By theorem 8.92 for planar motion \(T = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{G}}\dot\varphi^2\), the potential of the weight is \(V = mg\,y\), so

\[\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12 m\,(\dot x^2 + \dot y^2) + \tfrac12 J_{\mathrm{G}}\,\dot\varphi^2 - mg\,y . \tag{8.137}\]

The coordinates \(x\) and \(\varphi\) are cyclic, \(y\) is not.

  1. The generalized momenta by (8.136): \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = m\,\dot x\), \(p_{\mathrm{y}} = m\,\dot y\), \(p_\varphi = J_{\mathrm{G}}\,\dot\varphi\). By corollary 8.127 \(p_{\mathrm{x}}\) and \(p_\varphi\) are constant (the spacings and the angles between the positions are equal) and \(p_{\mathrm{y}}\) is not (the parabola): \(\dot p_{\mathrm{y}} = -mg\) from the equation for \(y\).
  2. We throw the bar from \(y = 0\) with velocity \((\dot x, \dot y) = (2, 5)\) m/s and angular velocity \(\dot\varphi = 3\) 1/s. Then \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = 30 \cdot 2 = 60\) kg m/s, \(p_\varphi = 10 \cdot 3 = 30\) kg m\(^2\)/s and \(p_{\mathrm{y}} = 30 \cdot 5 = 150\) kg m/s. The energy is \(\mathcal{E} = \tfrac12 \cdot 30 \cdot (4 + 25) + \tfrac12 \cdot 10 \cdot 9 + 0 = 480\) J.
  3. At the highest position \(\dot y = 0\), and \(\dot x = 2\), \(\dot\varphi = 3\) remain by step 1. Then \(\mathcal{E} = 480 = \tfrac12 \cdot 30 \cdot 4 + \tfrac12 \cdot 10 \cdot 9 + mg\,h = 105 + 294{,}3\,h\), so \(h = 1{,}2742\) m. This is the same as \(\dot y_0^2/(2g) = 25/19{,}62 = 1{,}2742\) m, the known result for a vertical throw, which we have now obtained from the energy.
  4. The bar falls back to \(y = 0\) after \(2 \dot y_0/g = 1{,}0194\) s. In this time the centre of mass moves horizontally by \(2 \cdot 1{,}0194 = 2{,}0387\) m and the bar rotates by \(3 \cdot 1{,}0194 = 3{,}0581\) rad. Figure 8.183 shows the centre of mass at equal time intervals: the horizontal distances are equal because \(p_{\mathrm{x}}\) is constant, the angle grows uniformly because \(p_\varphi\) is constant, and the height follows a parabola because \(p_{\mathrm{y}}\) is not constant.

For the free bar the coordinates were not coupled, and each cyclic coordinate gave the momentum of one motion. What does a cyclic coordinate tell when one body moves on another? Let us return to the prism with the cylinder of section 8.9 and first draw the system at three instants.

Example 8.129 Prism with a cylinder: conserved horizontal momentum (prizma z valjem: ohranjena vodoravna gibalna količina)

The prism \(A\) of mass \(m_{\mathrm{A}} = 1\) kg lies on a smooth horizontal floor, which does not hold it back horizontally, and it neither lifts off nor rotates. Its cross-section is a right triangle with the base \(3a = 0{,}3\) m, the inclined face makes the angle \(\alpha = 30^\circ\) with the floor. A homogeneous cylinder \(B\) of mass \(m_{\mathrm{B}} = 1\) kg and radius \(R = 0{,}05\) m rolls on the face without slipping. This is our model: the bodies are rigid, the floor has no friction, there is no air resistance. The coordinates are \(x\), the position of the vertical side of the prism relative to its starting position, and \(s\), the path of the centre of the cylinder along the face. At \(t = 0\) we have \(x = 0\), \(s = 0\) and everything is at rest. From section 8.9 we take the two energies, the potential measured from the starting position of the cylinder:

\[T = \tfrac12\,(m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}})\,\dot x^2 + m_{\mathrm{B}}\cos\alpha\,\dot x\,\dot s + \tfrac34\,m_{\mathrm{B}}\,\dot s^2 , \qquad V = -m_{\mathrm{B}}\,g\,s\sin\alpha . \tag{8.138}\]

Let us recall where the terms come from. The centre of mass of the prism has the horizontal position \(x_{\mathrm{A}} = x + a\) and the centre of the cylinder \(x_{\mathrm{B}} = x + R\sin\alpha + s\cos\alpha\), so the horizontal velocity of the cylinder is \(\dot x_{\mathrm{B}} = \dot x + \dot s\cos\alpha\) and the mixed term arises in \(T\). The number \(\tfrac34\) is \(\tfrac12\) from the motion of the centre along the face and \(\tfrac14\) from the rotation of the cylinder with \(J = \tfrac12 m_{\mathrm{B}} R^2\) and the angular velocity \(\dot s/R\). The cylinder descends by \(s\sin\alpha\), so \(V\) is negative.

  1. Which coordinate is cyclic? \(\mathcal{L} = T - V\) does not contain \(x\): if we move the prism and the cylinder sideways together, the floor under them is the same and nothing changes. It does contain \(s\), because the height of the cylinder depends on it. The coordinate \(x\) is therefore cyclic and \(s\) is not.

  2. The generalized momentum by (8.136) is \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = \dfrac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot x} = (m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}})\,\dot x + m_{\mathrm{B}}\cos\alpha\,\dot s = m_{\mathrm{A}}\,\dot x + m_{\mathrm{B}}\,\dot x_{\mathrm{B}}\). This is the horizontal momentum of the prism and the cylinder together. By corollary 8.127 it is constant, and since the system starts from rest, \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) at all times. The first equation of motion of the system, \((m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}})\,\ddot x + m_{\mathrm{B}}\cos\alpha\,\ddot s = 0\), is exactly \(\dot p_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\).

  3. Why is it conserved, said with forces? From outside only the two weights and the force of the floor act on the system. The weights are vertical and so is the force of the smooth floor, because a floor without friction cannot pull sideways. The forces between the cylinder and the prism are internal: as the cylinder presses on the prism, so the prism presses back on the cylinder (Newton's third law), and in the sum they cancel. So there is no horizontal external force and the horizontal momentum cannot change. This view adds which assumption carries the result: the smooth floor. If friction held the prism in place, \(x\) would be prescribed, the cylinder would roll with the acceleration \(\tfrac23 g\sin\alpha = 3{,}27\) m/s\(^2\) instead of \(4{,}36\) m/s\(^2\) and its horizontal momentum would grow, supplied by the friction force of the floor.

  4. The coordinate \(s\) is not cyclic and \(p_{\mathrm{s}} = m_{\mathrm{B}}\cos\alpha\,\dot x + \tfrac32\,m_{\mathrm{B}}\,\dot s\) is not conserved: \(\dot p_{\mathrm{s}} = \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial s = m_{\mathrm{B}}\,g\sin\alpha = 4{,}905\) N. From rest it grows in \(0{,}2\) s to \(4{,}905 \cdot 0{,}2 = 0{,}981\) kg m/s.

  5. What does the conservation of \(p_{\mathrm{x}}\) say about the position? The common centre of mass has the horizontal position \(x_{\mathrm{C}} = \dfrac{m_{\mathrm{A}} x_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}} x_{\mathrm{B}}}{m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}}}\) and its derivative is \(\dot x_{\mathrm{C}} = p_{\mathrm{x}}/(m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}}) = 0\). By corollary 1.86 \(x_{\mathrm{C}}\) is constant, equal to its starting value \(\dfrac{1 \cdot 0{,}1 + 1 \cdot 0{,}025}{2} = 0{,}0625\) m. In the figure this is the distance \(0{,}0625\) m from the starting position of the vertical side to the vertical line that passes through the common centre of mass at all three instants.

  6. We check at \(t = 0\), \(0{,}2\) s and \(0{,}39\) s with the solution of section 8.9, \(x = -0{,}944\,t^2\) and \(s = 2{,}18\,t^2\) (in metres, \(t\) in seconds).

    \(t\) (s) \(x\) (m) \(s\) (m) \(x_{\mathrm{A}}\) (m) \(x_{\mathrm{B}}\) (m) \(x_{\mathrm{C}}\) (m)
    \(0\) \(0\) \(0\) \(0{,}1000\) \(0{,}0250\) \(0{,}0625\)
    \(0{,}2\) \(-0{,}0378\) \(0{,}0872\) \(0{,}0622\) \(0{,}0628\) \(0{,}0625\)
    \(0{,}39\) \(-0{,}1436\) \(0{,}3316\) \(-0{,}0436\) \(0{,}1686\) \(0{,}0625\)

    The prism moves away to the left by \(0{,}038\) m and then \(0{,}144\) m, while the cylinder rolls along the face by \(0{,}087\) m and \(0{,}332\) m. Since the masses are equal, the centre of mass of the prism and the centre of the cylinder are always equally far from the line, one on each side, at the last instant \(0{,}106\) m. At \(t = 0{,}2\) s the prism has the velocity \(0{,}378\) m/s to the left and the centre of the cylinder the horizontal velocity \(0{,}378\) m/s to the right, so \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = 1 \cdot (-0{,}378) + 1 \cdot 0{,}378 = 0\). The radius mark shows the rotation of the cylinder by the angle \(s/R\): \(1{,}74\) rad at \(0{,}2\) s and \(6{,}63\) rad, a little more than one turn, at \(0{,}39\) s. In the figure this rotation is clockwise, in the book we count angles positive counter-clockwise, so the rotation is \(-s/R\). In \(T\) the square of the angular velocity appears and the sign has no effect.

  7. The last instant is \(0{,}39\) s and not \(0{,}4\) s, because the model holds only while the cylinder rolls on the face. The cylinder touches the floor when its centre is at the height \(R\), that is at \(s = 0{,}333\) m at \(t = 0{,}391\) s. From there on another constraint holds and the equations above no longer apply.

  8. Energy. The constraints do not contain time, the forces are the two weights and there are no other forces, so by theorem 8.121 \(\mathcal{E} = T + V\) is conserved, and it is zero at the start. At \(t = 0{,}2\) s the velocities are \(\dot x = -0{,}3776\) m/s and \(\dot s = 0{,}872\) m/s, so \(T = 0{,}1426 - 0{,}2851 + 0{,}5703 = 0{,}4277\) J and \(V = -9{,}81 \cdot 0{,}0872 \cdot 0{,}5 = -0{,}4277\) J. At \(t = 0{,}39\) s we have \(T = 1{,}6264\) J and \(V = -1{,}6264\) J. The sum is zero both times, so the closed-form solution conserves the energy.

  9. The two conserved quantities together give the speed without the equations of motion. From \(p_{\mathrm{x}} = 0\) we get \(\dot x = -\dfrac{m_{\mathrm{B}}\cos\alpha}{m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}}}\,\dot s = -0{,}433\,\dot s\). We insert this into \(T\) of (8.138) and get \(T = \Big(\tfrac34\,m_{\mathrm{B}} - \dfrac{m_{\mathrm{B}}^2\cos^2\alpha}{2\,(m_{\mathrm{A}} + m_{\mathrm{B}})}\Big)\,\dot s^2 = 0{,}5625\,\dot s^2\). From \(T + V = 0\) follows \(0{,}5625\,\dot s^2 = 4{,}905\,s\), so \(\dot s^2 = 8{,}72\,s\). At \(s = 0{,}0872\) m, that is at \(t = 0{,}2\) s, this gives \(\dot s = 0{,}872\) m/s, the same as from the solution.

In the lectures the energies are written \(E_{\mathrm{k}}\) and \(E_{\mathrm{p}}\), and the Lagrangian \(L\).

The energy law and cyclic coordinates answer our question: in systems with time-independent constraints and conservative forces \(T + V\) is conserved (level curve in the state plane), and for every coordinate that \(\mathcal{L}\) does not contain, one more generalized momentum is conserved (vertical line through the centre of mass for the prism). We will use the first law in section 8.12 to prove that a motion near a stable equilibrium position stays near it.
